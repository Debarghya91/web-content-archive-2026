Unit 4: Final assignment

Trace a payment end to end and write the investigation.

Free

Trace a payment end to end and write the investigation.

Lesson 4.1: The case

The case

Your final assignment is a payment investigation. Here is the case. A company in London instructs its bank to pay a supplier in Singapore USD 25,000 for invoice INV-8841. The end to end reference is LON-SIN-25000-01. The customer expected the supplier to be credited on the value date, but the supplier says nothing arrived. The customer has called twice, and the second call was angry. You have four messages to work with: the pain.001 from the customer, the pacs.008 the bank sent onward, a pacs.002 status report, and a pacs.004 return. Your job is to work out what happened, prove it from the messages, say who owns the fix, and say what not to do. Read the case twice before you touch the messages. A good investigation starts with the facts, not with a guess about the answer.

Worked example. The customer is a London company that has paid this supplier every month for a year. The payment is routine, the amount is the usual amount, and the invoice reference is the usual format. Nothing about the instruction looks unusual, which is why the case is a good test: the answer is in the messages, not in the story. Your first task is to list the four messages you have, in the order they were created, and to write down the UETR, the amount, the currency and the end to end reference from each one.

Try it yourself.

  1. Write the case facts in five short lines: who paid whom, how much, which invoice, what was expected, what the supplier says.
  2. List the four messages you have, in the order they were created.
  3. Write down the UETR, amount, currency and end to end reference from each message.
  4. Write one sentence saying what you do not yet know.

Lesson 4.2: Tracing the payment

Tracing the payment

Trace the payment message by message. Start with the pain.001: check the debtor, the creditor, the amount USD 25,000, the currency, the requested execution date and the end to end reference LON-SIN-25000-01. Then open the pacs.008 and check the same fields match, and write down its UETR. Then open the pacs.002 and find the transaction status block for that UETR: is it ACCP or RJCT, and does it carry a reason code. Finally open the pacs.004 and read the return reason code and the returned amount. Put the four messages in a timeline with the time each one was created. The timeline is the investigation: it shows what happened, in what order, and which message proves each step. If two messages disagree, trust the message that was created later for the current state, but keep the earlier message as evidence of what was first instructed.

Tracing the payment

  1. pain.001. Check the debtor, the creditor, the amount USD 25,000, the currency and the end to end reference LON-SIN-25000-01.
  2. pacs.008. Check the same fields match, and write down its UETR.
  3. pacs.002. Find the transaction status block for that UETR: RJCT, with a reason code for an incorrect account number.

Worked example. The analyst lays the four messages out in order. The pain.001 and the pacs.008 match: USD 25,000, invoice INV-8841, the same end to end reference, and the same UETR on the pacs.008. The pacs.002 shows RJCT for that UETR, with a reason code for an incorrect account number. The pacs.004 shows the money coming back to the sending bank with the same reason code. Try it yourself.

  1. Build a four-row timeline: message name, time created, UETR, and the key fact it proves.
  2. Check the amount, currency and end to end reference match across the pain.001 and the pacs.008.
  3. Find the status code and reason code in the pacs.002.
  4. Find the reason code and returned amount in the pacs.004, and compare the amount with the original.

Lesson 4.3: Writing the investigation

Writing the investigation

Write your investigation in four short parts. First, what happened: two or three sentences that tell the story in order, with the amount, the dates and the references. Second, which messages prove it: name each message, give its UETR and its key field, and say what it proves. Third, who owns the fix: name the party that must act next, whether it is the customer correcting an account number, the sending bank resending the payment, or the beneficiary bank confirming a credit. Fourth, what not to do: the mistakes to avoid, such as resending a payment before the reason code is understood, promising the customer a date you do not know, or telling the supplier the money is on its way when it has been returned. Keep it under one page. A good investigation note is short, factual, and provable from the messages attached to it.

Worked example. The analyst writes: the payment of USD 25,000 for invoice INV-8841 was instructed correctly, rejected for an incorrect account number, and returned to the sending bank. The pain.001 and pacs.008 prove the instruction, the pacs.002 with RJCT proves the rejection, and the pacs.004 proves the return. The customer owns the fix: correct the supplier account number and instruct the payment again. Do not resend the payment with the same account number, and do not tell the supplier the money is on its way. The note is half a page, and every sentence points at a message.

Try it yourself.

  1. Write part one: what happened, in two or three sentences with amount, dates and references.
  2. Write part two: which messages prove it, naming each message, its UETR and its key field.
  3. Write part three: who owns the fix, naming the party and the next action.
  4. Write part four: what not to do, listing at least two mistakes to avoid.

Quiz 4.3.4. What should you NOT do after a payment is rejected for a wrong account number?





Why: Resending with the same wrong account number repeats the failure.
