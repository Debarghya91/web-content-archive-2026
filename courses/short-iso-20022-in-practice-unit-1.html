Unit 1: Messages and fields

pacs.008, pacs.002, pacs.004, camt.056 and pain.001: what each message does and which fields matter.

Free

Lesson 1.1: pain.001 and pacs.008: the credit transfer pair

pain.001 and pacs.008: the credit transfer pair

A customer credit transfer starts with pain.001, the customer credit transfer initiation. The customer, or the customer's system, sends pain.001 to its bank to instruct a payment. It carries the debtor and creditor, the amount and currency, the requested execution date, the remittance information and the end to end reference the customer chose. The bank checks the instruction, then builds pacs.008, the FI to FI customer credit transfer, to move the money between banks. pacs.008 carries the same core payment details plus the agent chain, the settlement information and a UETR, the unique end to end transaction reference that follows the payment across banks. The two messages describe the same payment at different stages: pain.001 is the customer telling its bank what to do, pacs.008 is the banks doing it. When you investigate, compare the two: the amount, currency, creditor and references should match, and any difference is your first clue.

pain.001 and pacs.008, the credit transfer pair

  1. Customer. The customer instructs its bank to pay.
  2. pain.001. The customer credit transfer initiation carries debtor, creditor, amount, currency, execution date and the end to end reference.
  3. Sending bank. The bank checks the instruction, then builds the interbank message.
  4. pacs.008. The FI to FI customer credit transfer carries the same core details plus the agent chain, settlement information and a UETR.
  5. Banks in the middle. Each bank receives, checks, accepts and sends the payment onward.
  6. Beneficiary bank. The last bank in the chain credits the beneficiary account.
  7. Beneficiary. The beneficiary sees the reference on its statement and matches it to the invoice.

pain.001 is the customer telling its bank what to do. pacs.008 is the banks doing it.

Step 1 of 7
paymentstraininghub.com

Worked example. A furniture maker instructs its bank to pay a timber supplier 8,750 euros. The pain.001 carries the debtor (the furniture maker), the creditor (the supplier), the amount, the currency EUR, the requested execution date, and the end to end reference INV-4471. The bank accepts the instruction and sends a pacs.008 to the supplier bank with the same amount, currency and reference, plus a UETR. The supplier sees INV-4471 on its statement and matches it to the invoice without calling anyone.

Lesson 1.2: pacs.002: the payment status report

pacs.002: the payment status report

pacs.002 is the payment status report. A bank sends it to report what happened to a payment: accepted, rejected, pending or completed. Each pacs.002 points back to the original message using the original message identifier and the original instruction identifier, and it carries the UETR so you can match it to the payment it describes. The heart of the message is the transaction status block, which holds a status code such as ACCP for accepted or RJCT for rejected, and, when something failed, a reason code explaining why. A single pacs.002 can report on several transactions at once, so always read the status block for the transaction you care about, not just the first one in the file. Status reports are the fastest way to answer the question every customer asks: where is my payment, and what happened to it.

pacs.002, the payment status report

  1. Beneficiary bank. A bank reports what happened to the payment.
  2. pacs.002. The payment status report carries the status: accepted, rejected, pending or completed.
  3. Banks in the middle. The report travels back along the chain the payment came by.
  4. Sending bank. The sending bank receives the report for its payment.
  5. Customer. The customer is told the outcome, matched by the original identifiers and the UETR.

Status reports are the fastest way to answer where a payment is, and what happened to it.

Step 1 of 5
paymentstraininghub.com

Worked example. A customer calls to ask where her payment of 2,300 euros to a university has gone. The analyst opens the latest pacs.002 for that payment. The transaction status block shows ACCP, accepted, with the UETR matching the original pacs.008. She tells the customer the payment was accepted by the receiving bank and is on its way to the beneficiary account. One status code, one matched UETR, and the call is answered.

Lesson 1.3: pacs.004: the payment return

pacs.004: the payment return

pacs.004 is the payment return. It sends money back to where it came from when a payment cannot be completed as instructed. Common reasons include a closed beneficiary account, a wrong account number, or a beneficiary bank that cannot credit the account. A pacs.004 is itself a credit transfer in the opposite direction, and it carries the details of the original payment so the receiving bank can match the return to the payment it reverses: the original instruction identifier, the UETR, the amount and currency, and a return reason code. Read the reason code first, because it tells you why the money came back. Then check whether the returned amount matches the original amount. If it does not, note the difference and ask why before you tell the customer anything. A return is not a failure of the payment system. It is the system doing its job when the payment cannot reach its destination.

pacs.004, the payment return

  1. Cannot credit. The beneficiary bank cannot complete the payment: the account is closed, the number is wrong, or the account cannot be credited.
  2. pacs.004. The payment return sends the money back, carrying the original instruction identifier, the UETR and a return reason code.
  3. Sending bank. The sending bank receives the return and matches it to the original payment.
  4. Read the reason code first. The reason code tells you why the money came back.
  5. Compare the amounts. Check the returned amount against the original amount before you tell the customer anything.

Read the reason code first, because it tells you why the money came back.

Step 1 of 5
paymentstraininghub.com

Worked example. A salary payment of 1,850 euros is sent to an employee who closed that account last month. The beneficiary bank cannot credit it, so it sends a pacs.004 back to the sending bank with the reason code for a closed account and the original UETR. The sending bank matches the return to the original pacs.008, credits the employer, and the payroll team asks the employee for a current account. The money is safe, and the reason code explains why it came back.

Lesson 1.4: camt.056: the cancellation request

camt.056: the cancellation request

camt.056 is the cancellation request. A bank sends it when it wants another bank to cancel a payment instruction that has already been sent, for example because the customer asked for the payment to stop, or because the instruction was sent by mistake. The request points at the payment it wants cancelled using the original instruction identifier and the UETR, and it carries a reason for the cancellation. A camt.056 is a request, not a cancellation itself: the receiving bank decides whether the payment can still be stopped, and it answers with a resolution message. If the payment has already been credited to the beneficiary, cancellation may no longer be possible, and the answer will say so. Never tell a customer a payment is cancelled until you have seen the answer. The camt.056 only proves that cancellation was requested.

camt.056, the cancellation request

  1. Customer asks. The customer asks for the payment to stop, or the instruction was sent by mistake.
  2. camt.056. The bank sends a cancellation request naming the payment by its original instruction identifier and UETR, with a reason.
  3. Beneficiary bank checks. The receiving bank checks whether the payment has already been credited.
  4. Not yet credited. The bank can usually stop the payment and confirm the cancellation.
  5. Already credited. Cancellation is not possible, and the customer must ask the beneficiary for the money back.

A camt.056 only proves that cancellation was requested. Never tell a customer a payment is cancelled until you have seen the answer.

Step 1 of 5
paymentstraininghub.com

Worked example. A company realises it sent a payment of 9,600 euros to the wrong supplier. Its bank sends a camt.056 to the beneficiary bank, asking it to cancel the payment and giving the UETR and the reason. The beneficiary bank checks whether the payment has been credited. If it has not, it stops the payment and confirms the cancellation. If it has, it replies that cancellation is not possible, and the company must ask the wrong supplier for the money back.
