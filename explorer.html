MT103: Single Customer Credit Transfer

14 fields · Single version · Related messages listed below

MT103 carries a single customer credit transfer instruction from one bank to another through SWIFT. It tells the receiving bank to pay a named beneficiary a stated amount in a stated currency. Banks use it for cross-border customer payments, including trade, salary, invoice and personal transfers. The message holds ordering customer, beneficiary, value date, charges and remittance details needed for straight through processing.

Base message vs scheme implementation. This is a SWIFT MT (FIN) message. There is no single global scheme rulebook for MT messages: each bank and market practice defines its own format limits, cut-off times and usage rules on top of the SWIFT standard.

Sending and receiving participants. The sending participant is the bank holding the ordering customer account (the debtor agent). The receiving participant is the bank that will pay the beneficiary, either the beneficiary bank itself or the next bank in the correspondent chain.

Message structure. MT103 is a SWIFT MT (FIN) message made of blocks: header blocks 1 and 2, text block 4 with the tagged fields :20: to :77B:, and trailer block 5. Field :59a: names the beneficiary, :32A: carries value date, currency and amount, and :71A: states who pays the charges.

FieldNameM/O/CMeaning
:20:Transaction ReferenceMA unique reference assigned by the sending bank to identify this payment through its whole life. It must not repeat for the same sender within the same business day in most banks. Operations teams use it to trace the payment, match confirmations and answer customer queries about status and value date.
:23B:Bank Operation CodeMA four character code that tells the receiving bank how to handle the transfer. CRED means a normal credit transfer, SPAY means a SWIFT payment, SSTD means a standard payment and CRTS means a transfer related to a cheque. The code guides processing priority and the way charges and settlement are applied.
:32A:Value Date, Currency Code, AmountMThe value date on which funds become available to the beneficiary, the three letter ISO currency code and the amount to be paid. For USD 25,000 on 5 November 2026 the field reads 261105USD25000,00. Value date, currency and amount must match the debit and the settlement instruction exactly.
:33B:Currency Code, AmountOThe currency and amount in which the ordering customer instructed the payment, when that differs from the settlement currency in field 32A. It preserves the original instruction amount for reconciliation and customer reporting. It is used when a bank converts currency before sending the payment onward.
:50a:Ordering CustomerMThe customer who ordered the payment, identified by account number and name and address. Option K gives account, name and address in free text. Option F gives structured party details. Accurate ordering customer data is essential for sanctions screening, anti money laundering checks and beneficiary bank verification.
:52a:Ordering InstitutionOThe financial institution that ordered the transfer when it is not the sender of the message. It is normally the bank holding the ordering customer account in a chain of banks. The field helps the receiver identify the true originator bank and route any return or query to the correct institution.
:53a:Sender's CorrespondentOThe account held by the sender with the receiver, or the correspondent bank through which settlement will occur. It tells the receiving bank where to debit the funds for this payment. Correct correspondent details prevent settlement delays and avoid the payment being held for account identification.
:54a:Receiver's CorrespondentOThe account held by the receiver with another bank, used when the receiver does not hold the sender account directly. It identifies the next bank in the settlement chain. The field is common in multi hop correspondent chains where funds pass through an intermediary before reaching the beneficiary bank.
:57a:Account With InstitutionOThe bank where the beneficiary holds an account, when it is not the receiver of the MT103. It directs the receiving bank to forward the payment to the correct account with institution. Option A gives the BIC, option D gives name and address when no BIC is available.
:59a:Beneficiary CustomerMThe final recipient of the funds, identified by account number and name and address. Option A gives the account and BIC, option F gives structured name and address, and no letter option gives free text. The beneficiary bank credits this account on the value date after passing its own screening checks.
:70:Remittance InformationOFree text information for the beneficiary explaining what the payment is for, such as an invoice number, contract reference or salary month. It is limited to four lines of 35 characters. Clear remittance information helps the beneficiary reconcile the receipt against open invoices and reduces queries to the sending bank.
:71A:Details of ChargesMA three letter code stating who pays the transfer charges. OUR means the ordering customer pays all charges, BEN means the beneficiary pays all charges, and SHA means charges are shared between sender and receiver. The code drives fee deduction at each bank in the chain and must match the customer instruction.
:72:Sender to Receiver InformationOCoded or free text instructions from the sender to the receiver, such as cover payment indicators, regulatory reporting codes or special handling notes. It can carry the phrase /INS/ for intermediary details or /ACC/ for account instructions. Operations teams read it before releasing the payment for further processing.
:77B:Regulatory ReportingORegulatory information required by the authorities of the sending or receiving country, such as balance of payments codes or central bank reporting data. It is mandatory in some corridors and must follow local format rules. Missing regulatory data can cause the payment to be delayed or rejected by the receiving bank.

Scheme-specific rules. MT103 is a SWIFT MT (FIN) message, not a base ISO 20022 message: there is no single global scheme rulebook, and each bank and market practice sets its own format limits and cut-off times on top of the SWIFT standard. Under SWIFT rules the ordering customer and beneficiary must be identified clearly enough for sanctions screening, and field :71A: must state OUR, BEN or SHA so every bank in the chain knows who bears the charges. Under CBPR+ new cross-border customer payments use pacs.008 instead of MT103, so MT103 now survives mainly on corridors not yet migrated.

Valid example. Meridian Bank London sends an MT103, reference MER1035521, for USD 25,000 value 5 November 2024 to Harbour Bank Singapore to pay Bright Logistics for invoice INV-1187. This is a pre-migration example, from before coexistence ended on 22 November 2025. Field :71A: is SHA, the beneficiary account is given in :59a:, and the payment settles on the value date.

Invalid example. An MT103 arrives with field :32A: showing a value date in the past and no beneficiary account in field :59a:. The receiving bank cannot identify where to credit the funds and rejects the message for repair (error: missing mandatory beneficiary account).

Validation errors.

  • Missing mandatory field :59a: beneficiary customer: the receiving bank cannot identify the account to credit and rejects the message.
  • Invalid value date in field :32A:: a past or non-business-day value date fails validation and the payment is repaired or rejected.
  • Missing or invalid charge code in field :71A:: without OUR, BEN or SHA the banks in the chain cannot agree who pays the charges.

Related messages. MT202, pacs.008, MT900, pacs.002

Processing implications. The receiving bank validates the beneficiary, screens the ordering customer and beneficiary against sanctions lists, and checks the value date and charges code before crediting. A payment that fails screening is held for compliance review, and the sender is told the reason so it can correct and resend.

Accounting implications. The sending bank debits the ordering customer account and credits its nostro or settlement account on the value date. The receiving bank credits the beneficiary account and debits its nostro account with the sender. Charges are posted separately to the party named in field :71A:.

MT202: General Financial Institution Transfer

10 fields · Single version · Related messages listed below

MT202 carries a bank to bank transfer between financial institutions, with no customer beneficiary named in the message. Banks use it to move their own funds, settle a cover payment, fund a nostro account or pay another bank. It is the workhorse of interbank settlement and liquidity movement. The message holds sender, receiver, value date, amount and settlement account details needed to move bank funds cleanly.

Base message vs scheme implementation. This is a SWIFT MT (FIN) message. There is no single global scheme rulebook for MT messages: each bank and market practice defines its own format limits, cut-off times and usage rules on top of the SWIFT standard.

Sending and receiving participants. The sending participant is the financial institution moving its own funds (the debtor agent). The receiving participant is the beneficiary institution, or the next bank in the settlement chain that will pass the funds onward.

Message structure. MT202 is a SWIFT MT (FIN) message with header blocks 1 and 2, text block 4 with the tagged fields :20: to :72:, and trailer block 5. Field :58a: names the beneficiary institution, :32A: carries value date, currency and amount, and :21: links the transfer to a related customer payment when it is a cover.

FieldNameM/O/CMeaning
:20:Transaction ReferenceMA unique reference assigned by the sending bank for this interbank transfer. It identifies the payment in the sender and receiver systems and in any related MT103 cover message. Operations teams use it to reconcile nostro movements, match settlement confirmations and investigate any value date or amount mismatch.
:21:Related ReferenceOThe reference of a related message, most often the MT103 that this MT202 covers. Linking the cover to the customer payment lets the beneficiary bank and intermediaries match the two messages and reconcile the funds. It is essential in serial and cover payment models where customer and bank legs travel separately.
:32A:Value Date, Currency Code, AmountMThe value date, ISO currency code and amount of the interbank transfer. For example 261105USD25000,00 means USD 25,000 value 5 November 2026. The amount must equal the customer payment it covers, after any agreed deductions. Settlement systems use this field to post the nostro debit and credit.
:52a:Ordering InstitutionOThe financial institution ordering the transfer when it differs from the sender. In a cover payment this is the bank that sent the original customer instruction. It identifies the true source of funds in the chain and helps the receiver apply the correct nostro account and reporting treatment.
:53a:Sender's CorrespondentOThe correspondent account the sender uses for settlement with the receiver. It tells the receiver which account to debit. In nostro terms this is the account the sender holds with the receiver, or a third bank account named for settlement. Accurate data prevents settlement failures and manual repair.
:54a:Receiver's CorrespondentOThe correspondent account the receiver uses when it does not hold the sender account directly. It names the bank where the receiver expects to receive the funds. The field is used in chains where settlement passes through an intermediary bank before reaching the final receiving institution.
:56a:Intermediary InstitutionOAn intermediary bank in the payment chain between sender and receiver. It is used when the sender has no direct account relationship with the receiver and must route through a third bank. The intermediary forwards the funds and the message, and its BIC or name and address must be accurate.
:57a:Account With InstitutionOThe bank where the receiving institution holds the account to be credited. It directs the sender to the correct destination account for the funds. When the receiver is the account with institution, this field may be omitted, but in multi bank chains it prevents misrouting to the wrong branch or entity.
:58a:Beneficiary InstitutionMThe financial institution that is the final recipient of the funds in this bank to bank transfer. Option A gives the BIC, option D gives name and address. The beneficiary institution credits its own account or uses the funds to settle the related customer payment on the stated value date.
:72:Sender to Receiver InformationOAdditional instructions from sender to receiver, such as cover payment linkage, settlement notes or handling codes. It can carry /BNF/ or /ACC/ coded information that guides the receiver. Operations teams check this field for any condition that changes normal straight through processing of the transfer.

Scheme-specific rules. MT202 is a SWIFT MT (FIN) message, not a base ISO 20022 message: there is no single global scheme rulebook, and each bank sets its own settlement account and cut-off rules on top of the SWIFT standard. When MT202 is used as a cover, field :21: must carry the related MT103 reference so the beneficiary bank can match cover and customer legs. Under CBPR+ the cover model is replaced by pacs.009 COV in ISO 20022, so MT202 cover use survives only on corridors not yet migrated.

Valid example. Meridian Bank London sends an MT202, reference MER202118, for USD 25,000 value 5 November 2024 to Harbour Bank Singapore, with field :21: quoting the related MT103 reference MER1035521. This is a pre-migration example, from before coexistence ended on 22 November 2025. Harbour Bank matches the cover to the customer payment and releases the beneficiary credit.

Invalid example. An MT202 cover arrives with field :21: blank and an amount of USD 24,800 against a customer payment of USD 25,000. The beneficiary bank cannot match the cover to any MT103 and holds the funds as an unmatched receipt (error: cover amount mismatch).

Validation errors.

  • Missing mandatory field :58a: beneficiary institution: the receiver cannot identify the final institution for the funds and rejects the message.
  • Cover amount mismatch: when the MT202 amount differs from the related MT103 amount without an agreed charge deduction, the cover cannot be matched and is held.
  • Missing related reference :21: on a cover payment: the beneficiary bank cannot link the cover to the customer payment and the funds sit unmatched.

Related messages. MT103, MT202 COV, pacs.009 COV, MT900

Processing implications. The receiving bank checks the beneficiary institution, matches the transfer to any related customer payment by reference and amount, and posts the nostro credit on the value date. An unmatched cover is held in a suspense account and investigated with the sender before it is applied or returned.

Accounting implications. The sending bank debits its nostro account with the receiver and credits its own settlement account. The receiving bank credits the nostro account of the sender and debits its settlement account when it applies the funds. A cover leg posts no customer account entries; the customer credit is posted from the related MT103 or pacs.008.

pacs.008: FI to FI Customer Credit Transfer

16 fields · Has version options · Related messages listed below

pacs.008 is the ISO 20022 message that replaces MT103 for customer credit transfers between financial institutions. It carries the full customer payment instruction in structured XML, with rich data for debtor, creditor, charges, remittance and settlement. Banks use it under CBPR+ for cross-border payments. Its structured fields improve screening, reconciliation and straight through processing compared with the free text MT format.

Base message vs scheme implementation. This is a base ISO 20022 message. The structure and fields below are the base definition; scheme-specific rules (CBPR+, SEPA, Fedwire, CHAPS, TIPS) may make an optional element mandatory, restrict a code set, or fix a version. Always check the scheme rulebook for the rail you are working on.

Sending and receiving participants. The sending participant is the debtor agent, the bank holding the debtor account. The receiving participant is the creditor agent, the bank holding the creditor account, with any intermediary agents in between.

Message structure. pacs.008 is an ISO 20022 XML message with a GroupHeader (GrpHdr) followed by one or more CreditTransferTransactionInformation blocks (CdtTrfTxInf). Each transaction block holds payment identification (PmtId), interbank settlement amount and date (IntrBkSttlmAmt, IntrBkSttlmDt), settlement information (SttlmInf), debtor and creditor agents and accounts, charges information (ChrgsInf), purpose (Purp) and remittance information (RmtInf).

Message version. Selected version: pacs.008.001.06. Field rules below are for the base message; scheme rules may add or restrict elements.

FieldNameM/O/CMeaning
GrpHdrGroup HeaderMThe header of the message, holding the message identification, creation date and time, number of transactions and control sum. It identifies the file or message batch for tracking and reconciliation. A unique message identification prevents duplicate processing and lets the receiver confirm receipt of the complete group.
PmtIdPayment IdentificationMThe identification block holding instruction identification, end to end identification and transaction identification. End to end identification stays unchanged through the whole payment chain and lets the debtor and creditor track the payment. Instruction identification is unique per instructing agent and transaction.
IntrBkSttlmAmtInterbank Settlement AmountMThe amount and currency settled between banks for this payment, for example USD 25,000. It is the amount the debtor agent transfers to the creditor agent. It may differ from the instructed amount when charges are deducted or currency conversion occurs, and settlement systems use it to post nostro entries.
IntrBkSttlmDtInterbank Settlement DateOThe date on which settlement between banks takes place. It is the value date for the interbank leg and drives liquidity planning and nostro funding. The date must be a valid business day for the settlement currency and must match the date agreed with the correspondent or clearing system.
SttlmInfSettlement InformationMInformation about how settlement will occur, including the settlement method, settlement account and clearing system. Values include INDA for instructed agent settlement, INGA for instructing agent settlement and CLRG for clearing system settlement. It tells each bank which account and channel to use.
DbtrDebtorMThe customer who ordered the payment, with structured name, postal address and identification. Rich debtor data supports sanctions screening and anti money laundering checks. Under CBPR+ the debtor name and address must be complete, because truncated data can cause the payment to be stopped or returned.
DbtrAcctDebtor AccountOThe account of the debtor from which the payment is debited, identified by IBAN or other account identification. It links the payment to the correct customer account in the debtor agent system. Accurate account data is needed for debit posting, balance checks and customer statement reporting.
DbtrAgtDebtor AgentMThe bank holding the debtor account and sending the payment instruction onward. It is identified by BIC and sometimes by name and address. The debtor agent is responsible for debiting the customer, applying charges, screening the payment and choosing the settlement route to the next bank.
CdtrAgtCreditor AgentMThe bank holding the creditor account and receiving the payment for onward credit. It is identified by BIC and sometimes by name and address. The creditor agent screens the incoming payment, checks the creditor account and credits the beneficiary on the value date after its own controls pass.
CdtrCreditorMThe final beneficiary of the payment, with structured name, postal address and identification. The creditor agent credits this party on receipt of funds. Complete creditor data supports screening, fraud checks and beneficiary verification, and helps the creditor reconcile the receipt against expected payments.
CdtrAcctCreditor AccountOThe account of the creditor to be credited, identified by IBAN or other account identification. It tells the creditor agent exactly where to post the funds. A wrong account number is a common cause of returns and investigations, so banks validate the IBAN check digits before releasing the payment.
ChrgsInfCharges InformationODetails of the charges applied to the payment, including the amount, currency, bearer and the agent that applied each charge. The bearer can be debtor, creditor, shared or following service level. Transparent charges data lets the creditor understand any deduction and supports gpi tracking of fees.
PurpPurposeOA coded purpose for the payment, such as SUPP for supplier payment, SALA for salary, or GDDS for goods. Purpose codes support regulatory reporting, sanctions screening and customer analytics. Banks map customer purpose descriptions to the ISO external purpose code list before sending the message.
RmtInfRemittance InformationOStructured or unstructured information explaining what the payment is for, such as invoice numbers and dates. Structured remittance information lets the creditor reconcile automatically against open items. Unstructured text is limited in length and should carry only the key references the creditor needs.
InstrForCdtrAgtInstruction for Creditor AgentOAn instruction from the debtor agent to the creditor agent, such as hold the payment for pickup or contact the creditor before crediting. It is free text with coded options. The creditor agent must read and follow it before crediting, because it can change the normal credit process.
InstrForDbtrAgtInstruction for Debtor AgentOAn instruction for the debtor agent, normally carried from the original customer instruction. It can request a specific handling such as a telephone advice to the creditor. The debtor agent applies it before sending the payment onward and records it for audit and customer service.

Scheme-specific rules. pacs.008 is a base ISO 20022 message: the structure and fields below are the base definition, and each scheme adds or restricts elements for its rail. Under CBPR+ the message must carry a UETR for end to end tracking, the debtor and creditor names and addresses must be fully structured, and the charge bearer may be DEBT, CRED or SHAR. Under SEPA SCT the charge bearer is SLEV only, the IBAN is mandatory for both accounts, and the 2025 implementation guides fix version pacs.008.001.08.

Valid example. Meridian Bank London sends a pacs.008.001.08 for USD 25,000 value 5 November 2026 to Harbour Bank Singapore, with a UETR, full structured debtor and creditor names and addresses, charge bearer SHAR, and remittance reference INV-1187. Harbour Bank screens the payment, credits Bright Logistics, and reports the status in a pacs.002.

Invalid example. A pacs.008 arrives under CBPR+ with no UETR and a truncated debtor address. The creditor agent cannot track the payment end to end or complete sanctions screening and rejects it (error: missing UETR).

Validation errors.

  • Missing UETR under CBPR+: every pacs.008.001.08 must carry a Universally Unique Transaction Reference, and a message without one is rejected.
  • Incomplete structured debtor or creditor address: CBPR+ requires full name and address data, and truncated party data causes rejection or repair.
  • Invalid IBAN in the creditor account: a failed IBAN check digit means the creditor account cannot be validated and the payment is rejected.

Related messages. MT103, pacs.002, pacs.004, pain.001

Processing implications. The debtor agent debits the customer, screens the payment, and sends the pacs.008 to the next agent. Each agent validates the structure, screens the parties, and forwards or credits the payment. The creditor agent reports acceptance or rejection in a pacs.002, and a payment that cannot be credited is returned in a pacs.004.

Accounting implications. The debtor agent debits the debtor account and credits its nostro or settlement account for the interbank settlement amount. The creditor agent debits its nostro account and credits the creditor account. Charges are posted separately to the bearer named in the charges information, and the control sum in the group header must equal the sum of the transactions.

pacs.009 (Base): FI to FI Credit Transfer (Base)

12 fields · Has version options · Related messages listed below

Base message: pacs.009 is the base ISO 20022 Financial Institution Credit Transfer message. Under CBPR+ it has three implementation variants, each with its own explorer entry: pacs.009 Core (ordinary FI credit transfer), pacs.009 COV (cover payment) and pacs.009 ADV (advice of a cover payment). This entry describes the base message; use the variant entries for scheme-specific rules. pacs.009 is the ISO 20022 message that replaces MT202 for bank to bank credit transfers. It moves funds between financial institutions with no customer beneficiary named, for example to fund a nostro account, move bank liquidity, or settle a bank to bank obligation. It carries settlement amount, settlement date, debtor agent, creditor agent and any related customer payment reference. It is the interbank leg of a CBPR+ payment.

Base message vs scheme implementation. This is a base ISO 20022 message. The structure and fields below are the base definition; scheme-specific rules (CBPR+, SEPA, Fedwire, CHAPS, TIPS) may make an optional element mandatory, restrict a code set, or fix a version. Always check the scheme rulebook for the rail you are working on.

Sending and receiving participants. The sending participant is the debtor agent, the bank moving the funds. The receiving participant is the creditor agent, the bank receiving the funds, with any intermediary agents in between. No customer beneficiary is named in the base message.

Message structure. pacs.009 is an ISO 20022 XML message with a GroupHeader (GrpHdr) followed by one or more CreditTransferTransactionInformation blocks (CdtTrfTxInf). Each transaction block holds payment identification (PmtId), interbank settlement amount and date (IntrBkSttlmAmt, IntrBkSttlmDt), settlement information (SttlmInf), debtor and creditor agents, related payment information (RltdPmtInf) and purpose (Purp).

Message version. Selected version: pacs.009.001.06. Field rules below are for the base message; scheme rules may add or restrict elements.

FieldNameM/O/CMeaning
GrpHdrGroup HeaderMThe header holding message identification, creation date and time, number of transactions and control sum. It identifies the message for tracking, duplicate detection and reconciliation. The message identification must be unique for the sender, and the control sum must equal the sum of the transaction amounts in the group.
PmtIdPayment IdentificationMThe identification block with instruction identification, end to end identification and transaction identification. End to end identification links this bank leg to the related customer payment. It lets operations teams match the pacs.009 cover to its pacs.008 and confirm that settlement and customer instruction agree.
IntrBkSttlmAmtInterbank Settlement AmountMThe amount and currency settled between the debtor agent and the creditor agent, for example USD 25,000. Settlement systems post the nostro debit and credit from this amount. It must match the related customer payment amount after any agreed charges, or the cover will fail reconciliation at the creditor agent.
IntrBkSttlmDtInterbank Settlement DateOThe date on which interbank settlement occurs. It drives nostro funding, liquidity planning and value dating of the related customer payment. The date must be a business day for the settlement currency and must be consistent with the settlement method and the clearing system cut off times.
SttlmInfSettlement InformationMHow settlement will occur, including settlement method and settlement account. INDA means settlement through the instructed agent, CLRG means through a clearing system, and COVE means a cover payment. The method tells each bank which account to debit and credit and which channel carries the funds.
DbtrDebtorOThe debtor in this bank to bank transfer, normally the debtor agent itself or the institution whose funds are moving. It is identified by name and BIC. The field distinguishes the legal debtor from the sending agent when a bank moves funds on behalf of another institution in the chain.
DbtrAgtDebtor AgentMThe bank sending the funds and debiting its account for this transfer. It is identified by BIC. The debtor agent is responsible for funding the payment, choosing the settlement route and sending the message to the next agent in the chain on the stated settlement date.
CdtrAgtCreditor AgentMThe bank receiving the funds and crediting its account. It is identified by BIC. The creditor agent checks the incoming funds against its expected receipts, screens the payment and uses the funds to settle the related customer payment or to fund its own account as instructed.
CdtrCreditorOThe creditor institution receiving the funds, normally the creditor agent itself. It is identified by name and BIC. The field confirms the final destination of the bank funds and supports reconciliation when the creditor agent and the creditor are different legal entities in a group.
RltdRmtInfRelated Remittance InformationORemittance information related to the underlying customer payment, carried so the creditor agent can link the cover to the customer instruction. It can hold the end to end identification or the original remittance reference. Accurate linkage prevents the cover from being treated as an unmatched receipt.
PurpPurposeOA coded purpose for the bank transfer, such as a cover payment or a liquidity transfer. Purpose codes support reporting and internal accounting. Banks use them to classify nostro movements and to distinguish customer related covers from own account funding transfers in their ledgers.
RltdPmtInfRelated Payment InformationOInformation about a related payment, most often the pacs.008 customer credit transfer that this pacs.009 covers. It can hold the related message identification and amount. The creditor agent uses it to match cover and customer legs and to release the customer payment only when both legs agree.

Scheme-specific rules. pacs.009 is a base ISO 20022 message: the structure and fields below are the base definition, and each scheme adds its own rules for its rail. Under CBPR+ it has three implementation variants, each with its own explorer entry: pacs.009 Core for an ordinary FI transfer, pacs.009 COV for a cover payment carrying full customer details, and pacs.009 ADV for an advice of a cover payment. FedNow restricts pacs.009 to liquidity transfers only and forbids its use as a customer payment.

Valid example. Meridian Bank London sends a pacs.009.001.08 for USD 25,000 value 5 November 2026 to Harbour Bank Singapore to fund its nostro account, quoting the related funding instruction in RltdPmtInf. Harbour Bank credits the Meridian Bank nostro account on the value date.

Invalid example. A pacs.009 arrives with a settlement amount of USD 24,800 against a nostro funding instruction of USD 25,000 and no related payment information. The creditor agent cannot match the transfer to any funding instruction and holds the funds as an unmatched receipt (error: amount mismatch).

Validation errors.

  • Missing mandatory creditor agent (CdtrAgt): the receiver cannot identify the bank to credit and rejects the message.
  • Amount mismatch: when the settlement amount differs from the related instruction without an agreed charge deduction, the transfer cannot be matched.
  • Missing related payment information on a cover: without the end to end identification the creditor agent cannot link the pacs.009 to its pacs.008.

Related messages. pacs.009 Core, pacs.009 COV, pacs.009 ADV, pacs.008, MT202

Processing implications. The debtor agent funds the transfer, screens the payment, and sends the pacs.009 to the next agent. The creditor agent matches the transfer to the related instruction by end to end identification and amount before applying the credit. An unmatched transfer is held in suspense and investigated with the sender.

Accounting implications. The debtor agent debits its nostro or settlement account and credits the settlement account for the transfer. The creditor agent credits its nostro account and debits its settlement account when it applies the funds. The base message posts no customer account entries.

pain.001: Customer Credit Transfer Initiation

15 fields · Has version options · Related messages listed below

pain.001 is the ISO 20022 message a corporate customer or payment initiator sends to its bank to instruct a credit transfer. It is the first message in the customer to bank payment chain. It carries payment information, debtor, creditor, amount, execution date and remittance details. Banks receive it through host to host files, APIs or payment hubs, validate it, and then create the interbank pacs.008 to execute the payment.

Base message vs scheme implementation. This is a base ISO 20022 message. The structure and fields below are the base definition; scheme-specific rules (CBPR+, SEPA, Fedwire, CHAPS, TIPS) may make an optional element mandatory, restrict a code set, or fix a version. Always check the scheme rulebook for the rail you are working on.

Sending and receiving participants. The sending participant is the initiating party, usually a corporate customer. The receiving participant is the debtor agent, the bank holding the debtor account, which validates the file and executes the payments.

Message structure. pain.001 is an ISO 20022 XML message with a GroupHeader (GrpHdr) followed by one or more PaymentInformation blocks (PmtInf). Each block groups payments sharing the same debtor, debtor account, execution date and charge bearer, and holds one or more CreditTransferTransactionInformation entries (CdtTrfTxInf) with creditor, creditor account, amount and remittance information.

Message version. Selected version: pain.001.001.03. Field rules below are for the base message; scheme rules may add or restrict elements.

The bank uses it to acknowledge receipt, detect duplicate files and reconcile the total amount against the sum of the individual payments.
PmtInfPayment InformationMA block grouping payments that share the same debtor, debtor account, execution date and charges bearer. One pain.001 file can hold many payment information blocks. Grouping by common attributes simplifies validation and lets the bank process all payments in the block under one debit instruction.

  • Payment already settled: a cancellation request for a payment that has been executed or credited cannot be honoured and is rejected.

Processing implications. If the payment has already been credited, the debtor agent must ask the creditor to return the funds instead, usually by a pacs.004.

Accounting implications. A camt.056 posts no accounting entries itself; it only requests cancellation. If the payment is stopped before posting, nothing is booked. If the payment was already posted, the return is booked as a separate credit entry, and the original debit stays on the ledger for the audit trail.

MT101: Request for Transfer

14 fields · Single version · Related messages listed below

MT101 is a request for transfer that a customer sends to its bank to instruct one or more payments from its account. The bank receives the request, checks the debtor account and the payment details, and then executes each transfer through its own systems. It is used for bulk customer payments and standing instructions. The message carries ordering customer, beneficiary, value date, amount and remittance details for each payment.

Base message vs scheme implementation. This is a SWIFT MT (FIN) message. There is no single global scheme rulebook for MT messages: each bank and market practice defines its own format limits, cut-off times and usage rules on top of the SWIFT standard.

Sending and receiving participants. The sending participant is the customer that instructs the payment, usually a corporate. The receiving participant is the bank holding the debtor account, which validates the request and executes the transfers.

Message structure. MT101 has a header block with the transaction reference, then one or more transfer blocks. Each block holds ordering customer, beneficiary, value date, amount, charges and remittance information. A trailer block closes the message with control totals.

Domestic use continues in some markets, while cross-border customer payments now use pain.001 and pacs.008 under CBPR+ instead of MT101 for new flows.

Invalid example. An MT101 arrives with a value date in the past, a beneficiary account number that fails the IBAN check, and no transaction reference. The bank cannot identify the payment, cannot validate the beneficiary account, and rejects the whole request for repair.

Validation errors.

  • Missing mandatory field :20: transaction reference causes rejection because the bank cannot identify or trace the payment instruction.
  • Invalid IBAN in field :59: beneficiary customer causes rejection because the creditor account cannot be validated or credited.
  • Value date in the past causes rejection or repair because settlement cannot be back valued without an explicit instruction.

Related messages. pain.001, MT103, pacs.008, MT900

Processing implications. The receiving bank validates the debtor account, balance, signing authority and each beneficiary before execution. Failed items are reported to the customer for correction, while valid items proceed to settlement. Bulk files need item level tracking so one bad payment does not block the rest.

Accounting implications. Each executed transfer posts a debit to the debtor account on the value date and a corresponding credit to the settlement or nostro account. Charges are posted separately to the debtor account. Rejected items post nothing, so the ledger only reflects transfers that passed validation and were executed.

The ordering bank sends the customer instruction to the beneficiary bank, while the MT202 COV moves the funds through the correspondent chain. It carries the ordering customer and beneficiary details so every bank in the chain can screen the payment. It links cover and customer legs by reference.

Sending and receiving participants. The sending participant is the ordering bank that moves the settlement funds. The receiving participants are the intermediary and beneficiary banks in the correspondent chain, which screen the payment and apply the funds to the customer instruction.

Message structure. MT202 COV follows the MT202 block layout with a transaction reference, related reference, settlement amount, ordering and beneficiary institutions, and a mandatory sequence B.   • Related reference in field :21: not matching the customer instruction causes a reconciliation break because cover and customer legs cannot be linked.

Accounting implications. The cover posts a debit to the ordering bank nostro account and a credit to the beneficiary bank account on the value date. The beneficiary bank holds the funds in a settlement account until the customer leg is matched. Only after matching does it post the credit to the beneficiary customer account.

ExctnDtExecution DateMThe date on which the liquidity transfer is executed. It is normally the same business day the instruction is sent. The date drives the value dating of both account postings and must be a business day for the currency involved.
DbtrAgtDebtor AgentMThe bank holding the debtor account and instructing the transfer, identified by BIC. It is responsible for checking the source balance, applying the debit and sending the instruction to the system that holds the destination account.
CdtrAgtCreditor AgentMThe bank holding the creditor account, identified by BIC. It receives the instruction, checks the destination account and applies the credit. In most camt.050 flows the debtor and creditor agents are the same bank or members of the same group.
PurpPurposeOThe purpose of the liquidity transfer, using a coded value such as a funding transfer or a sweep. Purpose codes support treasury reporting and help the bank classify the movement. The code must match the treasury instruction or the transfer may be posted to the wrong internal account.
AddtlInfAdditional InformationOAdditional information about the transfer, such as the treasury instruction reference or a narrative description. It can carry the reference the treasury team uses to reconcile the movement. The receiving system uses it as a fallback matching key when the account data alone does not match.
InstrIdInstruction IdentificationMThe identification of the liquidity transfer instruction, assigned by the instructing treasury team. It lets the treasury system track the transfer through execution and settlement. It must be unique so a duplicate instruction can be detected before the funds move twice.
EndToEndIdEnd to End IdentificationOThe end to end identification for the transfer, which stays unchanged through the whole movement. It lets the treasury team and both account systems refer to the same transfer. It is useful when a transfer is queried by its own reference rather than by account or amount.
ChrgsInfCharges InformationOCharges applied to the liquidity transfer, when the bank charges for the internal movement. It can show the amount and bearer of the fee. Treasury uses it to reconcile internal charges against its cost schedule and to query any charge it does not recognise.

Scheme-specific rules. camt.050 is the base ISO 20022 liquidity transfer message, used mainly for internal treasury movements with no external scheme rulebook. CBPR+ does not define a liquidity scheme, so banks apply their own internal rules for cut off times, balance checks and authorisation. Domestic clearing systems may restrict camt.050 to accounts held within the same legal entity.

Valid example. Meridian Bank treasury sends a camt.050 to move EUR 5,000,000.00 from its central euro account to its nostro account at Harbour Bank on 6 November 2026, funding the afternoon payment run. The source balance is sufficient, the destination is an internal nostro account, and both accounts update at once on execution.

Invalid example. A camt.050 arrives instructing EUR 5,000,000.00 from an account that holds only EUR 2,000,000.00, with a creditor account outside the bank group. The transfer cannot be funded and the destination is not an internal account, so the instruction is rejected for insufficient funds and invalid destination.

Validation errors.

  • Insufficient funds in the source account causes rejection because the transfer cannot be funded without breaching the account balance.
  • Creditor account outside the bank group causes rejection because camt.050 moves liquidity internally and cannot pay an external account.
  • Execution date on a non business day causes a hold because the transfer cannot be value dated when the currency system is closed.

Related messages. camt.052, camt.053, MT202, pacs.009 Core

Processing implications. The receiving system checks the source balance, the destination account and the execution date before applying the transfer. Valid transfers post immediately and both accounts update at once. Failed transfers are reported to the treasury team, which corrects the amount or account and resends the instruction.

Accounting implications. The liquidity transfer posts a debit to the source account and a credit to the destination account within the same bank or group, with no customer entry involved. Treasury records it as an internal movement between its own accounts. The entries must net to zero across the group, or the liquidity position will be misstated.

camt.052: Bank to Customer Account Report

12 fields · Has version options · Related messages listed below

camt.052 is the ISO 20022 bank to customer account report that a bank sends during the day, between final statements. It lists entries posted since the last statement, with opening and interim balances. Corporate treasuries use it to watch intraday cash, confirm expected receipts, and manage liquidity. It carries booking date, value date, amount, currency and status for each entry posted so far.

Base message vs scheme implementation. This is a base ISO 20022 message. The structure and fields below are the base definition; scheme-specific rules (CBPR+, SEPA, Fedwire, CHAPS, TIPS) may make an optional element mandatory, restrict a code set, or fix a version. Always check the scheme rulebook for the rail you are working on.

Sending and receiving participants. The sending participant is the account servicing bank that holds the customer account. The receiving participant is the account owner, usually a corporate treasury team, which uses the intraday entries to manage liquidity during the day.

Message structure. camt.052 has a group header, then one or more report blocks. Each report block holds the account, report identification, period covered, balance blocks, and a repeating block of entries with entry details. The document outline follows the standard ISO 20022 account report structure with entries grouped by account.

Message version. Selected version: camt.052.001.02. Field rules below are for the base message; scheme rules may add or restrict elements.

Scheme-specific rules. camt.052 is the ISO 20022 equivalent of the MT942 interim report, used for intraday account reporting under CBPR+ and domestic reporting schemes. Each bank agrees the reporting frequency and intraday cut off times with the customer. The message reports account activity rather than carrying a payment instruction, so no payment scheme rules apply to its content.

Valid example. Meridian Bank sends a camt.052 to a corporate customer at 13:00 on 6 November 2026 for account 77012345, opening balance EUR 168,250.00, showing a credit of EUR 25,000.00 received that morning. The interim closing balance is EUR 193,250.00, and the treasury team uses it to confirm a supplier payment can be released that afternoon.

Invalid example. A camt.052 arrives at midday with no report identification and a closing balance that does not equal the opening balance plus the listed entries. The treasury team cannot trust the intraday position, so it discards the report and asks Meridian Bank for a corrected intraday report.

Validation errors.

  • Missing report identification causes a sequence break because the customer cannot confirm the report fits the intraday series.
  • Interim closing balance not equal to opening balance plus entries causes a load failure because the report is internally inconsistent.
  • Entry with an invalid value date causes a repair because the treasury system cannot post the entry to the correct period.

Related messages. camt.053, MT942, camt.054, MT940

Processing implications. The customer treasury system loads the report and updates its intraday cash position with each entry, marking them provisional until the final statement arrives. Entries are matched to expected receipts and payments. A mismatch between the report balance and the forecast triggers a check with the bank before further payments are released.

Accounting implications. The camt.052 itself posts nothing, since it only reports account activity. The entries it lists were already posted to the ledger when they settled. The customer uses the report to check its intraday position, and any difference between the report and the ledger creates a reconciliation item to investigate.

camt.054: Bank to Customer Debit Credit Notification

12 fields · Has version options · Related messages listed below

camt.054 is the ISO 20022 bank to customer debit credit notification that a bank sends when a single entry is posted to an account. It tells the customer that a stated amount was debited or credited on a stated value date. Corporates use it for real time reconciliation, matching each notification to an expected payment or receipt. It carries booking date, value date, amount, currency and entry details.

Base message vs scheme implementation. This is a base ISO 20022 message. The structure and fields below are the base definition; scheme-specific rules (CBPR+, SEPA, Fedwire, CHAPS, TIPS) may make an optional element mandatory, restrict a code set, or fix a version. Always check the scheme rulebook for the rail you are working on.

Sending and receiving participants. The sending participant is the account servicing bank that posted the debit or credit. The receiving participant is the account owner, usually a corporate, which needs immediate notice that a specific entry was posted to its account.

Message structure. camt.054 has a group header, then one or more notification blocks. Each notification block holds the account, notification identification, and a repeating block of entries with entry details and an optional balance. The document outline follows the standard ISO 20022 notification structure with one entry per notification in most use.

Scheme-specific rules. camt.054 is the ISO 20022 equivalent of the MT900 and MT910 confirmations, used for real time debit and credit notifications under CBPR+ and domestic reporting schemes. Each bank agrees with the customer whether to send camt.054, MT900, MT910, or both for the same entry. The message reports an account posting rather than carrying a payment instruction.

Valid example. Banco Santander Madrid sends a camt.054 to BNP Paribas Paris notifying a credit of USD 367,574.90 on 6 November 2026 to account 44098712, reference COV77812. The value date matches the settlement date, the amount matches the expected cover receipt, and BNP Paribas reconciles the credit and releases the customer payment.

Invalid example. A camt.054 arrives notifying a credit of USD 367,574.90 but the receiving customer expected the credit on a different value date and cannot find a matching account. The notification cannot be reconciled, so it sits unmatched until Meridian Bank confirms the account number and the value date.

Validation errors.

  • Unknown account number causes a reconciliation break because the customer cannot match the notification to any account it holds.
  • Credit amount differing from the expected receipt causes an exception because the customer cannot confirm full settlement of the underlying payment.
  • Notification identification duplicated causes a duplicate warning because the customer system cannot tell whether the entry was posted once or twice.

Related messages. camt.053, camt.052, MT900, MT910

Processing implications. The customer system matches the notification against expected receipts and payments by account, amount, value date and reference. Matched notifications close the expected item automatically. Unmatched notifications go to an exceptions queue, and the customer queries the bank before treating the entry as a confirmed receipt or payment.

Accounting implications. The camt.054 itself posts nothing, since the debit or credit was already posted by the bank. The customer uses it to confirm its own ledger entry for the same movement, matching amount and value date. Any difference creates a reconciliation break that must be resolved before the books can close.
