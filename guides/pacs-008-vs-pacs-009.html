pacs.008 vs pacs.009

By Payments Training Hub · Last reviewed 18 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • pacs.008 carries a customer credit transfer: a customer instructs a bank to pay another customer. pacs.009 carries a bank-to-bank transfer: one financial institution pays another.
  • The field structures differ because the parties differ: pacs.008 names a debtor and creditor, pacs.009 names an instructing agent and an instructed agent.
  • Cover payments use both: the customer leg travels as pacs.008 and the bank-to-bank cover leg travels as pacs.009.
  • Using pacs.009 for a customer payment, or pacs.008 for a bank settlement leg, is a message error that downstream systems and schemes will reject.

Reading time: about 6 min

What it is

pacs.008 (FIToFICustomerCreditTransfer) is the ISO 20022 message a bank sends to another bank to carry out a customer credit transfer: a customer of the sending bank pays a customer of the receiving bank. pacs.009 (FIToFIFinancialInstitutionCreditTransfer) is the message one financial institution sends to another to move its own funds: a bank-to-bank transfer, with no customer payment behind it. The two messages look similar, they share building blocks, and they are routinely confused. They are not interchangeable.

The MT equivalents make the distinction clear: pacs.008 is the successor to the MT103 (single customer credit transfer), and pacs.009 is the successor to the MT202 (general financial institution transfer). If you know which MT message a payment would have used, you usually know which pacs message it needs.

Why it matters

The distinction decides validation, charges, reporting and settlement. A pacs.008 carries customer data, charge options, and remittance information that a pacs.009 does not need. A pacs.009 carries settlement details between institutions that a pacs.008 handles differently. Schemes validate each message against its own usage guidelines, and a message sent with the wrong type fails validation, or worse, passes validation and settles with the wrong economics.

For a BA, the distinction matters in mapping, in testing, and in investigations. Mapping an MT103 to a pacs.009 loses the customer data. Testing a cover payment needs both messages, in the right roles. Investigating a payment starts by asking which message carried it, because that decides which parties, which accounts, and which rules apply.

How it works in a bank

pacs.008, the customer leg. A customer instructs their bank (often with a pain.001). The bank validates, debits the customer, and builds a pacs.008 naming the debtor, the debtor agent, the creditor agent, and the creditor, with the amount, the charge option, the remittance information, and the UETR. The message travels to the creditor's bank, directly or through intermediaries, and the creditor's bank credits the customer.

pacs.009, the bank leg. A bank needs to move its own funds: funding a nostro account, settling a position, or covering a customer payment. It sends a pacs.009 naming the instructing agent and the instructed agent, with the settlement details. No customer is named, because no customer payment is being made. The receiving bank credits the sending bank's account.

Cover payments, both together. In a cover payment, the customer instruction and the funds move on separate legs. The pacs.008 carries the customer payment details from the debtor's bank to the creditor's bank, and the pacs.009 carries the cover funds between the banks' accounts. The two legs are linked by references, and reconciliation matches them. This is the structure the MT202 COV handled in the MT world, with full transparency on the cover leg.

Settlement legs. When a clearing system or a central bank settles between institutions, the bank-to-bank leg is a pacs.009. Customer legs never settle directly between central bank accounts; the pacs.009 is the settlement instrument.

Worked example

A company in London pays a supplier in Tokyo 100,000 US dollars, through correspondent banks, using the cover method.

The pacs.008. The London bank builds a pacs.008: debtor is the company, creditor is the supplier, amount 100,000 dollars, charge option OUR, remittance information carries the invoice number, UETR assigned. The message goes to the Tokyo bank, which will credit the supplier.

The pacs.009. The London bank also needs to fund the payment: it sends a pacs.009 to its US dollar correspondent, instructing agent to instructed agent, amount 100,000 dollars, with the settlement account details. The correspondent debits the London bank's nostro account and the funds move to the Tokyo bank's account.

The match. The Tokyo bank receives the pacs.008 (the instruction) and the funds (via the pacs.009 chain). It matches them by reference, credits the supplier, and sends pacs.002 status reports on both legs. If the pacs.009 had been sent as a pacs.008, the correspondent would have received a customer payment it could not apply, and the chain would have broken.

Technical detail

Parties. pacs.008: Dbtr (debtor), DbtrAgt (debtor agent), CdtrAgt (creditor agent), Cdtr (creditor), plus intermediaries (IntrmyAgt). pacs.009: InstgAgt (instructing agent), InstdAgt (instructed agent), plus intermediaries. The party set is the fastest way to tell the messages apart.

Charges. pacs.008 carries charge bearer options (DEBT, CRED, SHAR, SLEV) that decide who pays. pacs.009 has no customer charges: interbank charges, where they exist, are handled by agreement between the institutions.

Remittance. pacs.008 carries structured and unstructured remittance information for the creditor's reconciliation. pacs.009 carries no customer remittance data.

References. Both messages carry the UETR, the instruction identification, and the end-to-end identification, but their roles differ: in pacs.008 the EndToEndId links debtor to creditor, while in pacs.009 the references link the institutions' settlement records.

Common mistakes

Using pacs.009 for a customer payment. The customer data has nowhere to go, the creditor's bank cannot apply the payment, and the scheme validation fails.

Using pacs.008 for a settlement leg. The message names customers who do not exist in the leg, and the receiving bank's settlement processing rejects it.

Mixing the legs in a cover payment. Sending the cover as a second pacs.008, or the customer leg as a pacs.009, breaks the match and strands the funds.

Copying MT103 fields into pacs.009. Field 50 (ordering customer) and field 59 (beneficiary) belong to the customer leg. The bank leg uses agent fields, and copying customer fields across corrupts the message.

Assuming the messages are interchangeable because they share blocks. Shared building blocks do not mean shared purpose. The usage guidelines for each message are separate, and validation enforces them separately.
