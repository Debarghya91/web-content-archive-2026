RTGS vs NEFT vs IMPS vs UPI: How Indian Payment Systems Differ

By Payments Training Hub · Last reviewed 19 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • RTGS settles each payment individually in real time, for high-value transfers of Rs 2 lakh and above.
  • NEFT settles in batches on a deferred net basis, with no minimum or maximum amount, and runs 24x7.
  • IMPS settles instantly, 24x7, for transfers up to Rs 5 lakh.
  • UPI is the instant, mobile-first system for person-to-person and person-to-merchant payments, typically up to Rs 1 lakh per transaction.
  • The right system depends on value, urgency and channel, not on habit. Banks encode the choice as routing rules.

Reading time: about 8 min

RTGS: Real-Time Gross Settlement

RTGS is the Reserve Bank of India's system for high-value payments. Real-time means settlement happens continuously as each payment arrives. Gross means each payment settles individually, not netted against others. Settlement happens in central bank money, so it is final and irrevocable the moment it completes. The minimum is Rs 2 lakh and there is no upper limit, which is why RTGS is used for property purchases, treasury movements and large interbank transfers. RTGS has run 24 hours a day, 7 days a week since 14 December 2020. Because each payment needs central bank funds at the moment it is sent, RTGS is not used for everyday retail payments.

NEFT: National Electronic Funds Transfer

NEFT is the RBI's batch-based system for electronic fund transfers. Instructions are collected, sorted into batches, and settled on a deferred net basis: only the net amount owed between banks is moved. There is no minimum or maximum amount, and NEFT has run 24x7 since December 2019, with batches settling every half hour. A NEFT payment typically reaches the beneficiary within a couple of hours. It is the default choice for salary payments, supplier payments and any transfer where immediate finality is not required.

IMPS: Immediate Payment Service

IMPS is NPCI's instant payment system, available 24x7 including Sundays and bank holidays. A payment settles in seconds, and the beneficiary is credited immediately. The limit is Rs 5 lakh per transaction. IMPS is the system to use when a payment is urgent and too large for UPI, or when it must go through at a time when other systems are closed. It works through internet banking, mobile banking and ATMs, using the beneficiary's account number and IFSC or mobile number and MMID.

UPI: Unified Payments Interface

UPI is NPCI's instant, mobile-first payment system, and the one most Indians use every day. A payment is initiated from a mobile app using a UPI ID, a mobile number or a QR code, and it settles in seconds, 24x7. The typical limit is Rs 1 lakh per transaction, with higher limits for specified categories such as capital markets and insurance. UPI covers person-to-person transfers and person-to-merchant payments, including small-value retail purchases. It is not the system for a Rs 50 lakh property payment; that goes through RTGS.

Which System for Which Payment?

A Rs 80 lakh house purchase. RTGS: the value is far above the IMPS and UPI limits, the payment must be final the same day, and the seller's bank needs certainty before releasing the property documents.

Monthly salary for 2,000 employees. NEFT: thousands of routine payments, no urgency to the second, and batch settlement keeps the cost low.

An urgent Rs 3 lakh transfer at 11 PM on a Sunday. IMPS: instant, 24x7, and within the Rs 5 lakh limit.

Paying a shopkeeper Rs 850. UPI: instant, mobile, no minimum, and built for exactly this.

A Rs 1.5 lakh transfer that is not urgent. NEFT or UPI, depending on the channel: UPI if the payer is on a mobile app and the amount is within the limit, NEFT if it is a bank-initiated bulk payment.

Clearing vs Settlement in India

The same distinction applies here as everywhere else: clearing is the exchange and sorting of instructions, settlement is the movement of funds. In RTGS, IMPS and UPI, clearing and settlement happen together, in real time. In NEFT, clearing happens through the day and settlement happens at each batch cycle. A payment that has been cleared but not yet settled has not truly been paid, which is why the timing of each system matters for the promise a bank makes to its customer. The full explanation is in Clearing vs Settlement, and the global comparison in RTGS vs ACH.

What This Means for a BA

On an Indian payments project, the BA documents the routing rules: which system a payment takes at which value, which channel offers which systems, what the customer is told about timing, and what happens when a payment fails in each system. The rules differ by bank, by channel and by product, and they change when the RBI or NPCI changes a limit or a cut-off. The BA also owns the failure handling: a UPI payment that times out, an IMPS payment that is debited but not credited, a NEFT payment that bounces on an incorrect account number. Each failure has a different resolution path, and the BA writes it down before the system goes live.
