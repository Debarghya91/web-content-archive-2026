camt.029 Explained

By Payments Training Hub · Last reviewed 09 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • The camt.029 is the Resolution of Investigation message.
  • The camt.029 is the second half of a conversation.
  • The camt.029 carries a status code that states the outcome in a single value.

Reading time: about 11 min

What it is

The camt.029 is the Resolution of Investigation message. It is an ISO 20022 message that a bank sends to answer an investigation about a payment. The most common investigations it answers are cancellation requests, but it is also used to answer other payment investigations, such as a request to confirm whether a payment was received, or a request to correct payment details.

The camt.029 is the second half of a conversation. The first half is the investigation request: a camt.056 (FI to FI Payment Cancellation Request) or a camt.055 (Customer Payment Cancellation Request). The camt.029 is the reply. It tells the requesting bank what happened: whether the cancellation was accepted, whether it is still being processed, or whether it was rejected and why.

The camt.029 carries a status code that states the outcome in a single value. The three statuses for a cancellation are RJCR (rejected), ACSP (accepted and pending), and ACCP (accepted and completed). A bank that receives a camt.029 can act on the status code without reading the whole message, which is why the codes are standardised across all banks.

The camt.029 does not move money. It moves information. When a cancellation is accepted, the funds are returned separately, usually as a pacs.004 return. The camt.029 tells the requesting bank that the return is coming. When a cancellation is rejected, the camt.029 tells the requesting bank that the funds are staying where they are.

Why it matters

A cancellation request without an answer is worse than no cancellation request at all. The sending bank has told the customer it is trying to stop the payment, but it does not know whether the payment was stopped. The customer is waiting, the money is in limbo, and the bank cannot give a straight answer. The camt.029 closes that loop. It gives the sending bank a definite outcome it can act on and report to the customer.

The camt.029 also matters because it carries the reason when a cancellation is rejected. A rejection with no reason leaves the sending bank guessing. A rejection with a reason code, such as the payment already being credited or the beneficiary refusing to return the funds, tells the sending bank exactly what happened and what its options are. The reason code is what turns a failed cancellation into a case the bank can work on, rather than a dead end.

For operations teams, the camt.029 is the message that drives the next step. An ACCP means credit the money back to the customer and close the case. An RJCR means tell the customer the payment cannot be stopped and consider the next step, which may be contacting the beneficiary directly. An ACSP means wait for a final answer and chase the holding bank if it does not come. Each status triggers a different workflow, and the operations team needs to know which one applies.

For a business analyst, the camt.029 appears in exception handling requirements alongside the camt.056 and the camt.055. The payment hub needs to receive the camt.029, match it to the original cancellation request, update the payment status, and trigger the right follow-up action. A hub that receives camt.029 messages but does not process them will leave cancellation cases open indefinitely.

How it works in a bank

The investigation request arrives. A bank receives a camt.056 from the previous bank in the chain, asking it to cancel a payment, or a camt.055 asking it to cancel on behalf of a customer. The payment hub matches the request to the original payment using the UETR and the original message identification. If no matching payment is found, the bank replies with a rejection, because it cannot cancel a payment it does not hold.

The bank checks whether it can act. The bank checks the status of the payment. If the payment is still in its queue and has not been forwarded or settled, the bank can cancel it. If the payment has already been forwarded, the bank forwards the camt.056 to the next bank in the chain and waits for that bank's answer. If the payment has already been settled and credited to the beneficiary, the bank must ask the beneficiary for consent to return the funds, which takes time.

The bank sends the camt.029. The outcome decides the status code. If the bank can cancel the payment and has done so, it sends a camt.029 with status ACCP. If the bank has accepted the request but needs more time, for example because it is waiting for the beneficiary's consent, it sends a camt.029 with status ACSP. If the bank cannot cancel the payment, it sends a camt.029 with status RJCR and a reason code explaining why.

The status travels back up the chain. Each bank in the chain that forwarded the camt.056 receives the camt.029 and forwards it to the bank that sent it the request. The camt.029 travels back up the chain until it reaches the bank that first asked for the cancellation. That bank reads the status and acts on it.

The requesting bank acts on the status. An ACCP means the cancellation is complete. The requesting bank expects the funds to be returned, credits the money back to the customer when it arrives, and closes the case. An ACSP means the cancellation is accepted but pending. The requesting bank waits for a final camt.029 and chases the holding bank if none arrives. An RJCR means the cancellation failed. The requesting bank tells the customer, and the case moves to the next step, which may be a direct approach to the beneficiary or a fraud report.

The funds are returned separately. The camt.029 is an information message. When a cancellation is accepted, the holding bank returns the funds as a pacs.004 return, which is a separate message. The requesting bank matches the pacs.004 to the original payment by the UETR and credits the customer. The camt.029 and the pacs.004 together complete the cancellation: one says it happened, the other moves the money.

Worked example

A UK company, Harrow Engineering Ltd, paid EUR 40,000 to a supplier in France, Lyon Precision SARL, for invoice INV-2026-2091. The payment was instructed on a Monday and credited to the supplier's account on Tuesday. On Wednesday, Harrow's finance team discovers the invoice had already been paid the previous week. The EUR 40,000 payment is a duplicate, and Harrow asks its bank to get the money back.

Harrow's bank sends a camt.056 to the correspondent bank, asking for a cancellation. The correspondent bank has already forwarded the payment, so it forwards the camt.056 to the supplier's bank in Lyon. The supplier's bank receives the camt.056 and checks the payment. It has already been credited to Lyon Precision's account, so the bank cannot simply take the money back. It must ask the beneficiary for consent.

The supplier's bank sends a camt.029 with status ACSP, accepted and pending, back up the chain. It means the bank has accepted the request and is waiting for the beneficiary's answer. Harrow's bank receives the ACSP and tells Harrow that the cancellation is in progress and the supplier has been asked to return the funds.

On Thursday, Lyon Precision agrees to return the money. The supplier's bank sends a second camt.029 with status ACCP, accepted and completed, and returns the EUR 40,000 as a pacs.004. Harrow's bank receives the pacs.004, matches it to the original payment by the UETR, and credits EUR 40,000 back to Harrow Engineering's account. The case is closed.

If Lyon Precision had refused to return the money, the supplier's bank would have sent a camt.029 with status RJCR and a reason code stating that the beneficiary refused. Harrow's bank would have told Harrow that the payment could not be recovered through the banking chain, and Harrow would have had to settle the matter directly with the supplier.

Technical detail

The three status codes. The camt.029 uses a standard set of investigation status codes. For a cancellation request, the three that matter are RJCR, ACSP, and ACCP. RJCR means the cancellation request is rejected. The message carries a reason code that explains why, such as the payment already being credited, the beneficiary refusing to return the funds, or the original payment not being found. ACSP means the cancellation request is accepted and the cancellation is pending. The bank has agreed to cancel but has not finished doing so, usually because it is waiting for the beneficiary's consent or for a check to complete. ACCP means the cancellation request is accepted and the cancellation is completed. The payment has been stopped or the funds have been returned.

ACSP is not a final answer. A camt.029 with status ACSP is an interim reply. It means the bank is working on the cancellation, not that the cancellation is done. The bank that sent the ACSP is expected to send a second camt.029 with ACCP or RJCR when the cancellation is finalised. A payment hub that treats an ACSP as a completed cancellation will close the case too early and may never notice that the funds did not come back. The hub should keep the case open on an ACSP and set a deadline for the final answer.

The reason code on a rejection. When a camt.029 carries RJCR, it also carries a reason code from the ISO 20022 external code list. Common reasons include the payment already being credited to the beneficiary, the beneficiary refusing to return the funds, the original payment instruction not being found, and the cancellation request arriving too late. The reason code is the most useful part of a rejection, because it tells the requesting bank whether there is anything else it can do. A rejection because the beneficiary refused is final through the banking chain. A rejection because the payment was not found may mean the request went to the wrong bank and can be re-sent correctly.

camt.029 and camt.055. The camt.029 also answers the camt.055, the Customer Payment Cancellation Request. The camt.055 is used when the cancellation is initiated on the customer side and the customer's bank sends the request into the chain. The camt.029 that answers it uses the same three status codes. A payment hub that handles customer cancellations needs to process camt.029 replies to camt.055 requests in the same way as replies to camt.056 requests.

Matching the camt.029 to the request. The camt.029 carries the identification of the original investigation, so the receiving bank can match it to the camt.056 or camt.055 it sent. The matching uses the original message identification and the UETR. A payment hub must store the investigation identification when it sends a cancellation request, so that it can match the camt.029 when it arrives. A camt.029 that matches no open investigation should be flagged for manual review, because it may relate to a payment the bank has lost track of.

Common mistakes

Treating ACSP as a completed cancellation. ACSP means the cancellation is accepted and pending. It is not a completion. A bank that tells the customer the money is coming back on the strength of an ACSP is making a promise it cannot keep. Wait for the ACCP, and chase the holding bank if a final answer does not arrive within the agreed timeframe.

Ignoring the reason code on an RJCR. A rejection without its reason code is a dead end. The reason code tells the bank what happened and whether anything else can be done. A rejection because the beneficiary refused needs a direct approach to the beneficiary. A rejection because the payment was not found needs the request to be checked and re-sent. Read the reason code before deciding the next step.

Not chasing a missing final answer. When a bank sends an ACSP, it should follow with an ACCP or an RJCR. If no final answer arrives, the requesting bank should chase the holding bank. Cases left open on an ACSP are a common source of lost money and unhappy customers, because no one is watching them. The payment hub should age open investigations and escalate those that pass their deadline.

Confusing the camt.029 with the return of funds. The camt.029 is an information message. It says the cancellation is accepted. The money comes back separately, as a pacs.004 return. A bank that credits the customer on the strength of an ACCP, before the pacs.004 arrives, is paying out money it has not yet received. Wait for the return, match it by UETR, and then credit the customer.

Sending a duplicate cancellation after an RJCR. A rejection is a final answer for that request. Sending the same camt.056 again will get the same rejection and will waste the holding bank's time. If the reason code shows the request was wrong, fix the request and send a corrected one. If the reason shows the beneficiary refused, the banking chain has done all it can, and the matter must be settled outside it.
