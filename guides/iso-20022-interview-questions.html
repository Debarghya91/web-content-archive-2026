ISO 20022 Interview Questions

By Payments Training Hub · Last reviewed 07 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Q1.
  • ISO 20022 is a common language for financial messages.
  • Q2.

Reading time: about 11 min

Q1. Explain ISO 20022 to a non-technical stakeholder. Why did the industry move to it?

ISO 20022 is a common language for financial messages. Before it, banks used MT messages, which are short text blocks with limited data. A beneficiary address was four lines of free text, and every bank read it differently. ISO 20022 replaces that with structured data: the address is broken into street, town, country and postcode, and the remittance information is structured so the beneficiary can reconcile automatically. The industry moved because regulators want better data for sanctions screening, corporates want automatic reconciliation, and banks want fewer errors. The move is not just a format change. It is a data quality change, and that is the point to make to a stakeholder.

Q2. A pacs.008 payment is rejected with reason code AC01. What happened and what do you do?

AC01 means incorrect account number. The beneficiary bank could not find the account stated in the pacs.008. The payment was rejected before settlement, so no money moved. I would read the pacs.002 to confirm the reason code and the original references, then check the creditor account in the pacs.008 against the customer's instruction. Usually the account number was typed wrong or the IBAN check digit is incorrect. I would contact the customer to confirm the correct account, correct the instruction, and resubmit as a new pacs.008 with a new message identification but the same UETR so tracking stays continuous. I would also check whether other payments to the same beneficiary have the same error.

Q3. What is the UETR and why is it critical in ISO 20022 payments?

The UETR is the Unique End to End Transaction Reference. It is a UUID generated by the first bank in the payment chain and carried unchanged by every bank that handles the payment. It is critical because it is the single key that links all the messages about one payment: the pacs.008, every pacs.002 status report, any pacs.004 return, and any camt.056 cancellation. Without the UETR, each bank sees only its own leg and cannot track the payment end to end. SWIFT gpi uses the UETR to show the customer where their payment is. If a bank drops or changes the UETR, the payment becomes untrackable and investigations become slow and manual.

Q4. Walk me through the difference between pacs.008 and pacs.009.

pacs.008 is a customer credit transfer. It moves money on behalf of a customer, and it carries the customer data: the debtor, the creditor, the instructed amount, the charge option, and the remittance information. pacs.009 is a bank to bank credit transfer. It moves a bank's own money, and there is no customer behind it. The pacs.009 carries the debtor and creditor banks, the settlement amount, and no customer charge option. The key test is simple: if there is a customer, it is pacs.008. If it is the bank's own money, it is pacs.009. Confusing them causes a rejection, because the beneficiary bank expects customer data in a pacs.008 and will not find it in a pacs.009.

Q5. A customer payment is accepted (ACCP) but never reaches ACSC. How do you investigate?

ACCP means the payment was accepted for processing, not that it settled. I would start by checking the payment's UETR in the gpi tracker to see the last recorded status and which bank holds it. Then I would check whether a further pacs.002 was received and missed, by looking at the hub logs for the UETR. If no further status arrived, I would send an investigation to the next bank in the chain, asking for the current status. I would also check whether the payment is stuck in a queue, held for sanctions review, or waiting for liquidity at an intermediary bank. I would not resend the payment, because that risks a duplicate. I would escalate to operations if the payment is past its expected settlement time, and I would keep the customer informed with a realistic timeline.

Q6. How does a pacs.002 status flow work, and what does each status mean?

When a bank sends a pacs.008, the receiving bank replies with a pacs.002. ACCP means the payment passed validation and was accepted. ACSP means it was accepted and settlement is in process. ACSC means it was accepted and settlement is completed, which is the final success status. RJCT means the payment was rejected, and a reason code explains why. PART means some transactions in a batch were accepted and some rejected. The sending bank updates its own status from each pacs.002 and tells the customer. The key point is that ACCP is not settlement. Only ACSC means the money has moved. A BA who reports ACCP to a customer as "paid" is giving wrong information.

Q7. A settled payment needs to be returned. Which message do you use and why?

I use pacs.004, the payment return message. A pacs.002 cannot return a settled payment because it is only a status report, it never moves money. Once settlement has happened, the funds must travel back through a pacs.004, which is a real settlement instruction in reverse. The pacs.004 carries the original payment references, including the UETR, so both banks can reconcile the return against the original. It travels back along the same chain in reverse. The returned amount may differ from the original if return charges were deducted. If the return follows a recall, the sending bank first sends a camt.056 cancellation request, the beneficiary bank replies with a camt.029, and then the pacs.004 moves the funds.

Q8. Explain the MT103 to pacs.008 mapping. What are the hardest fields to map?

MT103 maps to pacs.008. Field 32A, the value date, currency and amount, maps to the interbank settlement amount. Field 50, the ordering customer, maps to the debtor block. Field 59, the beneficiary, maps to the creditor block. Field 70, remittance information, maps to RmtInf. Field 71A, charges, maps to the charge bearer. The hardest fields are 59 and 70. Field 59 is four lines of free text, and the pacs.008 creditor block needs structured address elements: street, town, country, postcode. Parsing free text into structured fields is unreliable. Field 70 is 140 characters of free text, and the pacs.008 remittance information can be structured with the invoice number broken out. Banks that map these mechanically without enriching the data send MX messages that carry MT quality data and gain nothing from the migration.

Q9. What is CBPR+ and what is the current state of the migration?

CBPR+ is the Cross-Border Payments and Reporting Plus programme. It is SWIFT's framework for moving cross-border payments to ISO 20022. It started in November 2022 with a coexistence period during which banks could send either MT or MX messages. The coexistence period was extended and the migration continues. Banks are cutting over corridor by corridor, and some message types have been deferred. The practical point for a BA is that during coexistence a bank must handle both MT and MX, must translate between them, and must manage the risk that a counterparty has not yet migrated. The migration is not a single date. It is a programme that each bank works through over time, and the testing and data remediation work is usually larger than the technology work.

Q10. A pain.001 file has 100 payments. 95 are accepted and 5 are rejected. What happens next?

The bank returns a pain.002 status report showing 95 accepted and 5 rejected, with a reason code for each rejection. The 95 accepted payments are processed normally. The 5 rejected payments are not processed at all, because no money has moved for them. I would read the pain.002, identify the reason for each rejection, and fix the underlying data. Common reasons are an invalid account number, a missing mandatory field, or a payment that breaches a limit. I would correct the 5 payments and resubmit them as a new pain.001 file with new instruction identifications, so they are not flagged as duplicates. I would also check whether the same data error affects other files from the same customer, because a systematic error in their ERP system will keep producing rejections.

Q11. How do camt.052, camt.053 and camt.054 differ, and when is each used?

camt.052 is the Bank to Customer Account Report. It is sent intraday and shows the current balance and the transactions so far today. A treasurer uses it to see the intraday position. camt.053 is the Bank to Customer Statement. It is sent at end of day and is the formal statement with the opening balance, every debit and credit, and the closing balance. Reconciliation is done against camt.053. camt.054 is the Bank to Customer Debit Credit Notification. It is sent in real time when a specific debit or credit posts, and it is the trigger for crediting a customer account. The key distinction is timing and purpose: 052 is intraday, 053 is end of day and formal, 054 is real time and transactional. Confusing 052 with 053 causes false reconciliation breaks.

Q12. A payment is stuck because the beneficiary bank never sent a pacs.002. What do you do?

I do not assume the payment is fine and I do not resend it, because resending risks a duplicate. I would first check the payment's UETR in the gpi tracker to see the last recorded status. Then I would check our own hub logs to confirm the pacs.008 was sent and to see whether a pacs.002 arrived but was not processed. If no status exists, I would send an investigation to the next bank in the chain, asking for the current status of the payment by UETR. I would set a deadline for the response and escalate if it is not met. I would also check whether the receiving bank has a known outage or a queue backlog. I would keep the customer informed that the payment is being traced and give a realistic time for an answer, not a promise.

Q13. What is the difference between the instructed amount and the interbank settlement amount in a pacs.008?

The instructed amount is what the customer asked to pay, in the currency the customer specified. The interbank settlement amount is what actually moves between banks, after any currency conversion and after any charges are deducted. They are often different. For example, a customer instructs EUR 50,000, but the settlement amount is USD 54,000 because the payment was converted to dollars for settlement. Or the instructed amount is EUR 50,000 and the settlement amount is EUR 49,950 because a charge was deducted. A BA who confuses the two will misstate the payment to the customer and to reconciliation. The instructed amount is the customer's view. The settlement amount is the banks' view. Both are in the pacs.008, and both must be correct.

Q14. A bank is migrating from MT to MX. What are the three biggest risks, and how would you mitigate them?

First, data quality. MT fields are free text and MX needs structured data, especially addresses. The mitigation is a data remediation programme before migration, cleaning customer records and enriching addresses. Second, the translation layer. During coexistence the bank must translate between MT and MX, and fields with no direct equivalent get lost or misinterpreted. The mitigation is to define the mapping explicitly, test it with real messages, and monitor translation errors daily. Third, exceptions. Migration testing usually covers the happy path but not rejections, returns and recalls, which use different messages and reason codes in MX. The mitigation is to test the full exception lifecycle in the test environment before any production cutover, and to run a pilot with a small number of counterparties before a full cutover.

Q15. A customer asks why their payment took three days when the bank promised same day. How do you explain it?

I would start by tracing the payment using its UETR to find where the delay happened. Common causes are a missed cut-off, a sanctions hold, an intermediary bank queue, or a rejection and resubmission that the customer was not told about. I would explain in plain language what happened at each stage: when the instruction was received, when it was sent, where it waited, and when it settled. I would be specific about the cause, not vague. If the bank missed a cut-off, I would say so. If an intermediary held it for screening, I would say so. I would then explain what will be done differently: a clearer cut-off communication, an earlier submission, or a different routing. I would not blame another bank without evidence, and I would not promise a timeline I cannot control.
