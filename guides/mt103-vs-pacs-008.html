MT103 vs pacs.008: What's the Difference?

By Payments Training Hub · Last reviewed 19 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • MT103 and pacs.008 instruct the same movement of money: a customer credit transfer from one bank to another. They are the same payment in two different message formats.
  • MT103 is the SWIFT MT format: fixed-length fields with limited structured data. pacs.008 is the ISO 20022 XML format: richer structured data with a published schema.
  • Under CBPR+, SWIFT's cross-border payments migrated from MT103 to pacs.008, starting in November 2022, with MT messages for cross-border payments phased out by November 2025.
  • The hard part of the change is not the message itself but the mapping: every field must be mapped, truncation decisions recorded, and tests rebuilt.

Reading time: about 8 min

What Is an MT103?

The MT103 is the Single Customer Credit Transfer: the message a bank sends to move a customer's payment to another bank. It is part of the SWIFT MT standard, which uses fixed-length fields identified by numbers. Its fields tell the whole story. Field 20 is the sender's reference, the unique identifier for the payment. Field 32A gives the value date, currency and amount. Field 50 names the ordering customer. Field 59 names the beneficiary customer, with the account number and name. Field 70 carries the remittance information, what the payment is for. Field 71A states the charges: OUR means the sender pays all charges, SHA means charges are shared, BEN means the beneficiary pays them.

MT messages carry too little structured data for modern needs. An MT103 can name a beneficiary, but the address and remittance information are often free text, truncated or missing. That makes straight-through processing hard, sanctions screening slow, and reconciliation painful. Read the full field-by-field guide in MT103 Explained.

What Is a pacs.008?

The pacs.008 is the FI to FI Customer Credit Transfer: the ISO 20022 XML message a bank sends to another bank to carry a customer payment. Every ISO 20022 message has the same shape. A message definition sits inside a Document element, and the business content sits inside a single element named after the message, in this case FIToFICstmrCdtTrf. Inside that, the content is grouped into blocks: Group Header, then the transaction details. Each block contains elements, and each element has a defined data type, a defined length and a defined rule about whether it is mandatory or optional.

The pacs.008 carries far more than the MT103: full party details, structured addresses, purpose codes and complete remittance information. A receiving system can validate it against a published schema before it processes anything. Read the full guide in pacs.008 Explained.

How an MT103 Maps to a pacs.008

Migration is a mapping exercise. Each MT field has an ISO 20022 counterpart, but the mapping is rarely one field to one element. Field 20, the sender's reference, maps to the message reference in the Group Header. Field 32A, the value date, currency and amount, maps to the interbank settlement date and amount. Field 50, the ordering customer, maps to the debtor and debtor agent. Field 59, the beneficiary, maps to the creditor and creditor agent. Field 70, the remittance information, maps to the remittance information block, which in ISO 20022 can carry structured or unstructured data. Field 71A, the charges, maps to the charge bearer element.

The mapping must handle three problems: data that exists in MT but has no exact ISO equivalent, data that exists in ISO but never existed in MT, and data that gets truncated because the receiving system cannot accept the full ISO content. Work through the mapping in the MT to MX Field Mapping guide and the ISO 20022 Mapping Workbench.

Coexistence and Truncation

Truncation is the trap most projects underestimate. Under coexistence, a bank receiving a rich ISO 20022 message may have to forward it as an MT message to a bank that has not migrated. Fields get shortened, structured addresses collapse into free text, and remittance information is cut. The BA's mapping workbook must record, field by field, what survives, what is truncated, and what is lost, and the business must accept the loss in writing. A pacs.002 saying ACCP means the payment was accepted for processing, not that the beneficiary has been paid. The beneficiary is paid when settlement completes, reported as ACSC, and the account is credited.

A Worked Example

A customer in Mumbai pays a supplier in Frankfurt EUR 50,000. Before migration, the Indian bank sent an MT103 with field 20 as its reference, 32A as the value date and EUR 50,000, 50 as the customer, 59 as the supplier with its IBAN, 70 as the invoice number, and 71A as SHA. The receiving bank read those fields, credited the supplier, and applied its own charges under the SHA instruction.

After migration, the same payment travels as a pacs.008. The instruction carries the same reference, value date and amount, but the debtor and creditor are named with full structured addresses, the remittance information carries the invoice number in a structured block, and the charge bearer element carries SHA. Status reports flow back as pacs.002 messages: ACCP when the payment is accepted, ACSP while settlement is in process, and ACSC when settlement completes.

What This Means for a BA

On a migration project, the BA owns the mapping workbook, the gap analysis and the test scenarios. The mapping workbook records every field from source to target, with transformation rules and truncation decisions. The gap analysis records what the current system can produce against what the scheme requires, and what must be built or bought to close the gap. The test scenarios cover the happy path, every rejection reason, truncation cases, duplicate handling, and the full status reporting chain. The BA also owns the exception handling design: what happens to a payment that fails validation, who sees it, how it is repaired, and how long it may sit in a repair queue before it is returned.
