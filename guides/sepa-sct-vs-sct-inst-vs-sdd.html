SEPA SCT vs SCT Inst vs SDD: What's the Difference?

By Payments Training Hub · Last reviewed 19 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • SEPA has three main instruments: SCT for one-off credit transfers, SCT Inst for instant credit transfers, and SDD for direct debits.
  • SCT is push-based and reaches the beneficiary within one business day. SCT Inst is also push-based but settles in seconds, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.
  • SDD is pull-based: the creditor pulls the payment from the debtor's account under a mandate. It is the instrument for recurring collections.
  • All three are euro-only, run in 36 SEPA countries, and use ISO 20022 messages under European Payments Council rulebooks.

Reading time: about 8 min

What Is SCT?

SCT is the SEPA Credit Transfer: a one-off euro payment from one account to another inside the SEPA area. The payer pushes the payment: the debtor instructs its bank to credit the creditor's account. A standard SCT reaches the beneficiary within one business day. It is the instrument for supplier payments, salaries paid across borders inside Europe, and any one-off euro transfer where same-second settlement is not required. SCT uses ISO 20022 messages: pain.001 for the customer instruction and pacs.008 for the bank-to-bank leg.

What Is SCT Inst?

SCT Inst is the SEPA Instant Credit Transfer: a euro payment that settles in seconds, at any time of day, including nights, weekends and holidays. EU law requires the money on the payee's account within 10 seconds of the payer's bank receiving the order. Since the 2025 rulebook, in force from 5 October 2025, there is no maximum amount at scheme level: the old EUR 100,000 cap was removed, and each bank sets its own limits. SCT Inst is the instrument for urgent supplier payments, emergency transfers, and any euro payment where the beneficiary must have the money now rather than tomorrow. It uses the same ISO 20022 messages as SCT, under the SCT Inst rulebook, which adds the instant execution and availability requirements.

What Is SDD?

SDD is the SEPA Direct Debit: a pull-based instrument for recurring collections. The creditor pulls the payment from the debtor's account, under a mandate the debtor signed in advance. The mandate is the authorisation: without it, the debit cannot be collected. SDD comes in two schemes. SDD Core is for consumers and businesses, and the debtor can claim a refund of an authorised debit within eight weeks. SDD B2B is for business-to-business collections only, with no refund right once the mandate is valid, and the debtor's bank must check the mandate before each collection. SDD is the instrument for subscriptions, utility bills, rent and insurance premiums: any payment that repeats on a schedule.

Which Instrument for Which Payment?

A German company pays a French supplier EUR 40,000 on 30-day terms. SCT: a one-off payment, no urgency to the second, and the standard one-business-day timing fits the terms.

The same company must pay a supplier EUR 80,000 on a Friday night to release goods on Monday. SCT Inst: the payment settles in seconds, and it works at night and over the weekend.

A gym collects EUR 49 a month from 2,000 members. SDD Core: recurring, scheduled, pull-based, and each member signed a mandate when joining. If a member disputes a debit, the eight-week refund right applies.

A manufacturer collects EUR 120,000 a month from a business customer. SDD B2B: business-to-business, no refund right once the mandate is valid, and the debtor's bank checks the mandate before each collection.

What This Means for a BA

On a SEPA project, the BA must establish which instrument applies before anything else, because the instrument decides the message, the mandate handling, the refund rules and the test scenarios. An SCT and an SCT Inst share the same messages but differ in execution timing and availability. An SDD needs mandate storage, mandate amendment and cancellation flows, and a refund process that SCT never needs. The BA documents the instrument per product, the cut-off times, the amount limits, and what happens when a collection fails: a rejected SDD, a returned SCT Inst, an SCT that arrives after the value date. Learn the detail in SEPA and Faster Payments Training and the ISO 20022 Training Course, and compare the schemes in the SWIFT vs SEPA guide.
