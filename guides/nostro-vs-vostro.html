Nostro vs Vostro: The Same Account, Two Perspectives

What it is

Nostro and vostro are two names for the same bank account, seen from different sides. The words come from Latin. Nostro means "ours". Vostro means "yours".

A nostro account is an account that a bank holds with a correspondent bank in another country. From the perspective of the bank that owns the account, it is "our account with you". A bank in India that holds a US dollar account with a bank in New York calls that account its nostro account.

A vostro account is the same account, seen from the perspective of the bank that holds it for the other bank. From the perspective of the New York bank, the Indian bank's account is a vostro account, meaning "your account with us". It is the same account. The name changes depending on who is talking.

Why it matters

Nostro and vostro accounts are the foundation of correspondent banking and cross-border payments. Every time your bank sends a payment to another country, it uses a nostro account to settle it. If you work in payments, treasury, reconciliation, or operations, you will deal with nostro accounts daily.

Confusing nostro and vostro is one of the most common mistakes in payments. If you get them mixed up, your reconciliation will be backwards, your funding decisions will be wrong, and your investigation of a payment exception will start in the wrong place. Getting this right is a basic test of payments knowledge, and interviewers ask about it often.

How it works in a bank

The two perspectives

Bank A is in India. Bank B is in the United States. Bank A opens a US dollar account with Bank B. Bank A deposits USD 5,000,000 into the account.

From Bank A's perspective, this is a nostro account. Bank A calls it "our USD account with Bank B". Bank A records it as an asset on its own balance sheet, because the money belongs to Bank A and is held by Bank B.

From Bank B's perspective, this is a vostro account. Bank B calls it "Bank A's USD account with us". Bank B records it as a liability on its own balance sheet, because Bank B owes this money to Bank A.

It is one account, with one balance, but two names and two accounting treatments.

How a payment uses the nostro account

A customer of Bank A wants to pay USD 100,000 to a supplier in the United States. The supplier banks with Bank C, also in the United States.

Bank A sends an MT103 to Bank C, instructing Bank C to pay the supplier. Bank A also instructs Bank B to move USD 100,000 from Bank A's nostro account to Bank C. Bank B debits the nostro account by USD 100,000 and sends the funds to Bank C. Bank C receives the funds and the MT103, matches them, and credits the supplier.

After the payment, Bank A's nostro account balance is USD 4,900,000. Bank A's treasury team sees the lower balance and decides whether to top it up.

Worked example with two banks

HDFC Bank in Mumbai holds a US dollar nostro account with JPMorgan Chase in New York. The account balance is USD 20,000,000 on Monday morning.

During Monday, HDFC Bank customers send 50 payments to the United States, totalling USD 8,000,000. Each payment is settled from the nostro account. JPMorgan debits the nostro account USD 8,000,000. The balance falls to USD 12,000,000.

During Monday, HDFC Bank also receives 30 payments from the United States, totalling USD 5,000,000. These payments are credited to the nostro account. JPMorgan credits the nostro account USD 5,000,000. The balance rises to USD 17,000,000.

At the end of Monday, the nostro account balance is USD 17,000,000. HDFC Bank's treasury team reviews the balance. They forecast Tuesday's payments at USD 10,000,000. The balance is sufficient, so no funding is needed. If the forecast had been USD 25,000,000, treasury would have moved funds into the nostro account before Tuesday.

How nostro accounts are funded and reconciled

Funding

A bank's treasury team manages its nostro accounts. Treasury forecasts the payment volume for each currency and each correspondent. It ensures each nostro account has enough money to settle the day's payments. If an account is running low, treasury moves funds into it, either from another nostro account or from the bank's central bank settlement account.

Funding a nostro account is not free. The money sitting in a nostro account earns little or no interest. A bank wants to keep enough to settle payments, but not so much that it wastes money that could be invested elsewhere. This balance is called intraday liquidity management. Getting it right is a key treasury skill.

If a nostro account runs out of money, payments stop. The bank cannot settle. This is called a nostro funding failure. It is an operational incident that must be reported and fixed immediately, usually by moving funds from another account.

Reconciliation

Every day, the bank reconciles its nostro accounts. It compares its own records of payments sent and received against the statement provided by the correspondent bank. The correspondent sends a statement message, the MT950 or the ISO 20022 camt.053, which lists every debit and credit on the account.

The bank's reconciliation team matches each entry on the statement against its own payment records. If every entry matches, the account is reconciled. If an entry on the statement has no match in the bank's records, or a payment in the bank's records has no match on the statement, that is a reconciliation break. The team investigates the break. Common causes include a payment sent but not yet received by the correspondent, a timing difference, a duplicate posting, or a payment sent to the wrong account.

Nostro reconciliation is a daily control. Unreconciled breaks must be investigated and resolved quickly, because they can hide errors, fraud, or missing funds.

Common confusion points

  • Thinking nostro and vostro are different accounts. They are the same account. The name depends on whose perspective you take. If you are the bank that owns the account, it is your nostro. If you are the bank that holds the account for another bank, it is your vostro.
  • Getting the debit and credit directions wrong. When Bank A sends a payment from its nostro account, Bank A records a credit (money leaving its account) and Bank B records a debit (money leaving the vostro account). The directions are opposite because the two banks are looking at the same account from opposite sides. Always check which perspective you are using.
  • Confusing a nostro account with a settlement account. A nostro account is held with a correspondent bank. A settlement account is held with a central bank. They serve different purposes. Nostro accounts settle cross-border payments. Central bank settlement accounts settle domestic payments in that currency.
  • Forgetting that nostro balances are real money. The money in a nostro account belongs to the bank that owns it. If the correspondent bank fails, the nostro account holder can lose money. This is called correspondent bank risk, and it is why banks monitor the financial health of their correspondents.
  • Assuming nostro accounts are only in US dollars. Banks hold nostro accounts in every major currency: US dollars, euros, pounds sterling, Japanese yen, and many others. A bank that does significant business in euros will hold a euro nostro account with a correspondent in the eurozone.
