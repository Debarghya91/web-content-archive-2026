TARGET Services Explained

What it is

TARGET Services are the Eurosystem's market infrastructure services: the systems that settle payments, securities, and collateral in central bank money in the euro area. The Eurosystem is the European Central Bank plus the national central banks of the countries that use the euro. TARGET Services are operated by the Eurosystem, and they are the backbone of the euro payment system.

There are four TARGET Services. T2 is the real-time gross settlement system for large-value euro payments. TIPS is the instant payment settlement service. T2S is the securities settlement platform. ECMS is the collateral management system. Together they cover the full lifecycle: a payment settles in T2 or TIPS, a securities trade settles in T2S, and the collateral that supports all of it is managed in ECMS.

The name TARGET has been used since 1999, when the first TARGET system went live with the euro. The services have been rebuilt and consolidated since then. On 20 March 2023, the old TARGET2 system was replaced by T2, and the four services were brought together under the single TARGET Services banner with shared components for liquidity, reference data, and billing.

Why it matters

Every large euro payment settles in T2. Every instant euro payment settles in TIPS. Every euro securities trade settles in T2S. If you work in payments, treasury, securities operations, or collateral management in Europe, you will deal with TARGET Services daily, directly or through your bank.

TARGET Services matter because settlement in central bank money is the safest form of settlement. When a payment settles in T2, the receiving bank holds a claim on the central bank, not on another commercial bank. There is no credit risk on the settlement itself. This is why large-value payments, and the final settlement behind instant payments, use central bank money.

For a business analyst, TARGET Services are the settlement layer behind every euro payment product. A SEPA credit transfer, a SEPA Instant payment, a CHAPS-style high-value payment in euro, or a securities trade all end in one of these services. Requirements for cut-offs, liquidity, settlement finality, and reconciliation all trace back to how the relevant TARGET Service works. For an interview candidate, TARGET Services are the answer to "where does a euro payment actually settle".

How it works in a bank

A bank connects to TARGET Services through its national central bank, or through a service provider. The bank holds a settlement account at its central bank. Payments and securities trades are settled by debiting and crediting these accounts.

T2, the real-time gross settlement system. T2 settles large-value euro payments one by one, in real time, in central bank money. Each payment is settled individually, not netted against other payments. T2 runs on business days, roughly from 07:00 to 18:00 Central European Time, and it settles SEPA credit transfers that banks send to each other, central bank operations, and the final settlement behind other clearing systems. A bank that wants to send a large euro payment must have enough liquidity in its T2 account, or it must borrow intraday liquidity from its central bank against collateral.

TIPS, the instant payment service. TIPS settles instant payments in central bank money, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year. A SEPA Instant payment instructed by a customer is settled in TIPS within seconds. TIPS is a settlement service, not a payment scheme: the scheme is SCT Inst, which defines the rules, and TIPS is one of the places where the settlement happens. Other options are the private sector service RT1 and the STEP2-T clearing system. TIPS is multi-currency: the Swedish krona has been settled in TIPS since February 2024, and the Danish krone since April 2025.

T2S, the securities settlement platform. T2S settles securities trades in central bank money on a delivery-versus-payment basis: the securities and the cash move at the same time, so neither side takes settlement risk. T2S is used by central securities depositories across Europe. A bank that buys a bond settles the cash leg in its T2 account and the securities leg in its T2S account, and T2S links the two so they settle together or not at all.

ECMS, the collateral management system. ECMS manages the collateral that banks pledge to the Eurosystem, mainly for intraday liquidity and for central bank lending. It replaces the separate national collateral systems with a single Eurosystem service. A bank that needs intraday liquidity for T2 pledges collateral in ECMS, and the collateral is valued, checked, and managed in one place.

The shared components. The four services share a common set of components: a single sign-on and access system, common reference data, a shared billing service, and a common liquidity management function. A bank manages its liquidity across T2, TIPS, and T2S from one place, moving funds between the services as needed during the day.

Worked example

A German bank has three euro payments to settle on a Tuesday.

A large-value payment in T2. A corporate customer pays a supplier in France 2,500,000 euro. The bank's payment hub builds the instruction and sends it to T2 at 09:30. T2 checks the bank has enough liquidity in its T2 account, debits the German bank, and credits the French bank's central bank account. The payment settles immediately, in central bank money, and is final. The French bank credits its customer. Total time: seconds from the moment T2 receives it.

An instant payment in TIPS. A customer sends 250 euro to a friend in Italy using their mobile app at 22:40 on a Saturday. The bank sends the SCT Inst payment to TIPS. TIPS settles it in central bank money within seconds, even though it is a Saturday night, because TIPS runs around the clock. The friend's bank credits the friend immediately. The German bank's TIPS account is debited and the Italian bank's TIPS account is credited. No business day, no cut-off, no waiting until Monday.

A securities trade in T2S. The bank buys 10,000,000 euro of German government bonds from a French bank. The trade settles in T2S on a delivery-versus-payment basis: the bonds move to the German bank's securities account and the cash moves to the French bank's T2 account at the same moment. If either leg cannot settle, neither does. The bank's collateral for the intraday liquidity it used is managed in ECMS.

The liquidity picture. During the day, the bank's liquidity team watches its T2, TIPS, and T2S balances from one screen. In the morning it moves funds from T2 to TIPS to cover the day's instant payments. In the afternoon it moves funds back. At the end of the day it sweeps the remaining TIPS balance back to T2. This is the shared liquidity management that the TARGET Services consolidation made possible.

Technical detail

Settlement finality. A payment settled in T2 or TIPS is final and irrevocable. It cannot be reversed by the sending bank. This is set out in the EU Settlement Finality Directive, and it is the reason large-value payments settle in central bank money rather than through commercial bank accounts.

Liquidity in T2. T2 offers liquidity-saving features: payments are queued when funds are short, and the system looks for offsetting payments that can settle together. A bank can also reserve liquidity for urgent payments, set bilateral limits with other banks, and use intraday credit from its central bank against eligible collateral managed in ECMS.

TIPS and the SCT Inst scheme. TIPS settles the payment, but the SCT Inst scheme defines the rules: the maximum execution time, the message format (pacs.008), and the return and refund flows. A bank that offers instant payments must comply with the scheme and must have a settlement arrangement, and TIPS is the Eurosystem's settlement option. The scheme and the settlement service are different things, and confusing them is a common error.

T2S settlement model. T2S settles on a delivery-versus-payment basis in central bank money. It also supports settlement in commercial bank money for some markets, but the standard model is central bank money. T2S settles in euro and in Danish krone.

ISO 20022. All TARGET Services use ISO 20022 messages. T2 migrated to ISO 20022 as part of the 2023 consolidation. Banks connecting to T2 send pacs.008 and pacs.009 for payments, pacs.002 for status reports, and camt messages for statements and liquidity. A bank that has already migrated to ISO 20022 for SWIFT can reuse much of that work for TARGET Services, but the two migrations are separate programmes with separate testing.

Multi-currency. TIPS settles in euro, Swedish krona (since February 2024), and Danish krone (since April 2025). T2S settles in euro and Danish krone. The multi-currency design means a central bank outside the euro area can use the same platform for its own currency.

Common mistakes

Calling T2 "TARGET2". TARGET2 was the old system. It was replaced by T2 on 20 March 2023. The names look similar, but the systems, the message formats, and the liquidity arrangements are different. Using the old name in a requirement causes confusion about which system is meant.

Confusing TIPS with the SCT Inst scheme. TIPS is a settlement service run by the Eurosystem. SCT Inst is the payment scheme run under the European Payments Council rulebook. A bank needs both: the scheme for the rules, and a settlement service such as TIPS for the money movement.

Assuming instant means the beneficiary is credited instantly. TIPS settles in seconds, but the beneficiary bank still has to credit the beneficiary account. Most banks credit immediately, but the credit is the beneficiary bank's responsibility, not TIPS.

Forgetting T2 cut-offs. T2 runs on business days with fixed hours. A large-value payment instructed after the T2 cut-off is processed the next business day. Instant payments in TIPS have no cut-off, which is the point of the service, but large-value payments do.

Treating ECMS as a payment system. ECMS manages collateral. It does not settle payments or securities. A requirement that asks ECMS to settle a payment is asking the wrong system.

Ignoring liquidity. A bank can have a perfectly built payment instruction and still fail to settle if its T2 account is short. Liquidity management, intraday credit, and collateral are part of the payment design, not an afterthought.
