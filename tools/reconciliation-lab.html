Reconciliation Laboratory: Match the Statement to the Ledger

Reconciliation is the daily proof that the money is where the books say it is. A bank statement says what actually happened to an account. The internal ledger says what the bank thinks happened. When the two agree, the books are right. When they do not agree, something needs finding and fixing, and the difference must be explained to the last unit of currency. This laboratory lets you practise that matching on realistic cases. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, nothing is stored, and no payment is made.

How to use

  1. Read the worked example first. It shows a full nostro reconciliation with one real break and one timing difference, and the journals that fix the break.
  2. Then work the five exercises. In each one, click a bank statement line, then click the ledger entry that belongs to it. A green pair is an exact match. An amber pair is the same payment with a difference, and that difference is the break.
  3. When every pair is matched, the leftover lines are the breaks. Answer the two questions for the exercise: what the break is, and what the correct resolution is.
  4. Each exercise is worth 2 points, one for naming the break and one for the resolution. Your score out of 10 is shown at the top of the exercises and again at the end.
  5. Use the reset button on any exercise to try it again from the start.

Golden rule: a reconciliation difference is never written off as a rounding error and never parked in the suspense account to make the numbers agree. Every difference is either a timing difference that clears on the next statement, or a real error with a named owner and a correction posted the same day.

The four accounts you will reconcile

AccountWhat it isWhat you reconcile it against
Nostro accountOur account held with a correspondent bank abroad, in foreign currency. When we pay a customer in another currency, the money leaves this account.The correspondent bank statement, line by line, every day.
Vostro accountA foreign bank account held with us, in our books. The foreign bank sees it as its nostro account with us.Our own ledger and the statement we send to the account holder.
Settlement accountThe account at the clearing system or central bank where cleared funds finally settle. It is where a clearing cycle ends and the money actually moves.The clearing system settlement statement for each cycle.
Suspense accountA temporary holding account for items that cannot yet be posted to their proper account, usually because a reference is missing or unclear.Itself. The suspense account must clear to nil. Every entry needs an owner and a target date.

The matching rule is the same for all four: match by reference first, then check the amount, the currency, and the value date. A line that matches on reference but not on amount or date is not a match. It is a break, and it needs a reason.

How a payment flows through the accounts

Matching only works if you know where each entry is supposed to appear. Follow one outward payment from instruction to statement.

  1. Instruction arrives. A pacs.008 or MT103 is received and validated. The ledger posts it: debit the customer account, credit the outward payment clearing account.
  2. The payment is released. The clearing account is debited and the nostro account is credited or debited, depending on the direction of the payment. For an outward payment, the nostro account is debited when the correspondent bank pays out.
  3. Settlement. The funds move through the settlement account at the clearing system. The clearing system confirms the settlement cycle, and the settlement account is reconciled against the clearing system statement.
  4. Statement. The correspondent bank statement shows the debit or credit on the nostro account, with the value date, the amount, and the reference.
  5. Reconciliation. The statement line is matched to the ledger entry. Amount, currency, value date and reference must all agree. Anything unmatched is listed as a break with an owner and a deadline.

Transaction matching is step 5 for one payment. Settlement matching is the same check applied to a whole clearing cycle: every instruction in the cycle must appear in the settlement account, and the cycle total must agree with the clearing system statement.

Second, the outward payment REF-890 of 15,000.00 is in the ledger but not on the statement. It was released on 13 November and is still in transit. The difference is fully explained: 15,000.00 of timing difference plus 270.00 of transposition error equals the 15,270.00 gap between the two closing balances. The correction journal for the break is:

The in-transit payment needs no entry. When it appears on the next statement, the statement balance falls by 15,000.00 and both sides agree at 384,350.00 without any further action.

Suspense account handling

Sometimes money arrives and the bank cannot tell whose it is. A reference is missing, truncated, or unreadable. The money is real, so it must be recorded, but it cannot be forced into a customer account on a guess. That is what the suspense account is for: a clearly labelled waiting room, not a hiding place.

Example. An MT103 receipt of USD 12,600.00 arrives with an incomplete reference. The beneficiary cannot be identified, so operations posts it to suspense on the day it arrives:

Dr Nostro account 12,600.00 Cr Suspense account, unidentified receipts 12,600.00 Receipt with incomplete reference, beneficiary unknown. Debits equal credits.

Two days later the sending bank confirms the reference and the beneficiary is identified as Meridian Imports Ltd. The suspense entry is cleared in full:

Dr Suspense account, unidentified receipts 12,600.00 Cr Customer account, Meridian Imports Ltd 12,600.00 Beneficiary identified. Suspense balance after this entry: nil.

The rules that keep suspense honest:

  1. Every suspense entry has a named owner and a target date for clearing, written on the entry itself.
  2. The suspense account is reviewed every morning. Anything older than 5 business days is escalated to the operations manager.
  3. A suspense entry is cleared in full when the facts are known. It is never split across accounts to make a reconciliation agree.
  4. A break is never parked in suspense to hide it. Suspense is for money whose owner is genuinely unknown, not for errors. Errors get a correction journal, not a suspense entry.

Two differences that are not errors: FX and value date

FX difference

A EUR receipt is booked in the ledger at the rate agreed when the payment was instructed, but the correspondent bank settles it at the rate ruling when the funds arrive. The two rates are rarely identical, so the USD amounts differ. That is an FX difference, and it is resolved by recognising the gain or loss, not by treating the item as a break.

Example. A receipt of EUR 25,000.00 is booked at 1.0900, which is USD 27,250.00. The correspondent settles it at 1.0880, which is USD 27,200.00. The difference is USD 50.00, and the ledger expected 50.00 more than the bank credited. The correction is:

Dr FX loss 50.00 Cr Nostro account 50.00 EUR 25,000.00 settled at 1.0880 instead of the booked 1.0900. Debits equal credits.

If the settled rate had been higher than the booked rate, the same entry would run the other way round as an FX gain. Either way, the difference is explained and posted, and the nostro account then agrees with the statement.

Value-date difference

A payment can carry one date in the ledger and a different date on the statement, with the same amount and the same reference. That is a timing difference, not an error. It happens when the value date falls on a day the clearing system is closed.

Example. A payment is released with value date 25 December. 25 December is a TARGET2 holiday, so the clearing system does not settle it that day. The ledger shows 25 December, the statement shows the next TARGET2 business day, and the amount and reference are identical. No journal is needed. The item is noted as a timing difference and confirmed on the next statement. Weekends work the same way: a payment released on Friday 13 November appears on the statement on Monday 16 November.

The daily reconciliation routine

Use this checklist every morning until it is habit. It is the routine that keeps the five break types above from turning into losses.

  1. Pull the nostro and settlement account statements for the previous business day.
  2. Match every statement line to a ledger entry by reference, then check the amount, the currency and the value date.
  3. List every unmatched item and classify it: timing difference, missing entry, duplicate, amount difference, FX difference, or value-date difference.
  4. Post corrections for real errors the same day. A correction posted tomorrow is a day the books were wrong.
  5. Age the suspense account every morning. Escalate anything older than 5 business days to the operations manager.
  6. Confirm the adjusted statement balance equals the adjusted ledger balance. The difference after adjustments must be nil.
  7. Sign off the reconciliation and file it with the break list, so the next person can see exactly what is open and who owns it.

What good looks like: every item matched or explained, no difference unexplained, no suspense entry older than 5 business days, and a signed reconciliation filed before the next business day starts.
