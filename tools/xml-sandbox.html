Interactive Parsing Sandbox: pacs.008 and MT103

Paste a real (de-identified) payment message below and validate it the way a payment hub would. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, nothing is stored, and no payment is made.

How to use

  1. Paste a pacs.008 XML payload or an MT103 block into the box below. Remove customer names, account numbers and any live reference before you paste.
  2. Click Validate. Each finding names the field, explains what is wrong, and shows the rejection or return code that would come back.
  3. Try the two sample buttons first: one valid pacs.008 and one broken MT103, so you can see a clean pass and a failing message side by side.
  4. Fix one finding at a time and re-validate. A message is only ready to send when every finding is cleared.

Note on the calendar: the settlement-date check uses a simplified TARGET2 calendar for training: weekends, plus 1 January, Good Friday, Easter Monday, 1 May, 25 December and 26 December. Real TARGET2 calendars also close on other days and depend on the year, so always confirm a live value date against the official calendar before you release a payment.

What the validator checks

MessageCheckTypical rejection code
pacs.008Missing mandatory elements: DbtrAgt, CdtrAgt, InstdAmt with currency, DbtrAcct, CdtrAcct, PmtId with InstrId and EndToEndId, IntrBkSttlmDt or SttlmDtpacs.002 RJCT, RR04 or the specific code for the field
pacs.008BIC format: 8 or 11 characters, 4-letter bank code, 2-letter country code, 2-character location code, optional 3-character branch codepacs.002 RJCT, RC01
pacs.008Amount format: digits with an optional decimal point, no commas, no currency symbol, currency as a 3-letter ISO codepacs.002 RJCT, AM02
pacs.008Settlement date on a TARGET2 business day, and creation time before the 17:00 CET customer-payment cut-offpacs.002 RJCT, DT01 for a bad date, TM01 for a cut-off breach
MT103Missing mandatory fields: :20:, :32A:, :50a:, :59: and :71A:SWIFT NAK, or pacs.002 RJCT, RR04 after conversion
MT103:32A: format: date as YYMMDD, currency as 3 letters, amount with a comma decimal separatorSWIFT NAK, or pacs.002 RJCT, AM02 or DT01 after conversion
MT103BIC in :52A: or :57A: is 8 or 11 characters in the right positionsSWIFT NAK, or pacs.002 RJCT, RC01 after conversion

Rejection codes used here: BE01 means the instruction is inconsistent with the end customer, AC01 means an incorrect account number, AC04 means a closed account number, AM05 means duplication, RC01 means the bank identifier is incorrect, RR04 means a regulatory reason, DT01 means an invalid date, TM01 means the cut-off time was missed, and AM02 means the amount is not allowed. A pacs.002 RJCT is the formal rejection of a pacs.008. A SWIFT NAK is the network acknowledgement that rejects a malformed MT message.
