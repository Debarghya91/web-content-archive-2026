MT103 to ISO 20022 Migration

Move cross-border payments from legacy MT messages to ISO 20022 before the coexistence deadline

This project migrates cross-border payment instructions from SWIFT MT103, MT202 and MT202 COV messages to ISO 20022 pacs.008 and pacs.009 messages, following the end of MT coexistence on 22 November 2025. The work covers a 10,000 dollar payment from a London corporate to a New York supplier, a 50,000 euro payment to a Frankfurt beneficiary and a 25,000 pound payment to a Singapore exporter, so that transformation, truncation and screening are tested on real cases. The business analyst maps every MT field to its ISO 20022 target, defines rules for structured party and address data, handles fields that truncate or have no direct equivalent, and writes requirements that developers, testers and operations teams can use. The project ends with SWIFT certification, user acceptance testing and a controlled production cutover.

Read the brief · Download project brief (p1.md)

Phases

  1. Discovery and Current State Analysis
    • Document the as is MT103, MT202 and MT202 COV flows and volumes
    • Interview operations, compliance and technology stakeholders
    • Collect sample MT messages for USD, EUR and GBP payments
    • Identify downstream systems that consume MT data
  2. ISO 20022 Analysis
    • Analyse pacs.008, pacs.009 and pacs.002 structure and CBPR+ rules
    • Compare MT and MX fields and identify gaps and truncations
    • Document structured address and party data requirements
    • Review SWIFT MyStandards usage guidelines for each market
  3. Mapping and Transformation Design
    • Create the MT to MX field mapping sheet for all message types
    • Define truncation, enrichment and default value rules
    • Agree handling for fields with no direct MX equivalent
    • Validate mapping with compliance and screening teams
  4. Requirements and Design
    • Write the BRD and FSD with business and functional rules
    • Define API contracts for initiation and status reporting
    • Design repair, rejection and reversal workflows
    • Agree non functional requirements for performance and audit
  5. Build Support and Testing
    • Support developers during transformation engine build
    • Create UAT scenarios for standard, truncated and sanction hit cases
    • Support SWIFT certification and community testing
    • Track defects and update requirements where needed
  6. Cutover and Go Live
    • Plan the phased cutover from MT to MX traffic
    • Prepare operations runbooks and training material
    • Support hypercare and monitor message success rates
    • Close the project and hand over to operations

Test scenarios

  1. Input a 10,000 dollar MT103 from a London corporate to a New York supplier. Submit the message through the transformation engine and check the output. Expected result is a valid pacs.008 with the correct creditor, amount, charges, and remittance information, and no validation errors.
  2. Input a 50,000 euro MT103 with structured remittance data for a Frankfurt beneficiary. Submit the message and inspect the pacs.008 output. Expected result is structured remittance information preserved in full, with the creditor name and address mapped to the correct structured elements.
  3. Input a 25,000 pound MT103 where the beneficiary name exceeds the target length. Submit the message and review the handling. Expected result is the payment routed to repair with a clear truncation warning, and no data lost silently in the pacs.008 output.
  4. Input an MT103 where the beneficiary appears on a sanctions list. Submit the message and monitor the screening outcome. Expected result is the payment held for compliance review, a pacs.002 status report sent, and no pacs.008 released to the beneficiary bank.
  5. Input an MT103 with an invalid IBAN for the beneficiary account. Submit the message and check the validation response. Expected result is the payment rejected to repair with a clear error code, and the original MT103 retained for audit with no partial transformation.

Evaluation criteria

  1. Transformation accuracy: at least 99.5 percent of test messages transform with zero field errors, measured across 1,000 test messages in every test run.
  2. Structured data handling: 100 percent of structured party and address fields map to the correct pacs.008 elements in the test pack.
  3. Screening control: 100 percent of sanction hit test cases are held before release, with a pacs.002 status report sent in each case.
  4. Repair handling: at least 95 percent of truncation and validation cases route to repair with a clear error code and no silent data loss.
  5. Certification readiness: the full UAT pack passes with no open severity one defects and SWIFT certification evidence complete before the launch decision.

Build a Payment Hub

Design a single platform that validates, screens, routes and settles every payment type

This project builds a central payment hub that replaces separate silos for domestic, cross-border, instant and bulk payments with a single platform. The hub receives a payment from any channel, validates the instruction, checks limits and funds, screens it for sanctions and fraud, routes it to the best rail, and reports its status in real time. A 5,000 dollar Faster Payments transfer in the UK, a 20,000 euro SEPA credit transfer and a 100,000 dollar cross-border payment through SWIFT all flow through the same stages, with rail specific rules applied at routing. The business analyst writes the business requirements, designs the processing stages and routing rules, defines the screening and exception integrations, and specifies the API contracts that channels and corporates use to connect to the hub.

A 250 dollar UPI payment, a 2,000 euro SCT Inst payment and a 5,000 dollar FedNow payment must all complete within 10 seconds, including validation, sanctions screening and settlement, with a clear confirmation to the sender. The project covers liquidity management, 24 by 7 support, fraud monitoring and scheme certification, and it ends with a controlled launch and hypercare.

Payment Reconciliation

Match every clearing record to the ledger, find breaks fast and resolve them cleanly

This project builds payment reconciliation that matches every clearing record to the ledger, identifies breaks quickly and resolves them before they affect customers or financial reporting. A 10,000 dollar SWIFT payment, a 500 euro SEPA credit transfer and a 2,000 pound Faster Payments transfer must each match to the correct ledger entry, with fees, FX and value dates handled correctly. The business analyst defines the matching rules, the tolerance for charges and FX differences, the exception workflows for missing, duplicate and partial matches, and the reports that finance and operations use to track breaks. The project covers file receipt, auto matching, case management, suspense accounting and daily, weekly and monthly reconciliation reporting. It also covers suspense accounting, write off approval and audit trails that prove each break was investigated and resolved on time.
