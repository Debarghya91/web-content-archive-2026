Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

SWIFT: The Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication, a member owned cooperative that provides secure messaging services for financial institutions.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

India domestic interbank payments run on a messaging system most people outside Indian banking have never heard of: SFMS, the Structured Financial Messaging System. SFMS was developed by IDRBT, the Institute for Development and Research in Banking Technology, for the Reserve Bank of India. It is the messaging backbone for Indian interbank payments: when a bank sends an RTGS or NEFT payment, the instruction travels as an SFMS message. SFMS is to Indian domestic payments what SWIFT is to cross border payments: the trusted network that carries the instructions.

SFMS carries structured messages between banks. The message formats are defined, with headers, fields, and validation, so that a message sent by one bank is understood by every other bank and by the central systems. The formats cover payments, trade finance, and other interbank business, with message types for each purpose. Each message type has a defined layout, and the layout is published so that every participant implements it the same way. Straight through processing is the design goal: a message that arrives well formed is processed without manual intervention, from the sending bank system to the receiving bank system, through the central infrastructure. The structure is what makes the straight through processing possible.

Security in SFMS is built on public key infrastructure. Messages are digitally signed, so the receiver can verify who sent the message and that it has not been altered. Encryption protects the content in transit. The signing and encryption give the messages non repudiation: a sender cannot later deny having sent a message, which matters when the message moves money. Access to SFMS is controlled: only authorised institutions connect, and their identities are managed through the certificate infrastructure. The security model is the same idea as SWIFT public key infrastructure, applied to the domestic network.

SFMS underpins the systems Indian customers use every day. RTGS, the real time gross settlement system, carries high value payments over SFMS messages. NEFT, the national electronic funds transfer system, carries retail payments in batches, also over SFMS. The central systems, operated by the Reserve Bank, receive SFMS messages from sending banks, process them, and send SFMS messages to receiving banks. A customer instruction at a branch or on a mobile app becomes an SFMS message between banks, and the reply travels the same way back.

As Indian payment systems adopt ISO 20022 message content, SFMS remains the domestic messaging backbone. The content of the messages modernises, with richer data and international alignment, while the transport, the security, and the network continue to be SFMS. The separation of content and transport is a useful design lesson: changing what a message says does not require changing how it travels. SFMS proves the point at national scale, carrying the payment instructions of one of the world largest payment markets.

For a business analyst working on Indian payments, SFMS is foundational knowledge. Requirements for a payment product must name the SFMS message types involved, the fields they carry, and the validations that apply. Integration design must cover SFMS connectivity, certificate management, and straight through processing from the core banking system. And troubleshooting must follow the SFMS trail: a payment that fails between banks leaves its evidence in the SFMS messages, and the analyst who can read them can find the fault. Domestic rails have domestic networks, and SFMS is India.
