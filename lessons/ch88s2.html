Key terms

Direct participant: A bank or institution that holds an account in a payment system and sends and receives payments directly.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

Show transcript

STEP2 is the pan-European automated clearing house operated by EBA CLEARING, and it is the CSM most European banks use for SEPA credit transfers and SEPA direct debits. It is owned by its member banks, which are the direct participants. A bank that joins STEP2 sends its SEPA files to one place and reaches creditor banks across the whole SEPA area, instead of joining a separate clearing house in every country. STEP2 handles SCT and SDD. SCT Inst, the instant scheme, is handled by a separate EBA CLEARING platform called RT1, because instant payments need real-time settlement and cannot wait for a batch cycle. A payment submitted to STEP2 goes through a fixed sequence. First the debtor bank sends a file of pacs.008 messages for credit transfers, or pain.008 messages for direct debits. STEP2 validates each instruction: the IBAN format, the BIC, the amount, the value date, and the mandatory fields. An instruction that fails validation is rejected and reported back with a reason code, and it never enters the clearing. The valid instructions are sorted by creditor bank and grouped into clearing cycles. STEP2 runs several cycles a day for SCT. Each cycle has a submission cut-off, and a file that arrives after the cut-off waits for the next cycle. Direct debits run on their own cycle pattern because they are tied to due dates: a CORE direct debit must reach the CSM in time for the debtor bank to process it before the due date, and the timing rules are in the SDD rulebook. After sorting, STEP2 calculates the multilateral net position for each direct participant in each cycle. That is the single most important thing STEP2 does. Instead of settling every payment individually, each bank settles one net amount per cycle, either a net debit or a net credit. A bank that sent 50 million euro and received 47 million euro in one cycle settles a net debit of 3 million euro. The net positions are then settled in TARGET2, now T2, which is the Eurosystem real-time gross settlement system. Settlement happens in central bank money, which is the safest form of settlement, because the central bank cannot fail. Direct participants hold a T2 account and settle their own net positions. Indirect participants do not hold a T2 account. They settle through a direct participant, called their settlement bank, which debits or credits their account with it. A worked example makes the netting concrete. In one morning cycle, three banks exchange payments. Bank A sends 20 million euro and receives 12 million, so its net position is a debit of 8 million. Bank B sends 15 million and receives 22 million, so its net position is a credit of 7 million. Bank C sends 9 million and receives 10 million, so its net position is a credit of 1 million. The gross value moved is 44 million euro, but the settlement is three net entries totalling 8 million. The liquidity saving is the difference, and it is why banks prefer netting for high-volume retail payments. Rejects and returns follow the R-transaction rules. A payment rejected before settlement is reported with a pacs.002 status report and the reason code, for example an invalid IBAN or a closed account. A payment returned after settlement, because the creditor account is closed or the creditor refuses it, comes back as a pacs.004 return. A direct debit can be refused, returned, refunded, or reversed, each with its own time limits under the SDD rulebook. The operations team must be able to trace every R-transaction to the original payment and to the customer. Failure modes are practical and frequent. A file that fails validation late in the day misses the last cycle and settles the next business day, which changes the value date and can breach a customer promise. A direct participant that has not funded its T2 account cannot settle its net debit, and STEP2 will hold the cycle until the funding arrives or the participant is excluded. A duplicate file, sent twice by mistake, must be detected by the CSM using the file reference, or the payments will be processed twice. For a business analyst, STEP2 is a set of integration requirements: the file formats, the validation rules, the cycle cut-offs, the settlement account arrangements, and the R-transaction handling. The acceptance criteria must include a test where a file is submitted before the cut-off and settles in the same cycle, a test where a file is submitted after the cut-off and settles in the next cycle, and a test where a payment is rejected and the pacs.002 reason code reaches the customer.
