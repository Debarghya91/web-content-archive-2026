A bank sometimes receives funds it cannot apply. The money has arrived, but the bank cannot post it to the intended account: the account number is wrong, the account is closed, the beneficiary cannot be identified, or the reference does not match anything the bank holds. The payment is not lost, but it is stuck. The structured message for this situation is the camt.026, the UnableToApply notification. The receiving bank sends it to the previous bank in the chain, saying: I have your funds, I cannot apply them, tell me what to do.

The causes of unable to apply cluster around data. Wrong or closed account numbers are the most common: the payment names an account that does not exist or no longer exists. Missing or unrecognisable references: the remittance information does not identify the underlying obligation, and the beneficiary bank cannot match the payment to an expected receipt. Unidentified beneficiaries: the name on the payment does not match the name on the account, and the bank cannot safely credit a stranger. Incomplete data: a field the bank needs to post the payment is absent or invalid. In each case the funds are held, not applied, because applying funds to the wrong account is worse than holding them.

The camt.026 opens a case. The receiving bank sends it to the bank that sent the funds, carrying the original payment references, the amount held, the coded reason the funds cannot be applied, and a statement of what is needed to resolve the case. The previous bank investigates: it checks its own records, contacts its customer, and either supplies the missing information or asks for the funds back. The investigation follows the chain backwards, bank by bank, until it reaches the party that can supply the correction. Each step is a message, and the case is the thread that ties the messages together.

While the case is open, the funds sit in a suspense account. A suspense account is a holding account for funds that cannot yet be posted to their destination. It is a control account: every entry must be explained, aged, and cleared, and the balance must reconcile daily. Funds in suspense are a liability: the bank holds money it owes to somebody, and it must be able to say who, how much, and since when. Ageing matters: a suspense entry that sits for days ties up liquidity, creates reconciliation breaks, and signals a case that is not being worked. Operations teams review suspense ageing daily, and escalate the oldest entries.

Outcomes for an unable to apply case are few. Corrected details arrive, usually as additional information from the previous bank, and the funds are applied to the right account. The funds are returned, as a pacs.004, when the payment cannot be applied and the sender wants the money back. Or the case escalates, when neither correction nor return resolves it, and a human investigation takes over. The case closes with a camt.029, the resolution of investigation, recording the outcome. The MT equivalent of the camt.026 was the MT195 or MT295 query, a free text message asking what to do; the camt.026 replaces the free text with a structured case message, a coded reason, and a lifecycle.

For a business analyst, the camt.026 teaches the discipline of holding funds safely. The requirements are about the suspense account, the ageing controls, the case workflow, and the data needed to resolve. The message itself is simple; the operation around it is not. A bank that handles unable to apply well applies funds quickly, returns funds promptly when application is impossible, and never lets a suspense balance age unexplained. A bank that handles it badly holds customer money in limbo, and the customer discovers the problem before the bank does.
