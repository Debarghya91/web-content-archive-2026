Practical Exercise: Transform an MT103 into pacs.008

What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain practical exercise: transform an MT103 into pacs.008. Here is a pre-migration MT103 we will transform, sent before coexistence ended on 22 November 2025.

Why it matters. This matters because the message is the payment instruction. A wrong field, a missing element or a misunderstood format is a failed payment, a return, or a compliance breach.

Chapter objective: Learner navigates the complete ISO 20022 catalogue, selecting the right message for each business scenario across payments, cash management, securities, and reporting. Learner practises this skill on realistic banking cases, explaining the reasoning clearly and using correct terminology throughout the work.

Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Bank Operation Code: A code in field 23B of an MT103 that tells the receiving bank how to handle the transfer, such as CRED for a normal credit transfer, SPAY for a payment, or CRTS for a cheque related transfer.

Execution date: The date on which the bank executes a payment instruction, debiting the debtor account and sending the message onward.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

Here is a pre-migration MT103 we will transform, sent before coexistence ended on 22 November 2025. A payment of GBP 12,500.00 from a customer in London to a supplier in New York. Field 20, the transaction reference, is FT20241105001. Field 23B is CRED for a credit transfer. Field 32A is 241106GBP12500,00, meaning value date 6 November 2024, currency GBP, amount 12,500.00. Field 50K, the ordering customer, is Emma Wilson, 14 Oakfield Road, London NW3 2AB, United Kingdom, account GB29 NWBK 6016 1331 9268 19. Field 59, the beneficiary customer, is Hudson Trading LLC, 420 Madison Avenue, New York NY 10017, United States, account 987654321 with Chase Bank. Field 57A is the account with institution, CHASUS33XXX, and field 71A is SHA for shared charges. The MT103 continues with the details that explain the payment and its route. Field 70, remittance information, is INV-2024-0988 payment for consulting services October 2024. Field 72, sender to receiver information, is PHONBEN plus 1 212 555 0148 please advise beneficiary by telephone. Field 52A, the ordering institution, is NWBKGB2LXXX for NatWest in London. Field 53B, the sender correspondent, is account 12345678 with the sending bank. Field 56A, the intermediary institution, is CHASUS33XXX for JPMorgan Chase in New York. The message was sent by NatWest in London on 5 November 2024, and it instructs Chase Bank to credit Hudson Trading LLC with GBP 12,500.00 for value 6 November 2024. Mapping the header and amount fields is the most straightforward part of the transformation. Field 20, FT20241105001, becomes the instruction identifier in the pacs.008 payment identification, and a new message identifier such as MSG-2024-1105-009914 is created for the pacs.008 group header. Field 32A gives three pacs.008 values at once, the value date 2024-11-06 becomes the requested execution date and the interbank settlement date, the currency GBP becomes the instructed amount currency, and the amount 12,500.00 becomes the instructed amount. Field 23B, CRED, is not carried as a field in pacs.008, because a pacs.008 is by definition a credit transfer, and the mapping must record that the value was consumed rather than lost. Mapping the parties and accounts needs care, because MT103 and pacs.008 structure names and addresses differently. Field 50K becomes the debtor in pacs.008, with the name Emma Wilson, the account GB29 NWBK 6016 1331 9268 19, and the address 14 Oakfield Road, London NW3 2AB, United Kingdom, split into street, town, postcode, and country code GB. Field 59 becomes the creditor, with the name Hudson Trading LLC, the account 987654321, and the address 420 Madison Avenue, New York NY 10017, United States, with country code US. Field 57A, CHASUS33XXX, becomes the creditor agent, and field 52A, NWBKGB2LXXX, becomes the debtor agent. Field 56A, the intermediary, becomes the intermediary agent in pacs.008, a field that MT103 users sometimes overlook. Mapping the remaining fields shows where data loss and truncation risk live, and why the mapping must be checked line by line. Field 71A, SHA, maps directly to the charge bearer SHAR in pacs.008, a clean one to one mapping. Field 70, INV-2024-0988 payment for consulting services October 2024, maps to the remittance information, and the invoice number INV-2024-0988 should also be copied to the end to end reference so Hudson Trading LLC can match the payment. Field 72, the telephone instruction, has no direct equivalent in pacs.008, so it must be carried as supplementary data or as an instruction for the creditor agent, and the mapping must record that decision. Field 53B, the sender correspondent account, maps to the settlement information, not to a party field, and placing it in the wrong block would misdirect the settlement.

Transforming an MT103 into a pacs.008 is the practical exercise that turns the theory of ISO 20022 into working knowledge, and it is the exercise a business analyst must be able to do. The MT103 is the SWIFT customer payment message, and the pacs.008 is the ISO 20022 FI to FI customer credit transfer. The transformation is a field by field mapping, and each MT103 field maps to a pacs.008 element, or to a combination of elements, or it has no equivalent and must be dropped or carried in another field. The mapping must be specified in detail, because a wrong mapping will cause the payment to be rejected, or to be credited to the wrong account, or to lose information the beneficiary needs.

Here is the field mapping, in full. MT103 field 20, the sender transaction reference, maps to the pacs.008 payment identification, PmtId, instruction identification, InstrId. MT103 field 23B, the bank operation code, maps to the pacs.008 payment type information, PmtTpInf, and to the service level, SvcLvl, where CRED maps to a normal payment and SPAY, SLEV, or URGP maps to an urgent payment. MT103 field 32A, the value date, currency code, and amount, maps to the pacs.008 instructed amount, InstdAmt, and the interbank settlement date, IntrBkSttlmDt. MT103 field 50a, the ordering customer, maps to the pacs.008 debtor, Dbtr, with option A mapping to the debtor identification, option F mapping to the structured debtor, and option K mapping to the debtor name and address.

MT103 field 52a, the ordering institution, maps to the pacs.008 debtor agent, DbtrAgt. MT103 field 53a, the sender correspondent, maps to the pacs.008 settlement information, SttlmInf, and to the instructing agent, InstgAgt. MT103 field 56a, the intermediary institution, maps to the pacs.008 intermediary agent, IntrmyAgt. MT103 field 57a, the account with institution, maps to the pacs.008 creditor agent, CdtrAgt. MT103 field 59a, the beneficiary customer, maps to the pacs.008 creditor, Cdtr, with option A mapping to the creditor identification, option F mapping to the structured creditor, and option K mapping to the creditor name and address. MT103 field 70, the remittance information, maps to the pacs.008 remittance information, RmtInf, unstructured, Ustrd, limited to 140 characters.

MT103 field 71A, the details of charges, maps to the pacs.008 charge bearer, ChrgBr, where OUR maps to DEBT, BEN maps to CRED, and SHA maps to SHAR. MT103 field 71F, the sender charges, maps to the pacs.008 charges information, ChrgsInf, with the amount and the agent. MT103 field 71G, the receiver charges, also maps to the pacs.008 charges information, ChrgsInf. MT103 field 72, the sender to receiver information, maps to the pacs.008 instruction for the next agent, InstrForNxtAgt, or to the instruction for the creditor agent, InstrForCdtrAgt, depending on the code in field 72. MT103 field 77B, the regulatory reporting, maps to the pacs.008 regulatory reporting, RgltryRptg. MT103 field 33B, the currency and amount originally instructed, maps to the pacs.008 equivalent amount, EqvtAmt, and the exchange rate, XchgRate.

Here is a worked example. An MT103 from HSBC London to JPMorgan New York, field 20 reference PAY 2026 0512 001, field 32A value date 120526, currency USD, amount 10,000.00, field 50K ordering customer Atlas Holdings Ltd, London, field 59 beneficiary, a supplier in New York, field 70 remittance information INV 2026 0441, field 71A OUR. The pacs.008 that results has a group header with MsgId PAY 2026 0512 001, a credit transfer transaction information block with PmtId InstrId PAY 2026 0512 001, EndToEndId ATLAS-INV-0441, UETR generated by HSBC, InstdAmt USD 10,000.00, IntrBkSttlmDt 2026-05-12, Dbtr Atlas Holdings Ltd, London, Cdtr the supplier, RmtInf Ustrd INV 2026 0441, ChrgBr DEBT, because 71A OUR maps to DEBT. The pacs.008 carries the same business information as the MT103, but in a structured XML format, with the debtor and creditor as structured parties, and the remittance information as a separate element.

The tricky fields are the ones that do not map directly. Field 72 in the MT103 carries free text with codes, such as /ACC/ for the account, /INS/ for the instruction, and /BNF/ for the beneficiary information. The pacs.008 does not have a single field 72 equivalent, and the codes must be mapped to the correct pacs.008 element. /ACC/ maps to the creditor account, CdtrAcct. /INS/ maps to the instruction for the next agent, InstrForNxtAgt. /BNF/ maps to the instruction for the creditor agent, InstrForCdtrAgt. A bank that maps field 72 as a single block of text will lose the structure, and the receiving bank will not know which instruction applies to it. The mapping must parse the field 72 codes and route each one to the correct element.

The character set is another tricky area. The MT messages use the SWIFT x character set, which is a limited set of characters, including letters, digits, and a small set of punctuation. The ISO 20022 messages use UTF-8, which is a much larger character set. When a bank converts an MT103 to a pacs.008, it must handle the characters that are valid in ISO 20022 but not in MT, and it must handle the characters that are valid in MT but need to be escaped in XML, such as the ampersand and the angle brackets. A name with an ampersand, such as Smith and Jones Ltd, must be written as Smith andamp; Jones Ltd in the XML, and a bank that writes the raw ampersand will produce invalid XML that fails schema validation. The mapping must therefore include the character set conversion and the XML escaping.

For a business analyst, the transformation requirement must state the field mapping table, the handling of the tricky fields, and the character set conversion. The requirement should say that the system maps each MT103 field to the correct pacs.008 element, that it parses field 72 and routes each code to the correct element, that it escapes XML special characters, and that it validates the resulting pacs.008 against the XSD before sending it. A system that cannot map field 72 correctly, or that cannot escape XML characters, will produce pacs.008 messages that are rejected by the receiving bank, and the payments will fail.
