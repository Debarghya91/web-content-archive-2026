What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain value dates and booking dates. The value date and the booking date are two different dates for the same payment, and confusing them causes interest errors, reconciliation breaks and customer complaints.

Why it matters. Takeaway: the booking date is when the entry is posted, the value date is when interest starts and funds are considered available, and the two can differ by days.

Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Execution date: The date on which the bank executes a payment instruction, debiting the debtor account and sending the message onward.

Payment hub: A central system in a bank that receives payment instructions from all channels, validates them, applies screening and routing rules, and sends them to the correct clearing system or correspondent.

The value date and the booking date are two different dates for the same payment, and confusing them causes interest errors, reconciliation breaks and customer complaints. The booking date is the date the entry is posted to the account, the date the ledger balance changes. The value date is the date from which interest is calculated, the date the funds are considered to have been available to the holder. For a same day payment the two dates are the same, but for a future dated payment, a back valued payment, a cheque, or a cross-border payment, they can differ by days, and the difference is money. Source: Bank for International Settlements, Committee on Payments and Market Infrastructures; ISO 20022 pacs.008 and camt.053 date fields.

The rules for each date are set by the payment system, the bank and the law, and they must be stated in the product terms. The booking date is set by the bank posting process, and it is usually the date the payment is processed. The value date is set by the scheme or the settlement system, and it is the date the beneficiary bank must make the funds available, subject to cut-offs and non-working days. Under the EU Payment Services Directive, Directive (EU) 2015/2366, the value date for a credit to a payment account must be no later than the business day on which the amount is credited to the payee payment service provider account. These are legal duties, not service aspirations.

A worked example shows the money at stake. A corporate customer instructs a EUR 1 million payment on Friday 6 November 2026, with a value date of Monday 9 November 2026, because the payment is future dated to Monday. A second example is different: a cheque for GBP 50,000 is deposited on Monday, booked on Monday, but the value date is Wednesday, when the cheque clears, so the customer cannot earn interest on the GBP 50,000 for Monday and Tuesday, and cannot withdraw it until Wednesday. A third example is a cross-border payment received on Friday with a value date of Monday, because the nostro account is credited on Monday, and the beneficiary who expected Friday value will complain, and the complaint is about the value date, not the booking date.

The parties and the messages that carry the dates are the debtor agent, the creditor agent and the clearing system, and the ISO 20022 messages carry both dates as separate fields. In a pacs.008 the two dates are separate fields. In a camt.053 statement the booking date and the value date are reported as separate date fields for each entry, so the customer can reconcile interest. In a pain.001 the requested execution date is set by the customer, and the bank must honour it subject to cut-offs, or reject the instruction with a reason. A requirement that maps only one date, or that maps the value date into the booking date field, will produce a statement that cannot be reconciled, and the error will be found by the customer finance team, not by the bank test team.

Failure is a date error, and the remedy is a value-date correction, which is taught in full in ch71s7. A payment booked with the wrong value date causes the wrong interest to be calculated, and the correction is a camt.087 value-date correction message, with a compensating interest adjustment. A payment booked on the wrong date, for example a payment dated for a bank holiday, will be rejected by the clearing system or held until the next working day, and the customer must be told the new value date. A payment with a value date in the future that is never executed, because the instruction was cancelled, must release the hold on the available balance on the cancellation date, not on the value date.

The effect on the bank, the customer and operations is that dates are money. For the bank, the value date decides the interest it pays and receives, the liquidity it must hold, and the reporting it must make, because interest is calculated on value dated balances, not on booked balances. For operations, the booking date and the value date must be reconciled separately: the booking date reconciles against the processing file, and the value date reconciles against the settlement and interest calculations. A product that shows only one date to the customer will generate complaints that operations cannot answer without looking at the underlying message.

For a business analyst, product owner or subject matter expert, the test is to state both dates for every payment type, and to state the rule that sets each one. Ask what the value date is for a same day payment, a future dated payment, a cheque, a direct debit, a card clearing, and a cross-border payment, and ask what happens when the value date falls on a non-working day. Ask how the customer is told the value date, how it appears on the statement, and how a value-date error is corrected, with the message, the timeline and the interest adjustment. The ISO 20022 camt.087 message exists for exactly this correction, and the requirement must name it, or the correction will be done as a manual journal, which is slower, less auditable and more error prone.

Two mistakes are common and must be avoided. The first is to promise same day value for a payment that settles next day, which is a mis-selling of the value date, and the customer who relies on it for a contract deadline will suffer a loss. The second is to book a payment on the instruction date when the value date is later, which overstates the ledger balance and understates the available balance, and the error will appear as a reconciliation break. The correct design is to carry both dates through the payment hub, the core banking system and the statement, to show both to the customer where they differ, and to correct a wrong value date with a camt.087 and a compensating interest entry, not with a silent rebooking.

The value date is set by the scheme and the law, and a wrong value date is corrected with a camt.087 and a compensating interest adjustment. Takeaway: the booking date is when the entry is posted, the value date is when interest starts and funds are considered available, and the two can differ by days. The value date is set by the scheme and the law, and a wrong value date is corrected with a camt.087 and a compensating interest adjustment. State both dates for every payment type and show both where they differ. Further reading: Directive (EU) 2015/2366, PSD2, value date provisions; European Payments Council, SEPA Credit Transfer Rulebook 2025, version 1.1, effective 5 October 2025; ISO 20022, pacs.008 and camt.053 date fields.
