Transcript and full text

Show transcript

The MT941 is the Balance Report, the answer to the MT920 request. It sits in Category 9, the cash management family, and its job is narrow and precise: to report the balances on an account at a stated date and time. It does not list transactions. It does not explain movements. It states the position, at a moment, so that the reader can act on it. A treasurer deciding whether to fund an account, a liquidity manager checking a buffer, or an operations team confirming that an expected receipt has landed all read the same message for the same reason: to know where the account stands.

The MT941 is normally sent in response to an MT920 request, though banks may also agree to send it on a schedule. The report names the account, using field 25, and states the date and time of the snapshot, using field 13D. The balances themselves are reported by type: the closing balance at the snapshot time, the available balance, and any forward available balances that project the position ahead. Field 20 carries the transaction reference, and field 21 carries the related reference back to the MT920 that requested the report, so the requester can match the answer to the question.

The balance types in an MT941 deserve care, because they answer different questions. The closing balance is the booked position: the balance after all entries posted up to the snapshot time. The available balance is the position the account holder can actually use: the booked balance adjusted for holds, reservations, and pending items. A forward available balance projects the position to a future date, taking account of known future entries. Confusing booked with available is a classic funding error: the account looks healthy on a booked basis and short on an available basis, and the payment that relied on the booked figure fails.

The MT941 is an intraday tool. End of day statements, the MT940, tell the treasurer what happened. The MT941 tells the treasurer what is happening. During the operating day, a treasurer may request MT941s across a dozen nostro accounts, compare the available balances with the payments still to fund, and move money to where it is needed before the cut off times arrive. The snapshot timing matters: a report requested at noon answers the noon question, and a report requested at three in the afternoon answers a different one. The MT941 is useful precisely because it can be requested at the moment of need.

The MT941 sits between two neighbours that are easy to confuse with it. The MT942 is the interim transaction report: it lists the transactions since the last statement, plus balances. The MT940 is the customer statement: the full end of day record of transactions and balances. The MT941 carries balances only, no transactions. That narrowness is a feature: when the question is only what is the balance, a balance only report is faster to produce, faster to read, and harder to misread than a full statement. Choosing the right report for the question is part of the job.

In ISO 20022 the MT941 maps to the camt.052, the BankToCustomerAccountReport, which carries balances with richer typing: opening, closing, available, forward available, and more, each with an explicit code and a date and time. The camt.060 requests it, replacing the MT920. The migration adds precision to the balance types and structure to the report, but the business pattern is unchanged: ask for the position, receive a snapshot, act on it. The MT941 teaches the pattern in its simplest form, and the camt.052 carries it forward.
