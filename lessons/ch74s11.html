Transcript and full text

Show transcript

A customer credit transfer, CCT, moves money from a payer account to a payee account, and every NPP payment a customer sends or receives is a CCT. An outward CCT is a payment that leaves the bank, sent by a customer of the bank to a payee at another bank. An inward CCT is a payment that arrives at the bank, sent by a payer at another bank to a customer of the bank. The six flows are the outward CCT, the inward CCT, and the solicited and unsolicited returns in each direction, and a bank joining the NPP must build all six.

The outward CCT flow starts when a customer of the bank instructs a payment from a banking app or a business channel. The bank validates the instruction, checks the PayID or account details, shows the payee name for confirmation, and runs fraud and sanctions screening in milliseconds. The bank then sends a pacs.008 message, the customer credit transfer message, through the NPP, and settlement follows in real time through the Fast Settlement Service. A pacs.002 status report confirms acceptance or rejection, and the bank shows the result to the payer within seconds.

The inward CCT flow starts when a pacs.008 message arrives at the bank through the NPP for a customer of the bank. The bank validates the message, checks that the destination account exists and can be credited, runs its screening, and posts the credit to the customer account. The bank then sends a pacs.002 status report back, confirming acceptance, and the customer sees the credit with the remittance data the payer supplied. A bank slow to post an inward CCT breaks the instant promise, because the payer has already been told the payment succeeded.

A solicited return is a return sent in response to a camt.056 cancellation request from the sending bank, and it follows a fixed sequence. The sending bank discovers an error, for example a duplicate payment, and sends a camt.056 message, the FI to FI Payment Cancellation Request, asking the receiving bank to return the funds. The receiving bank investigates, checks whether the funds are still available, and either returns the funds with a pacs.004 return message or rejects the recall with a reason. The sending bank receives the pacs.004 or the rejection, updates the payment status, and tells the payer the outcome.

An unsolicited return is a return the receiving bank sends on its own initiative, without any camt.056 request from the sending bank. The receiving bank returns the payment because it cannot apply the credit, for example because the account is closed, the account details are wrong, or the payee cannot be identified. The receiving bank sends a pacs.004 return message with the original references and a reason code, and the funds travel back to the sending bank, which credits the payer account and notifies the payer. An unsolicited return is normal in instant payments, and the payer experience depends on how quickly the bank explains the reason.

A worked example follows a solicited return from the mistake to the refund. A bookshop in Perth pays a distributor in Adelaide AUD 9,600 twice by mistake, minutes apart. The bookshop bank spots the duplicate and sends a camt.056 cancellation request for the second payment, and the distributor bank confirms the duplicate credit is still available and returns AUD 9,600 with a pacs.004 message. The bookshop bank credits AUD 9,600 back, links the return to the original payment, and notifies the bookshop that the duplicate has been recovered.

The BA specification for NPP flows is a matrix of six flows, and each flow needs its own statuses, queues, messages and customer wording. The CCT flows need validation, screening, posting and pacs.002 handling, the solicited return flow needs the camt.056 queue and the pacs.004 return path, and the unsolicited return flow needs the reason codes and the payer notification. The test is to run one payment through each flow, including a solicited return driven by a camt.056 and an unsolicited return driven by a closed account. Every flow must end in a clear status, a reconciled ledger and a customer who knows what happened.
