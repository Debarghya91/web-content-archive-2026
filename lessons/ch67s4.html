Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Sanctions screening: Checking payment parties, countries and references against sanctions lists published by governments and international bodies.

Market practice: An agreed way of using a message standard in a particular market or community, covering field usage, codes and validation rules.

The Payments Market Practice Group, known as the PMPG, is an industry group that writes market practice guidelines on top of the ISO 20022 standard. The standard says what is possible, and the PMPG says what banks should actually do, for example which fields are mandatory for a cross border payment, how the debtor and creditor data must be populated, and how charges and returns should be handled. The PMPG guidelines are not law, but banks follow them because a payment that ignores market practice is likely to be rejected or delayed by the next bank in the chain. A business analyst should treat the PMPG guidelines as requirements, because the development team will ask which practice applies, and the answer decides the field mapping.

CBPR+ is the market practice for cross border payments and reporting on SWIFT, and it defines how pacs.008, pacs.002, pacs.004 and camt messages are used between banks. Since the coexistence period ended on 22 November 2025, cross border payments on SWIFT are ISO 20022 native, and MT messages are no longer accepted for the migrated flows. CBPR+ requires full debtor and creditor data, including names and addresses, a UETR for tracking, and a structured approach to charges and returns. A payment that met the old MT standard may fail under CBPR+, because the old format allowed truncated addresses and missing data that CBPR+ does not accept. The change is not cosmetic, it is a data quality change, and banks spent years cleaning their customer data to meet it.

SEPA is the euro area market practice, and it defines how pacs.008 is used for SEPA Credit Transfer and SCT Inst. A SEPA payment uses the IBAN, a BIC that is optional in many cases, and a structured or unstructured remittance, and it settles through a SEPA clearing system. The Instant Payments Regulation, now in force, requires instant euro payments to be available, to be priced no higher than a standard credit transfer, and to include verification of payee. A SEPA pacs.008 is therefore simpler than a CBPR+ one, because the data is standardised, the currency is always euro, and the settlement is domestic to the euro area. A business analyst must not copy a CBPR+ mapping into a SEPA flow, because the mandatory fields differ.

Fedwire, CHAPS and FedNow each add their own market practice on top of the same ISO 20022 base. Fedwire, the US real time gross settlement system, went live with ISO 20022 in July 2025, and it requires the full party data and the extended remittance information that US banks need for sanctions screening and reconciliation. CHIPS, the US private clearing system, migrated in April 2024, and CHAPS, the UK high value system, uses ISO 20022 with its own usage guidelines for sterling payments. FedNow, the US instant payment service, uses ISO 20022 from the start, with its own message usage rules for instant settlement. The same EUR or USD 10,000 payment will therefore carry different mandatory fields, different character sets, and different cut off rules on each rail, and the mapping must be built per rail, not once for all rails.
