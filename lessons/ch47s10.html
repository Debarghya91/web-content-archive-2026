Key terms

Suspense account: A temporary account used to hold funds or entries that cannot yet be posted to their final account. Banks use suspense accounts for unidentified receipts, pending investigations and items awaiting...

Payment hub: A central system in a bank that receives payment instructions from all channels, validates them, applies screening and routing rules, and sends them to the correct clearing system or correspondent.

Idempotency: The property that repeating the same request has the same effect as sending it once. In payments, an idempotency key lets a client safely retry a request after a timeout without creating a duplicate...

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

Every payment integration makes three calls into the core banking system, and each call answers a different question before money moves. Balance enquiry asks whether the funding account holds enough money, account validation asks whether the destination account can receive the payment, and posting asks the core system to write the entries that move the money. The three calls run in that logical order, with the two checks completed before the posting that cannot easily be undone, and each call returns an answer the payment hub must record. An integration that skips a check to save time trades a fast journey for a payment that may debit an account with no funds or credit an account that cannot accept it.

Balance enquiry returns the ledger balance and the available balance for the account that will fund the payment, and the difference between the two balances decides whether the payment may proceed. The ledger balance reflects the entries already posted to the account, while the available balance reflects what the account can actually spend after holds and reservations are taken into account. A funding account might show a ledger balance of GBP 5,000 and an available balance of GBP 4,200, so a payment of GBP 4,500 must be refused even though the ledger balance looks large enough.

Account validation confirms that the destination account exists, is open, matches the payment currency, and can be debited or credited as the payment requires. Each part matters, because a closed or blocked account cannot accept a credit, and an account in the wrong currency cannot accept the payment without conversion. A payment of EUR 3,000 to an account that only holds pounds fails validation on currency, and the payment must stop before any debit is made, rather than debiting the payer and discovering the problem afterwards. Validation checks the status and the currency of the account, while a separate name matching check answers whether the account belongs to the expected payee.

Posting writes the debit entry and the credit entry that move the money and create the accounting record for the payment. The debit removes funds from the payer account, the credit adds funds to the receiving account, and the two entries are written as one unit, so a crash cannot create one entry without the other. A payment of GBP 1,200 therefore appears as a debit of GBP 1,200 on the payer account and a matching credit of GBP 1,200 on the receiving side, linked by one transaction identifier. Posting returns a confirmation that the entries were written, and the hub must record that confirmation before it tells the scheme or the customer that the payment has succeeded.

Timeout handling protects the payment when a core banking call does not answer in time, because a timeout means the outcome is unknown rather than failed. A posting call that times out may already have written its entries, so a blind retry without protection can post the same payment twice and debit the customer twice for one instruction. Idempotency prevents that harm, because every call carries the same transaction identifier on every attempt, and the core system recognises a repeated identifier and returns the original result instead of writing a second posting. The hub must therefore check the status of the original call using the same identifier, and retry only with that identifier.

An unavailable core banking system forces a clear choice between queuing the payment and rejecting it with a status the customer can act on, and losing the payment silently is never an acceptable third option. Queuing holds the payment and retries it when the core system recovers, with a time limit after which the payment is rejected. A failed posting needs reversal or suspense handling, because a debit that succeeded while its matching credit failed must be reversed back to the payer or parked in a suspense account for operations to resolve. The business analyst must specify the queue rule, the rejection message, the reversal path, and the suspense handling before the integration goes live.
