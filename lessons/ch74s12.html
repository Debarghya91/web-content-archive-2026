Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Liquidity: Cash or assets that a bank can use immediately to meet payment obligations. Banks manage liquidity by forecasting inflows and outflows, funding nostro and settlement accounts, and using central bank...

Clearing: The process of exchanging payment instructions between banks and calculating what each bank owes the others.

  1. What to do. List the three levels of the message and name the field that carries the amount and the field that carries the end-to-end reference.

Correct result. The amount is carried in the instructed amount element and the reference in the end-to-end identification. The three levels are the group header, the payment information, and the credit transfer transaction information.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The group header identifies the message, the payment information identifies the batch, and the transaction information carries the single payment.

The four rejection and cancellation flows are the paths an NPP payment takes when it does not complete normally, and each one has a different cause, timing and customer message. A payment rejected in clearing never reaches settlement, and a payment rejected in settlement could not settle, and is returned instead. A recall asks for funds already credited to be returned, and it ends in either a return or a rejection of the recall. A BA who treats all four as one generic failure will write one generic error message, and customers who receive it will call the bank.

A rejection in clearing happens when a message fails validation or scheme checks before settlement, and the payment never settles. The sending bank or the NPP platform checks the message format, the mandatory fields, the addressing and the scheme rules, and a message that fails any check is rejected with a pacs.002 status report carrying a reason. A payment to an unregistered PayID, a payment above the sending bank limit, or a message with a missing field all fail in clearing. The payer is told at once and can correct the details and try again.

A rejection in settlement happens when a payment reaches settlement but cannot settle, and the payment is returned rather than completed. The usual cause is insufficient settlement funds, where the sending bank settlement account cannot fund the payment at that moment. The payer is not left in doubt, because the rejection is reported back and the payer account is not debited for a payment that never settled. The bank must restore its settlement funding and decide on a retry, and the customer message must name the delay and the next step.

A recall is a camt.056 request asking the receiving bank to return funds already credited, and it starts an investigation, not an automatic reversal. The sending bank sends the camt.056 message, the FI to FI Payment Cancellation Request, naming the original payment and the reason, often a duplicate payment or a wrong amount. The receiving bank investigates, checks whether the funds are still available, and either returns the funds with a pacs.004 message or rejects the recall with a reason. The sending bank updates the status and tells the payer, and a rejected recall leaves the payer with the bank mistaken payment process.

A worked example follows a duplicate payment through a recall to a successful return. A catering company in Canberra pays a supplier AUD 4,800, and a system fault sends the payment twice. The catering company bank sends a camt.056 recall within the hour, and the supplier bank finds the duplicate credit untouched and returns AUD 4,800 with a pacs.004 message. The catering company bank credits the refund and notifies the catering company the same day, but if the supplier had spent the duplicate credit, the receiving bank would reject the recall.

The customer experience differs sharply across the four flows, and the bank must write a different message for each one. A clearing rejection is instant and actionable, while a settlement rejection is a delay the payer did not cause, so the message must explain the delay and the retry plan. A successful recall is a relief, because the money comes back, but the payer waited for an investigation they could not see. A rejected recall is the hardest message of all, because the payer learns the money cannot be recovered through the recall, and the message must explain the next step honestly.

The BA requirement must separate the four flows in statuses, queues, messages and customer wording, and must never merge them into one failure path. Clearing rejections need validation rules and pacs.002 handling, settlement rejections need liquidity monitoring and retry rules, and recalls need the camt.056 queue, the pacs.004 return path and the rejection reasons. The test is to drive one payment into each flow and check that the status, the ledger entry, the queue item and the customer message all match the flow it took. A payment in the wrong failure queue will be worked by the wrong team with the wrong expectation.
