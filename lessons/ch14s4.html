Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Cover payment: A payment method where the customer instruction travels one route while the funds travel separately as a bank to bank cover message.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Exercise

Scenario: you are a payments BA advising a corporate treasurer. The company needs to pay USD 250,000 to a supplier in Japan. The company bank is in London. The supplier bank is in Tokyo. There is no direct correspondent relationship between the London bank and the Tokyo bank. The London bank has a USD correspondent in New York, and the Tokyo bank has a USD correspondent in New York. The payment is urgent and the supplier needs the full USD 250,000. Determine whether to use the serial method or the cover method, justify the choice with the routing, the settlement, the charges and the timing, and write the message flow for the chosen method.

Serial method: a single MT103 travels from the London bank to the New York correspondent, then to the Tokyo bank correspondent in New York, then to the Tokyo bank. Each bank deducts its charges from the amount as it passes through, unless OUR is used. Cover method: an MT103 travels direct from the London bank to the Tokyo bank, and a separate MT202 COV travels from the London bank to its New York correspondent, then to the Tokyo bank correspondent, then to the Tokyo bank. The MT103 and the MT202 COV are linked by field 21.

Charges: the London bank charges USD 25, the New York correspondent charges USD 20, the Tokyo bank charges USD 15. The payment is urgent, value date today.

  1. What to do. Choose serial or cover. Justify the choice with four points: routing, settlement, charges and timing. Then write the message flow for the chosen method, naming each message, each bank, and the amount at each hop. State what the beneficiary receives.

Correct result. Choose the cover method. Routing: the MT103 goes direct from London to Tokyo, so the Tokyo bank receives the full instruction immediately and can prepare the credit, while the funds travel separately through the New York correspondents. Settlement: the MT202 COV moves the funds from London to New York to Tokyo, and the Tokyo bank matches the cover to the MT103 by field 21 before crediting. Charges: with OUR, the sender pays all charges, so the beneficiary receives the full USD 250,000; the London bank charges USD 25, the New York correspondent charges USD 20, and the Tokyo bank charges USD 15, all billed to the sender, total USD 60, so the sender pays USD 250,060 in total.

Timing: the cover method is faster for the beneficiary bank because it receives the instruction direct and does not wait for the funds to pass through each intermediary before it can act. The serial method would be slower because each intermediary must receive the MT103, deduct its charges, and forward it, and the beneficiary would receive less than USD 250,000 unless OUR is used and each bank honours it. Message flow for cover: MT103 from London to Tokyo (USD 250,000, field 21 PAY-JP-20261107); MT202 COV from London to New York correspondent (USD 250,000, field 21 PAY-JP-20261107); MT202 COV from New York correspondent to Tokyo bank correspondent (USD 250,000, field 21 PAY-JP-20261107); MT202 COV from Tokyo bank correspondent to Tokyo bank (USD 250,000, field 21 PAY-JP-20261107); Tokyo bank credits the supplier USD 250,000.

Worked reasoning: the cover method is chosen when the beneficiary bank needs the instruction quickly and the funds can travel separately, and when the sender wants the beneficiary to receive the full amount. The serial method is chosen when the payment is small, the chain is short, or the banks prefer a single message. Here the payment is large and urgent, and there is no direct relationship between London and Tokyo, so cover is the right choice.

Why. Hint 1: the key question is whether the beneficiary bank needs the instruction before the funds arrive; if yes, use cover. Hint 2: with OUR, the sender pays all charges, so the beneficiary receives the full amount; with SHA or BEN, the beneficiary receives less. Hint 3: field 21 links the MT103 and the MT202 COV; without it, the beneficiary bank cannot match the cover to the instruction. The worked explanation shows the decision discipline: routing, settlement, charges and timing are the four factors, and the choice must be justified on all four, not just one.

Show transcript

The clearest way to compare the two methods is to look at the same payment through both lenses. In the serial method, the MT103 travels from bank to bank, and the funds travel with it, so the instruction and the money are never separated. In the cover method, the MT103 travels directly from the sending bank to the receiving bank, and the MT202 COV travels through the correspondent chain carrying the funds. In the serial method, each intermediary bank sees the full payment details and can deduct its charge from the amount.

In the cover method, the intermediary banks in the cover chain see the MT202 COV, which carries a copy of the originator and beneficiary information, and they deduct their charges from the cover amount, while the receiving bank sees the full MT103 directly. The comparison is about the methods, and it holds for the ISO 20022 forms too, a pacs.008 in serial, and a pacs.008 plus a pacs.009 COV in cover. Speed, cost and transparency differ in ways that matter for the customer. Serial is simpler to set up, because it needs no separate reconciliation between an instruction and a cover payment, but it is slower when the chain is long, because each bank processes the payment in turn.

Cover can be faster for the beneficiary, because the instruction arrives directly, but it is more complex to operate, because the receiving bank must match the cover funds to the instruction, and a mismatch creates a reconciliation break. Serial can be cheaper when the chain is short, because there is only one payment to process at each step. Cover can be more expensive when there are many banks in the cover chain, because each one charges for moving the funds. The risk profile also differs. In serial, if one bank in the chain fails or stops the payment, the payment is stuck and the information is stuck with it.

In cover, if the cover funds are delayed, the beneficiary bank still has the instruction and can explain to the beneficiary that the payment is expected and the funds are in transit, which is a better customer experience. In serial, a compliance query by an intermediary bank stops the whole payment. In cover, a compliance query by a bank in the cover chain stops the funds but the instruction has already reached the beneficiary bank, so the beneficiary bank can start its own checks. Neither method is always better.

The right method depends on the banks, the currencies, the amounts and the service levels, and the analyst must be able to recommend the right one for a given corridor.
