Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Clearing: The process of exchanging payment instructions between banks and calculating what each bank owes the others.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

The four-corner model describes a SEPA Credit Transfer as a flow between four parties. The originator is the customer who instructs the payment, and the beneficiary is the customer who receives it. The originator bank holds the originator account and debits it, while the beneficiary bank holds the beneficiary account and credits it. Between the two banks sits a clearing and settlement mechanism, which exchanges the interbank messages and settles the funds. No single party controls the whole journey, so each corner must pass accurate information to the next corner, and each handover must be recorded so the payment can be traced end to end.

Our worked example runs through the whole chapter. Loire Components SAS, a French company based in Lyon, buys precision parts from Rhine Metals GmbH, a supplier based in Frankfurt. Loire Components holds its euro account with Meridian Bank Paris, and Rhine Metals holds its euro account with Harbour Bank Frankfurt. On Monday 9 November 2026 Loire Components owes EUR 48,250 for invoice INV-2026-0873, and it instructs Meridian Bank Paris to pay that amount to Rhine Metals. The payment carries end to end identification LOIRE-RHINE-2026-1148, which will travel with it from the first instruction to the final credit.

The originator bank and the beneficiary bank have distinct duties in the four-corner model, and confusing them is a common beginner error. Meridian Bank Paris, as originator bank, must validate the instruction, check the account balance, screen the payment, debit EUR 48,250 from Loire Components, and create the interbank payment message. Harbour Bank Frankfurt, as beneficiary bank, must validate the incoming message, check the beneficiary account, credit EUR 48,250 to Rhine Metals, and report the credit to its customer.

Neither bank can do the other bank job, and neither bank sees the other bank customer account directly. The clearing and settlement mechanism is the fourth element that makes the model work across banks and borders. In the SEPA area, the mechanism exchanges pacs.008 messages between banks and settles the resulting obligations, usually through central bank money. Meridian Bank Paris does not send money directly to Harbour Bank Frankfurt. Instead, it sends a pacs.008 message into the clearing system, the system nets or settles the obligation, and Harbour Bank Frankfurt receives the funds and the instruction to credit Rhine Metals.

The mechanism also sets cut-off times, so a payment sent after the cut-off waits for the next exchange period.
