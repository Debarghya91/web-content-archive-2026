Key terms

Authorisation: Approval by the issuer for a card transaction to proceed, confirming the card is valid and funds or credit are available.

Creditor: The party that is owed money and receives a payment, such as a supplier, a seller or a service provider.

Mandate: An authorisation given by a debtor to a creditor to collect payments from the debtor account by direct debit.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

PayTo is the mandate service that runs on the NPP, and it lets a payee collect payments only under a mandate the payer authorised in their own bank app. A mandate names the payee, the account, the amount rules and the frequency, and the payee initiates each payment under that mandate. PayTo replaces direct debit arrangements with mandates the payer can see, change, pause and cancel from their own bank app. The payments travel over the NPP rails as instant credit transfers, so the payee is paid in seconds rather than waiting for a batch cycle.

The weakness of a direct debit arrangement is that the mandate lives with the biller, and the payer cannot see it or stop it without contacting the biller. A payer who ends a subscription must chase the biller to cancel the debit, and a debit that continues after cancellation becomes a dispute. A payer who never sees the mandate cannot check the amount before it is taken. PayTo answers that weakness by placing the mandate inside the payer bank app, where the payer authorises it, watches it and controls it.

The mandate lifecycle has five stages, and each stage is a state the mandate can be in. Creation is the stage where the payee sends the mandate terms to the payer bank, and authorisation is the stage where the payer approves or declines them in their own bank app. Amendment is the stage where the terms change, for example when the amount or the frequency changes, and the payer must approve the new terms. Suspension pauses the mandate so no payment can be taken until it resumes, and cancellation ends the mandate so no further payment can be taken under it.

A worked example follows a mandate from creation to cancellation. A fitness studio in Brisbane, Harbourview Fitness, asks a member to authorise a mandate for AUD 65 a month, and the request appears in the member bank app showing the studio name, the amount, the frequency and the account. The member authorises the mandate, and the first payment of AUD 65 is taken the next month and credited to the studio within seconds. Six months later the studio raises the fee to AUD 72 a month, the member approves the amendment, and when the membership ends the member cancels the mandate from the app.

The two messages behind a mandate are pain.013 and pain.014, and they carry the mandate conversation between the payee bank and the payer bank. The pain.013 message, the Creditor Payment Activation Request, carries the mandate request to the payer bank, and it is used to create and amend a mandate. The pain.014 message, the Creditor Payment Activation Request Status Report, carries the answer back, and it reports whether the mandate was authorised, declined, amended or cancelled. Both banks therefore hold the same record of what the payer decided, with the request travelling one way and the status travelling the other.

The failure paths show why payer control matters in practice. A mandate the payer declines never becomes active, so no payment can be taken under it, and the payee must ask again with corrected terms. A payment attempted under a suspended mandate is refused, because suspension is a hard stop, and a payment attempted when the payer account holds insufficient funds fails and the payee must be told promptly. A mandate cancelled before a scheduled payment date blocks that payment, and a payee that attempts to collect after cancellation has no mandate to rely on.

The BA requirement for PayTo must cover the mandate display, the authorisation step, the amendment flow, the suspension and cancellation controls, and the status reporting between the banks. The display must show the payee name, the amount rules, the frequency and the account. The test is to create one mandate, authorise it, amend the amount, suspend it, resume it and cancel it, checking at each stage that the pain.014 status matches the payer decision. No payment may be taken outside an active authorised mandate, and any test that allows one has failed.
