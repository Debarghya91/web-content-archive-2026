The Bank of England RTGS renewal programme: what changed and what it means for banks

What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain the Bank of England RTGS renewal programme and apply it to a real banking scenario.

Why it matters. RTGS renewal is the biggest UK infrastructure change in a decade, and the analyst must track it.

Chapter objective: Learner explains the UK payment systems in depth, distinguishes BACS, CHAPS, Faster Payments and Open Banking payments, applies Confirmation of Payee outcomes, explains the Bank of England RTGS renewal, and applies the APP scam reimbursement rules to realistic UK payment scenarios with correct terminology.

Key learning points

  • Bank of England RTGS renewal programme
  • New messages carrying structured data: LEI, address, purpose, UETR
  • Synchronisation removing the settlement risk of legs settling hours apart
  • Intraday liquidity provision against eligible collateral
  • MT message transfer
  • Design and operating day

Key terms

Sanctions screening: Checking payment parties, countries and references against sanctions lists published by governments and international bodies.

Intraday liquidity: Cash available to a bank during the business day to settle payments as they fall due. Intraday liquidity comes from account balances, incoming payments and central bank facilities.

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

The Bank of England RTGS renewal programme is the rebuild of the infrastructure that settles sterling payments in central bank money. The old RTGS system was built in the 1990s, and it was reliable, but it was hard to change, hard to connect to, and it closed at 18:00. The renewal replaces the core settlement engine with a modern platform, moves CHAPS and other sterling settlement onto ISO 20022 messages, extends operating hours, and opens access to a wider range of payment service providers, including non-bank firms. The new core settlement engine went live in 2025, and the programme continues with further releases that add new functions. The most visible change is the move to ISO 20022. The old RTGS used SWIFT MT messages, and the new platform uses the ISO 20022 MX messages, pacs.008 for customer payments, pacs.009 for bank to bank payments, and pacs.002 for status reporting. The new messages carry structured data: the legal entity identifier of the payer and payee, a structured postal address, a purpose code, and a unique end-to-end transaction reference. The structured data improves sanctions screening, because the screening engine can match on a legal entity identifier instead of a free-text name, and it improves reconciliation, because the beneficiary can match the payment to an invoice automatically. Extended hours are the second major change. The renewed RTGS stays open longer than the old system, and the Bank has consulted on moving towards near 24-hour operation. Longer hours matter because sterling is used around the world, and a payment that arrives from Asia in the evening currently waits until the next morning. Extended hours also matter for synchronised settlement, where a sterling payment and a foreign currency payment, or a payment and a securities transfer, settle at the same instant. Synchronisation removes the settlement risk that exists when one leg settles hours before the other. Wider access is the third change. The Bank has opened RTGS settlement accounts to non-bank payment service providers that meet its criteria, where previously only banks could hold them. A non-bank that holds a settlement account can settle its own payments in central bank money, instead of settling through a bank, which reduces its dependency and its cost. The access criteria cover financial soundness, operational resilience, and compliance with anti-money-laundering rules, and the Bank supervises the account holders. The change is gradual, and the Bank admits firms one at a time, but the direction is clear: central bank money is no longer only for banks. Liquidity saving mechanisms are the fourth change. The renewed RTGS includes queuing and offsetting algorithms that look at the queue of payments waiting to settle and find payments that can be settled against each other, reducing the liquidity each participant needs. The Bank also provides intraday liquidity against eligible collateral, as it did before, and the auto-collateralisation mechanism continues. The practical effect is that a bank needs less central bank money to settle the same volume of payments, which lowers the cost of participating in CHAPS, and which matters most on days when payment volumes spike, such as the end of the financial year. What the renewal means for a bank is a multi-year change programme, not a single upgrade. The bank must rebuild its payment engine to send and receive ISO 20022 messages, must change its liquidity management to use the new queuing and offsetting, must test its connectivity to the new platform, and must retrain its operations team. The Bank ran a long testing period before the new core went live, and banks that had not completed their testing could not connect on day one. A bank that sends an MT message to the renewed RTGS will have it rejected, so the migration is not optional. The most common failure during migration is a data mapping error, where a field that was free text in MT becomes structured in MX, and the bank's system truncates it or puts it in the wrong place. For a business analyst, the RTGS renewal is a requirements programme that touches every sterling payment the bank makes. The acceptance criteria must state the ISO 20022 message set, the extended hours the bank will support, and the liquidity arrangements. A test must prove that a pacs.008 sent to the renewed RTGS settles in central bank money, and that the pacs.002 status report is received and processed. The requirements must cover the synchronised settlement use cases the bank wants to support, and the access model if the bank settles on behalf of non-bank firms. The design must handle the longer operating day, including staffing for the operations team and the liquidity desk. Last reviewed 8 November 2026.
