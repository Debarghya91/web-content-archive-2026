Key terms

Market practice: An agreed way of using a message standard in a particular market or community, covering field usage, codes and validation rules.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

T2 is the Eurosystem real time gross settlement system, the platform on which euro high value payments settle in central bank money, and every message that moves through it is an ISO 20022 message. The T2 message catalogue is the definitive list of those messages, defined in the User Detailed Functional Specifications, the UDFS, published by the Eurosystem. The catalogue is not a free choice: a participant that wants to send a payment, move liquidity, query a balance or receive a statement must use the catalogue message for that function, in the T2 usage guideline for that message. The analyst who builds a T2 connection starts from the catalogue, not from the general ISO 20022 standard.

The payment messages are the core of the catalogue. A customer credit transfer arrives as a pacs.008, and a financial institution credit transfer arrives as a pacs.009, in both cases with the T2 usage guideline that constrains the general message to the fields and rules T2 accepts. The payment status report, pacs.002, carries the acceptance or rejection of a payment, and the payment return, pacs.004, carries a return of funds. The payment status request, pacs.028, lets a participant ask T2 for the status of a payment it has sent. These five messages carry the payment business, and the analyst must know which pacs message applies to each leg: a customer payment and a bank to bank payment are different messages, and sending the wrong one will be rejected by validation before settlement is even attempted.

The cash management messages carry the liquidity and reporting business. Liquidity moves between accounts with camt.050, the liquidity credit transfer, which is how a participant moves funds between its own accounts in the different T2 components, and between T2 and the other TARGET services. Account statements arrive as camt.053, the bank to customer statement, and intraday account reports arrive as camt.052. Debit and credit notifications arrive as camt.054. The payment cancellation request, camt.056, lets a participant ask for a payment to be cancelled before it settles. These messages are as important as the payment messages, because a bank that cannot move liquidity or read its statements cannot operate in T2, however good its payment messages are.

The administration messages carry the system business. Every ISO 20022 message in T2 travels inside a business application header, head.001, which carries the sender, the receiver, the message type and the business references, and which T2 uses for routing and for duplicate detection. System event notifications arrive as admi.004, telling participants about system events such as the start and end of settlement windows. Error messages arrive as admi.002, telling the sender that a message failed validation and why. The analyst must handle the administration messages with the same care as the business messages, because a participant that ignores system events will miss window changes, and a participant that ignores error messages will never know why its payments are failing.

The usage guidelines are what turn the general standard into the T2 catalogue. A general pacs.008 allows many optional fields and many code values, but the T2 usage guideline for pacs.008 restricts the message to the fields T2 processes, makes some optional fields mandatory, and limits code values to the T2 list. The guidelines follow the HVPS+ market practice, the harmonised usage guidelines for high value payment systems, with T2 specific additions. A message that is valid against the general ISO 20022 schema but violates the T2 usage guideline will be rejected, and the rejection will cite the guideline rule, not the schema. The analyst must validate against the usage guideline, not just the schema, and must track guideline versions, because T2 updates its guidelines on the annual standards release cycle.

A worked example shows the catalogue in use. A bank wants to send a euro payment for a customer to another bank. It builds a pacs.008 under the T2 usage guideline, wraps it in a head.001 header, and sends it to T2. T2 validates the message against the guideline, checks the balance, settles the payment, and returns a pacs.002 confirming settlement. The bank then moves surplus liquidity to its account in another TARGET service with a camt.050, and at the end of the day it receives a camt.053 statement showing all movements. Every step used a catalogue message, every message followed its usage guideline, and the catalogue is what made the whole day interoperable. The analyst who knows the catalogue can read any T2 flow; the analyst who does not will guess.
