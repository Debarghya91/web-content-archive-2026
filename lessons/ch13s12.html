Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Payment hub: A central system in a bank that receives payment instructions from all channels, validates them, applies screening and routing rules, and sends them to the correct clearing system or correspondent.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

A nostro account is our account with you: a bank holds an account with a correspondent bank in another country, in that country currency, so it can make and receive payments there. The correspondent bank keeps the account. The account holding bank keeps a mirror: its own record of the same account, in its own ledger, under its own control. The mirror is the holder view of the account. The correspondent record is the counterparty view. The two records describe the same money, and they must agree. When they do not, something is wrong, and finding it is reconciliation.

The mirror exists because a bank cannot run its business on another bank statements alone. The holder needs its own balance, its own transaction history, and its own ability to check. The mirror is posted in real time as the holder instructs payments and receives funds, so the holder always has a current position. The correspondent statement arrives later, daily or intraday, and the holder compares the two. Without a mirror, the holder would discover errors, missing receipts, and unexpected charges only when the statement arrived, which is too late to act on them.

Mirror mechanics follow a simple rule: every transaction on the nostro is posted to the mirror with debits and credits reversed. When the holder instructs a payment, the holder debits its mirror, because its money has gone. The correspondent credits the account from its own perspective, because the same entry is a credit on its books. When funds arrive, the holder credits the mirror and the correspondent debits. The reversal confuses newcomers, but it is just double entry bookkeeping seen from two sides: the same entry is a debit in one ledger and a credit in the other. Value dates, amounts, and references are mirrored exactly, so that each entry in the mirror matches one entry on the statement.

Reconciliation is the daily comparison of the mirror against the statement. The servicing bank sends its statement, as an MT940 or a camt.053, and the holder matches each statement entry to a mirror entry. Matched items clear. Outstanding items are entries in one record but not yet in the other, usually timing differences: a payment instructed late in the day that the correspondent has not yet posted. Breaks are entries that do not match: different amounts, different references, different value dates, or entries that appear on one side and never on the other. Breaks are investigated, explained, and cleared, and their ageing is monitored.

Breaks have a small number of causes, and each has a resolution path. Timing differences clear on their own when the next statement arrives, and need only monitoring. Charges levied by the servicing bank appear on the statement but not in the mirror until the holder posts them, and need a charge posting. Errors, a wrong amount, a duplicate posting, a misdirected payment, need investigation and correction, sometimes involving the correspondent. Foreign exchange differences arise where a transaction involves conversion, and need the rate and the calculation checked. A break that ages without explanation is a control concern, because it may hide an error, a missing receipt, or a fraud.

Mirrors are the foundation of nostro control. They feed liquidity reporting, because the mirror balance is the holder current position. They feed fraud detection, because an unexpected entry on the statement with no mirror counterpart is visible immediately. They feed the daily reconciliation that auditors and regulators expect. Automation keeps mirrors current at scale: straight through posting from the payment hub, automatic matching against statements, and break workflows that route exceptions to the right team. A mirror that is not maintained daily is a blind spot, and a bank that cannot see its nostro positions cannot manage its liquidity or its risk.
