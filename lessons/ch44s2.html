Key terms

Payment hub: A central system in a bank that receives payment instructions from all channels, validates them, applies screening and routing rules, and sends them to the correct clearing system or correspondent.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

A cut-off time is the last moment a payment instruction can be accepted for processing on a given value date. After the cut-off, the instruction is either held for the next processing day or rejected back to the channel with a clear message. For CHAPS in the United Kingdom, the customer cut-off is often 5 in the afternoon and the bank to bank cut-off is 6 in the evening, while for Fedwire in the United States the closing time is half past 6 in the evening Eastern Time. A customer who submits a payment of 500,000 pounds at 5 past 5 in the afternoon for CHAPS will therefore see a value date of the next working day, even though the money leaves the account immediately.

Operations must apply these times exactly, because one late release can breach a contract. Processing calendars are the reference tables that tell the payment hub which days are working days for each currency and each clearing system. A payment in euros follows the T2 calendar, which is closed on New Year Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, 1 May, Christmas Day and 26 December. A payment in US dollars follows the United States federal holiday calendar, which closes Fedwire on Thanksgiving and Independence Day.

A payment from London to Tokyo must respect both the United Kingdom calendar and the Japanese calendar, because the beneficiary bank in Tokyo cannot credit an account on a Japanese public holiday. The hub reads these calendars automatically when it sets the value date. Weekends create a regular pattern that operations teams plan around every week. On a Friday afternoon, the volume of payments rises sharply as companies pay suppliers before the weekend and customers send money home. A bank may process 2.4 million payments on a Friday compared with 1.9 million on a Tuesday, and the team adds extra staff to the exception queue to cope.

On a Monday morning, the team faces the payments that arrived over the weekend through Faster Payments and the standing orders due on Monday, plus the files that corporate customers uploaded on Sunday evening. A pain.001 file with 12,000 payments worth 18 million pounds may be waiting at 6 in the morning on Monday. Public holidays in different countries cause the most confusion, because a payment can be a working day in one country and a holiday in another. A payment of 750,000 US dollars from a customer in London to a supplier in Hong Kong on the day of the Chinese New Year holiday will be held by the correspondent bank in Hong Kong until the next working day.

The sending bank must warn the customer at the point of instruction, not after the payment is stuck. Good banks publish a holiday calendar for each currency on their website and in their corporate portal, and the payment hub uses the same calendar to calculate the value date automatically so the customer sees the correct date before confirming. When a cut-off is missed, the operations team follows a clear procedure instead of improvising. First, the analyst checks the value date on the instruction and confirms whether the customer asked for same day value or accepted next day value.

Second, the team checks whether an alternative rail is still open, for example sending a payment of 100,000 euros through Faster Payments is not possible, but a SEPA Instant payment may still be available. Third, the team contacts the customer with the new value date and the reason for the delay. Fourth, the incident is logged so the bank can see whether one channel repeatedly sends payments after the cut-off and needs a reminder or a system fix.
