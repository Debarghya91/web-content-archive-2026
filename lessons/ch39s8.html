Show transcript

A treasury team needs to know the balance of its euro account at 11 in the morning, before it decides whether to fund a large payment. The end of day statement will not arrive until tomorrow, and the intraday report may not have been sent yet. The team sends a camt.060, which is the ISO 20022 account reporting request. It is a request, not a report. It asks the account servicing bank for a report on a specific account, for a specific period, in a specific format. The servicing bank replies with a camt.052 intraday report or a camt.053 end of day statement, depending on what was requested. The camt.060 carries four things. First, the account it refers to, identified by IBAN or by the account number the servicing bank uses. Second, the period it covers, which can be a single point in time, for example the balance at 11:00, or a range, for example all movements between 09:00 and 11:00. Third, the type of report requested, which can be an intraday report, an end of day statement, or a notification of debits and credits. Fourth, the format and the delivery channel, so the servicing bank knows whether to send the report as a file, through SWIFT, or through an API. Without those four, the servicing bank cannot know what to send. The difference between camt.060 and the other camt messages is the direction. camt.052, camt.053 and camt.054 are reports and notifications that the servicing bank sends to the account holder, usually on a schedule. camt.060 is the account holder asking for a report outside that schedule. A corporate treasury team uses it when it needs a balance now, not at the next scheduled report. A liquidity manager uses it to check a prefund balance before a large instant payment run. An operations team uses it to get a fresh report after a system outage, so it can rebuild its position from a known point. The camt.060 also matters for reconciliation, because a report requested on demand can be compared with the report received on schedule. If the intraday report at 11:00 shows a balance of EUR 2,450,000 and the end of day statement shows EUR 2,450,000 plus the afternoon movements, the two must agree on the morning position. If they do not, there is a break, and the operations team must find whether a movement was missed, duplicated, or recorded with the wrong value date. The camt.060 therefore does two jobs: it gives the treasury team the balance it needs now, and it gives the reconciliation team a second source to check against. For a business analyst, the camt.060 is a small message with a clear use case, and the requirements around it are straightforward. The request must identify the account unambiguously, because a request for the wrong account returns the wrong balance. The period must be precise, because a report for the wrong period is worse than no report. The response must be matched to the request, so the system knows which camt.052 or camt.053 answers which camt.060. And the request must be logged, because a treasury decision made on a balance from a camt.060 must be auditable later. The lesson to carry forward is that camt.060 is the question, camt.052 and camt.053 are the answers, and a bank that cannot ask the question cannot manage its liquidity in real time.

camt.060 is the account reporting request. It is the message a customer sends to its bank to ask for a statement, a report, or a notification. It is the request that causes the bank to send a camt.052, a camt.053, or a camt.054. The camt.060 has a group header, GrpHdr, which carries the message identification, MsgId, the creation date and time, CreDtTm, and the message recipient, MsgRcpt. It has a reporting request block, RptgReq, which carries the requested message name identification, ReqstdMsgNmId, which states the message requested, for example camt.053.001.08 for a statement, the account, Acct, the account owner, AcctOwnr, the account servicer, AcctSvcr, and the reporting period, RptgPrd, which states the from date and time, FrDtTm, and the to date and time, ToDtTm, and the reporting sequence, RptgSeq, which states the range, FrToSeq, and the sequence number, SeqNb.

The requested message name identification is the field that states which message the customer wants. If the customer wants a statement, it requests camt.053.001.08. If it wants an intraday report, it requests camt.052.001.08. If it wants a debit credit notification, it requests camt.054.001.08. The bank will send the requested message, for the account and the period stated in the request. The customer can request a statement for a specific period, for example from 1 May 2026 to 31 May 2026, or it can request a statement for the current day, or it can request a report from a specific sequence number. The reporting period and the reporting sequence are the controls that define what the bank sends back.

The account in a camt.060 is identified by the account identification, Acct, which carries the IBAN or the account number, and the account owner, AcctOwnr, which names the customer, and the account servicer, AcctSvcr, which names the bank. The customer may request a statement for one account, or it may request statements for several accounts, by sending one camt.060 per account, or by sending one camt.060 with several reporting request blocks. The bank will send a separate camt.053 for each account, because each statement is for one account. A corporate with 20 accounts will send 20 camt.060 requests, or one request with 20 blocks, and will receive 20 camt.053 statements, one per account.

The reporting period is stated as a from date and time and a to date and time. The from date and time is the start of the period, and the to date and time is the end of the period. The bank will include in the statement all entries with a booking date within the period, and it will state the opening balance at the from date and the closing balance at the to date. The customer should request a period that matches its reconciliation cycle, normally daily, weekly, or monthly. A daily statement covers one day, a weekly statement covers one week, and a monthly statement covers one month. The customer can also request a statement for a custom period, for example from 15 May to 20 May, if it needs to investigate a specific entry.

The reporting sequence is an alternative to the reporting period. The customer can request a statement from a specific sequence number, for example from sequence number 100 to sequence number 150, where the sequence number is the electronic sequence number, ElctrncSeqNb, that the bank assigns to each statement. The sequence number is a control that ensures the customer receives every statement in order, and that it does not miss one. If the customer receives sequence number 101 after sequence number 99, it knows that sequence number 100 is missing, and it can request it with a camt.060. The sequence number is therefore a reconciliation control, and it is important for a corporate that receives statements automatically.

The bank response to a camt.060 is the requested message, sent as a separate message. The bank does not send a confirmation that it received the camt.060, it sends the statement or the report. If the bank cannot provide the requested statement, for example because the account does not exist, or because the period is too long, it will send a rejection, normally as an admi.002 message system event acknowledgement, or as a free format message, stating the reason. The customer should check that it receives the requested statement within the expected time, and should re-send the camt.060 if it does not. A statement that is not received is a gap in the reconciliation, and it must be followed up.

For a business analyst, the camt.060 requirement must state the requested message, the account, the period, and the sequence. The requirement should say that the system generates a camt.060 with the requested message name identification, the account, and the reporting period or the reporting sequence, that it sends the request to the bank, that it checks that the requested statement is received, and that it re-sends the request if the statement is not received within the agreed time. A system that cannot request a statement by sequence number cannot detect a missing statement, and a system that cannot request a statement for a custom period cannot investigate a specific entry.
