Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

A treasury team needs to know the balance of its euro account at 11 in the morning, before it decides whether to fund a large payment. The end of day statement will not arrive until tomorrow, and the intraday report may not have been sent yet. The team sends a camt.060, which is the ISO 20022 account reporting request. It is a request, not a report. It asks the account servicing bank for a report on a specific account, for a specific period, in a specific format.

The servicing bank replies with a camt.052 intraday report or a camt.053 end of day statement, depending on what was requested. The camt.060 carries four things. First, the account it refers to, identified by IBAN or by the account number the servicing bank uses. Second, the period it covers, which can be a single point in time, for example the balance at 11:00, or a range, for example all movements between 09:00 and 11:00. Third, the type of report requested, which can be an intraday report, an end of day statement, or a notification of debits and credits.

Fourth, the format and the delivery channel, so the servicing bank knows whether to send the report as a file, through SWIFT, or through an API. Without those four, the servicing bank cannot know what to send. The difference between camt.060 and the other camt messages is the direction. camt.052, camt.053 and camt.054 are reports and notifications that the servicing bank sends to the account holder, usually on a schedule. camt.060 is the account holder asking for a report outside that schedule.

A corporate treasury team uses it when it needs a balance now, not at the next scheduled report. A liquidity manager uses it to check a prefund balance before a large instant payment run. An operations team uses it to get a fresh report after a system outage, so it can rebuild its position from a known point. The camt.060 also matters for reconciliation, because a report requested on demand can be compared with the report received on schedule. If the intraday report at 11:00 shows a balance of EUR 2,450,000 and the end of day statement shows EUR 2,450,000 plus the afternoon movements, the two must agree on the morning position.

If they do not, there is a break, and the operations team must find whether a movement was missed, duplicated, or recorded with the wrong value date. The camt.060 therefore does two jobs: it gives the treasury team the balance it needs now, and it gives the reconciliation team a second source to check against. For a business analyst, the camt.060 is a small message with a clear use case, and the requirements around it are straightforward. The request must identify the account unambiguously, because a request for the wrong account returns the wrong balance.

The period must be precise, because a report for the wrong period is worse than no report. The response must be matched to the request, so the system knows which camt.052 or camt.053 answers which camt.060. And the request must be logged, because a treasury decision made on a balance from a camt.060 must be auditable later. The lesson to carry forward is that camt.060 is the question, camt.052 and camt.053 are the answers, and a bank that cannot ask the question cannot manage its liquidity in real time.
