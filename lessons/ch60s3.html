What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain pain.001 initiates pacs.008,. The journey from pain.001 to pacs.008 begins when the customer system creates the pain.001 file and sends it to the debtor agent, which is the bank that holds the debtor account.

Why it matters. This matters because the message is the payment instruction. A wrong field, a missing element or a misunderstood format is a failed payment, a return, or a compliance breach.

Key terms

Execution date: The date on which the bank executes a payment instruction, debiting the debtor account and sending the message onward.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

pain.001: An ISO 20022 message a customer sends to its bank to instruct one or more credit transfers. It carries payment information, debtor and creditor details, amount, execution date and remittance data.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

The journey from pain.001 to pacs.008 begins when the customer system creates the pain.001 file and sends it to the debtor agent, which is the bank that holds the debtor account. The debtor agent first checks the file structure, the group header, the control sum, and the format of each instruction. If the file passes those checks, the bank sends a pain.002 report with status ACCP for the accepted instructions. If an instruction fails, for example because the creditor IBAN is invalid or the amount exceeds a limit, the bank reports RJCT with a reason code such as AC01 or AM09, and that instruction never becomes a pacs.008 message. Once an instruction is accepted, the debtor agent checks the debtor account for sufficient funds and for any hold, freeze, or sanction flag. A corporate account in Frankfurt holding EUR 900,000 can fund a EUR 750,000 payment, but the same payment would fail if EUR 600,000 of the balance were already reserved for a tax payment due that morning. The bank also checks the requested execution date, because a payment dated for a bank holiday cannot be executed on that day and must be moved to the next business day. Only when funds, date, and compliance checks all pass does the bank debit the debtor account and create the interbank instruction. The debtor agent then creates a pacs.008.001.08 Customer Credit Transfer message, which is the interbank version of the customer instruction. The pacs.008 carries the debtor and creditor details from the pain.001, but it adds interbank fields that the customer never sees, including the debtor agent, the creditor agent, the settlement method, the clearing system reference, and the interbank settlement amount and date. The end to end identification from the pain.001 is copied into the pacs.008 so the payment can be traced across both messages. The instruction identification may change, because the bank assigns its own transaction reference for its internal systems. The pacs.008 is sent through the clearing and settlement route that matches the payment, and the route depends on currency, geography, and urgency. A EUR 25,000 payment from a bank in Paris to a bank in Madrid under SEPA becomes a pacs.008 sent through the SEPA clearing system for same day or next day settlement. A USD 180,000 payment from a bank in London to a bank in New York becomes a pacs.008 sent through the cross border correspondent chain, with a UETR assigned for tracking. A EUR 2,400,000 payment that needs immediate final settlement becomes a pacs.008 sent to TARGET for real time gross settlement on central bank accounts. After the pacs.008 is sent, the debtor agent waits for a pacs.002 Payment Status Report from the next agent in the chain, and it uses that report to update the customer. A positive pacs.002 with status ACCP confirms the next agent accepted the payment, while a negative pacs.002 with status RJCT means the payment was rejected downstream and must be returned or corrected. The debtor agent then sends a further pain.002 to the customer, reporting the final outcome. This chain of reports is what lets a corporate treasurer in Munich know, within hours, that a EUR 96,000 payment to a supplier in Warsaw has settled, rather than learning about a failure days later from the supplier.
