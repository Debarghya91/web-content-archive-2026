The trade begins in Lyon, where a French exporter, Rhone Packaging SA, sells EUR 250,000 of pharmaceutical packaging to a Spanish importer, Iberia Pharma SL in Madrid.

Why it matters. This matters because cross-border payments involve more banks, more charges, more time and more ways to fail than any domestic payment. Getting the chain right is the job.

Chapter objective: Learner explains documentary collections, the roles of remitting and collecting banks, and the SWIFT Category 4 messages used at each stage. Learner uses real examples with amounts, banks and message names to show how the topic works in practice.

Key learning points

  • French exporter EUR 250,000 of pharmaceutical packaging
  • November Rhone Packaging document set
  • Banco Santander
  • Iberia Pharma
  • MT400: formal advice
  • Completed collection: exporter paid, importer supplied, every step evidenced

Key terms

Bill of exchange: A written order by one party instructing another party to pay a stated amount to a named person on a stated date.

Suspense account: A temporary account used to hold funds or entries that cannot yet be posted to their final account. Banks use suspense accounts for unidentified receipts, pending investigations and items awaiting...

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

The trade begins in Lyon, where a French exporter, Rhone Packaging SA, sells EUR 250,000 of pharmaceutical packaging to a Spanish importer, Iberia Pharma SL in Madrid. The contract states documents against payment at sight, charges for the account of the importer, and presentation through Banco Santander in Madrid. Rhone Packaging ships the goods from Marseille on 3 November 2026, and the carrier issues an original bill of lading for 18 pallets. Rhone prepares a commercial invoice for EUR 250,000, a packing list, a certificate of origin and an insurance certificate, and draws a sight draft for EUR 250,000 on Iberia Pharma SL. On 5 November 2026 Rhone Packaging delivers the document set and a signed collection instruction to its bank, BNP Paribas in Lyon. The instruction states D/P, EUR 250,000, drawee Iberia Pharma SL, collecting bank Banco Santander Madrid, and instructions to warehouse the goods and advise BNP Paribas if the importer refuses to pay. BNP Paribas logs the collection under reference BNP COLL FR 5521, checks the instruction for completeness, and sends the original documents by secure courier to Banco Santander. BNP Paribas also sends a SWIFT message alerting Santander that documents for EUR 250,000 are on their way under reference BNP COLL FR 5521. Banco Santander receives the documents on 10 November 2026 and sends an MT410 Acknowledgement to BNP Paribas the same day. The MT410 confirms receipt of the collection, quotes the Santander reference COLL 2026 0847, and states the amount EUR 250,000 and the presentation date. Santander contacts Iberia Pharma SL and presents the collection schedule at its Madrid branch on 11 November 2026. Iberia Pharma examines the bill of lading, the invoice and the certificate of origin, confirms the goods match the order, and agrees to pay in full. The importer has no reason to refuse, because the documents are complete and the shipment is already at the port of Barcelona. Iberia Pharma pays EUR 250,000 to Banco Santander on 12 November 2026 by debit to its euro current account at Santander. Santander checks that the funds are good and available, releases the original bill of lading and the other documents to Iberia Pharma, and the importer uses the bill of lading to clear the 18 pallets from Barcelona port. Santander deducts its collection charges of EUR 350, leaving EUR 249,650 to remit. On 12 November 2026 Santander sends an MT400 Advice of Payment to BNP Paribas, with Field 20 COLL 2026 0847, Field 21 BNP COLL FR 5521, Field 32A 12 November 2026 EUR 250,000, Field 33A EUR 249,650, and Field 72 stating remittance by pacs.008 to BNP Paribas. The MT400 is the formal advice that payment has been made. The money and the credit close the collection over the next two days. Santander sends a pacs.008 for EUR 249,650 to BNP Paribas on 12 November 2026, value 13 November 2026, through its correspondent account. BNP Paribas receives the MT400 on 12 November, matches Field 21 BNP COLL FR 5521 to its open collection file, and on receipt of the pacs.008 on 13 November credits Rhone Packaging SA EUR 249,650 to its euro current account in Lyon. BNP Paribas sends a credit advice to Rhone Packaging, closes collection file BNP COLL FR 5521, and files the MT410, the MT400 and the pacs.008 together as the audit trail. The exporter has been paid, the importer has its goods, and every step is evidenced by a document or a message.

This worked example traces a EUR 250,000 documentary collection from a French exporter to a Spanish importer, from the sale contract to the final credit, with the exact amounts, dates, parties, and messages at each step. The exporter is Loire Vins SARL, a wine exporter in Bordeaux, France. The importer is Madrid Distribuciones SA, a distributor in Madrid, Spain. The sale is 2,000 cases of Bordeaux wine, invoice value EUR 250,000, payment terms documents against payment, D/P, at sight. The exporter bank, the remitting bank, is Banque de Bordeaux, France. The importer bank, the collecting bank, is Banco de Madrid, Spain.

Step 1, 2 May 2026. Loire Vins and Madrid Distribuciones agree the sale. The contract states the price EUR 250,000, the payment terms D/P at sight, the documents required, commercial invoice, bill of lading, certificate of origin, insurance certificate, and packing list, and the banks, Banque de Bordeaux as remitting bank and Banco de Madrid as collecting bank. The contract states that the collection is subject to URC 522. Step 2, 5 May 2026. Loire Vins ships the wine from Bordeaux to Madrid, and obtains the bill of lading from the carrier, the commercial invoice for EUR 250,000, the certificate of origin, the insurance certificate, and the packing list. It draws a sight bill of exchange on Madrid Distribuciones for EUR 250,000, payable at sight.

Step 3, 6 May 2026. Loire Vins gives the documents, the bill of exchange, and the collection instruction to Banque de Bordeaux. The instruction states the documents enclosed, the amount EUR 250,000, the terms D/P at sight, the charges to be borne by the drawee, and that the collecting bank should protest the bill if the drawee refuses to pay. Banque de Bordeaux checks that the instruction is complete and that the documents listed are present. Step 4, 7 May 2026. Banque de Bordeaux sends the documents to Banco de Madrid by courier, and sends a covering SWIFT message. Banco de Madrid receives the documents, checks them against the instruction, registers the collection as COLL 2026 0512 001, and sends an MT410 acknowledgement to Banque de Bordeaux, confirming receipt.

Step 5, 9 May 2026. Banco de Madrid presents the documents to Madrid Distribuciones, and notifies it that documents have arrived and that payment of EUR 250,000 is required for their release. Madrid Distribuciones examines the documents, confirms that they match the wine it ordered, and agrees to pay. Step 6, 12 May 2026. Madrid Distribuciones pays EUR 250,000 to Banco de Madrid. Banco de Madrid releases the documents to Madrid Distribuciones, which takes the bill of lading to the carrier and takes delivery of the wine. Banco de Madrid deducts its charges of EUR 150, leaving EUR 249,850.

Step 7, 12 May 2026. Banco de Madrid sends an MT400 advice of payment to Banque de Bordeaux. Field 20 carries COLL 2026 0512 001. Field 21 carries REM 2026 0501 014, the Banque de Bordeaux reference. Field 32A carries 120526EUR249850,00, the value date 12 May 2026 and the amount EUR 249,850. Field 71B states OUR CHARGES EUR 150,00. Field 72 states PROCEEDS OF COLLECTION FOR INVOICE INV 2026 0441. Banco de Madrid remits EUR 249,850 to Banque de Bordeaux through its correspondent account. Step 8, 13 May 2026. Banque de Bordeaux receives the MT400 and the proceeds, checks the amount against the collection instruction, deducts its own charges of EUR 100, and credits Loire Vins with EUR 249,750. The collection is closed.

The accounting entries are as follows. At Banco de Madrid, on receipt of the payment from the drawee, the bank debits the drawee account EUR 250,000 and credits a collection suspense account EUR 250,000. On remittance, it debits the collection suspense account EUR 250,000, credits its charges income EUR 150, and credits the nostro account EUR 249,850. At Banque de Bordeaux, on receipt of the proceeds, it debits its nostro account EUR 249,850, credits its charges income EUR 100, and credits the Loire Vins account EUR 249,750. The exporter receives EUR 249,750 for a EUR 250,000 invoice, and the difference of EUR 250 is the total bank charges, EUR 150 at the collecting bank and EUR 100 at the remitting bank. The exporter should have agreed the charges in advance, and should have priced the sale to cover them.

The edge cases in this example are instructive. If Madrid Distribuciones had refused to pay, Banco de Madrid would have sent an MT416 advice of non-payment to Banque de Bordeaux, and would have held the documents. Banque de Bordeaux would have advised Loire Vins, which would have decided whether to find another buyer in Spain, to ship the wine back to France, or to take legal action. If Madrid Distribuciones had paid only EUR 200,000, Banco de Madrid would have sent an MT400 for EUR 200,000, stated in field 72 that it was a partial payment, and held the documents for the balance. If the documents had been wrong, for example if the bill of lading stated a different quantity from the invoice, Madrid Distribuciones might have refused the documents, and the collection would have stopped until the documents were corrected.

For a business analyst, this example shows the whole collection in one narrative, with the parties, the documents, the messages, the amounts, and the accounting. The analyst should be able to trace the EUR 250,000 from the importer to the collecting bank, to the remitting bank, to the exporter, naming the MT410 and the MT400, the charges at each bank, and the value dates. The analyst should also be able to explain what happens if the drawee refuses, if the payment is partial, or if the documents are wrong. A professional who can walk through this example, with the field numbers and the amounts, can design a trade finance system that handles a documentary collection correctly.
