Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Liquidity: Cash or assets that a bank can use immediately to meet payment obligations. Banks manage liquidity by forecasting inflows and outflows, funding nostro and settlement accounts, and using central bank...

Queue: A holding area in a payment system where payments wait for sufficient funds or for their turn to settle.

A payment system can have perfect technology and still fail if every participant submits all its payments at the end of the day. End of day bunching creates gridlock, liquidity spikes, and operational risk: the system is idle in the morning and overwhelmed in the afternoon, liquidity that could have recycled through the day sits unused until the last hour, and a failure in the last hour has no time left to recover. Throughput requirements fix the behaviour: they oblige participants to submit payments steadily through the operating day, spreading the load and the liquidity.

Throughput requirements take the form of targets through the day. A system may require that a defined percentage of daily value, or volume, is submitted by midday, and a higher percentage by mid-afternoon, with the remainder by the close. The targets are set by the system operator, in consultation with participants, and reflect the system liquidity model: the earlier payments are submitted, the more liquidity recycles, because incoming payments fund outgoing ones. The targets are guidelines in some systems and enforced rules in others, with monitoring, reporting, and challenge for persistent breach.

Measurement is per participant, through the day. The system tracks each participant submission profile: the value and volume submitted in each hour, against the throughput targets. The profile is reported to the participant and to the operator, and persistent bunching is visible: a participant that submits most of its value in the last hour, day after day, stands out. The monitoring is not punitive by default: the operator challenges, discusses, and agrees improvement, because bunching often has an operational cause, a batch process, a late data feed, or a downstream dependency, that can be fixed.

Throughput interacts with liquidity in both directions. Early submission recycles liquidity: a payment received early funds a payment sent later, so the same funds settle several payments through the day. Late submission hoards liquidity: funds sit idle in the morning, and the afternoon needs more funding than the system holds. A participant that submits early helps the whole system, including itself, because the liquidity it releases returns as incoming payments. A participant that submits late free rides on the early submitters liquidity, and the throughput requirements exist to prevent the free riding.

Throughput also interacts with risk. A system that processes steadily through the day has time to recover from a failure: an outage at noon leaves hours to catch up. A system that bunches at the end of the day has no recovery time: an outage in the last hour becomes a settlement failure. Operational risk management therefore favours throughput: spreading the load spreads the risk. The requirement is a behavioural control, not a technical one, and it works because the participants behaviour, not the system capacity, is usually the constraint.

For a business analyst, throughput belongs in the requirements alongside the technical targets. Submission profiles must be specified: the expected pattern through the day, by value and volume. Monitoring dashboards must show the profile against the targets, per participant, in real time. Alerts must fire when a participant falls behind its profile, while there is still time to act. And the link to queue and limit design must be explicit: throughput, queues, and limits are one liquidity system, and a requirement that covers one without the others will miss the behaviour that actually determines whether payments settle.
