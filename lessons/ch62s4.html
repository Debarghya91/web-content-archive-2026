Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Creditor: The party that is owed money and receives a payment, such as a supplier, a seller or a service provider.

  1. What to do. List the main blocks of the message, name the field that carries the amount, and name the field that identifies the payment.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

Show transcript

The collection begins when Stadtwerke Linden creates a pain.008.001.02 Customer Direct Debit Initiation file and sends it to its bank, Harbour Bank Frankfurt. The file carries one collection instruction for EUR 89.99, naming Stadtwerke Linden as creditor, Jonas Weber as debtor, the debtor IBAN, the mandate reference SWL-JW-2026-0417, the creditor identifier DE98ZZZ00001234567, the due date of Monday 2 November 2026, and the remittance information naming the October electricity bill. The group header carries the message identification, the creation date and time, one transaction, and a control sum of EUR 89.99.

The file must reach Harbour Bank Frankfurt before the SDD cut-off for the due date. Before the collection, the creditor must pre-notify the debtor, and that notice is a scheme rule, not a courtesy. Stadtwerke Linden sends Jonas Weber a pre-notification at least fourteen calendar days before the due date, unless a shorter period was agreed in the mandate, telling him that EUR 89.99 will be collected on Monday 2 November 2026. The pre-notification gives the debtor time to check the amount, to make sure funds are available, and to raise a query if the amount is wrong.

A creditor that collects without pre-notification gives the debtor grounds to claim a refund, and a business analyst should make the pre-notification step visible in every collection design. Harbour Bank Frankfurt validates the pain.008 file, checks the creditor identifier and the mandate data, and creates a pacs.003.001.02 FI to FI Customer Direct Debit message for the interbank leg. The pacs.003 carries the creditor and debtor details, the mandate reference, the amount of EUR 89.99, the due date, and the interbank settlement date.

It travels through the clearing system to Meridian Bank Munich, the debtor bank. The pacs.003 is the interbank version of the collection, just as the pacs.008 is the interbank version of a credit transfer, and it is the message the debtor bank acts on when the due date arrives. On the due date, Meridian Bank Munich checks the collection against the mandate and the account. It confirms that mandate SWL-JW-2026-0417 is valid, that it has not been cancelled, and that the amount of EUR 89.99 matches the mandate terms.

It then checks that the Jonas Weber account holds at least EUR 89.99, and it debits the account on Monday 2 November 2026. The bank sends a pacs.002 status report back through clearing, confirming that the collection was accepted, and it credits the funds through settlement to Harbour Bank Frankfurt, which credits the Stadtwerke Linden account. Both banks record the debit and the credit with the mandate reference and the end to end identification.
