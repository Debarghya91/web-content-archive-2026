What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain channels and the gateway. Channels are the places where a payment starts.

Why it matters. This matters because the architecture decides what the bank can build, how fast it can change, and where a payment can fail. Most payment requirements live in the architecture.

Chapter objective: Learner designs a payment hub architecture, explaining intake, validation, routing, transformation, screening, and settlement modules, and maps a payment through each module. Learner applies this skill in realistic banking scenarios, with clear examples and correct terminology throughout.

Key learning points

  • Channels and gateway (master lesson ch48s1)
  • Channels: places where payment starts
  • Read ch48s1 for the full treatment

Key terms

Payment hub: A central system in a bank that receives payment instructions from all channels, validates them, applies screening and routing rules, and sends them to the correct clearing system or correspondent.

Gateway: A service that connects a merchant website or app to payment networks and acquirers. It encrypts card data, routes authorisation requests, and returns the result to the merchant.

Practise this lesson

Exercise

Read ch48s1 Hub components: what a hub is and what it contains. Then trace a EUR 100,000 supplier payment from the online banking channel to the rail.

ch48s1 covers channels, the gateway, the hub core, and routing. The gateway validates and enriches. The hub core routes by currency, amount and urgency.

  1. What to do. Name each component the payment passes through and what it does, and name the rail for a EUR 100,000 euro payment to Germany.

Correct result. Online banking captures the instruction. The gateway validates the format and enriches the beneficiary details. The hub core checks limits and sanctions and routes by currency and amount. A EUR 100,000 euro payment to Germany goes through T2 as a SEPA Credit Transfer.

Why. The gateway is the door, the hub is the building. A gateway validates and converts, but it never routes, screens or settles.
