Key terms

Irrevocable: A payment or instruction that cannot be cancelled or reversed once it has been made, except by agreement of all parties or by a court order.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Refund: A repayment of funds to a customer after a payment fails, is cancelled or is disputed. In card payments a refund returns money to the cardholder account.

Correct result. The per-transaction limit is typically INR 5,000, so sending INR 10,000 needs two transactions of INR 5,000 each. Show transcript

DMT, Domestic Money Transfer, is India service that lets a person send money to any bank account in India using only the recipient mobile number or account number, without needing the recipient bank details or a bank account of their own. It was built to serve migrant workers who earn money in a city and need to send it to their families in their home village, and who may not have a bank account or may not be comfortable using a banking app. The sender goes to a retail agent, gives cash, and the agent transfers the money to the recipient bank account through the Immediate Payment Service, known as IMPS. DMT is a large and growing business, processing billions of rupees every month.

Why DMT exists is that a migrant worker who earns INR 15,000 a month in Mumbai and needs to send INR 10,000 to their family in Bihar faces a practical problem: they may not have a bank account, they may not know the recipient IFSC code, and they may not be able to use a banking app. DMT solves this by letting the sender walk into a local shop, hand over cash, and have the money credited to the recipient account within minutes. The sender does not need a bank account, does not need a smartphone, and does not need to know anything about banking. The agent handles the technology, and the sender pays a small fee for the service.

The parties are the sender, the retail agent, the DMT service provider, the NPCI IMPS switch, and the recipient bank. The sender gives cash to the agent and provides the recipient mobile number or account number. The agent enters the details into the DMT application, which is provided by the service provider. The service provider validates the recipient, either by looking up the mobile number in its database or by using the account number and IFSC, and then sends the transfer through IMPS. The NPCI switch routes the transfer to the recipient bank, which credits the account immediately. The agent receives a commission from the service provider, and the sender pays a fee, typically 1 percent of the amount.

In practice the flow works like this. A sender wants to send INR 5,000 to their mother. They go to a retail agent, give INR 5,000 in cash plus a fee of INR 50, and provide their mother mobile number. The agent enters the number into the DMT application, which shows the mother name and her bank account, because she is already registered in the system. The agent confirms the details with the sender, and the application sends an IMPS transfer of INR 5,000 to the mother account. The mother receives an SMS confirming the credit within seconds. The agent prints a receipt, and the sender keeps it as proof. The whole process takes under two minutes, and the sender does not need to know the IFSC code or the bank name.

A worked example shows the limits. The Reserve Bank of India sets a monthly limit for DMT transactions, typically INR 25,000 per sender per month for a sender who is not fully KYC verified, and INR 100,000 or more for a fully verified sender. The per-transaction limit is typically INR 5,000, although some providers allow higher amounts. A sender who wants to send INR 10,000 in one month would need to make two transactions of INR 5,000 each, paying the fee twice. The fee is typically 1 percent, so sending INR 5,000 costs INR 50, and sending INR 10,000 in two transactions costs INR 100. The agent commission is typically 0.5 percent, so the agent earns INR 25 on a INR 5,000 transaction.

What happens when DMT fails is usually a wrong number or a limit breach. If the sender provides the wrong mobile number, the money may go to the wrong person, and recovering it is difficult, because the transfer is instant and irrevocable. The agent must therefore confirm the recipient name with the sender before sending, and the application must display the name clearly. If the sender has reached their monthly limit, the transaction is declined and the sender must wait until the next month or complete full KYC to raise the limit. If the IMPS transfer fails for a technical reason, the money is returned to the service provider, and the agent must refund the sender. The service provider must have a complaint handling process, because a failed DMT transaction is a customer who has lost money.

The operational and accounting implications are that the service provider must manage its agent network, monitor the transaction volumes, and reconcile the IMPS settlement daily. On the ledger, the cash received from the sender is a liability to the recipient until the transfer is completed, and the IMPS settlement is a debit to the service provider settlement account. The finance team must track the fees earned, the commissions paid, and the float, which is the money held between receiving the cash and completing the transfer. The risk team must monitor for money laundering, because DMT is a cash-based service and is vulnerable to being used to move illicit funds, and the provider must file suspicious transaction reports as required by the Prevention of Money Laundering Act.

For a business analyst, DMT is an agent network and compliance subject. You must specify the sender onboarding, the recipient validation, the limits, the fee structure, the agent commission, the IMPS integration, and the complaint handling. You must also specify the anti-money laundering controls: the sender KYC, the transaction monitoring, the velocity checks, and the reporting. A common gap is failing to specify the refund procedure when a transfer fails, because the sender has given cash and expects it back immediately, and a delay will lose the customer.

Takeaway: DMT turns a neighbourhood shop into a money transfer service, and the agent is the bank for people the banking system does not reach. Further reading: the NPCI Immediate Payment Service (IMPS) procedural guidelines; the Reserve Bank of India master directions on money transfer; the Prevention of Money Laundering Act for the compliance obligations.
