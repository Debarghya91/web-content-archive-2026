Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

pacs.002: An ISO 20022 message one financial institution sends to another to report the status of a payment instruction.

  1. What to do. List the three levels of the message and name the field that carries the amount and the field that carries the end-to-end reference.

Correct result. The amount is carried in the instructed amount element and the reference in the end-to-end identification. The three levels are the group header, the payment information, and the credit transfer transaction information.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The group header identifies the message, the payment information identifies the batch, and the transaction information carries the single payment.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

When a bank receives a payment instruction, the first thing it checks is whether the message itself is valid: is it well formed, properly authenticated, and compliant with the schema and the usage guideline? The status that reports a pass on those checks is ACTC, AcceptedTechnicalValidation. ACTC travels in the pacs.002 status report between banks, and in the pain.002 status report back to the customer. Some training material misspells the code as ACPT, but the ISO 20022 code is ACTC, and the distinction matters when searching documentation or mapping statuses.

Technical validation checks the message, not the business. Authentication: the message genuinely comes from the claimed sender, and its signature or authentication is valid. Syntax: the message conforms to the XML schema, with the right elements in the right places. Semantics: the field formats are valid, the code values come from the allowed lists, and the cross field rules of the usage guideline hold. ACTC means all of those checks passed. It does not mean the payment will settle, the funds are available, or the beneficiary exists. It means the message is valid. Confusing a valid message with a successful payment is the most common status misreading in ISO 20022.

ACTC is one rung of a status ladder, and the ladder is cumulative: each rung guarantees everything the rungs below it guarantee, plus more. RCVD, received, acknowledges receipt. ACTC, accepted technical validation, adds message validity. ACCP, accepted customer profile, adds that the customer profile checks passed and the payment is accepted for processing. ACSP, accepted settlement in process, adds that settlement has been ordered. ACSC, accepted settlement completed, adds that settlement has completed: only ACSC means the money arrived. RJCT, rejected, with a reason code, can arrive at any stage. PDNG, pending, means the payment is waiting. The ladder is a shared vocabulary, and each code is a specific guarantee.

Where ACTC travels depends on the flow and the agreements. Between banks, the pacs.002 carries it, and positive status reporting, including ACTC, is bilaterally agreed between the banks: not every bank sends every positive status to every counterparty. Rejection reporting, by contrast, is mandatory under CBPR+ rules: a rejected payment must be reported. Back to the customer, the pain.002 carries the status, and the customer bank decides which statuses to expose. CBPR+ restricts the pacs.002 to a single transaction, so each status report covers one payment, keeping the reporting precise.

ACTC and ACCP are the pair most often confused. ACTC is about the message: it is well formed and valid. ACCP is about the customer and the agreement: the payment passed the customer profile checks, the customer is enabled for the service, and the payment is accepted for processing. A message can be ACTC and still fail ACCP: a perfectly valid instruction from a customer who is not enabled for that payment type, or whose agreement does not cover that currency, is technically valid and commercially unacceptable. The two codes answer different questions, and a status design that merges them loses information the sender needs.

For a business analyst, status mapping is precision work. Each internal system status must map to the ISO 20022 code that means the same thing, not to the nearest convenient code. Customer communication must not say accepted when only ACTC has been reached: the customer hears accepted and assumes the money is on its way, and the complaint arrives when it is not. Status reporting design must specify which statuses are sent, when, to whom, and under what agreements. The ladder is a shared vocabulary across the whole payment chain, and misusing it misleads counterparties, customers, and operations teams alike.
