What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain reporting messages matter. A reporting message tells an account holder what has happened to its account, after the payments have been made.

Why it matters. If the two records differ by even one cent, or by one day, the item becomes a break that an analyst must investigate.

Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

Liquidity: Cash or assets that a bank can use immediately to meet payment obligations. Banks manage liquidity by forecasting inflows and outflows, funding nostro and settlement accounts, and using central bank...

Exercise

An operations analyst needs the overnight statement for a nostro account to reconcile yesterday cross-border payments. A corporate treasurer wants the same view for its own account. Explain which message each one should look for, and why the MT103 and pacs.008 are the wrong messages.

Use the reporting message roles in this lesson: statement, confirmation of credit, and payment instruction are different jobs.

  1. What to do. Name the message for each need, state who sends it to whom, and explain why a payment message cannot do the job of a statement.

Correct result. The overnight nostro statement is the MT940, sent by the account servicing bank to its customer for a defined period, usually one business day. The MT950 is the bank to bank statement used for nostro reconciliation. The MT910 only confirms a single credit, and the MT103 and pacs.008 are payment instructions, not statements, so they cannot show the full account activity.

Why. Reconciliation starts from the statement, so picking the right message matters. A payment instruction shows one payment, while a statement proves the whole account.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

A reporting message tells an account holder what has happened to its account, after the payments have been made. A corporate treasurer at a manufacturing company in Munich does not watch every MT103 as it leaves the bank. The treasurer watches the statement the next morning, checks that the EUR 320,000 supplier payment left the account, that the EUR 150,000 customer receipt arrived, and that the balance is enough to fund the payroll run. Without a reliable statement, the treasurer cannot know the true cash position, cannot forecast liquidity, and cannot release the next batch of payments with confidence.

Reporting messages matter because they feed reconciliation, the daily discipline of proving that internal records match bank records. A company records a payment of USD 85,000 to a supplier in its own accounting system, and the bank statement must show the same amount, the same value date and the same reference. If the two records differ by even one cent, or by one day, the item becomes a break that an analyst must investigate. A large corporate may reconcile 20,000 statement lines a day across 40 accounts in 12 currencies, and it can only do that automatically if the statements arrive in a standard structured format.

Reporting messages also matter for audit, control and regulatory reporting. Auditors ask for bank statements to confirm cash balances at year end, to trace a specific payment, and to test that controls operated during the year. A missing statement for 31 December creates an audit gap that must be explained. Regulators require banks to report liquidity positions and large exposures, and those reports are built from the same account data that statements carry. A statement is therefore not just a customer service document. It is evidence, and it must be complete, accurate and retained.

The timing of reporting messages shapes how treasury teams work. An intraday statement may arrive every two hours during the business day, showing credits and debits as they post, so a treasurer can react to a shortfall before the cut off time. An end of day statement arrives overnight and gives the final position for the day, which is the basis for the next morning cash forecast. A weekly statement may be enough for a small account with few transactions. The choice of frequency is a business decision, agreed between the bank and the customer, and it is recorded in the statement delivery arrangement for the account.

A final reason reporting messages matter is that they close the loop on exceptions. When a payment is returned, when a fee is charged, or when a value date is corrected, the statement is usually the first place the customer sees it. A EUR 45,000 payment returned by the beneficiary bank because of a wrong account number will appear as a credit on the statement with a return reference. A treasurer who reads the statement promptly can reissue the payment the same day. A treasurer who ignores the statement may discover the failure a week later, after the supplier has chased the overdue invoice. Reading statements is a control, not a clerical task.
