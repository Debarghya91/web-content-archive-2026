Field 52a identifies the ordering institution, the financial institution that ordered the payment on behalf of the ordering customer, when that institution is different from the sender of the MT103. For example, a regional bank in Lyon called Banque Rhone Alpes may hold no account with Banco Santander in Madrid, so it asks BNP Paribas in Paris to send the MT103 on its behalf. BNP Paribas is the sender, but Banque Rhone Alpes is the ordering institution, and field 52a must name it so the receiver and the beneficiary bank know where the instruction truly began. Field 52a has three common formats. Option A, written as 52A, carries the BIC of the ordering institution, such as BRALFRPP for the Lyon bank. Option D, written as 52D, carries the full name and address when no BIC exists or when the BIC is not enough to identify the institution. Option F is rarely used in MT103 practice. The choice depends on whether the ordering institution has a BIC and whether the receiver needs a postal address for screening. When a BIC is available, 52A is preferred because it is precise, machine readable and easy to screen. Field 52a appears whenever the sender is acting for another bank, and it must not appear when the sender itself took the instruction from the customer. If BNP Paribas receives an instruction directly from its own customer Atelier Lumiere SARL and sends the MT103 itself, BNP Paribas is both the ordering institution and the sender, so field 52a is omitted. Adding it in that case would be wrong, because it would suggest a second bank was involved. Operations teams check this rule first when they validate a payment, because a wrong 52a can misdirect a compliance query. A concrete example makes the rule clear. A customer of Credit Agricole in Marseille instructs a payment of 85,000 euros to a supplier in Milan. Credit Agricole has no direct account with the Milan bank, so it sends the instruction to its correspondent Deutsche Bank in Frankfurt, which sends the MT103 to Milan. Deutsche Bank is the sender, Credit Agricole is the ordering institution in field 52A, and the Milan bank screens both names. If Deutsche Bank omitted field 52a, the Milan bank would think Deutsche Bank itself had ordered the payment, and its sanctions screening would miss the true originator. Field 52a also protects the ordering institution in the chain. When a later bank in the serial chain forwards the payment, it keeps field 52a unchanged so the origin remains visible to every bank that handles the funds. That continuity matters for anti money laundering checks, for investigations, and for returns, because a return must go back to the institution that ordered the payment, not merely to the last sender. A payment that loses its 52a in the middle of a chain becomes harder to trace, harder to return, and harder to explain to a regulator who asks where the money came from.

Worked example with real numbers: Banque Rhone Alpes in Lyon instructs a EUR 120,000 payment to a supplier in Milan, but it holds no account with the Milan bank, so it asks BNP Paribas in Paris to send the MT103. BNP Paribas is the sender, Banque Rhone Alpes is the ordering institution in field 52A with BIC BRALFRPP, and the Milan bank screens both names before crediting the supplier. BNP Paribas deducts its EUR 15 handling charge from its own account, so the supplier receives EUR 120,000 in full.

If BNP Paribas had omitted field 52a, the Milan bank would have screened only BNP Paribas and would have missed the true originator entirely. That miss matters because the sanctions check is the control that stops a payment to a blocked party, and a check run against the wrong name is no check at all. The ordering institution must therefore be visible to every bank in the chain, not just the sender.

Failure and recovery: field 52a fails in two ways, and both are expensive. If the sender omits it when acting for another bank, the receiver screens the wrong institution, and a sanctions hit on the true ordering institution is missed until a later investigation finds it, by which time the funds have been credited and must be frozen or recalled. If the sender enters the wrong BIC, a return or a compliance query goes to the wrong bank, and the payment sits in a repair queue while operations teams exchange MT199 messages to find the true ordering institution.

For a business analyst, product owner or subject matter expert, the field 52a requirement must state the rule in one sentence: field 52a is mandatory when the sender acts for another bank, and forbidden when the sender took the instruction from its own customer. The requirement must name the three options, 52A with a BIC, 52D with a name and address, and the rare 52F, and must state that 52A is preferred when a BIC exists. In ISO 20022 the ordering institution maps to the debtor agent and ultimate debtor fields of the pacs.008. Source: SWIFT MT103 format specifications; last reviewed 7 November 2026.
