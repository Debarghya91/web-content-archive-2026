What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain message routing: from channel to rail. Routing begins with the information in the payment instruction itself.

Why it matters. This matters because the architecture decides what the bank can build, how fast it can change, and where a payment can fail. Most payment requirements live in the architecture.

Chapter objective: Learner applies system design to payments, covering throughput, latency, idempotency, exactly-once processing, and failure recovery, and designs a system for a stated load. Learner practises this skill on realistic banking cases, explaining the reasoning clearly and using correct terminology throughout the work.

Key terms

Payment hub: A central system in a bank that receives payment instructions from all channels, validates them, applies screening and routing rules, and sends them to the correct clearing system or correspondent.

Liquidity: Cash or assets that a bank can use immediately to meet payment obligations. Banks manage liquidity by forecasting inflows and outflows, funding nostro and settlement accounts, and using central bank...

Clearing: The process of exchanging payment instructions between banks and calculating what each bank owes the others.

Practise this lesson

Exercise

Read Book chapter 82, Lesson 1 Hub components: what a hub is and what it contains. Then explain the routing decision for a sterling payment of 500,000 pounds to a UK supplier.

Book chapter 82, Lesson 1 covers the routing decision: currency, amount, urgency and value date decide the rail. Faster Payments is for smaller sterling payments, CHAPS for high value same day sterling.

  1. What to do. Name the rail, say why, and state what changes if the payment were 500 pounds instead.

Correct result. A 500,000 pound sterling payment to a UK supplier goes through CHAPS, because it is high value and same day. If the amount were 500 pounds, it would go through Faster Payments, because it is below the CHAPS threshold and Faster Payments settles in seconds.

Why. Routing is not a preference, it is a rule. Amount, currency, urgency and cut-off decide the rail, and the same payment type can take a different rail when any one of them changes.
