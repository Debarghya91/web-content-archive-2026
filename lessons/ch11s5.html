Key terms

Sanctions screening: Checking payment parties, countries and references against sanctions lists published by governments and international bodies.

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Exercise

Scenario: you are an operations analyst. A customer sent a EUR 4,200 SEPA Instant payment on Sunday at 23:40. The payment was expected to reach the beneficiary in 10 seconds. It is now Monday at 09:00 and the beneficiary has not received it. The payment status is RJCT. Investigate: determine why the instant payment failed, identify the reason code, and state the action to take and what to tell the customer.

The payment: EUR 4,200, SEPA Instant, sent Sunday 23:40 from a bank in Germany to a bank in France. The debtor bank sent a pacs.008 to TIPS. TIPS returned a pacs.002 with status RJCT and reason code AC04 (closed account number). The beneficiary account was closed on Friday. The customer was not notified.

The SCT Inst maximum execution time is 10 seconds under Regulation (EU) 2024/886. The payment was debited from the customer account on Sunday at 23:40.

  1. What to do. Determine why the payment failed, identify the reason code and what it means, state whether the customer account should be credited back and by when, and write the message to the customer.

Correct result. Why it failed: the beneficiary account was closed on Friday, so when the pacs.008 reached the beneficiary bank via TIPS, the bank rejected it with reason code AC04 (closed account number). The pacs.002 RJCT confirms the rejection. The payment was debited from the customer account on Sunday, but because the payment was rejected, the funds must be returned to the customer. Action: the debtor bank must credit the customer account with EUR 4,200 immediately, because an SCT Inst payment that is rejected must be returned without delay.

The bank should also notify the customer that the payment failed because the beneficiary account is closed, and ask the customer to confirm the correct beneficiary account details before resending. Message to the customer: your instant payment of EUR 4,200 sent on Sunday at 23:40 could not be delivered because the beneficiary account is closed. The funds have been credited back to your account. Please check the beneficiary account details and resend the payment with the correct account number. Worked reasoning: SCT Inst is designed to settle in 10 seconds, so a failure is fast and the rejection is immediate.

The reason code AC04 tells you exactly why it failed, so there is no need to investigate further. The key operational point is that a rejected instant payment must be returned to the customer immediately, not held, because the customer expects instant settlement.

Why. Hint 1: the pacs.002 RJCT and the reason code tell you exactly why the payment failed; start there. Hint 2: AC04 means the account is closed, so the payment cannot be delivered to that account. Hint 3: a rejected instant payment must be returned to the customer immediately. The worked explanation shows the instant payment investigation discipline: check the pacs.002 first, read the reason code, act on it, and tell the customer what happened and what to do next.

An instant payment settles in seconds, not days. An SCT Inst payment is credited to the payee account within ten seconds, twenty four hours a day, seven days a week, including weekends and holidays. Once the payee bank has credited the money, the payment is final and cannot be undone by the payer bank alone. Speed is the whole point of the scheme, and every part of the flow has to be built for a ten second budget. The payer starts with a pain.001, as in SCT.

The instruction reaches the payer bank as a pain.001, exactly like a standard credit transfer. The difference is what happens next. Fraud screening, sanctions screening, the funds check, and the verification of payee must all complete in real time, in milliseconds, because there is no batch window to hide behind. A bank that screens in minutes cannot offer instant payments. Verification of payee checks the name against the IBAN.

Before the money moves, the payer bank checks whether the name the payer typed matches the name on the beneficiary account. The answer is a match, a close match, or no match. A no match warns the payer that the IBAN may belong to someone else, which is the classic pattern in authorised push payment fraud. Under the Instant Payments Regulation this check is mandatory. The payer bank sends a pacs.008 through an instant clearing system.

The interbank leg is a pacs.008, sent through an instant clearing system such as RT1 or settled in central bank money through TIPS, the TARGET instant payment settlement service. The message must reach the payee bank within seconds. There is no overnight batch and no next clearing cycle. The payee bank answers with a pacs.002 in seconds. The payee bank must reply with a pacs.002 status report, accepted with status ACCP or rejected with status RJCT, and on acceptance it must credit the payee account at once.

The payer bank passes the result to the payer inside the same ten second window, so the payer sees a clear success or a clear failure. A payment that is accepted but not yet credited is a defect, not a delay. A timeout is a rejection, not a mystery. If the payee bank does not answer within the deadline, the clearing system rejects the payment and sends a pacs.002 with a timeout reason. The payer bank does not debit the payer, or it reverses any reservation it made, and the payer is told clearly that the payment failed.

Nothing is left pending. A payment stuck in an unknown state is worse than one that failed cleanly. Rejects use the same reason codes as SCT. The payee bank rejects with a pacs.002 and a familiar reason code, such as AC01 for an incorrect account number, AC04 for a closed account, AG01 for a forbidden transaction, AM04 for insufficient funds, or AM05 for a duplicate. If the payee bank does not support SCT Inst, the payer bank must fall back to a standard SCT payment and say the money will take up to one business day.

Recalls work the same way, but speed changes the risk. A payment sent in error can be recalled with a camt.056 cancellation request, and the payee bank answers with a camt.029. The payee must consent before the money comes back, and by the time a recall is raised the money has usually already been spent, because ten seconds leaves no time to stop it. Prevention matters more than cure, which is why verification of payee and real time fraud screening sit before the payment. The regulation made instant the default.

The Instant Payments Regulation requires banks in the euro area to receive instant payments from January 2025 and to send them from October 2025. An instant payment must cost the customer no more than a standard credit transfer, and verification of payee is mandatory. Sanctions screening moved from a check on every payment to a daily check against the sanctions lists, because a per payment check cannot fit inside ten seconds. The analyst checklist for instant payments. Four checks decide whether an instant product works.

The ten second budget must hold across fraud, sanctions, and liquidity. Both ends must be ready, or the payment falls back to SCT. Real time status screens must show accepted, rejected, or timed out. And reconciliation must match every pacs.008 against its pacs.002 within seconds.
