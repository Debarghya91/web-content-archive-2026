Key terms

Correspondent banking: A service where one bank holds accounts for another bank so the second bank can make payments in currencies or countries where it has no branch.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Exercise

Work out what the beneficiary receives for a $10,000 payment under OUR, SHA and BEN.

Use the charge arrangement rules in this lesson and the correspondent and beneficiary bank fees named in the lesson.

  1. What to do. Calculate the amount credited under each of the three arrangements and state who pays each fee.

Correct result. Under OUR the sender pays all charges and the beneficiary receives the full amount. Under SHA the sender pays the sending charges and the beneficiary pays the receiving charges. Under BEN the beneficiary pays all charges and receives the amount less all fees.

Why. The charge arrangement decides who pays and how much arrives. A customer who was promised the full amount and receives less will complain, and the complaint lands on the analyst.

SWIFT gpi, which stands for Global Payments Innovation, is a service launched by SWIFT in 2017 that transformed cross border payments by making them faster, more transparent and trackable. Before gpi, a cross border payment was a black box. The sender knew the payment had been sent, the beneficiary knew it had arrived, and nobody in between knew where it was or how much had been deducted. gpi changed that by requiring participating banks to follow a set of rules, to process the payment on the same day, to provide a status update, to confirm the credit to the beneficiary, and to make the payment trackable through a central tracker.

Today most cross border payments travel on gpi, and the tracker is the standard tool for answering the question that every customer asks, where is my money. gpi works through a set of business rules and a shared tracker, not through a new payment system. The payment itself still travels through correspondent banking and through the existing SWIFT messages, but the banks that handle it must follow the gpi rules. They must send the payment promptly, they must not deduct charges without disclosing them, they must confirm when the beneficiary has been credited, and they must update the tracker at each step.

The tracker is a central service operated by SWIFT that collects the status updates from each bank and presents them in a single view, so that the sending bank, and the customer if the bank offers the service, can see the payment moving from bank to bank in real time. This is a major improvement over the old world, where tracing a payment meant sending a SWIFT query and waiting days for a reply. The impact of gpi on the customer experience is significant. A customer who sends a payment can now see that it has been received by the intermediary bank, that it has been forwarded, and that it has been credited to the beneficiary, with timestamps at each step.

A customer who is waiting for a payment can be told exactly where it is, instead of being told that it is in transit. An operations team that handles exceptions can see where a payment is stuck and can contact the right bank, instead of sending a query to every bank in the chain. A business analyst who works on cross border payments must understand gpi as a service level, not as a technology. The technology is the tracker, but the value is the commitment by banks to process quickly, to disclose charges, and to confirm credit.
