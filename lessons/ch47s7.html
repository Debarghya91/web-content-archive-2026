Key terms

Suspense account: A temporary account used to hold funds or entries that cannot yet be posted to their final account. Banks use suspense accounts for unidentified receipts, pending investigations and items awaiting...

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Payment hub: A central system in a bank that receives payment instructions from all channels, validates them, applies screening and routing rules, and sends them to the correct clearing system or correspondent.

Start with the scenario and the constraints, because they decide which patterns are possible. Your bank, a mid-sized bank in Spain, is joining a new instant payment rail that settles in euros within ten seconds, twenty-four hours a day. The bank expects 200,000 payments a day, with an average value of EUR 180, and a peak of 40 payments per second at lunchtime. The core banking system is fifteen years old and exposes a synchronous service for balance checks, but only a nightly file interface for bulk posting. The sanctions provider offers a real-time API, and the scheme requires ISO 20022 messages. List every system you must connect, and note the constraint on each one before you draw anything.

Draw the integration map next, showing every system, every connection, and the direction of each flow. Place the channels on the left, the payment hub in the centre, the core banking system, the sanctions service, the fraud service, the ledger, and the scheme on the right. Draw the customer payment as a line from the mobile app through the hub to the scheme, and draw the settlement as a line from the scheme back to the ledger. Label each line with its transport, such as API, queue, or file, and with its format, such as JSON or pacs.008. A good map fits on one page, and a colleague who has never seen the project should be able to trace a EUR 500 payment from start to finish without asking a question.

Choose the pattern for each connection and write down why you chose it. Use a synchronous API between the channel and the hub, because the customer waits for an answer. Use a synchronous call from the hub to the sanctions API, because screening must complete before the payment proceeds. Use asynchronous messaging between the hub and the core banking system for the debit, with a response queue that returns the result, because the core system cannot always answer in under a second. Use a file interface for the end-of-day ledger summary, because finance needs a complete and auditable record. Use event streaming for status updates, so the mobile app, the notification service, and the warehouse all learn about a settled payment at the same time.

Define the contract for each interface, with enough detail for a developer to build it without guessing. For the hub to scheme interface, specify the pacs.008 message, the required fields, the character set, the timeout of five seconds, and the error codes for an invalid account, an amount above the limit, and a duplicate instruction. For the sanctions API, specify the request fields, the response time of under 200 milliseconds, and the behaviour when the service is unavailable. For the core banking queue, specify the message format, the retry policy, and the dead letter queue that holds messages that fail three times. A contract that leaves the error codes undefined will cause arguments during testing, when it is too late to fix them cheaply.

Finish with failure handling, certification, and the go-live plan, and test your design against a real failure. Describe what happens if the sanctions service is down for thirty minutes, if the scheme rejects a payment after the core debit has succeeded, and if the ledger file arrives with a total that does not match the hub total. Define the reconciliation job that compares the hub, the core system, and the scheme every fifteen minutes, and the suspense account that holds unmatched items. Add a certification plan with at least ten test cases, a limited go-live with ten payments of EUR 50, and a rollback trigger. Present your map, your contracts, and your failure table, and ask a colleague to find the connection you missed.
