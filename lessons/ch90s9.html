Exercise

Decide which settlement model fits the scenario in this lesson and explain why.

Use the settlement models, the liquidity trade-off and the finality rules in this lesson.

  1. What to do. Name the model, state when settlement becomes final, and explain what happens if the sending bank does not have enough liquidity.

Correct result. Real time gross settlement settles each payment individually in central bank money with immediate finality, but it needs liquidity for every payment. Deferred net settlement nets payments and settles the net position, which needs less liquidity but leaves settlement risk until the net settles.

Why. Settlement is where money actually moves. The model decides the risk, the liquidity need, and when the payment is truly final.

Every bank runs on a core banking system: the system of record for accounts, balances, and transactions. Four products dominate the global market, and a payments analyst will meet at least one of them in any large bank: T24, now Temenos Transact, from Temenos; Finacle, from Infosys and its EdgeVerve subsidiary; FLEXCUBE, from Oracle; and BaNCS, from TCS. Each is a universal banking platform, covering retail, corporate, and private banking, with payments, lending, and trade finance. The products differ in architecture, footprint, and strength, and the survey matters because the core shapes every payment integration.

Temenos Transact, formerly T24, is the core from Temenos, a Swiss banking software company. Transact is modular and real time: a single data model, a product factory for defining accounts and products, and broad coverage across retail, corporate, private banking, and wealth. Its footprint is international, with particular strength in private banking and in emerging markets. Temenos has invested heavily in cloud native deployment, offering Transact as a cloud service, and in an open API layer for integration. The product is known for configurability: banks define products and workflows through configuration rather than code, which speeds change but needs strong configuration governance.

Finacle, from Infosys through its EdgeVerve subsidiary, has one of the largest installed bases in the world, with particular strength in India and in emerging markets. Finacle covers retail, corporate, and wealth banking, with strong payments, trade finance, and treasury capabilities. It is known for scale: some of the world largest banks by transaction volume run Finacle, processing very high daily volumes. Finacle provides a product factory, a customer information file, and an integration layer, and it is often deployed alongside Infosys payment and channel products. For an analyst working on Indian banking, Finacle is the core most likely to be in the room.

Oracle FLEXCUBE is the core banking product of Oracle Financial Services. FLEXCUBE has particular strength in corporate banking, trade finance, and payments, and a large footprint in the Middle East, Africa, and Asia. It is part of the Oracle banking suite, which includes payments, lending, and risk products that integrate with the core. FLEXCUBE is known for its corporate banking depth: complex account structures, liquidity management, and trade finance workflows. Banks that choose FLEXCUBE often do so for the breadth of the Oracle suite and the corporate banking capability, and the integration design must cover the suite, not just the core.

TCS BaNCS, from Tata Consultancy Services, is a universal banking platform with a very large installed base, including some of the world largest banks by customer count. BaNCS covers retail, corporate, private banking, and capital markets, with a single platform and a single data model across business lines. Its footprint is global, with particular strength in India, where several of the largest banks run BaNCS, and in large scale retail operations. BaNCS is known for its breadth and for the scale of its deployments: hundreds of millions of accounts on a single instance. The platform includes payments, channels, and risk capabilities alongside the core.

For a business analyst, the comparison is less about which product is best and more about what each product means for the work. All four are configurable product factories plus ledgers plus channels, and all four expose their capabilities through APIs, files, and data models that the analyst must understand. Selection depends on the bank footprint, the product needs, and the operating model, and it is a board level decision with a multi-year implementation. The analyst works with the core that exists: understanding its payment capabilities, its message handling, its ledger posting, and its integration points is essential for any payment product, any channel, and any reporting change. Core migration is a programme; core understanding is a daily skill.
