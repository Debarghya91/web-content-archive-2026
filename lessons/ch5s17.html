Key terms

Authorisation: Approval by the issuer for a card transaction to proceed, confirming the card is valid and funds or credit are available.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

  1. What to do. Name the model, state when settlement becomes final, and explain what happens if the sending bank does not have enough liquidity.

Correct result. Real time gross settlement settles each payment individually in central bank money with immediate finality, but it needs liquidity for every payment. Deferred net settlement nets payments and settles the net position, which needs less liquidity but leaves settlement risk until the net settles.

Why. Settlement is where money actually moves. The model decides the risk, the liquidity need, and when the payment is truly final.

Category 2 of the SWIFT MT standard holds the messages banks use to move money between themselves. Four of them form a family that every payments analyst should be able to tell apart: the MT200, the MT201, the MT203, and the MT204. The MT202, the general financial institution transfer, sits at the centre of the family and is covered in its own lesson. The four messages in this lesson extend the same idea in two directions: own account versus general purpose, and single versus multiple. Getting the distinctions right matters, because sending the wrong member of the family misstates whose money is moving.

The MT200 is the Financial Institution Transfer for its Own Account. A bank uses it to move its own funds between its own accounts at two different banks: for example, moving euros from its nostro account at one correspondent to its nostro account at another, to fund the account that will pay the next batch of customer transfers. The sender and the ultimate owner of the funds are the same institution. The message is simple by design: a reference, a value date and amount, and the account chain. There is no customer, no third party, and no underlying trade to describe.

The MT201 is the multiple version of the MT200: Multiple Financial Institution Transfer for its Own Account. One message carries several own account transfers, each with its own amount and account details, all between the same sender and receiver. The MT203 is the multiple version of the general transfer: Multiple General FI Transfer. It carries several transfers in one message where the funds belong to third parties or to other institutions, not only to the sender own account. The multiple messages exist for efficiency: a bank funding several accounts, or settling several obligations with one correspondent, sends one message instead of many, and the receiver processes each transfer block in turn.

The MT204 is the odd one out, and the most easily misunderstood. It is the Financial Institution Direct Debit: a bank instructs another bank to debit its account. The sender pulls funds from the receiver, rather than pushing funds to it. The classic use is collecting charges or fees that one bank owes another under a standing agreement, or sweeping funds under a cash management arrangement. Like the MT104 in the customer world, the MT204 needs an authorisation behind it: the bank being debited must have agreed that the sender may pull funds. Without that agreement the debit is refused.

The field structure of the family is shared. Field 20 carries the transaction reference and field 21 a related reference. Field 32A carries the value date, currency, and amount. The account chain is carried by fields 52, 53, 54, 56, 57, and 58, which name the ordering institution, the sender correspondent, the receiver correspondent, the intermediary, the account with institution, and the beneficiary institution, as the routing requires. In the multiple messages, the transfer blocks repeat, each carrying its own amount and account chain, so the receiver can post each transfer separately. Field 72 carries sender to receiver information for any handling instruction that does not fit elsewhere.

In ISO 20022 the MT200, MT201, MT202, and MT203 all map to the pacs.009, the FinancialInstitutionCreditTransfer, with the own account and general cases distinguished by the parties and the purpose rather than by different message types. The MT204 maps to the pacs.010, the FinancialInstitutionDirectDebit, which preserves the pull direction in the new standard. The family distinction therefore survives migration in the data, even though the message count shrinks. An analyst mapping legacy Category 2 traffic must still ask, for every message, whose money is moving and whether the instruction pushes or pulls, because that answer decides the correct target message and the correct validation.
