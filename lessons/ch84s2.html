Key terms

Nostro reconciliation: Reconciliation of a bank nostro account against the statement received from the correspondent bank. Operations teams match each nostro entry to a statement entry, investigate breaks, and confirm the...

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Charge bearer: A code that states who pays the charges for a payment. In ISO 20022 the values are DEBT for debtor, CRED for creditor, SHAR for shared and SLEV for service level.

Exercise

Use the message structure, fields and example in this lesson.

  1. What to do. List the three levels of the message and name the field that carries the amount and the field that carries the end-to-end reference.

Correct result. The amount is carried in the instructed amount element and the reference in the end-to-end identification. The three levels are the group header, the payment information, and the credit transfer transaction information.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The group header identifies the message, the payment information identifies the batch, and the transaction information carries the single payment.

A business requirements document for a payment product starts with the business problem, not with the solution. The problem might be that the firm cannot process SEPA Instant payments, that its straight-through processing rate is 75 percent, or that its exception handling takes three days. The BRD states the problem, the impact, the stakeholders, the scope, the constraints, and the success criteria. A BRD that starts with the solution has skipped the problem, and the solution will be wrong. The success criteria must be measurable: the STP rate will rise from 75 percent to 95 percent, or the exception handling time will fall from three days to four hours.

The functional specification is the document the developers build from, and it must be precise enough to build from. For a payment product the specification is structured around the payment lifecycle: initiation, authentication, validation, enrichment, screening, routing, processing, clearing, settlement, status reporting, reconciliation, and exception handling. Each stage gets its own section, with the inputs, the processing, the outputs, the error handling, and the audit requirements. A specification that omits the exception handling for a stage is incomplete, because the exception is where the product fails.

The payment lifecycle is the spine of the specification, and every requirement hangs from it. A requirement about the pacs.008 message hangs from the processing stage. A requirement about the nostro reconciliation hangs from the reconciliation stage. A requirement about the camt.056 recall hangs from the exception stage. The spine ensures that no stage is forgotten, and that the requirements are complete. A specification that is organised by screen, or by user role, will miss the stages that have no screen and no user, such as the settlement stage, which is entirely automated.

Message specifications are the most technical part of the document. Each message must be specified field by field, with the field name, the format, the mandatory or optional status, the validation rule, and the error code if the validation fails. For a pacs.008 the specification must state the debtor agent, the creditor agent, the instructed amount, the settlement method, and the charge bearer, with the code set for each one. The specification must also state the mapping from the internal data model to the message, because the internal model and the message are never the same.

Non-functional requirements in payments are often more important than the functional ones. The specification must state the availability target, the recovery time objective, the recovery point objective, the throughput, the maximum processing time, the data retention period, and the audit requirements. A specification that omits the recovery time objective will produce a product that cannot recover, and the first outage will prove it. Each non-functional requirement must have a target, a measurement method, and a test.

The sign-off is the moment the specification becomes a commitment. The product owner, the operations lead, the compliance lead, and the technology lead must all sign, and each signature means something different. The product owner signs that the specification meets the business need. The operations lead signs that the exception handling is workable. The compliance lead signs that the screening and reporting are correct. The technology lead signs that it can be built. A specification signed by only the product owner is not signed off, it is a wish.

For a business analyst, the specification is judged by whether the developers can build from it without asking questions. A good test is to give the specification to a developer who has never seen the product and ask them to estimate the work. If they cannot estimate it, the specification is not precise enough. The acceptance criteria must include a review where the developers, the testers, and the operations team confirm the specification is complete and unambiguous. Last reviewed 7 November 2026. Further reading: Karl Wiegers, Software Requirements; the International Institute of Business Analysis, A Guide to the Business Analysis Body of Knowledge; the ISO 20022 message definitions as specification sources; the EPC rulebooks as specification sources.
