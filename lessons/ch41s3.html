What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain payment repair, return, recall and cancellation. Repair means correcting a payment that has been stopped before settlement, so that it can proceed without the customer having to start again.

Why it matters. A payment of USD 60,000 may be held because the beneficiary address is incomplete, or because the intermediary bank details are missing, and the operations team can repair it by adding the missing information from the customer instruction or from a standing arrangement.

Chapter objective: Learner investigates payment exceptions, including non-receipt, wrong amount, duplicate, and delayed payments, and runs a structured investigation from first report to resolution. Learner demonstrates this skill using realistic banking examples, explaining each step clearly and using correct terminology throughout.

Key learning points

  • Repair: correcting payment
  • Return: payment has been executed
  • Recall: request to return funds
  • Cancellation stops payment before execution
  • Cut-off between those two states
  • Good exception handling

Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Exception: A payment that cannot be processed normally and needs manual handling, investigation or repair. Exceptions arise from missing data, failed screening, account errors or system faults.

pacs.004: An ISO 20022 message used to return a payment that was already settled but cannot be credited to the beneficiary.

Repair means correcting a payment that has been stopped before settlement, so that it can proceed without the customer having to start again. A payment of USD 60,000 may be held because the beneficiary address is incomplete, or because the intermediary bank details are missing, and the operations team can repair it by adding the missing information from the customer instruction or from a standing arrangement. Repair is the cheapest and fastest remedy, because the money has not yet moved and no second payment is needed. The repaired payment keeps its original reference, but the repair itself must be logged, including who made the change and what was changed, so that auditors can trace it. A return happens after a payment has been executed but the beneficiary bank cannot credit it, so the money is sent back to the sender. The beneficiary bank initiates the return by sending a pacs.004 message, which carries the original reference, the returned amount, and a reason code such as AC04 for a closed account or BE04 for a missing creditor address. The sending bank matches the pacs.004 to the original pacs.008, credits the customer account with the returned funds, and notifies the customer. A return is not a failure of the sending bank, but the customer still needs to know why the payment came back and what they should do differently next time. A recall is a request to return funds that have already been credited to the beneficiary, and it is the hardest remedy to obtain because the money is no longer in the banking system. The sending bank sends a camt.056 message, known as a payment cancellation request, asking the beneficiary bank to return the funds, and the beneficiary bank replies with a camt.029 message stating whether the recall was accepted or rejected. A recall may succeed if the beneficiary agrees, or if the credit was made in error and the beneficiary bank can reverse it, but it may fail if the beneficiary has already spent the money. A recall is therefore a request, not a guarantee, and the customer must understand that distinction. Cancellation stops a payment before it is executed, and it is the remedy that customers most often ask for when they realise they have made a mistake. A customer who instructed a payment of GBP 15,000 to the wrong supplier may call the bank within minutes and ask for it to be cancelled. If the payment is still held in the payment engine and has not yet been sent to the clearing system, the bank can cancel it immediately and restore the funds. If the payment has already been sent, cancellation is no longer possible, and the bank must use a recall instead. The cut-off between those two states is measured in minutes, which is why cancellation requests are treated as urgent. The analyst's job across all four remedies is to record the full audit trail, from the original instruction to the final outcome, so that the customer, the auditor, and the regulator can all see what happened. That trail includes the original pacs.008, the pacs.002 or pacs.004 that reported the problem, the camt.056 and camt.029 if a recall was attempted, and the ledger entries that moved the money back to the customer. A remedy without an audit trail is a risk, because the bank cannot prove that it acted correctly or that the customer received their money back. Good exception handling is therefore as much about documentation as it is about moving money.
