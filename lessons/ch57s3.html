Key terms

Indirect participant: A bank or institution that uses a payment system through a direct participant, without holding its own settlement account.

Direct participant: A bank or institution that holds an account in a payment system and sends and receives payments directly.

Multi-addressee: A payment system arrangement where one participant, usually a bank, acts as the addressee for payments to several other institutions, such as its branches or subsidiaries.

Use the four participation types in this lesson and the rule that settlement is always booked on a direct participant RTGS DCA.

Correct result. Code 05, direct participant, holds its own RTGS Dedicated Cash Account in T2 and connects directly through ESMIG, and settlement is booked on its own RTGS DCA. Code 06, indirect participant, holds no RTGS DCA and settles through a direct participant, so settlement is booked on the direct participant account. Code 07, multi-addressee, is a branch or group entity authorised to send and receive payments directly to and from T2, using the direct participant RTGS DCA, so settlement is booked there too.

Show transcript

A direct participant, code 05 in the RTGS Directory, is a bank that holds its own RTGS Dedicated Cash Account in T2 and connects directly to the system through ESMIG. It sends and receives its own payments, it manages its own liquidity, and it is responsible for its own settlement. Deutsche Bank in Frankfurt, BIC DEUTDEFFXXX, is a direct participant, and a EUR 10 million payment from Deutsche Bank settles on the Deutsche Bank RTGS DCA. Direct participation is the full form of membership, and it is the type that most large banks choose, because it gives them control over timing, liquidity and queues.

An indirect participant, code 06 in the RTGS Directory, is a bank that has no RTGS Dedicated Cash Account of its own and settles through a direct participant. It is listed in the directory so that other banks can address payments to it, but the settlement always happens on the direct participant RTGS DCA. A small regional bank in Italy that settles through UniCredit, BIC UNCRITMMXXX, is an indirect participant, and a EUR 50,000 payment to that bank settles on the UniCredit RTGS DCA, with UniCredit then crediting the small bank in its own books. The indirect participant never touches T2 directly.

A multi-addressee, code 07 in the RTGS Directory, is a branch or a group entity that a direct participant authorises to send and receive payments directly to and from T2, using the direct participant RTGS Dedicated Cash Account. The multi-addressee holds no RTGS DCA of its own, and it has its own BIC for addressing, but every payment it sends or receives settles on the direct participant account. Commerzbank Madrid, BIC COBAESMXXXX, can act as a multi-addressee of Commerzbank Frankfurt, BIC COBADEFFXXX, and a EUR 250,000 payment it sends settles on the Frankfurt RTGS DCA, not on any Madrid account.

An addressable BIC holder, code 08 in the RTGS Directory, is a BIC that can be addressed in a payment, but that has no direct technical connection to T2 and no RTGS Dedicated Cash Account. It is usually a branch or a subsidiary that receives payments through a direct participant, which credits the funds in its own books after settlement. A branch of a French bank in Lyon, with its own BIC, may be an addressable BIC holder settled through the Paris head office, and a EUR 20,000 payment to that branch settles on the head office RTGS DCA. The branch is reachable, but it never sends a message to T2 itself.

The four codes matter because a payment engine must treat each type differently. A payment to a direct participant, code 05, settles on the payee own account, and the instructed agent is the payee bank itself. A payment to an indirect participant, code 06, or to an addressable BIC, code 08, settles on the direct participant account, and the instructed agent is that direct participant, not the named payee BIC. A payment from a multi-addressee, code 07, is sent by the branch BIC, but the instructing agent is the direct participant that holds the account. A bank that confuses the codes will address the payment to the wrong agent and the payment will fail.
