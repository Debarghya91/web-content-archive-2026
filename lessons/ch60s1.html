What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain pain.001.001.09 customer credit transfer initiation: what it is and what it carries. pain.001.001.09 is the ISO 20022 Customer Credit Transfer Initiation message, and it is the standard way a customer tells a bank to move money from the customer account to a named beneficiary.

Why it matters. This matters because the message is the payment instruction. A wrong field, a missing element or a misunderstood format is a failed payment, a return, or a compliance breach.

Chapter objective: Learner explains pain.001 and pain.002 in depth, describes the relay scenario, authorised party initiation, and financial institution initiation, and traces a payment to pacs.008. Learner uses real amounts, banks and message names to show how customer initiation works in practice.

Key learning points

  • Pain.001.001.09: ISO 20022 Customer Credit
  • Group header of a pain.001 file
  • Payment information block grouping instructions by debtor and date
  • Remittance information in pain.001
  • Service level of SEPA marks

Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Execution date: The date on which the bank executes a payment instruction, debiting the debtor account and sending the message onward.

Charge bearer: A code that states who pays the charges for a payment. In ISO 20022 the values are DEBT for debtor, CRED for creditor, SHAR for shared and SLEV for service level.

pain.001.001.09 is the ISO 20022 Customer Credit Transfer Initiation message, and it is the standard way a customer tells a bank to move money from the customer account to a named beneficiary. The message carries the debtor, the debtor account, the creditor, the creditor account, the amount, the currency, the requested execution date, the charge bearer, and the remittance information that explains why the payment is being made. A single pain.001 file can contain many payment instructions under one group header, so a corporation can send hundreds of supplier payments in one file. Each instruction carries an instruction identification and an end to end identification, and those identifiers travel with the payment so every party can trace it later. The group header of a pain.001 file sets the context for everything that follows it. It carries a message identification, a creation date and time, the number of transactions, the control sum of all the amounts, and the initiating party that created the file. If the control sum does not match the sum of the individual instructions, the receiving bank can reject the whole file before it processes any payment. That check protects both sides, because a file with 250 payments worth EUR 1,250,000 in total should never be processed when the header claims EUR 1,200,000. The header also records whether the file is a single instruction or a batch, which affects how errors are reported. Each payment information block inside pain.001 groups instructions that share the same debtor, the same debtor account, the same requested execution date, and the same charge bearer. Inside that block, each credit transfer transaction information element describes one payment to one creditor. It names the creditor, the creditor agent, the creditor account, the instructed amount, the purpose code, and the remittance information. For example, a German manufacturer paying EUR 48,500 to a supplier in Milan would name the supplier, give the supplier IBAN IT60X0542811101000000123456, state EUR 48,500, set the execution date, and add the invoice number in the remittance field so the supplier can reconcile the receipt. Remittance information in pain.001 can be structured or unstructured, and the choice matters for straight through processing. Structured remittance carries a reference such as an RF creditor reference or an invoice number in a defined field, so the creditor system can match the payment automatically. Unstructured remittance is free text, for example INV-2026-0841, payment for March delivery of steel fittings, which a person can read but a system cannot match reliably. Banks and corporates prefer structured references for high volume flows, because a payment of EUR 12,750 with reference RF18539007547034 can be reconciled in seconds, while a free text note may sit unmatched for days and generate a query to the accounts team. pain.001.001.09 also carries the service level, the local instrument, and the category purpose, and those three fields tell the bank how to treat the payment. A service level of SEPA marks a payment for the SEPA Credit Transfer scheme, while a service level of URGP can mark an urgent payment that needs same day handling. The local instrument can identify a specific domestic product, and the category purpose can mark a payment as salary, pension, or supplier payment. Getting these fields wrong can send a routine EUR 3,200 supplier payment down an urgent route with higher charges, or delay a salary run that 400 employees expect on the last working day of the month.
