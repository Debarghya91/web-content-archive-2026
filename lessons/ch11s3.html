Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

  1. What to do. Name the model, state when settlement becomes final, and explain what happens if the sending bank does not have enough liquidity.

Correct result. Real time gross settlement settles each payment individually in central bank money with immediate finality, but it needs liquidity for every payment. Deferred net settlement nets payments and settles the net position, which needs less liquidity but leaves settlement risk until the net settles.

Why. Settlement is where money actually moves. The model decides the risk, the liquidity need, and when the payment is truly final.

Show transcript

A credit transfer is a push payment. The payer tells their own bank to send a defined amount of euro to the payee, and the payee does nothing. The payer bank moves the money through the clearing system to the payee bank, and the payee bank credits the account. The scheme rulebook sets a maximum execution time of one business day, and the full amount must be credited. The payer starts the payment with a pain.001 message.

Whether the customer uses online banking, a mobile app, a branch, or a bulk file, the instruction reaches the payer bank as a pain.001, a customer credit transfer initiation. It carries the payer and payee names, the payee IBAN, the amount, the remittance information, and the end to end reference. A wrong field here is the most common cause of failure. The payer bank validates the instruction, then debits the payer. The bank checks that the IBAN is well formed, that the account holds enough money, that the payment passes sanctions and fraud screening, and that it arrived before the daily cut off.

If the checks pass, the bank debits the payer account, accepts the payment, and the status becomes ACCP. If a check fails, the bank rejects the instruction at once and tells the payer through a pain.002 status report. The payer bank sends a pacs.008 to the payee bank. The interbank leg is a pacs.008, a financial institution to financial institution customer credit transfer, carrying the debtor and creditor agents, the amount, and the remittance information. It travels through a clearing system, most often STEP2.

Settlement between the banks happens through T2, the Eurosystem settlement system. While settlement is in process the status is ACSP, and on completion it becomes ACSC. The payee bank credits the payee account in full. On receipt, the payee bank checks that the creditor IBAN belongs to an open account and that nothing blocks the credit. It then credits the full amount, with no deduction, and the status becomes ACCC.

The customer can be told about the credit with a camt.054 notification, and the credit appears on the statement reported in a camt.053. Every step leaves a trail for operations and reconciliation. Rejects stop the payment before the money moves. When a payment cannot be delivered, the payee bank or the clearing system reports it with a pacs.002 carrying status RJCT and a reason code. Common codes are AC01 for an incorrect account number, RC01 for a wrong bank identifier, AG01 for a forbidden transaction, AM04 for insufficient funds, and FF01 for an invalid file format.

The payer bank passes the rejection back in a pain.002. Returns send the money back after settlement. A return happens after settlement when the payee bank cannot credit the payment, and it travels back as a pacs.004, a payment return. Typical codes are AC04 for a closed account, AC06 for a blocked account, MD07 for a deceased account holder, and AG01 for a forbidden transaction. The payee bank returns the full original amount.

The reason code decides whether the payment can simply be sent again. Recalls ask for the money back after it has been credited. When a payment was sent by mistake, duplicated, or sent to the wrong payee, the payer bank asks for it back with a camt.056 cancellation request. The payee bank answers with a camt.029, a resolution of investigation, and must ask the payee for consent first, because the credit is now the payee money. If the payee agrees, the funds come back as a pacs.004 return.

A refusal means no automatic recovery. Duplicates and cut off times cause the failures analysts see most. A duplicate carries the same end to end reference, amount, and value date as an earlier payment, and it is rejected with reason code AM05. Duplicate detection must run at the payer bank before the pacs.008 is sent, because stopping one after settlement means a return or a recall. A payment instructed after the daily cut off waits for the next clearing cycle, and one instructed on a Friday may not settle until Monday.

The scheme rules the analyst must check. Four rules decide whether an SCT implementation is correct. The maximum execution time is one business day. The full amount must be credited, with no deductions in transit. The payment needs only the payee IBAN, and no bank identifier code has been required since 2016.

The scheme covers euro payments inside the SEPA area. The analyst checks cut off times, pacs.008 validation, code mapping, and reconciliation of every pacs.002 against the original pain.001.
