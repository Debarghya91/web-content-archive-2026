Key terms

Authorisation: Approval by the issuer for a card transaction to proceed, confirming the card is valid and funds or credit are available.

Cut-off: The latest time a bank or payment system accepts a payment instruction for processing on a given value date.

Break: A difference found during reconciliation between two records that should agree, such as a bank statement and a ledger.

A good prompt is not a clever trick. It is a clear brief, and it has four parts that you should include every time. First, the context. Tell the tool what the flow is, who uses it, and what systems are involved. Second, the artefact. Say exactly what you want, a BRD section, five user stories, a test case table, not just a document. Third, the constraints. Give the limits, the amounts, the cut-off times, and the rules it must not break. Fourth, the format. Say how you want the answer laid out, a table, a numbered list, or a set of headings.

Then add two requests that make the output safer. Ask for a checklist, so you can tick off each item as you verify it. And ask the tool to flag what it is unsure about, so the gaps are visible instead of hidden.

Here is a prompt that follows this pattern. You are helping a BA at a bank. The flow is a supplier payment in euros, for payments up to EUR 20,000, with a single authoriser below EUR 10,000 and two authorisers above it. Draft the acceptance criteria for the authorisation step as a numbered list. The cut-off time is four in the afternoon. Flag anything you are unsure about, and end with a checklist I can use to verify each criterion.

Notice what that prompt does. It gives the context, the artefact, the constraints, and the format, and it asks for a checklist and for honesty about uncertainty. Use it for BRDs, for stories, for process maps, and for test cases, and keep a small library of prompts that worked, with the flow name and the date, so your team reuses the good ones instead of starting from a blank box every time.
