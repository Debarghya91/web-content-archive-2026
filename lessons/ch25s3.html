What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain validation, enrichment and the processing engine. Validation is the set of checks that confirm a payment is complete, correct and allowed before any money moves.

Why it matters. For example, take a payment from a corporate customer who sends EUR 50,000 to a supplier but leaves the beneficiary bank identifier blank.

Chapter objective: Learner designs a payment hub architecture, explaining intake, validation, routing, transformation, screening, and settlement modules, and maps a payment through each module. Key terms

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Clearing: The process of exchanging payment instructions between banks and calculating what each bank owes the others.

Exercise

Validate and enrich a corporate payment with a missing beneficiary bank identifier.

A corporate customer sends EUR 50,000 to a supplier but leaves the beneficiary bank identifier blank. Business validation checks that the mandatory fields are present, that the currency is supported, that the value date is valid, and that the payment amount is within the customer limits. Reference data validation checks that the beneficiary bank identifier is real.

  1. What to do. State which checks run, whether the payment should be rejected or enriched, and what happens if a service is unavailable.

Correct result. Business validation checks that the mandatory fields are present, that the currency is supported, that the value date is valid, and that the amount is within the customer limits. Account validation checks that the debit account exists, belongs to the customer, is in the right status, and has sufficient funds or an approved overdraft. Reference data validation checks that the beneficiary bank identifier is real, that the country and currency combination is supported, and that the payment type is allowed for that channel. A missing beneficiary bank identifier can be enriched if the hub can derive it reliably from the IBAN or reference data, but if a mandatory field cannot be completed or validated, the payment is rejected back to the channel with a clear reason code, so the customer or the corporate system can fix it and resend. If a service is unavailable, the engine can retry, park the payment, or route it to manual repair, depending on the configuration.

Why. Validation is the set of checks that confirm a payment is complete, correct and allowed before any money moves, so a failure here must never become a payment that moves.
