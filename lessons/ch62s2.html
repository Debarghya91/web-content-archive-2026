Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Creditor: The party that is owed money and receives a payment, such as a supplier, a seller or a service provider.

Clearing: The process of exchanging payment instructions between banks and calculating what each bank owes the others.

Exercise

Jonas Weber signs a mandate on the Stadtwerke Linden website, stored under reference SWL-JW-2026-0417. Explain the two mandate flows, what the collection instruction carries, and what must happen if Jonas Weber changes his bank account.

Use the mandate flows in this lesson: creditor driven, debtor driven, the mandate fields, and the amendment rule.

  1. What to do. State who stores the mandate in each flow, what the collection instruction carries, and what happens if Jonas Weber changes his bank account.

Correct result. In the creditor driven mandate flow, the debtor gives the mandate to the creditor, and the creditor stores it. In the debtor driven mandate flow, the debtor gives the mandate to its own bank, and the bank stores it. The mandate carries the debtor and creditor names, the debtor IBAN, the creditor identifier, and whether the mandate is one off or recurrent. When the collection is due, Stadtwerke Linden sends the collection instruction to Harbour Bank Frankfurt, carrying the mandate reference SWL-JW-2026-0417, the creditor identifier, and the debtor IBAN.

If any of these facts change, for example if Jonas Weber changes his bank account, the mandate must be amended, and the amendment must travel with the next collection.

Why. The mandate is the legal basis for the whole flow, and without a valid mandate the collection must not happen. An amendment that does not travel with the next collection will be rejected by the debtor bank.

The four-corner model for SDD has the same shape as the SCT model, but the direction of the instruction is reversed. The creditor is the party that initiates the collection, the debtor is the party whose account is debited, the creditor bank receives the collection and passes it into clearing, and the debtor bank debits the debtor account. The clearing and settlement mechanism sits between the two banks, exchanging the collection messages and settling the funds. The creditor drives the flow, but the debtor bank holds the control, because it can reject a collection that has no valid mandate or insufficient funds.

In the creditor driven mandate flow, the debtor gives the mandate to the creditor, and the creditor stores it. Jonas Weber signs the mandate on the Stadtwerke Linden website, and the utility stores the signed mandate in its own systems. When the collection is due, Stadtwerke Linden sends the collection instruction to its bank, Harbour Bank Frankfurt, and the instruction carries the mandate reference SWL-JW-2026-0417, the creditor identifier, and the debtor IBAN. The debtor bank receives the collection through clearing and checks the mandate data against what it holds, but the original mandate document stays with the creditor.

In the debtor driven mandate flow, the debtor gives the mandate to its own bank, and the bank stores it. This flow is used in some countries and for some B2B arrangements, where the debtor bank must have the mandate on file before it will allow any collection. The debtor signs the mandate and sends it to the bank, the bank records the mandate reference, the creditor identifier, and the maximum amount, and it then authorises collections that match those details. The creditor still initiates the collection, but the debtor bank checks it against a mandate it holds itself, which gives the debtor stronger control.

The mandate itself carries the facts that every later message must repeat accurately. It names the debtor and the creditor, it gives the debtor IBAN and the creditor identifier, it states whether the mandate is for a one off or a recurring collection, it gives the mandate reference, and it records the date and place of signature. For Jonas Weber, the mandate states a recurring collection of EUR 89.99, signed online in Hanover on 14 September 2026, with the reference SWL-JW-2026-0417. If any of these facts change, for example if Jonas Weber changes his bank account, the mandate must be amended, and the amendment must travel with the next collection.
