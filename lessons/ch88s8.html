Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Liquidity: Cash or assets that a bank can use immediately to meet payment obligations. Banks manage liquidity by forecasting inflows and outflows, funding nostro and settlement accounts, and using central bank...

Clearing: The process of exchanging payment instructions between banks and calculating what each bank owes the others.

Exercise

Use the clearing rules and the netting logic in this lesson.

  1. What to do. List the payments in the cycle, calculate the net position for each bank, and state what settles.

Correct result. Clearing validates, sorts, matches and nets the instructions, then produces a net position per bank. Only the net position settles, which needs far less liquidity than settling every payment individually.

Why. Netting is what makes high volume payments possible. Without it, every payment would need full liquidity at the moment it is sent.

EBA CLEARING, the company owned by European banks, operates the euro payment services that sit alongside the central bank systems. Two of its services cover the large value and retail ends of the market: EURO1, for large value euro payments, and STEP1, for smaller value euro payments. Both are net settlement systems: payments are exchanged through the day, positions are netted, and the net amounts settle in central bank money. Both serve banks that need euro clearing without building their own infrastructure, and both predate the SEPA schemes that now dominate retail euro payments.

EURO1 is the large value service. Participants send euro payments through the day, and the system processes them continuously, applying risk controls: bilateral and multilateral limits cap the exposure between participants, and liquidity saving features, including offsetting, reduce the funding needed. At the end of the day, the net positions settle in central bank money through TARGET, the Eurosystem real time gross settlement system. EURO1 is a deferred net settlement system: the payments are final during the day under the system rules, and the money moves at settlement. The combination of intraday processing and end of day settlement gives participants the speed of continuous processing with the efficiency of netting.

STEP1 is the service for smaller value euro payments, and for banks that do not need EURO1 scale. STEP1 participants send payments through the same EBA CLEARING infrastructure, with net settlement in central bank money. The requirements for STEP1 participation are lighter than for EURO1: lower volumes, simpler technical and liquidity arrangements, and access for banks that would not justify a EURO1 connection. STEP1 serves the long tail of European banks: institutions with euro payment business that need a reliable, shared clearing service without the cost and complexity of the large value system.

STEP2 is the sibling service, and the distinction matters. STEP2 is the SEPA retail clearing service: it processes SEPA credit transfers and direct debits in bulk, under the SEPA rulebooks. STEP2-T, covered in its own lesson, is the continuous gross settlement service within STEP2. EURO1 and STEP1 are not SEPA scheme services: they are EBA CLEARING services with their own rules, serving payments that are not SEPA scheme payments, including large value and cross border euro payments. A bank routes a payment to EURO1, STEP1, or STEP2 based on the payment type, the amount, the urgency, and the scheme.

Participation shapes the experience. EURO1 participants are large banks with high volumes, strict liquidity arrangements, and direct technical connections. They manage their positions intraday, monitor their limits, and fund their settlement obligations. STEP1 participants include smaller banks, with lighter requirements and, in some cases, access through a settlement agent. The tiered structure lets the European banking system share infrastructure efficiently: the largest banks run the large value service directly, and the smaller banks clear through the service that fits their scale.

The euro landscape has a place for each system. TARGET, the central bank RTGS, settles urgent large value payments one by one, in real time. EURO1 nets large value payments efficiently through the day. STEP1 clears smaller value payments for banks of all sizes. STEP2 clears SEPA retail payments in bulk. Routing decides which system a payment uses, and the routing decision weighs urgency, amount, cost, and scheme. For a business analyst, the lesson is that clearing is a landscape, not a single system, and the routing rules are where the landscape becomes a working operation.
