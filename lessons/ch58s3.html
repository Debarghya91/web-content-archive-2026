What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain pacs.004 payment return. A pacs.004 is a separate settlement instruction from the original payment, and that separation is the most important thing to understand about returns.

Why it matters. Operations teams must read the settlement method on every pacs.004 before they post it, because the method is the settlement instruction itself.

Chapter objective: Learner explains reject and return in CBPR+, describes when pacs.002 is mandatory for a final negative status, and shows how pacs.004 works as a separate settlement instruction. Learner uses real amounts, banks and message names to show how reject and return work in practice.

Key learning points

  • Pacs.004: separate settlement instruction
  • Returned Interbank Settlement Amount
  • Return that silently changed
  • Smaller credit seen without the return elements, like lost money
  • Settlement method read on every pacs.004 before posting
  • Return from beneficiary bank all way

Key terms

Correspondent banking: A service where one bank holds accounts for another bank so the second bank can make payments in currencies or countries where it has no branch.

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

  1. What to do. List the three levels of the message and name the field that carries the amount and the field that carries the end-to-end reference.

Correct result. The amount is carried in the instructed amount element and the reference in the end-to-end identification. The three levels are the group header, the payment information, and the credit transfer transaction information.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The group header identifies the message, the payment information identifies the batch, and the transaction information carries the single payment.

A pacs.004 is a separate settlement instruction from the original payment, and that separation is the most important thing to understand about returns. When a bank returns a payment, it does not reopen the original pacs.008, and it does not reverse it in place. It creates a brand new instruction with its own message identification, its own instruction identification, and its own settlement date. The pacs.004 references the original payment through the return identification and the original references, so the chain can be audited, but the settlement itself is independent. That independence means the return can be processed, queued, and settled like any other payment, with its own cut off times and its own nostro funding. The Returned Interbank Settlement Amount in a pacs.004 may differ from the Original Interbank Settlement Amount, and the difference is usually caused by charges or by a partial return. For example, an original payment of EUR 50,000 may be returned as EUR 49,950 because the returning bank deducted EUR 50 in charges, or it may be returned as EUR 30,000 because only part of the payment is being returned and the rest stays with the beneficiary. The pacs.004 carries both amounts, the original amount for reference and the returned amount for settlement, so that every bank in the chain can reconcile the difference. A return that silently changed the amount without showing both figures would break reconciliation at the next bank. A pacs.004 also has charges-related elements, and they explain where the missing money went. The message can carry the charges amount, the charges bearer, and information about which agent deducted the charge and why. In correspondent banking, each agent in the chain may deduct its own fee before passing the return onward, so a USD 100,000 payment might arrive back at the debtor agent as USD 99,920 after three banks each took a small fee. The charges elements make those deductions visible, so the debtor customer can see that the shortfall is charges, not an error. Without those elements, the customer would see a smaller credit and assume the bank had lost money. The Settlement Method in a pacs.004 matters most in correspondent banking, where the values INDA and INGA decide how the return is settled. INDA means instructed agent, which tells the receiving bank to settle the return by debiting the account of the instructed agent, usually a nostro account held by the sender. INGA means instructing agent, which tells the receiving bank to settle by debiting the account of the instructing agent. The choice depends on which side holds the account and which side is giving the instruction, and getting it wrong means the debit hits the wrong account and the return fails. Operations teams must read the settlement method on every pacs.004 before they post it, because the method is the settlement instruction itself. The Return Chain element in a pacs.004 captures all the parties in the return, and it exists because a return travels back through the same chain that carried the original payment. The element records the returning agent, the previous agents, and the ultimate debtor, so that each bank knows who sent the return and who should receive it next. For a payment that travelled from HSBC in Hong Kong through Standard Chartered in Singapore to a beneficiary in Sydney, the return chain lists each of those banks in order, so the funds retrace their steps. That chain also supports investigations, because an operations analyst can follow the return from the beneficiary bank all the way back to the original debtor without guessing at the route.
