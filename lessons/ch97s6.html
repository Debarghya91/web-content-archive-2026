Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Capture: The step in a card transaction where the merchant collects the authorised amount from the issuer, usually when goods are shipped.

This is a real case from the industry, and it is the most important lesson in this chapter. A bank asked an AI assistant built for banking to draft a business requirements document for a new payment flow. The BRD arrived in minutes, and it looked complete. It had a purpose, a scope, actors, a flow, business rules, and exceptions. It read like a document written by an experienced BA. Build started on that BRD. Then, during testing, the team discovered the problem. The settlement leg for EUR 100,000 was missing from the document entirely. There was EUR 100,000 and nowhere to settle. The payment could be captured, validated, authorised, and sent, but the document said nothing about where the money settled, which account it settled into, or when the settlement happened. Nobody had noticed, because the document looked finished. The lesson is not that AI is useless. The lesson is that an AI draft is fast, but only a person who owns that flow can say whether a leg is missing. Fast drafting is not complete requirements. The failure has two parts, and you need to guard against both. The first part is the hallucinated field, a detail the AI invents that looks right but does not exist, like a message field or a fee that your bank never uses. The second part is the missing leg, a real step the AI leaves out because it was never mentioned in the prompt, or because it seemed too obvious to state. The defence is the same for both. Walk the flow with a real payment, end to end, from capture to settlement to reconciliation, and check every leg against the draft. If you cannot trace a leg in the draft, the draft is not ready, however good it looks.
