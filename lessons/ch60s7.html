Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Execution date: The date on which the bank executes a payment instruction, debiting the debtor account and sending the message onward.

Charge bearer: A code that states who pays the charges for a payment. In ISO 20022 the values are DEBT for debtor, CRED for creditor, SHAR for shared and SLEV for service level.

A corporation based in Rotterdam, with its EUR operating account held by a bank in Frankfurt, needs to pay EUR 750,000 to a supplier in Vienna for a delivery of industrial pumps. The corporation uses a bank in Amsterdam as its payment platform, so it creates a pain.001.001.09 file in Amsterdam and sends it to the Amsterdam bank on Monday morning. The file contains one payment instruction, with the debtor named as the Rotterdam corporation, the debtor account held in Frankfurt, the creditor named as the Vienna supplier, the creditor IBAN AT611904300234573201, the amount EUR 750,000, the requested execution date of Tuesday, the charge bearer SLEV, and remittance information naming invoice INV-2026-1147 for the pump delivery.

The Amsterdam bank receives the pain.001 file and performs its first validation. It checks the group header, the control sum of EUR 750,000, the IBAN format, the execution date, and the sanction screening for the supplier name and country. All checks pass, so the Amsterdam bank sends a first pain.002.001.10 report to the corporation with status ACCP at transaction level, confirming that the instruction has been accepted for relay. The bank then forwards the instruction to the Frankfurt bank, which is the debtor agent, preserving the end to end identification so the payment can be traced. The corporation now knows its instruction is valid and is moving to the account holding bank.

The Frankfurt bank receives the relayed instruction on Monday afternoon and checks the debtor account. The account holds EUR 1,150,000, so the EUR 750,000 payment is covered, and there is no hold or freeze on the account. The bank also checks the requested execution date of Tuesday and confirms that Tuesday is a business day in Germany and Austria. It debits EUR 750,000 from the Rotterdam corporation account on Tuesday morning, leaving a balance of EUR 400,000, and it creates a pacs.008.001.08 message for EUR 750,000 to the supplier bank in Vienna. The Frankfurt bank sends a status report back through Amsterdam, and the corporation receives a second pain.002 with status ACSP, meaning accepted settlement in process.

The pacs.008 travels through SEPA clearing to the supplier bank in Vienna, which receives it on Tuesday and credits the supplier account with EUR 750,000. The Vienna bank sends a pacs.002 report back to the Frankfurt bank confirming acceptance, and the Frankfurt bank passes the confirmation through the Amsterdam bank to the corporation. The corporation receives a third pain.002 report, this time confirming that the payment has been settled and that the supplier has been credited. The treasurer in Rotterdam can now match the payment to invoice INV-2026-1147, close the payable in the accounting system, and tell the supplier that EUR 750,000 was paid on Tuesday as agreed in the contract.

If the payment had failed at any stage, the pain.002 chain would have shown the failure clearly and the corporation could have acted quickly. If the Frankfurt account had held only EUR 600,000, the Frankfurt bank would have reported RJCT with reason AM09 or with an insufficient funds code, and the corporation would have known on Monday that it needed to move EUR 150,000 into the account before the Tuesday execution date. If the Vienna IBAN had been wrong, the Amsterdam bank would have reported RJCT with reason AC01 at the first validation, and the corporation could have corrected the IBAN and resent the file the same day. The worked example shows why pain.001 and pain.002 are a pair, because the instruction without the report leaves the customer blind, and the report without the instruction has nothing to report on.
