What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain ai in reconciliation matching. Reconciliation is proving that the money that left the bank matches the money that was supposed to leave, and AI helps by matching payments to statements faster than a person can by hand.

Why it matters. This matters because it is a building block for the payments work in the rest of this course.

Chapter objective: AI in reconciliation, exceptions, fraud and customer service, writing requirements for AI systems, and the capstone.

Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

Queue: A holding area in a payment system where payments wait for sufficient funds or for their turn to settle.

  1. What to do. Compare the instruction, the settlement record and the statement, name the break type, and state the corrective action.

Correct result. A break is found by matching amount, currency, date and reference across the three sources. The root cause is a timing difference, an amount difference, a missing entry, or a duplicate, and the corrective action follows the cause.

Why. Reconciliation is the last line of defence. A break that is not investigated is money that cannot be proved.

Reconciliation is proving that the money that left the bank matches the money that was supposed to leave, and AI helps by matching payments to statements faster than a person can by hand. A typical case is a corporate client who sends one bulk payment of two lakh fifty thousand rupees to cover forty seven separate supplier invoices, and the bank statement shows one lump sum with one reference. A person would tick each invoice off by hand, but an AI matcher looks at the amount, the date, the reference and the beneficiary name, and proposes that this lump sum covers those forty seven invoices. That is where it helps, in speed and volume, and in spotting near matches like a payment of one lakh two thousand rupees that is two hundred rupees short because of a bank charge. But there is a line it must not cross. AI must never guess a match and post it as final when the evidence is weak. The rule is simple and it belongs in your requirement. A match is automatic only when the amount matches exactly, or when the difference sits inside a written tolerance, for example plus or minus five hundred rupees, and the reference or the beneficiary also matches. Anything outside that tolerance goes to a human queue with the reason, the amount difference, the missing reference, and the suggested match. You also need rules for partial matches, for value date differences, and for who can approve a forced match and what evidence they must record. With those rules written clearly, AI becomes a fast assistant that clears eighty percent of the work and leaves the risky twenty percent to a person. Without them, it becomes a machine that quietly books wrong matches and hides the break until month end.
