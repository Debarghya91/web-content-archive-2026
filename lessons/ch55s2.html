What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain CBPR+ after migration: what comes next for cross-border payments. CBPR+ moved cross border payments onto ISO 20022, but migration alone did not make payments faster or cheaper.

Why it matters. A business analyst should ask, for every new requirement, whether it uses ISO 20022 data to remove a manual step or only adds another field to an old process.

Chapter objective: Learner explains one leg out, CBPR+ after migration, open banking and cryptocurrencies, and applies them in a 2026-ready payment product design. Learner uses real examples with amounts, banks and message names to show how the topic works in practice.

Key learning points

  • CBPR+ moving payments onto ISO 20022, not faster or cheaper
  • Structured party records cutting screening false positives
  • Analyst question: does ISO 20022 data remove a manual step
  • Payment straight through in ISO 20022
  • Cross-border payment landscape
  • Reporting migration

Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Sanctions screening: Checking payment parties, countries and references against sanctions lists published by governments and international bodies.

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Exercise

Read the ISO 20022 message described in this lesson and identify its key parts.

Use the message structure, fields and example in this lesson.

  1. What to do. List the main blocks of the message, name the field that carries the amount, and name the field that identifies the payment.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

CBPR+ moved cross border payments onto ISO 20022, but migration alone did not make payments faster or cheaper. The real benefit comes from using the richer data that ISO 20022 carries. A pacs.008 now holds structured creditor and debtor details, a clear purpose code and detailed remittance information, so a receiving bank can post a 250,000 dollar payment without asking the customer for missing details. Banks that still treat the new message as a longer version of the old MT103 waste the investment and keep the same manual repair work they had before. The next priority is straight through processing. With better data, banks can automate more decisions, reduce repair queues and shorten the time from instruction to credit. A corporate treasurer sending 80 salary payments to five countries expects each one to settle without a phone call, and rich data makes that possible when banks align their validation rules. The work is not glamorous. It means cleaning customer master data, agreeing field usage with correspondent banks and fixing the small mapping errors that still push payments into manual repair. Sanctions screening also improves after migration, because structured names and addresses are easier to match than free text. A screening engine can compare a structured party record against a watchlist with far fewer false positives, which means fewer payments held for review and faster release for genuine customers. The benefit only arrives if banks keep the data structured all the way through. If an upstream bank converts structured data back into free text, the screening gain is lost and the downstream bank returns to guessing. CBPR+ also opens the door to new customer services. Banks can offer payment tracking, richer status updates and clearer fee breakdowns, because the message now carries the data needed to explain where a payment is and why it costs what it does. A small exporter in Mumbai sending 15,000 dollars to a buyer in Rotterdam can see each step, from debit to correspondent to final credit, instead of waiting for a vague reply from a helpdesk. That transparency becomes a competitive advantage, not just a compliance exercise. The work after migration is therefore continuous, not a single project with an end date. Banks need a roadmap that covers data quality, correspondent alignment, screening tuning and customer facing services, with owners and measures for each item. A business analyst should ask, for every new requirement, whether it uses ISO 20022 data to remove a manual step or only adds another field to an old process. The banks that answer honestly will turn a costly migration into lower operating cost, fewer exceptions and a visibly better service for corporate and retail customers alike. Worked example with numbers: a corporate treasurer sends 80 salary payments of USD 3,000 each to employees in five countries, and before migration each payment needed a phone call to confirm the beneficiary details, because the MT103 carried only a free text name and address. After migration the pacs.008 carries structured creditor details, a purpose code for salary, and the employee reference, so the receiving banks post all 80 payments without a single query, and the treasurer sees each one reach ACCC status on the same day. The saving is not the message cost, it is the 80 phone calls that no longer happen, and the operations team can measure it in hours saved per month. Failure and recovery: the benefit of richer data disappears when a bank in the chain converts the payment back to MT, because the structured data is flattened and the receiving bank loses the detail it needs to post automatically. A payment that was straight through in ISO 20022 can land in a repair queue after conversion, and the recovery is a manual repair by an operations analyst who reads the free text and re-enters the data. The bank must therefore track how many of its payments cross a conversion boundary, and must agree with its correspondents which fields survive, because a migration that is rich in the middle and poor at the ends is a migration that has not finished. A payment that fails after migration is usually a data problem, not a message problem. For a business analyst, product owner or subject matter expert, the post migration requirement must measure straight through processing, not message adoption. The requirement should set a target, such as 95 percent of cross border payments reaching ACCC without manual repair, and must name the fields that drive automation, structured addresses, purpose codes and remittance data. The roadmap must cover data quality, correspondent alignment, screening tuning and customer facing services, with an owner and a measure for each item, because a migration without measures drifts. Source: SWIFT CBPR+ benefits guide; last reviewed 7 November 2026. After the CBPR+ migration, the cross-border payment landscape has three layers. The first is the ISO 20022 messages, which are now the standard for interbank payments. The second is the SWIFT gpi service, which provides tracking, and which now covers the majority of cross-border payments by value. The third is the emerging instant cross-border systems, such as Project Nexus and the bilateral links between instant payment systems, which offer settlement in seconds rather than hours. A bank must be able to use all three, because the customer will choose the one that meets their need, and the bank that offers only one will lose the customers who need the others. The exceptions and investigations migration is the largest remaining work. The MT19x and MT29x messages, which are used for investigations, were deferred from the November 2025 cutover, and are now in the SR2026 package, with a target date of 12 June 2027. Until then, investigations continue in MT, while the payments themselves are in ISO 20022, and the bank must be able to link an MT investigation to an ISO 20022 payment, using the UETR as the common reference. The UETR is the thread that ties the investigation to the payment, and the bank's investigation system must be able to search by UETR across both formats. The reporting migration is the longest tail. The MT940, MT942 and MT950 account statements continue after November 2025, and the migration to camt.052, camt.053 and camt.054 continues to November 2028. During the coexistence, the bank must be able to receive both formats, must be able to reconcile both, and must be able to produce both for its customers. The reconciliation must handle the differences: the camt.053 carries more detail than the MT950, including the structured remittance information and the charge details, and the bank must decide whether to show the extra detail to the customer or to hide it. The migration plan must cover the full period to 2028, not just the 2025 cutover.
