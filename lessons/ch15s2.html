Key terms

Exercise

Scenario: you are calculating what a beneficiary will receive. A customer sends USD 10,000 to a supplier in India. The sending bank charges USD 25. The correspondent bank charges USD 25. The beneficiary bank charges USD 15. Calculate what the beneficiary receives under OUR, SHA and BEN. Then calculate the total cost to the sender under each option, and advise the customer which option to choose if the supplier contract requires the supplier to receive exactly USD 10,000.

Amount sent: USD 10,000. Sending bank charge: USD 25. Correspondent bank charge: USD 25. Beneficiary bank charge: USD 15. OUR means the sender pays all charges.

SHA means the sender pays the sending bank charges and the beneficiary pays the correspondent and beneficiary bank charges. BEN means the beneficiary pays all charges. The supplier contract requires the supplier to receive exactly USD 10,000.

  1. What to do. Calculate the beneficiary receipt and the total sender cost for OUR, SHA and BEN. Show the arithmetic for each. Then recommend an option and explain why.

Correct result. OUR: the beneficiary receives USD 10,000. The sender pays USD 10,000 plus USD 25 sending bank charge plus USD 25 correspondent charge plus USD 15 beneficiary bank charge, total USD 10,065. SHA: the beneficiary receives USD 10,000 minus USD 25 correspondent charge minus USD 15 beneficiary bank charge, total USD 9,960. The sender pays USD 10,000 plus USD 25 sending bank charge, total USD 10,025. BEN: the beneficiary receives USD 10,000 minus USD 25 sending bank charge minus USD 25 correspondent charge minus USD 15 beneficiary bank charge, total USD 9,935.

The sender pays USD 10,000. Recommendation: use OUR, because the supplier contract requires the supplier to receive exactly USD 10,000, and only OUR guarantees that. The sender pays USD 10,065 in total, which is USD 40 more than SHA and USD 65 more than BEN, but the contract requirement is met. Worked reasoning: under SHA, the correspondent and beneficiary bank charges are deducted from the amount as it travels, so the beneficiary receives less. Under BEN, all three charges are deducted, so the beneficiary receives the least.

Under OUR, the sender pays all charges separately, so the full amount reaches the beneficiary.

Why. Hint 1: work out who pays each of the three charges under each option, then subtract the beneficiary-paid charges from the amount. Hint 2: the total cost to the sender is the amount plus the sender-paid charges. Hint 3: if the contract requires an exact receipt, only OUR works. The worked explanation shows the arithmetic discipline: list each charge, assign it to sender or beneficiary under each option, then do the subtraction.

Consider a payment of ten thousand US dollars from a company in India to a supplier in Germany, where the sending bank charges twenty five dollars, the intermediary bank charges fifteen dollars, and the receiving bank charges ten dollars. If the charge arrangement is OUR, the sender pays twenty five dollars to the sending bank, and the sending bank pays the intermediary and receiving banks, so the supplier receives exactly ten thousand dollars. The total cost to the sender is ten thousand dollars plus the charges, which in this example is ten thousand and fifty dollars if the sending bank absorbs the intermediary and receiving charges into its own fee, or ten thousand dollars plus twenty five dollars plus the separate charges, depending on how the bank prices the service.

The key point is that the beneficiary receives the full ten thousand dollars. If the same payment is sent with SHA, the sender pays the twenty five dollar charge of the sending bank, and the intermediary bank deducts fifteen dollars and the receiving bank deducts ten dollars from the amount in transit. The supplier receives ten thousand dollars less fifteen dollars less ten dollars, which is nine thousand nine hundred and seventy five dollars. The sender has paid ten thousand and twenty five dollars in total, and the supplier has received nine thousand nine hundred and seventy five dollars, so twenty five dollars has been lost to charges in the chain, and the supplier is twenty five dollars short of the invoice amount.

This is why SHA is a problem when the invoice must be settled in full. The supplier will ask for the missing twenty five dollars, and the buyer will have to send a second payment, which will incur more charges. If the payment is sent with BEN, every bank deducts its charge from the amount. The sending bank deducts twenty five dollars, the intermediary deducts fifteen dollars, and the receiving bank deducts ten dollars, so the supplier receives ten thousand dollars less fifty dollars, which is nine thousand nine hundred and fifty dollars.

The sender pays exactly ten thousand dollars, and the beneficiary bears all fifty dollars of charges. BEN is the cheapest arrangement for the sender and the most expensive for the beneficiary, and it is only fair when the beneficiary has agreed to it in advance. A business analyst who prepares a worked example like this for a customer or for a training session makes the abstract codes concrete, and the customer can then make an informed choice instead of discovering the deduction when the supplier complains.
