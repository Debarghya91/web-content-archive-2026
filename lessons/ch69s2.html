Fiat money

What money is, Lesson 2 of 6, Narration 6:41, D01 Foundations of money and payments

Note saved.

SettlementValue dateISO 20022

What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain fiat money. Fiat money is money whose value rests on trust and on law rather than on a commodity backing it.

Why it matters. Takeaway: fiat money is declared by the state, issued by the central bank, and trusted because it settles taxes, debts and central bank settlement.

Chapter objective: Learner explains the four functions of money and why a payment can succeed on one function and fail on another. Learner distinguishes fiat money, central bank money, commercial bank money and e-money as a claim, reads a bank balance sheet, and explains how the form of money changes the speed, finality, data and risk of a payment.

Key learning points

  • Fiat money definition
  • Physical and digital fiat
  • Deposit protection limit
  • Inflation and loss of trust
  • Stablecoin, claim on issuer
  • Judging new money against fiat

Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Exercise

A customer holds EUR 100,000 in cash and EUR 100,000 in a deposit at Bank A, and Bank A fails. Work out what happens to each holding.

Use the deposit guarantee limit of EUR 100,000 per depositor per bank under the EU Deposit Guarantee Schemes Directive.

  1. What to do. State what happens to the cash, what happens to the deposit, and why the two are different.

Correct result. The EUR 100,000 cash is unaffected because it is fiat held directly; the EUR 100,000 deposit is a claim on a failed bank, recovered only up to the EUR 100,000 guarantee limit through the scheme payout process.

Why. Cash is a direct claim on the central bank, while a deposit is a claim on the commercial bank, so the two fail differently.

Fiat money is money whose value rests on trust and on law rather than on a commodity backing it. A euro banknote cannot be exchanged at the central bank for a fixed weight of gold, and a pound sterling balance is not a claim to silver. Its value comes from the fact that the state accepts it for taxes, the central bank issues it, courts enforce debts written in it, and the public accepts it in exchange for goods and services. The word fiat comes from the Latin for let it be made, and the point is that the money exists because the issuing authority declares it and the economy accepts it. Source: Bank of England, What is money; European Central Bank, What is money. Fiat money exists in two forms, and the distinction drives payment design. Physical fiat is notes and coins, issued by the central bank and held directly by the public, and it settles a payment the moment it changes hands, with no intermediary and no ledger entry. Digital fiat is the balance in a bank account or in a central bank reserve account, and it moves only by a ledger entry, which is why every electronic payment is a message plus a posting. A customer who withdraws EUR 500 in cash converts digital fiat into physical fiat, and a customer who deposits EUR 500 does the reverse. The payment system must handle both forms and the conversion between them, including cash handling, ATM replenishment and branch vault limits. The parties are the central bank as issuer, commercial banks as distributors, and the public and businesses as holders. The central bank controls the supply through monetary policy, interest rates and open market operations, and it is the lender of last resort to banks. Commercial banks distribute fiat through accounts, cards and payment services, and they create commercial bank money when they lend, which is a claim on the bank rather than fiat itself. The holder of fiat, whether in cash or in a central bank account, holds a direct claim on the central bank. The holder of a commercial bank deposit holds a claim on the commercial bank, and that difference is the single most important fact in this lesson. A worked example shows the difference in a crisis. A customer holds EUR 100,000 in cash and EUR 100,000 in a deposit at Bank A. If Bank A fails, the cash is unaffected, because it is fiat held directly, while the deposit is a claim on a failed bank and is recovered only through deposit protection, up to the scheme limit, and after a process. In the euro area the Deposit Guarantee Schemes Directive protects deposits up to EUR 100,000 per depositor per bank, so the customer recovers the deposit in full in this example, but only up to that limit and only after the scheme pays out. A business analyst must never describe a deposit as risk free, because the protection has a limit, a scope and a payout process, and the customer communication must say so. Failure of fiat money takes two familiar forms, and both appear in payment operations. Central banks target low and stable inflation, and the European Central Bank targets 2 percent over the medium term, so a payment product that holds customer funds for weeks must consider inflation only at the margin, while a product that holds them for years must consider it seriously. The second is a loss of trust, where holders refuse the currency and demand foreign currency, goods or gold instead. In that case the payment system still processes instructions, but the money itself is rejected, and no message standard can fix it. The effect on the bank, the customer and operations follows from who issues the money. For the customer, fiat in cash is final and anonymous, while fiat in an account is traceable, reversible only through a formal process, and subject to holds, freezes and screening. For the bank, holding fiat as reserves at the central bank is the safest asset on its balance sheet, and it is the asset used to settle with other banks, which is why central bank reserves are the settlement asset for high value systems such as T2 and Fedwire. For operations, a payment in fiat through a central bank system settles with finality in central bank money, while a payment in commercial bank money settles only when the interbank leg completes, and the value date and the settlement date can differ. For a business analyst, product owner or subject matter expert, fiat money is the baseline against which every new form of money must be judged. When a vendor proposes a stablecoin, a tokenised deposit or a central bank digital currency, the first questions are whether it is fiat, a claim on fiat, or a claim on something else, who the issuer is, whether it is legal tender, and what settles in the end. A stablecoin is a claim on its issuer and its reserves, not fiat itself, and its value depends on the quality and the audit of those reserves. Writing these distinctions into the product definition prevents a stablecoin from being sold to customers as if it were central bank money. Fiat money also explains why payment systems are built in layers. The central bank layer moves fiat between banks and settles with finality. The commercial bank layer moves claims on banks between customers and settles through the central bank layer. The scheme layer, such as SEPA or Faster Payments, sets the rules, the messages and the timing for moving those claims. A payment that looks like one movement to the customer is therefore three movements in the system: a customer instruction, a bank to bank message, and a central bank settlement. The ISO 20022 pacs.008 message carries the customer instruction between banks, and the settlement system, such as T2, moves the fiat that makes it final. Confusing the layers is the most common design error in payment products. Takeaway: fiat money is declared by the state, issued by the central bank, and trusted because it settles taxes, debts and central bank settlement. Physical fiat settles on handover, digital fiat settles by ledger entry in central bank money, and a commercial bank deposit is a claim on the bank, not fiat itself, protected only up to the deposit guarantee limit. Judge every new money proposal against fiat on issuer, legal tender status, settlement asset and failure. Further reading: Bank of England, What is money; European Central Bank, What is money; Bank for International Settlements, Committee on Payments and Market Infrastructures, Central bank digital currencies.
