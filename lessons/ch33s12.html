Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Clearing: The process of exchanging payment instructions between banks and calculating what each bank owes the others.

FX: Foreign exchange, the conversion of one currency into another at an agreed exchange rate. FX is needed when a payment is instructed in one currency and settled or credited in another.

Exercise

Work out the net settlement position for the clearing cycle described in this lesson.

Use the clearing rules and the netting logic in this lesson.

  1. What to do. List the payments in the cycle, calculate the net position for each bank, and state what settles.

Correct result. Clearing validates, sorts, matches and nets the instructions, then produces a net position per bank. Only the net position settles, which needs far less liquidity than settling every payment individually.

Why. Netting is what makes high volume payments possible. Without it, every payment would need full liquidity at the moment it is sent.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

A central counterparty, a CCP, stands between buyers and sellers in financial markets, guaranteeing settlement even if one side defaults. In India, that role is played by the Clearing Corporation of India, CCIL. CCIL clears and settles trades in government securities, foreign exchange, money market instruments, and rupee interest rate derivatives. It is the infrastructure that lets Indian banks trade with each other without worrying, trade by trade, whether the other side will perform. CCIL guarantees the settlement, and the guarantee is backed by margin, a default fund, and rigorous risk management.

CCIL clears several markets. Government securities: outright trades and repo transactions in central and state government securities, traded on the NDS-OM platform. Foreign exchange: US dollar and Indian rupee spot and forward trades, traded on the FX-CLEAR platform. Money market: repo and triparty repo transactions, through the TREPS and CROMS platforms, and call money. Rupee interest rate derivatives: interest rate swaps and forward rate agreements. Each market has its own clearing rules, margin requirements, and settlement timetable, and CCIL operates the clearing for all of them under one risk framework.

The CCP mechanism is novation. When two members trade, CCIL steps in between them: it becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer. The original bilateral contract is replaced by two contracts, each with CCIL. The members no longer face each other; they face CCIL. If a member defaults, CCIL completes the settlement to the surviving members, using the defaulter margin, the default fund, and its own resources, in a defined waterfall. Novation concentrates risk in the CCP, and the CCP manages it with margin, monitoring, and default procedures that no bilateral arrangement could match.

Margin is the first line of defence. Members post initial margin, covering the potential future exposure of their positions, and variation margin, covering the daily mark to market movement. Margin is collected daily, and intraday when markets move sharply. The default fund, contributed by members, absorbs losses beyond the defaulter margin. CCIL own capital sits further down the waterfall. The waterfall order is published and tested: members know exactly how a default would be handled, and the certainty is part of the guarantee. Stress testing, which simulates extreme market moves and member defaults, validates that the resources are enough.

Settlement through CCIL is guaranteed and final. The funds leg settles in central bank money, through the Reserve Bank of India. The securities leg settles through the Subsidiary General Ledger, the SGL, for government securities. Foreign exchange settlement follows payment versus payment principles, reducing Herstatt risk. The guarantee means that a member that has performed its side of a trade receives its funds or securities even if its counterparty fails. That certainty is what lets the markets trade at scale: participants do not need to assess each counterparty on each trade, because CCIL stands in the middle.

For a business analyst, CCIL teaches the CCP pattern that applies well beyond India. Novation, margin, default waterfall, and guaranteed settlement are the same concepts that govern CCPs in every market, and the same concepts that shape payment system design: centralised risk management, prefunded resources, and defined default procedures. CCIL is also a reminder that payment and settlement infrastructure is national as well as global: India built its own CCP, its own trading platforms, and its own settlement arrangements, and the analyst working on Indian markets must understand them as a system, not as isolated products.
