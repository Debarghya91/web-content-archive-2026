Key terms

Authorisation: Approval by the issuer for a card transaction to proceed, confirming the card is valid and funds or credit are available.

E-mandate: An electronic mandate for direct debit, created and signed online by the debtor through a secure channel, such as internet banking.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

  1. What to do. List the main blocks of the message, name the field that carries the amount, and name the field that identifies the payment.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

An e-mandate is a mandate that the debtor signs electronically, usually on the creditor website, and it is validated by the debtor bank during the signing process. Jonas Weber goes to the Stadtwerke Linden website, enters his IBAN and his name, and is redirected to his own bank, Meridian Bank Munich, to authenticate himself. He logs in with his normal online banking credentials, confirms the mandate details on the bank screen, and is redirected back to the utility website. The mandate is then created with a digital record of the authentication, and the creditor stores it as a valid mandate under the SDD scheme rules.

The e-mandate flow uses ISO 20022 messages to carry the mandate information between the creditor, the debtor bank, and the service providers involved. The mandate initiation and the mandate acceptance are exchanged as pain.009 and pain.010 messages, while the mandate amendment and cancellation use pain.011 and pain.012 messages. The pain.009 carries the mandate request, including the debtor, the creditor, the account, and the collection details. The pain.010 carries the acceptance, confirming that the debtor bank has validated the mandate and that the debtor has authenticated the instruction.

The debtor bank plays an active role in the e-mandate flow, and that role is what makes the electronic signature trustworthy. Meridian Bank Munich authenticates Jonas Weber, checks that the account he named belongs to him, and confirms that the mandate details match the account. It then returns a confirmation that the mandate has been accepted, and it records the mandate reference SWL-JW-2026-0417 against the account. If Jonas Weber had named an account that did not belong to him, the bank would have refused the mandate, and the utility would never have received a valid authorisation to collect.

The e-mandate service also handles amendments and cancellations through the same message family, so the mandate stays current without paper forms. If Jonas Weber changes his bank account, he can amend the mandate through an online flow, and the amendment travels as a pain.011 message to the creditor and to the debtor bank. If he cancels the mandate, the cancellation travels as a pain.012 message, and the creditor must stop collecting from the next due date. The creditor must check the mandate status before each collection, because a collection sent against a cancelled mandate will be rejected by the debtor bank and may generate a complaint.
