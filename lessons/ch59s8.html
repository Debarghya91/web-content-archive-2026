Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Liquidity: Cash or assets that a bank can use immediately to meet payment obligations. Banks manage liquidity by forecasting inflows and outflows, funding nostro and settlement accounts, and using central bank...

Clearing: The process of exchanging payment instructions between banks and calculating what each bank owes the others.

  1. What to do. Name the model, state when settlement becomes final, and explain what happens if the sending bank does not have enough liquidity.

Correct result. Real time gross settlement settles each payment individually in central bank money with immediate finality, but it needs liquidity for every payment. Deferred net settlement nets payments and settles the net position, which needs less liquidity but leaves settlement risk until the net settles.

Why. Settlement is where money actually moves. The model decides the risk, the liquidity need, and when the payment is truly final.

Show transcript

RT1 is the instant payment system operated by EBA Clearing for SCT Inst payments in euro, and it clears and settles those payments between banks across Europe around the clock. SCT Inst is the scheme rulebook for instant credit transfers in euro, with a maximum execution time of ten seconds from instruction to funds available for the payee. The other main settlement route is TIPS, the Eurosystem instant settlement service, so RT1 is best understood as the private sector alternative to a central bank operated service. From the customer point of view the two routes are indistinguishable, because the money arrives in seconds either way.

Settlement in RT1 runs on prefunded positions held in T2, with each participating bank funding its position before it sends instant payments. RT1 validates each payment, forwards it to the payee bank, and settles the payment between the prefunded positions of the two banks once the payee bank accepts, so settlement is final and in central bank money. Participants monitor their prefunded balance continuously and top it up when it runs low, because a bank whose position is empty cannot send further instant payments until it adds funds. A bank expecting EUR 2,000,000 of outgoing instant payments on a busy morning must fund its position before the payments begin.

Participation in RT1 suits banks that offer SCT Inst payments and can fund and manage a prefunded position alongside their other clearing activity. A bank that already clears other payments with EBA Clearing can add RT1 through that existing relationship, with one operator, one set of scheme rules, and one prefunded position to manage. Smaller banks gain a route into instant payments without building a direct connection to TIPS, because RT1 provides clearing and settlement in one place. Participation still demands operational readiness, because the ten second deadline applies every hour of every day, including weekends and public holidays.

The difference between RT1 and TIPS lies in who operates the service and how settlement is funded, rather than in what the customer experiences. TIPS is operated by the Eurosystem and settles each payment directly in central bank money on the TIPS account of the participant, while RT1 is a commercial service operated by EBA Clearing and settles on prefunded positions held in T2. TIPS acts as a settlement engine, while RT1 acts as a clearing and settlement system in one, because it routes the messages between the banks and settles the funds between their positions. Both routes carry SCT Inst payments, both must meet the ten second deadline, and both settle finally in central bank money.

The choice between RT1 and TIPS turns on reach, existing clearing relationships, liquidity arrangements, and cost, and many banks connect to both routes rather than choosing only one. A bank with a long clearing relationship with EBA Clearing may start with RT1, because the operator, the rules, and the funding pattern are already familiar to its operations team. A larger bank may connect to both, so it can reach every SCT Inst counterparty and keep an alternative route if one service is unavailable. Alpine Bank, a fictional mid-sized bank, might weigh the cost of funding two positions against the reach gained, and the business analyst must record that trade-off for each corridor.

Timeout and reject handling follows the SCT Inst scheme on both RT1 and TIPS, so the customer outcome is the same whichever system carries the payment. If the payee bank does not respond within the time limit, the payment is treated as timed out and is not settled, and if the payee bank rejects the payment because the account is closed, a pacs.002 status report carries the reason back to the payer bank. The payer account is not debited for a payment that was never settled, or it is credited back if a debit was already applied, and the payer bank must tell its customer clearly and quickly that the payment failed and why. The payer bank can also send a recall request if a payment was sent in error, and the payee bank must respond within the scheme deadline.
