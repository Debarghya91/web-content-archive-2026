An audit trail is the record that lets you explain, after the fact, why an AI system did what it did with money. Without it, you have an outcome and no evidence, and that is not enough for a customer, a manager, or an auditor. For every AI decision that touches a payment, record the same set of facts. What the input was, what data the model used, what the model recommended, how confident it was, what the human did, if a human was involved, and what the final outcome was.

Take a payment of 32,000 pounds that was held and then released after review. The trail should show the payment details, the risk score, the reason for the hold, the data the model looked at, the name of the person who released it, the time of the release, and the evidence they saw.

Explainability is the other half. It is not a technical diagram of the model. It is a plain-language answer to a plain question. Why was this payment held, why was that one released, and why was this one treated differently from a similar one last week. The explanation must be understandable to the person who has to act on it, and to the person who has to check it later.

An auditor will ask for evidence in a particular way. They will pick a sample of payments, ask to see the trail for each one, and check whether the control was followed, whether the limit was respected, and whether the approval was real. Build the trail into the product from the start, keep it in a form that cannot be changed after the fact, and test it by picking a real payment and trying to reconstruct the decision from the record alone.
