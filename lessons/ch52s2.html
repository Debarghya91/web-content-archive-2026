What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain MT910 confirmation of credit. The MT910 Confirmation of Credit is sent by an account servicing bank to its account holder to advise that a specified amount has been credited to a specified account.

Why it matters. If the MT910 arrives but the statement line is missing, the operations team must raise a query with the bank the same day.

Chapter objective: Learner explains MT940 and MT950 statements, MT910 confirmations, and how opening, closing booked, closing available and forward balances are calculated. Learner uses real examples with amounts, banks and message names to show how the topic works in practice.

Key learning points

  • MT910 Confirmation of Credit
  • Single credit advice
  • Field 50A or 50K
  • Without Field 21 and Field 50K
  • Automatic MT910 for credits above the threshold, EUR 100,000
  • Credit still appearing on the MT940 for matching

Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

The MT910 Confirmation of Credit is sent by an account servicing bank to its account holder to advise that a specified amount has been credited to a specified account. The sender is the bank that holds the account, for example Barclays in London, and the receiver is the customer, for example a UK importer whose euro account has just received EUR 180,000 from a customer in France. The message is sent when the credit is posted, not when the payment instruction is received, so the receiver can rely on the funds being in the account. It is a single credit advice, not a statement of all activity. The MT910 carries a compact set of fields that identify the credit precisely. Field 20 gives the transaction reference number assigned by the sender. Field 21 gives the related reference, usually the reference of the original payment instruction, such as the MT103 reference that caused the credit. Field 25 gives the account number that was credited. Field 32A gives the value date, the currency and the amount credited, for example 4 November 2026, EUR 180,000. Field 50A or 50K gives the ordering customer, and Field 52A gives the ordering institution, so the receiver can see who sent the money. A typical MT910 might read as follows for a real trade receipt. Field 20 reads CREDIT 2026 1188. Field 21 reads pacs.008 REF FR 99214. Field 25 reads GB29 BARC 2030 0488 1234 56. Field 32A reads 4 November 2026, EUR 180,000. Field 50K reads Lefevre Textiles SA, Lyon, France, and Field 52A reads BNP Paribas, Paris. The London importer can now match EUR 180,000 to the Lefevre invoice for EUR 180,000, mark that invoice as paid, and release the goods held against it. Without Field 21 and Field 50K, the treasurer would have to guess which invoice the credit belongs to. The MT910 is used when a customer needs immediate confirmation of a single credit, rather than waiting for the end of day statement. A supplier who will only release goods on proof of payment may ask the buyer bank to send an MT910 as soon as the EUR 250,000 is credited. A treasury team managing a large acquisition may ask for MT910 advices on each incoming tranche, so it can track the funding build up during the day. A bank may also send an MT910 automatically for credits above a threshold, such as EUR 100,000, while smaller credits appear only on the statement. The arrangement is agreed in the account terms. The MT910 has a counterpart, the MT900 Confirmation of Debit, which advises that a specified amount has been debited from an account. Together the two messages give real time confirmation of single entries, while the MT940 and MT950 give the full statement picture. A treasurer should treat an MT910 as a notification, not as a substitute for reconciliation. The credit must still appear on the MT940 statement for the same value date, with the same amount and reference, and the reconciliation process must match the two records. If the MT910 arrives but the statement line is missing, the operations team must raise a query with the bank the same day.
