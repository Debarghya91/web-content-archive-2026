What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain inbound and outbound liquidity transfers via transit accounts. A transit account in TIPS is a technical account that sits between TIPS and TARGET, and it is used to move liquidity between the two services.

Why it matters. This matters because the rail decides how fast the money moves, what it costs, when it settles and what can go wrong. Choosing the wrong rail is a product and an operations failure.

Chapter objective: Learner explains TIPS instant payments and liquidity transfers, describes SCT Inst, Non-Euro and SIP flows, and distinguishes TIPS DCA, AS Technical Account and Transit Accounts. Learner uses real amounts, banks and message names to show how instant payments and liquidity transfers work.

Key learning points

  • Transit account in TIPS
  • One Transit Account per settlement currency in TIPS
  • Euro Transit Account belongs
  • Transit account
  • Transit account reversal
  • TIPS AS Technical Accounts cannot

Key terms

Liquidity transfer: A movement of funds between accounts held by the same participant to manage liquidity, for example from a main cash account to a dedicated cash account.

Transit account: An internal account used to hold funds temporarily while they move between systems or accounts. Transit accounts record the movement of money during settlement, currency conversion or internal...

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Practise this lesson

Exercise

A bank moves EUR 500,000 from its TARGET main cash account to its TIPS DCA and later moves funds back. Explain the role of the transit account, how many transactions each transfer is, and what happens if the second transaction fails.

Use the transit account rules in this lesson: one per currency, ECB ownership, two transactions, and the TIPS DCA requirement.

  1. What to do. State the role of the transit account, how many transactions the transfer is, who owns the euro transit account, and which account can be the TIPS side.

Correct result. When a bank moves EUR 500,000 from its TARGET main cash account to its TIPS DCA, the funds pass through the euro transit account on their way, and when it moves funds back, they pass through the same account in the opposite direction. There is only one Transit Account per settlement currency in TIPS, and the euro Transit Account belongs to the European Central Bank. Each liquidity transfer through a transit account is at least two separate transactions, not one. Both transactions must settle for the transfer to complete, and if the second transaction fails, the first must be reversed, so the funds do not sit stranded in the transit account. Only a TIPS DCA can be the TIPS side of an inbound or outbound transfer.

Why. A transit account is a technical account, not a place to hold money. A transfer that leaves funds stranded in it has failed, and the reversal rule is what keeps the architecture auditable.

A transit account in TIPS is a technical account that sits between TIPS and TARGET, and it is used to move liquidity between the two services. When a bank moves EUR 500,000 from its TARGET main cash account to its TIPS DCA, the funds pass through the euro transit account on their way, and when it moves funds back, they pass through the same account in the opposite direction. The transit account is not a customer account, and it does not hold liquidity overnight as a bank would. It is a pass through account that exists so that the two services can settle the movement cleanly, with each service recording its own leg of the transfer in its own books. There is only one Transit Account per settlement currency in TIPS, and that rule keeps the architecture simple and auditable. All euro liquidity transfers between TARGET and TIPS pass through the single euro transit account, and all Swedish krona transfers pass through the single krona transit account. That single point of passage means the Eurosystem can reconcile every liquidity movement for a currency in one place, and it means there is no confusion about which transit account a transfer should use. A bank that instructs a euro liquidity transfer does not need to choose a transit account, because there is only one, and TIPS routes the transfer through it automatically. The euro Transit Account belongs to the European Central Bank, known as the ECB, and that ownership matters for the legal and operational structure of TIPS. The ECB, as the owner of the euro transit account, is the central bank that stands behind the settlement of euro liquidity transfers between TARGET and TIPS. When funds move through the euro transit account, they move through an account owned by the ECB, which gives the transfer the backing of central bank money at every step. For other currencies, the transit account belongs to the relevant central bank for that currency, so the same principle holds. The transit account is owned by the central bank that issues the currency. Each liquidity transfer through a transit account is at least two separate transactions, not one, and that is a point that surprises many learners. The first transaction moves the funds from the source account to the transit account, and the second transaction moves the funds from the transit account to the destination account. For an inbound transfer of EUR 500,000, the first transaction debits the TARGET main cash account and credits the euro transit account, and the second transaction debits the transit account and credits the TIPS DCA. Both transactions must settle for the transfer to complete, and if the second transaction fails, the first must be reversed, so the funds do not sit stranded in the transit account. An Inbound Liquidity Transfer, which moves funds from TARGET into TIPS, can only be triggered in RTGS, because the source account is a TARGET main cash account and only RTGS can debit it, while an Outbound Liquidity Transfer is triggered in TIPS, because the source account is a TIPS DCA. A bank that tries to trigger an inbound transfer from inside TIPS will find the instruction rejected. There is one more restriction. TIPS AS Technical Accounts cannot be used in Inbound or Outbound Liquidity Transfers, because those accounts are for the technical settlement of ancillary system transactions, not for funding TIPS liquidity, so only a TIPS DCA can be the TIPS side of an inbound or outbound transfer.
