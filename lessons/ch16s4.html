Key terms

Cover payment: A payment method where the customer instruction travels one route while the funds travel separately as a bank to bank cover message.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

camt.056: An ISO 20022 message used to request cancellation of a payment instruction that was already sent. It is used for recalls, where the sender asks the receiving bank to return the funds.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

A recall, also called a payment recall or a request for recall, happens when the sending bank asks the receiving bank to return a payment that has already been credited to the beneficiary, usually because the payment was sent by mistake, was sent to the wrong beneficiary, was sent for the wrong amount, or was sent as a result of fraud. The sending bank sends a recall message, using a SWIFT MT192 request for cancellation or a pacs.007 payment cancellation request in ISO 20022, which carries the original reference, the UETR, the amount, and the reason for the recall. The recall is not guaranteed to succeed, and the analyst must make that clear to the customer.

If the beneficiary has already withdrawn the funds, there is nothing to recall, and the sending bank cannot recover the money through the payment system. If the beneficiary disputes the recall, the receiving bank may refuse, and the sender must pursue the matter through legal channels or through negotiation with the beneficiary. If the recall is for fraud, the receiving bank may be able to freeze the account and return the funds, but only if it acts quickly and only if the funds are still there.

The time factor is critical. A recall sent within hours has a good chance of success, a recall sent after days has a poor chance, and a recall sent after weeks is usually futile. Recalls also have a cost, and the question of who pays is part of the design. The sending bank may charge a fee for processing the recall, the receiving bank may charge a fee, and the intermediary banks may charge for handling the recall message. If the recall succeeds, the funds are returned less any charges.

If the recall fails, the customer may have paid the recall fee and still not recovered the money. The analyst must include the recall fee, the timeline, and the likelihood of success in the customer communication, so that the customer can make an informed decision about whether to request a recall. A customer who is told that a recall is free and certain will be disappointed; a customer who is told that a recall costs a fee, takes days, and may not succeed will make a better decision and will trust the bank more.
