What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain TIPS as a CSM for instant payments and apply it to a real banking scenario.

Why it matters. TIPS liquidity is a weekend problem, and the forecast must be right on Friday.

Chapter objective: Learner explains what a clearing and settlement mechanism is, how STEP2, national CSMs and TIPS clear and settle payments, how a bank chooses a CSM, and how payments route when debtor and creditor banks use different CSMs, with correct terminology.

Key terms

Liquidity transfer: A movement of funds between accounts held by the same participant to manage liquidity, for example from a main cash account to a dedicated cash account.

Irrevocable: A payment or instruction that cannot be cancelled or reversed once it has been made, except by agreement of all parties or by a court order.

Payment hub: A central system in a bank that receives payment instructions from all channels, validates them, applies screening and routing rules, and sends them to the correct clearing system or correspondent.

TIPS, which stands for TARGET Instant Payment Settlement, is the Eurosystem platform that settles instant payments in central bank money, in real time, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year. It is operated by the Eurosystem and it is the CSM for SCT Inst, the SEPA instant credit transfer scheme. Where STEP2 batches payments and settles them in net cycles, TIPS settles each payment individually and immediately. That difference is the whole point of instant payments: the creditor is paid in seconds, not hours. The flow is short and strict. The debtor bank sends a pacs.008 to TIPS. TIPS validates the message and immediately reserves the amount on the debtor bank's TIPS account, which is called a TIPS dedicated cash account. TIPS then forwards the payment to the creditor bank. The creditor bank must respond with a pacs.002, confirming acceptance or rejecting the payment. When the positive confirmation arrives, TIPS moves the reserved amount from the debtor bank's TIPS account to the creditor bank's TIPS account. That movement is settlement in central bank money, and it is final and irrevocable. The entire round trip must complete within 10 seconds under the SCT Inst rulebook, and the Instant Payments Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2024/886, makes that a legal expectation. Funding is the operational heart of TIPS. A TIPS account is funded from the bank's TARGET2, now T2, account through a liquidity transfer, a camt.050 message. The bank moves money from T2 to TIPS at the start of the day, and tops it up during the day as instant payments drain it. Because TIPS runs 24 hours a day, including weekends and holidays when T2 is closed, the bank cannot rely on intraday funding on a Saturday night. It must forecast weekend instant payment volumes on Friday and prefund the TIPS account before the weekend. A bank that underfunds on a Friday evening will start rejecting instant payments on Saturday morning. The 10-second rule has hard consequences. If the creditor bank does not respond within the timeout, TIPS rejects the payment and releases the reservation, and the debtor bank must tell its customer the payment failed. If the creditor bank responds late, after TIPS has already timed out, the response is ignored and the payment is treated as failed, which can create a duplicate if the creditor bank already credited the customer. The payment hub must handle the timeout as a first-class outcome, not as an error, and must be able to investigate a payment whose status is uncertain. That investigation uses a pacs.028 status request. Verification of Payee, known as VoP, is mandatory for instant payments under the Instant Payments Regulation from 9 October 2025. Before the debtor bank sends the payment, the creditor payment service provider must check that the payee name supplied by the payer matches the name on the creditor account. If it does not match, the payer is warned and can still proceed, but the warning must be recorded. The VoP check adds a step before the 10-second clock starts, and the bank must design its flow so that the check does not eat into the settlement window. A worked example shows the timing. A customer sends 250 euro at 14:00:00 on a Sunday. The debtor bank sends the pacs.008 to TIPS at 14:00:01. TIPS reserves 250 euro and forwards the payment at 14:00:02. The creditor bank confirms at 14:00:04. TIPS settles at 14:00:05. The creditor customer sees the money at 14:00:06. Six seconds from instruction to credit, on a Sunday, when the batch clearing houses are closed. That is what instant settlement means. Failure modes are specific to real-time settlement. Insufficient liquidity on the TIPS account is the most common: the payment is rejected immediately, and the bank must top up the account. A creditor bank that is unreachable, because its own system is down, causes timeouts, and the debtor bank must decide whether to retry or to offer the customer a non-instant payment instead. A duplicate pacs.008, sent twice because the first send timed out at the network level, must be detected by TIPS using the message identification, or the creditor will be paid twice. For a business analyst, TIPS is a set of requirements about liquidity forecasting, timeout handling, VoP integration, and duplicate detection. The acceptance criteria must include a test where a payment settles within 10 seconds, a test where the creditor bank times out and the payment is rejected with the reservation released, and a test where the TIPS account runs out of funds over a weekend and the top-up process restores service.
