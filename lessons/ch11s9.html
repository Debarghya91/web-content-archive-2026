Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Charge bearer: A code that states who pays the charges for a payment. In ISO 20022 the values are DEBT for debtor, CRED for creditor, SHAR for shared and SLEV for service level.

Purpose code: A code that states the commercial reason for a payment, such as supplier payment, salary or goods. Purpose codes are taken from an ISO external code list and support regulatory reporting, screening...

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

All SEPA messages are based on ISO 20022, using XML syntax. The European Payments Council publishes three main documents for each scheme, the rulebook, the Customer-to-Bank Implementation Guidelines, and the Interbank Implementation Guidelines. New versions are usually published every year in November. The messages flow in two spaces. The customer-to-bank space is where customers and banks exchange messages.

The interbank space is where banks exchange messages among themselves, directly or through a clearing and settlement mechanism. In the customer-to-bank space, messages sent from customer to bank are orders or instructions. Messages sent from bank to customer are reporting. The bank informs the customer about the execution of its payments. In the interbank space, banks exchange messages directly or through a CSM such as EBA STEP2.

The message names all begin with four letters inherited from ISO 20022. PAIN stands for Payment Initiation. CAMT stands for Cash Management. PACS stands for Payment Clearing and Settlement. Pain messages are used exclusively in the customer-to-bank space.

A customer sends pain.001, Customer Credit Transfer Initiation, to instruct its bank to make a credit transfer. The bank replies with pain.002, Customer Payment Status Report, to report whether the instruction was accepted, rejected, or completed. Pain messages are never used between banks. Camt messages appear in both spaces. In the customer-to-bank space, camt messages inform the customer about its cash position as a result of executed instructions or received payments.

Camt.052 is the Bank to Customer Account Report for intraday positions. Camt.053 is the Bank to Customer Statement for end of day positions. Camt.054 is the Bank to Customer Debit Credit Notification for a single booking. Customers can also send camt messages to cancel orders. In the interbank space, camt messages are used to cancel orders or transactions and to reply to cancellation requests, for example camt.056, FI to FI Payment Cancellation Request, and camt.029, Resolution of Investigation.

Pacs messages are used exclusively in the interbank space. They transfer money from one financial institution to another. Pacs.008, FI to FI Customer Credit Transfer, is the SCT interbank message. Pacs.009, Financial Institution Credit Transfer, is the bank to bank message. The numbers after the name carry meaning.

In pacs.008.001.02, 008 is the message number, 001 is the variant, and 02 is the version. Only a subset of ISO 20022 message elements is allowed in SEPA messages, and SEPA rules further restrict some fields. For example, a party name can have up to 140 characters under ISO 20022 rules, but in SEPA only 70 characters are allowed. These differences are highlighted in the Implementation Guidelines for each scheme.
