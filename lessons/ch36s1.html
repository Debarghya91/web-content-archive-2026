Payment Hub Architecture Inside a Bank

Payment hubSettlement

What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain payment hub architecture inside a bank. A payment hub is the central processing engine that receives payment instructions from every channel in a bank and routes each one to the correct destination.

Why it matters. This matters because the architecture decides what the bank can build, how fast it can change, and where a payment can fail. Most payment requirements live in the architecture.

Chapter objective: Learner applies system design to payments, covering throughput, latency, idempotency, exactly-once processing, and failure recovery, and designs a system for a stated load. Learner practises this skill on realistic banking cases, explaining the reasoning clearly and using correct terminology throughout the work.

Key terms

Payment hub: A central system in a bank that receives payment instructions from all channels, validates them, applies screening and routing rules, and sends them to the correct clearing system or correspondent.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Practise this lesson

Exercise

Read ch48s1 Hub components: what a hub is and what it contains. Then list the five hub layers and name one component in each.

ch48s1 covers channels, gateway, hub core, rails and settlement. This lesson is a pointer only.

  1. What to do. Write the five layers in order, with one component and its job in each layer.

Correct result. Channels: online banking captures the instruction. Gateway: validation and enrichment convert the instruction to the hub canonical model. Hub core: routing, screening, limits and value dating. Rails: T2, SEPA, Faster Payments, CHAPS, SWIFT. Settlement: central bank accounts move the funds.

Why. A hub is a layered platform, not a single box. Each layer has one job, and a payment that breaks is found by asking which layer it stopped in.

Check your understanding

Practice question

A bank routing table says that all euro payments above EUR 100,000 to Germany go through T2, while euro payments below that amount go through a different rail. What does this show about hub routing?
