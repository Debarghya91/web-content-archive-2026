Exercise

Work out what the beneficiary receives for a $10,000 payment under OUR, SHA and BEN.

Use the charge arrangement rules in this lesson and the correspondent and beneficiary bank fees named in the lesson.

  1. What to do. Calculate the amount credited under each of the three arrangements and state who pays each fee.

Correct result. Under OUR the sender pays all charges and the beneficiary receives the full amount. Under SHA the sender pays the sending charges and the beneficiary pays the receiving charges. Under BEN the beneficiary pays all charges and receives the amount less all fees.

Why. The charge arrangement decides who pays and how much arrives. A customer who was promised the full amount and receives less will complain, and the complaint lands on the analyst.

Writing requirements for an AI feature is different from writing requirements for a normal screen or report, because you describe not only what the system must do, but how it must behave when it is uncertain, and what evidence it must leave behind. Start with a normal example. A normal requirement says the reconciliation screen must show unmatched items older than three days.

An AI requirement must go further. It must name the data the model may use, for example the last twelve months of matched payments, the statement lines and the invoice references, and the data it must never use. It must describe the model behaviour in plain words, what counts as a confident match, what counts as a weak match, and what happens in each case.

Then come the guardrails, the hard limits that no model output may cross, for example never auto post a match above one lakh rupees without human approval, never change a beneficiary, and never decide a fraud block alone. Then the fallback, what the system does when the model is down or unsure, which is to route the work to the human queue with the reason shown, not to stop the whole operation.

Then the evidence, every AI suggestion, every score and every override must be logged with the time, the user and the reason, so an auditor can replay the decision later. Finally, write the acceptance test in the same way, with real examples, including the tricky ones, a payment two hundred rupees short, a new beneficiary at midnight, a missing reference.

If your requirement set covers data, behaviour, guardrails, fallback and evidence, developers and testers have something they can build and prove. If it only says use AI to match payments, they have a slogan, not a requirement.

Writing Requirements for AI Systems

Writing requirements for an AI feature is different from writing requirements for a normal screen or report, because you describe not only what the system must do, but how it must behave when it is uncertain, and what evidence it must leave behind. Start with a normal example. A normal requirement says the reconciliation screen must show unmatched items older than three days.

An AI requirement must go further. It must name the data the model may use, for example the last twelve months of matched payments, the statement lines and the invoice references, and the data it must never use. It must describe the model behaviour in plain words, what counts as a confident match, what counts as a weak match, and what happens in each case.

Then come the guardrails, the hard limits that no model output may cross, for example never auto post a match above one lakh rupees without human approval, never change a beneficiary, and never decide a fraud block alone. Then the fallback, what the system does when the model is down or unsure, which is to route the work to the human queue with the reason shown, not to stop the whole operation.

Then the evidence, every AI suggestion, every score and every override must be logged with the time, the user and the reason, so an auditor can replay the decision later. Finally, write the acceptance test in the same way, with real examples, including the tricky ones, a payment two hundred rupees short, a new beneficiary at midnight, a missing reference.

If your requirement set covers data, behaviour, guardrails, fallback and evidence, developers and testers have something they can build and prove. If it only says use AI to match payments, they have a slogan, not a requirement.
