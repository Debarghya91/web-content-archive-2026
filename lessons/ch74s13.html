Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

  1. What to do. Name the model, state when settlement becomes final, and explain what happens if the sending bank does not have enough liquidity.

Correct result. Real time gross settlement settles each payment individually in central bank money with immediate finality, but it needs liquidity for every payment. Deferred net settlement nets payments and settles the net position, which needs less liquidity but leaves settlement risk until the net settles.

Why. Settlement is where money actually moves. The model decides the risk, the liquidity need, and when the payment is truly final.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

The Fast Settlement Service, FSS, is the settlement engine behind every NPP payment, and it is operated by the Reserve Bank of Australia. Settlement is the movement of money between banks that makes a payment final, and in the FSS that movement happens in central bank money. Every NPP payment settles through the FSS, so every instant payment a customer sends is backed by a settlement movement between the two banks at the Reserve Bank of Australia. A bank joining the NPP must hold an Exchange Settlement Account at the Reserve Bank of Australia and keep it funded.

Settlement in the FSS happens payment by payment, in real time, and each NPP payment is settled individually as it arrives. The FSS debits the sending bank Exchange Settlement Account and credits the receiving bank Exchange Settlement Account for the same amount, and the payment becomes final at that moment. There is no end-of-day batch, no netting, and no wait for a settlement window, because the FSS operates continuously, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. A payment sent at midnight on a public holiday settles at midnight on a public holiday, which is the behaviour an instant payment promise requires.

A worked example shows one payment moving settlement balances between two banks. A winery in the Barossa Valley pays a packaging supplier in Melbourne AUD 33,000 on a Saturday morning, and the payment travels as an NPP credit transfer from the winery bank to the supplier bank. The FSS debits the winery bank Exchange Settlement Account by AUD 33,000 and credits the supplier bank Exchange Settlement Account by AUD 33,000, and the supplier bank credits the supplier account within seconds. Both banks see the movement at once, so reconciliation starts from a position both sides agree on.

The Bulk Electronic Clearing System, BECS, is the batch system that sits beside the NPP, and it carries bulk direct-entry credits and debits in files rather than one payment at a time. A business paying 400 staff salaries or a company collecting 2,000 direct debits sends a file of instructions, and BECS clears the file in a batch and settles the net result on a deferred basis. Settlement is deferred and net, so the banks settle the net difference across many payments at a set time. BECS remains the right rail for high-volume, low-urgency batches.

The contrast between the NPP and BECS decides which rail a payment should use, and the decision turns on urgency, volume and finality. An NPP payment settles individually and in real time, so the payee can use the money within seconds, which suits a supplier payment due today. A BECS payment settles as part of a deferred net batch, so the payee waits, which suits a salary run where no single payment is urgent. A payment sent on the wrong rail arrives too late or costs more effort than it should, so the routing rule belongs in the requirement.

The liquidity duty is different on each system, and the bank must staff and fund for both. On the FSS, the bank must keep its Exchange Settlement Account funded at all hours, because NPP payments arrive continuously and a payment that cannot be funded cannot settle. Liquidity monitoring is therefore a continuous duty, with alerts and top-up procedures that work at night and on public holidays. On BECS, the bank must fund the net settlement at the set time, a scheduled duty with a known amount.

The BA requirement must name the settlement rail, the funding check and the reconciliation point for every payment type the bank offers. For NPP payments, the requirement must name the FSS, the Exchange Settlement Account funding check, the per-payment settlement reporting and the continuous monitoring duty. For BECS payments, the requirement must name the batch cut-off times, the net settlement time and the file reconciliation. The test is to send one NPP payment at night and one BECS file in the morning, and check that the NPP payment settled in seconds and the BECS file settled as a net batch.
