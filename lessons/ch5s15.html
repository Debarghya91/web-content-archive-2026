Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

Show transcript

Not every message between banks fits a structured payment format. Sometimes one bank needs to ask another a question, answer one, or send a message that does not fit any other type. The SWIFT MT family provides a set of messages for exactly this purpose, organised by category number and by function. The second and third digits of the message type carry the function: n92 is a request for cancellation, n95 is a query, n96 is an answer, n98 is a proprietary message, and n99 is a free format message, where n is the category number. This lesson covers the category 1 and category 2 members of that family: MT195, MT196, MT199 and MT299, and the family pattern they belong to.

MT195 is the Queries message in category 1, customer payments and cheques. A bank sends MT195 to ask another bank a question about a payment, a cheque, or an account matter: where is the payment, why has it not arrived, what does a field mean, or can a detail be confirmed. The message carries the sender reference, the related reference pointing to the payment or message being asked about, and a narrative field with the question. MT196 is the Answers message, sent in reply to a query, carrying the same references so that the asking bank can match the answer to its question, and a narrative with the answer. The pair forms a question and answer exchange, and the references are what keep the conversation coherent when dozens of queries are open at once.

MT199 is the Free Format Message in category 1, and MT299 is the same in category 2, financial institution transfers. A free format message carries a narrative with no fixed business structure, and it is used when no other message type fits the need. In practice, free format messages were heavily used for exceptions and investigations: a bank that could not apply a payment, needed to request a cancellation, or had to explain an unusual situation would send an MT199 or MT299 with the details in the narrative. The flexibility was also the weakness: because the content was free text, the receiving bank could not process it automatically, and every free format message needed a human to read it, understand it and act on it. That manual handling is slow, expensive and error prone, and it is why the industry has moved these exchanges to structured messages.

The family pattern extends across all categories. In category 2, the same functions appear as MT292 for cancellation requests, MT295 for queries, MT296 for answers, MT298 for proprietary messages, and MT299 for free format. In category 9, cash management, they appear as MT992, MT995, MT996, MT998 and MT999. The function digits stay the same, only the category changes, so a bank that understands the pattern can read any member of the family. The n98 proprietary messages, MT198, MT298 and MT998, are a special case: they carry content defined by a bilateral agreement between two banks, or by a market practice, rather than by the SWIFT standard, and they are used for exchanges that are specific to one relationship or one community.

The migration to ISO 20022 replaces these messages with structured equivalents, and the mapping is part of the migration work. Queries about a payment that cannot be applied become camt.026, the Unable to Apply message. Queries about a payment that has not arrived become camt.027, the Claim Non-Receipt. Requests for a duplicate become camt.033, and the duplicate itself becomes camt.034. Answers that resolve an investigation become camt.029, the Resolution of Investigation. Case management exchanges become camt.030, camt.031 and camt.032. For exceptions and investigations that do not fit the structured set, the newer camt.110 and camt.111 carry the investigation request and response. The free format habit does not disappear overnight, and the analyst must map each existing MT199 and MT299 use case to its structured replacement, rather than assuming the narrative will somehow survive the migration.

A worked example shows the exchange. A bank receives a customer payment that it cannot apply because the beneficiary account number is wrong. In the MT world, it sends an MT195 query to the sending bank, asking for the correct account number, with the payment reference in the related reference field. The sending bank checks with its customer and replies with an MT196 answer carrying the corrected account number. The receiving bank applies the payment. In the ISO 20022 world, the same exchange uses camt.026 to report the inability to apply and camt.028 to provide the additional information, and both messages are structured, so the receiving system can route them automatically. The business conversation is the same; the format is what changed.
