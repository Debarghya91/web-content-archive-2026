Transcript and full text

Show transcript

A reject happens before the due date, when the debtor bank refuses the collection during validation. The debtor bank sends a pacs.002.001.10 Payment Status Report with status RJCT, carrying a reason code that explains the refusal. Common reject reasons include an invalid mandate reference, a closed account, or a collection that arrived after the cut-off for the due date. A rejected collection never debits the debtor account, so no money moves, and the creditor must correct the problem and send a new collection for the next due date.

The reject is the cheapest failure, because it costs only a correction. A return happens after the due date, when the debtor bank has already debited the account but must send the money back. The debtor bank sends a pacs.004.001.09 Payment Return, carrying the original mandate reference, the returned amount, and a reason code. A return can happen because the account had insufficient funds on the due date, because the account was closed, or because the debtor asked for the money back within the refund period.

Unlike a reject, a return moves money back through settlement, and the creditor bank debits the creditor account, so the utility must chase the unpaid bill through a different channel. A refund is the debtor right to claim the money back after a collection has settled, and the SDD Core scheme gives the debtor two different refund windows. Within eight weeks of the debit, the debtor can claim a refund with no questions asked, and the debtor bank must return the funds. Within thirteen months, the debtor can claim a refund if the collection was unauthorised, which means there was no valid mandate or the mandate did not cover the collection.

The eight week refund needs no reason, while the thirteen month refund needs the debtor to show that the collection was unauthorised, and the creditor must be able to produce the mandate to defend it. For the worked example, the refund rules shape how Stadtwerke Linden manages its risk. If Jonas Weber notices on 20 November that the EUR 89.99 collection was twice the amount he expected, he can ask Meridian Bank Munich for a refund within eight weeks, and the bank must return EUR 89.99 without asking why.

If Jonas Weber discovers in March 2027 that a collection was taken after he had cancelled the mandate, he can claim an unauthorised refund within thirteen months, and the utility must produce the mandate or accept the return. The creditor therefore prices the risk of refunds into its business, and it keeps mandates available for thirteen months after the last collection.
