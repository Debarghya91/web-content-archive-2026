Key terms

Correspondent banking: A service where one bank holds accounts for another bank so the second bank can make payments in currencies or countries where it has no branch.

Sanctions screening: Checking payment parties, countries and references against sanctions lists published by governments and international bodies.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Show transcript

The debtor agent is the bank or payment service provider that holds the debtor account and executes the payment instruction on behalf of the debtor. It validates the instruction, checks that funds are available, applies fraud and sanctions screening, debits the debtor account, and sends the payment onward through the clearing or correspondent banking chain. The creditor agent is the bank that holds the creditor account and receives the payment on behalf of the creditor. It validates the incoming instruction, checks that the account exists and can accept the payment, credits the creditor account, and notifies the creditor that funds have arrived.

In a domestic payment where both parties use the same bank, the debtor agent and creditor agent are the same institution, and the payment is a simple internal book transfer. In most payments, however, they are different banks, and the payment must travel between them through a clearing system, a central bank settlement system, or a correspondent banking relationship. Each agent has its own cut off times, its own validation rules, and its own fees, and a payment can fail at either end. A payment rejected by the debtor agent never leaves the sending bank, while a payment rejected by the creditor agent may have already left the sender and must be returned.

For a business analyst, the agent roles are essential for tracing a payment and for assigning ownership of a failure. If a customer says the money has left their account but has not arrived, you need to know whether the debtor agent has sent it, whether it is stuck in clearing, or whether the creditor agent has rejected it. The agent BICs, the account numbers, and the message references are the keys that let operations teams trace the payment across systems. Your requirements should always name the debtor agent and creditor agent explicitly, and should state what each agent must do on success, on rejection, and on return.

Worked example with real numbers: a customer of Bank A in Paris pays EUR 25,000 to a supplier at Bank B in Berlin. Bank A is the debtor agent, because it acts for the debtor, and it debits the customer EUR 25,000 and sends a pacs.008. Bank B is the creditor agent, because it acts for the creditor, and it receives the pacs.008, credits the supplier EUR 25,000, and sends a pacs.002. The debtor agent and the creditor agent are banks, not customers, and each has duties: the debtor agent must validate, screen and debit, and the creditor agent must validate, screen and credit, and each must report its status. If Bank A sends the pacs.008 but Bank B never credits, the payment is stuck between the agents, and the investigation starts with the pacs.002, not with the customer.

Failure and recovery: a debtor agent that debits the customer but never sends the pacs.008. A creditor agent that receives the pacs.008 but cannot credit, because the account is closed, returns the payment with a pacs.004, reason code AC04, and the debtor agent credits the customer. A debtor agent that sends to the wrong creditor agent, because the BIC was wrong, will have the pacs.008 rejected, and the recovery is to correct the BIC and resend. Each failure is an agent failure, and the message that proves it is the pacs.002 or the pacs.004, with the status and the reason code, so the operations team must be able to read both.

For a business analyst, product owner or subject matter expert, the test is to name the debtor agent and the creditor agent for every payment, with their BICs, their duties and their reporting, and to state what each agent does when the other fails. The ISO 20022 pacs.008 carries the debtor agent and the creditor agent as separate parties, distinct from the debtor and the creditor, and the requirement must map the business agents to the message fields. A requirement that confuses the debtor with the debtor agent will write the wrong party into the message, and the payment will be rejected, or worse, sent to the wrong bank, and the error will be found by the beneficiary, not by the test team.
