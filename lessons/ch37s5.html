Key terms

Direct participant: A bank or institution that holds an account in a payment system and sends and receives payments directly.

Payment hub: A central system in a bank that receives payment instructions from all channels, validates them, applies screening and routing rules, and sends them to the correct clearing system or correspondent.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

Barclays runs one of the largest payment hubs in the United Kingdom, processing more than 1 billion payments a year for retail customers, corporate clients, and correspondent banks. The hub connects to Faster Payments for instant payments up to 1 million pounds, to CHAPS for high value same-day payments with no upper limit, and to BACS for direct debits and direct credits. A retail customer who sends 500 pounds to a friend using the Barclays mobile app will have the payment sent through Faster Payments and credited within seconds. A corporate client who pays 50 million pounds for a property purchase will have the payment sent through CHAPS and settled at the Bank of England within the hour. The hub decides the route based on the amount, the urgency, and the destination sort code.

NatWest runs a similar operation, with a payment hub that serves its NatWest, Royal Bank of Scotland, and Ulster Bank brands from a single platform. The bank is one of the largest participants in Faster Payments and in BACS, and it is a direct participant in CHAPS. NatWest processes around 10 million payments a day, including salary payments for large employers, direct debits for utility companies, and standing orders for millions of retail customers. A key feature of the NatWest hub is its handling of bulk files, a company that pays 100,000 employees on the last Friday of the month sends a single BACS file, and the hub validates, splits, and submits it to the BACS system in time for the money to arrive on payday.

The ISO 20022 migration for CHAPS is the largest change to UK payments . The Bank of England moved CHAPS to ISO 20022 in June 2023, replacing the old SWIFT MT format with the new pacs.008 and pacs.009 messages. Both Barclays and NatWest had to rebuild the CHAPS part of their payment hubs to carry richer data such as the purpose of the payment and the full address of the beneficiary, and to handle the new rules for structured addresses. The migration was phased. The banks ran the old and new formats in parallel, tested with the Bank of England for more than a year, and moved to the new format on a single cutover weekend. A business analyst on the CHAPS programme spent much of the time mapping fields between the old and new formats and testing that a payment of 10 million pounds would still settle correctly.

Faster Payments is the system that most UK customers notice, because it is the one they use every day. Launched in 2008, it lets a payment of up to 1 million pounds move between UK banks in seconds, 24 hours a day. Both Barclays and NatWest were founding members, and both have built their mobile and internet banking around it. The technical challenge is availability. Faster Payments never closes, so the payment hub, the core banking system, and the fraud screening must all run 24 hours a day, with no overnight batch window to catch up. Both banks run their hubs in active-active mode across two data centres, so that if one centre fails, the other takes over without any customer noticing.

BACS is the quiet workhorse of UK payments, processing more than 6 billion payments a year, mostly direct debits and direct credits. Both Barclays and NatWest are major BACS participants, and their hubs handle the three-day BACS cycle, day one for submission, day two for processing, and day three for settlement. The cycle means a salary file submitted on Monday is paid on Wednesday, and the hub must track every file through the cycle and report any rejections back to the employer. For a business analyst, BACS is a good example of a system where the business rules matter more than the technology, the direct debit guarantee, the indemnity claim process, and the rules for failed collections are all defined by the scheme, not by the bank.
