Worked Example: The Same EUR 10,000 Payment as pacs.008 Under CBPR+ and SEPA

ISO 20022 Technical Implementation, Lesson 7 of 8, Narration 5:43, D04 Messaging and standards

Note saved.

What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain worked example: the same EUR 10,000 payment as pacs.008 under CBPR+ and SEPA. The payment is EUR 10,000 from a Paris customer, Atelier Lumiere, to a Frankfurt supplier, Rheinwerk GmbH, for a delivery of lighting equipment.

Why it matters. A business analyst should build a per rail mapping table, a tester should run the same payment through both rails and compare the results, and a product owner should make sure the screen asks for the right data for the rail the customer chose.

Chapter objective: Learner reads an ISO 20022 XSD, explains currency and amount precision, UTC offsets and timestamps, and the market practice layers of PMPG, CBPR+ and SEPA. Learner traces the EUR 10,000 Atelier Lumiere payment as a pacs.008 under both CBPR+ and SEPA, explains LEI and FATF Recommendation 16, and validates an ISO 20022 XML file against schema and business rules.

Key learning points

  • Payment: EUR 10
  • Message: built for cross border
  • UETR and own tracking and charge bearer
  • CBPR+ version
  • SEPA style message

Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Execution date: The date on which the bank executes a payment instruction, debiting the debtor account and sending the message onward.

Charge bearer: A code that states who pays the charges for a payment. In ISO 20022 the values are DEBT for debtor, CRED for creditor, SHAR for shared and SLEV for service level.

Exercise

Read the pacs.008 message described in this lesson and identify its key parts.

Use the message structure, fields and example in this lesson.

  1. What to do. List the three levels of the message and name the field that carries the amount and the field that carries the end-to-end reference.

Correct result. The amount is carried in the instructed amount element and the reference in the end-to-end identification. The three levels are the group header, the payment information, and the credit transfer transaction information.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The group header identifies the message, the payment information identifies the batch, and the transaction information carries the single payment.

The payment is EUR 10,000 from a Paris customer, Atelier Lumiere, to a Frankfurt supplier, Rheinwerk GmbH, for a delivery of lighting equipment. Under CBPR+ rules, the pacs.008 carries the full debtor data, including the name Atelier Lumiere, the full postal address in Paris, the account number, and the LEI where available. It carries the full creditor data, including the name Rheinwerk GmbH, the Frankfurt address, the account, and the LEI. It carries a UETR for tracking, the instructed amount EUR 10,000.00, the charge bearer, the purpose code, and the remittance information with the invoice number. The message is built for the cross border chain, so it assumes the payment may pass through a correspondent bank and must be readable by every bank in that chain. Under SEPA rules, the same EUR 10,000 payment is a simpler message. The debtor is identified by name and IBAN, and the full postal address is not required, because the SEPA scheme standardises the data and the payment stays inside the euro area. The creditor is identified by name and IBAN, the BIC is optional, and the remittance information carries the invoice number in structured or unstructured form. The UETR is not required in the same way, because the SEPA clearing system provides its own tracking, and the charge bearer is always shared under the SEPA rule. The instructed amount is still EUR 10,000.00, the currency is still EUR, and the requested execution date is still present, but the message is shorter, because the scheme does not need the cross border data. The fields that change between the two versions are the party data, the tracking, and the charge handling. The CBPR+ version carries the full address and the LEI for both parties, while the SEPA version carries only the name and the IBAN. The CBPR+ version carries a UETR and a detailed charge bearer, while the SEPA version relies on the clearing system and the shared charge rule. The CBPR+ version may carry an equivalent amount if a conversion is involved, while the SEPA version never does, because the currency is always euro. The reason for each difference is the market, CBPR+ serves a global chain of banks that do not share a common clearing system, while SEPA serves a single currency area with a common clearing system and a common rule book. If a message built for one rail is sent on the other, it will fail, and the failure mode teaches the rule. A SEPA style message sent on the CBPR+ route will be rejected for missing debtor address and missing UETR, and the receiving bank will return a pacs.002 with a reason code for missing data. A CBPR+ style message sent on the SEPA route may be rejected for carrying fields the SEPA scheme does not expect, or it may be accepted but with the extra data ignored, depending on the clearing system. Either way, the payment is delayed, and the customer must be told why. A business analyst should build a per rail mapping table, a tester should run the same payment through both rails and compare the results, and a product owner should make sure the screen asks for the right data for the rail the customer chose.
