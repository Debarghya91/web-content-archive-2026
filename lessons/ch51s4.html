What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain collection process. The process begins with the sales contract between exporter and importer, which must state the collection terms clearly.

Why it matters. This matters because cross-border payments involve more banks, more charges, more time and more ways to fail than any domestic payment. Getting the chain right is the job.

Chapter objective: Learner explains documentary collections, the roles of remitting and collecting banks, and the SWIFT Category 4 messages used at each stage. Learner uses real examples with amounts, banks and message names to show how the topic works in practice.

Key learning points

  • Process beginning with the sales contract
  • Exporter shipping the goods and obtaining the transport document
  • Documents matching the contract on amount, currency, quantity and consignee
  • Collecting bank
  • Final steps close

Key terms

Bill of exchange: A written order by one party instructing another party to pay a stated amount to a named person on a stated date.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

Return: A payment that is sent back to the sender after settlement because it cannot be credited to the beneficiary.

Exercise

Put the documentary collection process in order, from the sales contract to the final credit, naming the bank at each step and the message sent.

Use the collection process steps in this lesson, including the MT410 acknowledgement and the MT400 advice of payment.

  1. What to do. List the steps in order, name the bank at each step, and state what happens if the drawee refuses to pay or accept.

Correct result. The sales contract states the collection terms. The exporter gives the documents, the bill of exchange and the collection instruction to the remitting bank. The remitting bank sends the documents to the collecting bank. The collecting bank presents them to the drawee. The drawee pays or accepts.

The collecting bank remits the proceeds to the remitting bank, less its own charges, and sends an MT400 Advice of Payment. If the drawee refuses, the collecting bank notifies the remitting bank promptly, holds the documents, and waits for fresh instructions from the exporter.

Why. A collection is a sequence of handoffs, and a skipped step, such as remitting proceeds without an MT400, leaves the exporter unable to reconcile. An analyst must be able to walk the sequence in order.

Show transcript

The process begins with the sales contract between exporter and importer, which must state the collection terms clearly. The contract should name the payment term as D/P or D/A, state the amount and currency, name the remitting bank and the collecting bank if known, and state who pays the bank charges. For example, a contract for EUR 250,000 of pharmaceutical packaging from Lyon to Madrid might state D/P at sight, charges for the account of the importer, documents to be presented through Banco Santander.

If the contract is silent on these points, the collection instruction must fill the gap, and disputes become more likely. The exporter then ships the goods and obtains the transport document from the carrier. The carrier issues the bill of lading, or an air waybill for air freight, showing the shipper, the consignee, the port of loading, the port of discharge and the description of the goods. The exporter prepares the commercial invoice, the packing list, the certificate of origin and any inspection certificate required by the contract.

The exporter also draws the draft if the collection is D/A, or a sight draft if the instruction requires one. Every document must match the contract on amount, currency, quantity and consignee name. The exporter delivers the full document set and a signed collection instruction to the remitting bank. The instruction lists the documents enclosed, states D/P or D/A, gives the amount and currency, names the drawee and the collecting bank, and states what to do if the importer refuses to pay or accept.

It may instruct the bank to warehouse the goods, to return them, or to contact a named agent. The remitting bank logs the collection, assigns a reference such as COLL 2026 0847, and forwards the documents and instruction to the collecting bank by courier or by secure electronic channel, with a SWIFT message to alert the collecting bank. The collecting bank receives the documents, checks that the instruction is complete, and presents them to the importer. Presentation means the bank contacts the importer, shows the collection schedule, and asks for payment under D/P or acceptance under D/A. The importer may examine the documents at the bank counter before deciding.

If the terms are D/P, the importer pays EUR 250,000 into the collecting bank and the bank releases the documents. If the terms are D/A, the importer signs the draft for EUR 250,000 payable in 90 days, and the bank releases the documents against that signed acceptance. The final steps close the collection and move the money. Under D/P the collecting bank sends the proceeds to the remitting bank, less its own charges, and sends an MT400 Advice of Payment to confirm the collection. The remitting bank credits the exporter account for EUR 250,000 less charges, and closes the collection file.

Under D/A the collecting bank holds the accepted draft until maturity, presents it for payment on the due date, and then remits the proceeds in the same way. If the importer refuses to pay or accept, the collecting bank notifies the remitting bank promptly, holds the documents, and waits for fresh instructions from the exporter.
