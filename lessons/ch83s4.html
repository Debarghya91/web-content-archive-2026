Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Refund: A repayment of funds to a customer after a payment fails, is cancelled or is disputed. In card payments a refund returns money to the cardholder account.

Every market has a local payment method that the product must support, and the method shapes the product. In Brazil, Pix is an instant payment system run by the Central Bank of Brazil, and it is free for individuals and cheap for businesses. A product entering Brazil that does not support Pix will not be used, because every Brazilian expects it. In India, UPI is the dominant method, and a product that does not support UPI is invisible. In the Netherlands, iDEAL is the online payment method, and in Germany, Giropay and Sofort play the same role. The product must support the local method, not just the international one. The local method has its own user experience. Pix uses a key, which can be a phone number, an email, a tax number, or a random key, and the payer enters the key instead of an account number. UPI uses a virtual payment address, which looks like an email address, and the payer enters the address instead of the account details. iDEAL redirects the payer to their own bank's website to authorise the payment. Each of these is a different journey, and the product must implement the journey the local customer expects, not the journey the product already has. The local method has its own settlement model. Pix settles instantly in central bank money, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. UPI settles instantly through the National Payments Corporation of India. iDEAL settles through the bank's own account, usually the next business day. The product must therefore handle different settlement times, different cut-offs, and different failure modes for each method. A Pix payment that fails is retried immediately, while an iDEAL payment that fails is abandoned and the customer starts again. The local method has its own fraud pattern. Pix fraud is often social engineering, where the victim is tricked into sending money to a fraudster's Pix key, and the Central Bank of Brazil has introduced a special refund mechanism for Pix fraud. UPI fraud is often a fake payment request, where the victim is asked to enter their PIN to receive money and instead sends it. The product must implement the local fraud controls, including the refund mechanism, the transaction limits, and the cooling-off period for a new payee, because the regulator will hold the firm responsible. The local method has its own regulatory requirements. In Brazil the Central Bank requires Pix participants to offer the refund mechanism and to report fraud statistics. In India the Reserve Bank requires UPI participants to support the UPI PIN and to limit the transaction value. In the European Union the Payment Services Directive requires strong customer authentication for electronic payments, which affects how the local method authenticates the payer. The requirements must state the local regulatory obligations for each method, and the product must be tested against them. Supporting multiple local methods is an architecture decision. The product needs a payment method abstraction, a layer that presents a common interface to the rest of the product while hiding the differences between Pix, UPI, iDEAL and the others. Without that layer, every new method requires changes throughout the product, and the cost of adding a method grows with each one. With the layer, adding a method means building one connector and one journey, and the rest of the product does not change. The business analyst must specify the abstraction in the requirements. For a business analyst, the local method is a requirements package per method. Each method gets its own journey, its own settlement model, its own fraud controls, and its own regulatory obligations. The acceptance criteria must include a test where a payment is made with each local method, from initiation to settlement, with the correct user experience and the correct status reporting. Last reviewed 7 November 2026. Further reading: the Central Bank of Brazil Pix regulation; the National Payments Corporation of India UPI procedural guidelines; the European Payments Council work on local payment methods; the World Bank Global Payments Systems Survey.
