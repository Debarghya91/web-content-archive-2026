Key terms

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

Exception: A payment that cannot be processed normally and needs manual handling, investigation or repair. Exceptions arise from missing data, failed screening, account errors or system faults.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Exercise

Scenario: you are an investigations analyst. A customer payment of USD 10,000 was sent from London to a beneficiary in Brazil on 3 November 2026 via the cover method: an MT103 went direct to the beneficiary bank and an MT202 COV went through the correspondent chain to cover the funds. The beneficiary bank says it received the MT103 but has not received the funds. The MT202 COV cannot be found. Investigate: trace what should have happened, identify where the MT202 COV should be now, list the investigation messages to send, and state what to tell the customer.

The payment chain: ordering bank NatWest London (NWBKGB2L), sender correspondent JPMorgan Chase New York (CHASUS33), intermediary Citibank New York (CITIUS33), beneficiary bank Banco do Brasil Sao Paulo (BRASBRRJ). The MT103 was sent direct from NatWest to Banco do Brasil on 3 November with reference PAY20261103. The MT202 COV was sent from NatWest to JPMorgan with the same reference and the field 21 related reference PAY20261103. JPMorgan should have sent an MT202 COV onward to Citibank, and Citibank should have sent an MT202 COV onward to Banco do Brasil.

The value date was 3 November 2026. Today is 5 November 2026. The customer has called twice asking where the money is.

  1. What to do. Write the investigation plan: the expected path of the MT202 COV with each hop, the exact investigation message to send to each bank (MT192, MT196, MT199 or MT299, with the field 21 reference and the field 79 narrative), and the message to send to the customer. Then state the most likely cause and the next step if the MT202 COV is still not found.

Correct result. Expected path: NatWest to JPMorgan (MT202 COV, 3 November), JPMorgan to Citibank (MT202 COV, 3 November), Citibank to Banco do Brasil (MT202 COV, 3 November), Banco do Brasil credits the beneficiary on value date 3 November. Investigation steps: send an MT192 (request for cancellation is not right here; use MT199 free format) to JPMorgan asking whether it received the MT202 COV with reference PAY20261103 and whether it sent it onward to Citibank, quoting field 21 PAY20261103 and field 79 with the full payment details. Send the same MT199 to Citibank asking whether it received the MT202 COV from JPMorgan and whether it sent it onward to Banco do Brasil.

Send an MT199 to Banco do Brasil confirming it holds the MT103 and asking whether it has received any cover. Most likely cause: the MT202 COV was stopped at JPMorgan or Citibank because the field 21 related reference did not match the MT103 reference, or because the cover amount did not match, so the receiving bank held it pending investigation. Next step if still not found: ask JPMorgan for a copy of the MT202 COV it sent, check the UETR on the gpi tracker, and if the funds are confirmed held at an intermediary, request a recall or a re-send of the cover with the correct reference.

Tell the customer: the instruction reached the beneficiary bank but the funds are held at an intermediary bank, we have raised investigations with all three banks, and we will update you by end of day 6 November. Worked reasoning: in the cover method the MT103 and the MT202 COV travel separately, so the beneficiary bank can hold the instruction while waiting for funds. The investigation must follow the cover chain hop by hop, because the break could be at any hop. Field 21 is the link between the MT103 and the MT202 COV; if it does not match, the banks cannot pair them.

Why. Hint 1: in the cover method, always investigate the cover chain separately from the instruction, because they travel different routes. Hint 2: field 21 is the link between the MT103 and the MT202 COV; a mismatch there is the most common cause of a missing cover. Hint 3: the UETR on the gpi tracker shows the status of the payment at each bank, so check it before sending investigation messages. The worked explanation shows the investigation discipline: trace the expected path first, then ask each bank in the chain the same question with the same reference, and tell the customer what is known and when the next update will come.

An investigation is the process of finding out what happened to a payment that has not arrived, has arrived for the wrong amount, or has arrived with the wrong information, and it is the most common exception task in cross border operations. The investigation starts with the customer reporting the problem, and the operations team then traces the payment using the UETR, the gpi tracker, the SWIFT messages, and the correspondent account statements. The team contacts the banks in the chain, asks for the status, and follows up until the payment is found.

The investigation can take days, because each bank must respond, and because the banks are in different time zones and speak different languages. The analyst must design the investigation process to be systematic, log the query, trace the payment, contact the right bank, record the response, and update the customer at each step. Reason codes are the standardised language of investigations, and the analyst must know the most important ones. In the SWIFT system, return and reject reason codes include AC zero one for incorrect account number, AC zero four for closed account, AC zero six for blocked account, AM zero five for duplication, BE zero five for unknown beneficiary, and MD zero seven for end customer deceased.

In the ISO 20022 system, the same reasons are carried in the pacs.002 and pacs.004 messages with a code and a description. In the gpi tracker, the status codes show whether the payment is accepted, in progress, credited, rejected or returned. The investigation must also produce a record that can be used to prevent the next failure. If the investigation finds that the account number was wrong because the customer copied it from an old invoice, the fix is to update the invoice template. If it finds that the payment was delayed because the intermediary bank stopped it for a compliance query, the fix is to improve the originator information.

If it finds that the payment was returned because the beneficiary bank had changed its BIC, the fix is to update the bank directory. The analyst should review the investigation data regularly, should identify the repeat causes, and should propose the fixes. An investigation that ends with the payment found but no lesson learned is a missed opportunity, and the analyst who closes the loop is the analyst who improves the product.
