  1. What to do. List the main blocks of the message, name the field that carries the amount, and name the field that identifies the payment.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

Show transcript

A corporation may hold an operating account, a tax account, and a payroll account at the same bank, and it may need to pay from more than one of them in a single run. MT101 supports this by allowing field 25, the account number, to appear in Sequence A for the message as a whole, and again in each Sequence B for that transaction. If field 25 appears in a Sequence B, it overrides the Sequence A account for that transaction only. The bank therefore debits the account named in the Sequence B, not the account named in Sequence A, for that payment.

The rule is simple to apply in practice. Sequence A field 25 names the default debit account, which is used for any Sequence B that does not name its own account. A Sequence B that names a different account uses that account instead. A company called Northgate Engineering may send an MT101 with Sequence A field 25 naming its operating account GB15 NWBK 6000 1234 5678 90, and then carry three Sequence B blocks, two of which use the operating account by default, and one of which names its tax account GB15 NWBK 6000 1234 5678 91 for a tax payment of 95,000 pounds to HM Revenue and Customs.

The bank checks funds separately for each debit account. It will not use the balance in one account to cover a shortfall in another, unless the customer has a sweeping or pooling agreement in place. A payment of 300,000 euros from an account that holds only 200,000 euros will be rejected or queued, even if another account in the same message holds 1 million euros. The treasurer must therefore check each account balance before sending the message, and must fund each account for the payments that will be debited from it on the execution date.

Field 25 must be given in a format the bank can read, usually the IBAN or the domestic account number, and it must match an account held by the ordering customer. A message that names an account belonging to another company will be rejected, unless that company has given a mandate to debit its account. A corporation that pays from a subsidiary account must ensure that the subsidiary has authorised the debit, and that the bank holds that mandate on file. A missing mandate is a common reason for a payment from a second account to fail.

A practical example shows the benefit. A hotel group with three accounts at Standard Chartered in Singapore sends one MT101 each Monday, with five Sequence B blocks. Two payments for food suppliers, 22,000 Singapore dollars and 18,500 Singapore dollars, are debited from the operating account. Two salary advances, 9,000 Singapore dollars each, are debited from the payroll account. One payment of 40,000 Singapore dollars for licence fees is debited from the tax reserve account.

The bank executes all five payments from the three accounts, and the group treasurer reconciles each account from one MT940 statement, with each debit linked to its own transaction reference.
