What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain exercise: address three payments in T2. Read the three cases carefully, and write down your answer for each one before you check it against the text that follows.

Why it matters. This matters because the rail decides how fast the money moves, what it costs, when it settles and what can go wrong. Choosing the wrong rail is a product and an operations failure.

Chapter objective: Learner explains addressing in T2, distinguishes Direct, Indirect, Multi-addressee and Addressable BIC participation, and describes how the BAH and the instructed agent differ in routing. Learner uses real BIC examples and payment amounts to show how addressing works in practice.

Key learning points

  • Three cases to answer before checking
  • RTGS Directory codes 05, 06, 07 and 08
  • Payment to an addressable BIC
  • Lyon BIC rejection
  • Madrid branch later
  • R answer case receiving bank as BAH

Key terms

Indirect participant: A bank or institution that uses a payment system through a direct participant, without holding its own settlement account.

Direct participant: A bank or institution that holds an account in a payment system and sends and receives payments directly.

Addressable BIC: A BIC that is addressable in a payment system, meaning the system can route payments to the institution that holds it, either directly or through another participant.

Exercise

Address three payments in T2: case one, direct to direct, EUR 1 million from Deutsche Bank to BNP Paribas; case two, payment to an addressable BIC; case three, payment to a multi-addressee. For each case, state the instructed agent, the settlement account, and the BAH To field.

Use the three cases in this lesson and the participation type rules for codes 05, 07 and 08.

  1. What to do. For each of the three cases, state the instructed agent, the account settlement is booked on, and the BAH To field.

Correct result. Case one, direct to direct, is the simplest payment in T2: EUR 1 million from Deutsche Bank in Frankfurt, BIC DEUTDEFFXXX, to BNP Paribas in Paris, BIC BNPAFRPPXXX, settles on the direct participant RTGS DCAs, and the BAH To field names the T2 service. Case two, payment to an addressable BIC, tests whether you remember that the addressable BIC holds no account, so settlement is booked on the direct participant RTGS DCA. Case three, payment to a multi-addressee, tests whether you remember that the multi-addressee sends and receives directly using the direct participant RTGS DCA. A payment that names the Lyon BIC as instructed agent will be rejected.

Why. Addressing is the skill that decides whether a T2 payment settles or is rejected. The three cases test the three patterns an analyst will meet in live work.

Read the three cases carefully, and write down your answer for each one before you check it against the text that follows. Case one is a direct to direct payment of EUR 1 million from Deutsche Bank in Frankfurt, BIC DEUTDEFFXXX, to BNP Paribas in Paris, BIC BNPAFRPPXXX. Case two is a payment of EUR 75,000 from Deutsche Bank to a Lyon branch of BNP Paribas, whose branch BIC is listed in the directory as an addressable BIC holder, code 08, settled through the Paris head office, BIC BNPAFRPPXXX, which holds the direct participant account. Case three is a payment of EUR 300,000 to the Commerzbank Madrid branch, BIC COBAESMXXXX, a multi-addressee of Commerzbank Frankfurt, BIC COBADEFFXXX. Use the RTGS Directory codes 05, 06, 07 and 08 as you decide each answer. Case one, direct to direct, is the simplest payment in T2, and it is the pattern every other case is measured against. The BAH From is DEUTDEFFXXX, the BAH To is the T2 service, the instructing agent is DEUTDEFFXXX, and the instructed agent is BNPAFRPPXXX. The debtor agent is DEUTDEFFXXX and the creditor agent is BNPAFRPPXXX. The payment settles on the Deutsche Bank RTGS DCA, debited by EUR 1 million, and on the BNP Paribas RTGS DCA, credited by EUR 1 million. The header and the payment agree in the simplest way, because the sender holds the account and the receiver holds the account, with no branch or intermediary in between. Case two, payment to an addressable BIC, tests whether you remember that the addressable BIC holds no account. The Lyon branch BIC is the creditor agent in the pacs.008, because the branch holds the customer account, but the instructed agent is BNPAFRPPXXX, the Paris head office direct participant, code 05, which holds the RTGS DCA that T2 will credit. The BAH From is DEUTDEFFXXX, the BAH To is the T2 service, the instructing agent is DEUTDEFFXXX, and the instructed agent is BNPAFRPPXXX. The EUR 75,000 settles on the Paris head office RTGS DCA, and the head office then credits the Lyon branch in its own books. A payment that names the Lyon BIC as instructed agent will be rejected. Case three, payment to a multi-addressee branch, tests the mirror image of the last case. The Madrid branch, COBAESMXXXX, is the creditor agent, because the branch holds the customer relationship, but the instructed agent is COBADEFFXXX, the direct participant that holds the RTGS DCA. The BAH From is the sending bank BIC, the BAH To is the T2 service, the instructing agent is the sending bank, and the instructed agent is COBADEFFXXX. The EUR 300,000 settles on the Commerzbank Frankfurt RTGS DCA, and Frankfurt credits the Madrid branch internally. If the Madrid branch later sends a payment itself, the BAH From will be COBAESMXXXX, while the instructing agent will still be COBADEFFXXX. Check your three answers against two rules that catch most errors. First, the BAH To is always the T2 service, never the receiving bank, in all three cases. Second, the instructed agent is always the direct participant that holds the RTGS DCA, never an addressable BIC, never an indirect participant, and never a multi-addressee branch on its own. If your answer for case two or case three names the branch BIC as instructed agent, change it to the direct participant BIC and the payment will settle. If your answer for any case names the receiving bank as BAH To, change it to the T2 service. Those two corrections fix the large majority of addressing failures in T2 testing.
