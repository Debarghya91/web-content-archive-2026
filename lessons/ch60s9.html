Key terms

Creditor: The party that is owed money and receives a payment, such as a supplier, a seller or a service provider.

Mandate: An authorisation given by a debtor to a creditor to collect payments from the debtor account by direct debit.

Debtor: The party that owes money and makes a payment, such as a buyer, an employer or a borrower. In ISO 20022 messages the debtor is the ordering customer, with name, address and account details.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

Show transcript

The pain.014 Creditor Payment Activation Request Status Report is the answer to a pain.013 activation request, and it tells the creditor whether the activation was accepted or rejected. The debtor bank sends pain.014 after it has checked the request against the debtor identity, the debtor account, and the mandate details. The report names the original pain.013 message, so the creditor can match the answer to the request without guessing. The creditor must treat the activation as pending until pain.014 arrives, and it must not send collections on the strength of the request alone.

The structure of pain.014 centres on the original message reference, the transaction status, and the reason for that status. The group header carries the message identification and the creation date and time for the status report itself. The original message information carries the identification and creation date of the pain.013 being answered, together with the status for the group or for each request inside it. The status block carries an accepted or rejected code, and a rejected status carries a reason code that explains the problem. The reason might point to an unknown debtor account, a mandate that does not match the debtor record, or a missing mandatory field.

The full request to pay sequence starts with the creditor asking for the payment route to be activated, and it ends with the debtor approving or declining each payment request. The creditor sends pain.013 to activate the arrangement, and the debtor bank answers with pain.014. The creditor then sends a payment request for a specific amount and due date, and the debtor bank presents that request to the debtor for a decision. The debtor approves the request, declines it, or lets it expire, and the debtor bank carries that decision back to the creditor. An approved request leads to a payment instruction, while a declined request ends the attempt without moving money.

A worked example with a supplier invoice shows the sequence in concrete terms. Harborview Retail Group owes EUR 9,600 to a packaging supplier for a delivery of cartons, with payment due on the twentieth day of the month. The supplier sends a payment request for EUR 9,600 through the activated route, naming the invoice reference and the due date. The debtor bank shows the request to the Harborview Retail Group treasurer, who checks the invoice, confirms the goods arrived, and approves the payment. The debtor bank then processes the payment for EUR 9,600, and the supplier receives the funds with the invoice reference attached. The treasurer never typed the supplier account details, and the request carried the details while the approval released the money.

The same pain.013 and pain.014 pair powers PayTo mandates, where the activation creates a standing authority for future collections. The creditor sends pain.013 to propose a mandate, naming the amount pattern, the frequency, and the validity period for the authority. The debtor bank presents the mandate proposal to the debtor, who authorises it in the banking channel, amends it, or declines it. The debtor bank then sends pain.014 to report the outcome, and the creditor records the mandate as active only when pain.014 confirms acceptance. Later collections under the mandate proceed within the authorised terms, and the debtor can pause or cancel the mandate through the debtor bank.

The failure handling in the pain.013 and pain.014 flow protects the debtor and gives the creditor a clear next step. A pain.014 rejection with a data reason, such as a wrong account number, calls for a corrected pain.013 with the right data. A rejection with a mandate reason, such as a mandate the debtor does not recognise, calls for the creditor to contact the debtor and obtain a fresh agreement. A payment request that the debtor declines is not an error, and the creditor must follow it up as a commercial matter. An expired request must not be treated as an approval, and the creditor must send a fresh request if the payment is still due.
