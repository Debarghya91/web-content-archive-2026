What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain customer-to-bank: pain.001, pain.002, pain.008. The pain.001 message, Customer Credit Transfer Initiation, carries a customer instruction to move money from the debtor account to one or more creditor accounts.

Why it matters. This matters because the message is the payment instruction. A wrong field, a missing element or a misunderstood format is a failed payment, a return, or a compliance breach.

Chapter objective: Learner navigates the complete ISO 20022 catalogue, selecting the right message for each business scenario across payments, cash management, securities, and reporting. Learner practises this skill on realistic banking cases, explaining the reasoning clearly and using correct terminology throughout the work.

  • Customer Payment Status Report

Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Execution date: The date on which the bank executes a payment instruction, debiting the debtor account and sending the message onward.

Charge bearer: A code that states who pays the charges for a payment. In ISO 20022 the values are DEBT for debtor, CRED for creditor, SHAR for shared and SLEV for service level.

  1. What to do. List the main blocks of the message, name the field that carries the amount, and name the field that identifies the payment.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

The pain.001 message, Customer Credit Transfer Initiation, carries a customer instruction to move money from the debtor account to one or more creditor accounts. It starts with a group header that identifies the message, the initiating party, and the number and total amount of the transactions inside it. Each transaction then carries the payment identification, the instructed amount and currency, the debtor and creditor names and accounts, the debtor and creditor agents, the charge bearer, and the remittance information. A single pain.001 from a company in Amsterdam may contain five thousand salary payments totalling EUR 8,400,000, each with its own creditor account and end to end reference, all carried in one file. A pain.001 file must be validated before the bank accepts it, and the most common failures are easy to prevent. The group header total must equal the sum of the individual transactions, or the file is rejected as a whole. Each IBAN must pass its check digit test, each amount must have the correct number of decimal places for its currency, and each execution date must be a valid working day. A file with a salary payment of EUR 3,250.00 to an employee with an IBAN of NL91 ABNA 0417 1643 00 will pass, but the same file will fail if one IBAN has a single digit wrong. Banks return a pain.002 to report these failures, so the customer can correct the file and resend it. The pain.002 message, Customer Payment Status Report, tells the customer what happened to each instruction in a pain.001 or pain.008 file. It uses status codes that every payment professional should know, including RJCT for rejected, ACSP for accepted for settlement in progress, ACSC for accepted for settlement completed, and ACCP for accepted for customer profile checks. A status of RJCT is always accompanied by a reason code, such as AC01 for an incorrect account number or AM02 for insufficient funds, and the original instruction identifier so the customer can find the failed payment. A treasurer who receives a pain.002 with three rejections out of five thousand payments can fix those three and resend only those three. The pain.008 message, Customer Direct Debit Initiation, carries instructions to collect money from debtors, and it is the mirror image of pain.001. A utility company in Madrid may send a pain.008 with ten thousand collections of EUR 85.00 each, debiting customers across Spain and France for their monthly electricity bills. Each collection carries the mandate reference, such as MAND-2026-008812, the mandate date, the creditor name and account, the debtor name and account, and the collection date. The bank checks that a signed mandate exists for each debtor before it releases the collection, because a direct debit without a mandate can be returned by the debtor bank and charged back to the creditor. Direct debit timing and returns deserve special attention, because they differ from credit transfers in important ways. A pain.008 collection for a date of 10 November 2026 must be sent several days earlier, so the debtor bank has time to check the mandate and the balance. If the debtor has insufficient funds, the debtor bank returns the collection with a pacs.004, and the creditor sees a return reason such as MS03 for no reason specified or AM04 for insufficient funds. If the debtor disputes the collection, the return may arrive up to eight weeks later under the European direct debit rules. A creditor that plans cash flow on direct debits must allow for these returns, and must keep every mandate on file for inspection.

pain.001 is the customer credit transfer initiation. It is the message a corporate customer sends to its bank to instruct one or more credit transfers. It is the ISO 20022 equivalent of the MT101, and it is used on domestic, SEPA, and cross border channels. The pain.001 has a group header, GrpHdr, which carries the message identification, MsgId, the creation date and time, CreDtTm, the number of transactions, NbOfTxs, and the control sum, CtrlSum. It has one or more payment information blocks, PmtInf, each carrying the payment method, the debtor, the debtor account, the debtor agent, and the requested execution date. It has one or more credit transfer transaction information blocks, CdtTrfTxInf, each carrying the payment identification, the amount, the creditor, the creditor account, the creditor agent, the charge bearer, the purpose, and the remittance information.

The group header is the control for the message. MsgId is the reference the sender gives the message, and it must be unique. CreDtTm is the date and time the message was created, in ISO format, for example 2026-05-12T09:00:00. NbOfTxs is the number of transactions in the message, and CtrlSum is the total of the amounts, which the bank checks against the sum of the individual transactions. If the control sum does not match the sum of the transactions, the bank will reject the message, because it cannot be sure that all transactions were received. The group header also carries the initiating party, InitgPty, which names the corporate that sent the message, and it may carry the forwarding agent, FwdgAgt, if the message was sent through a service bureau.

The payment information block, PmtInf, is the equivalent of Sequence A in an MT101. It carries the payment information identification, PmtInfId, the payment method, PmtMtd, which is normally TRF for a credit transfer, the number of transactions in this block, NbOfTxs, the control sum for this block, CtrlSum, the payment type information, PmtTpInf, which may carry the service level, SvcLvl, such as SEPA or URGP, and the category purpose, CtgyPurp, the requested execution date, ReqdExctnDt, the debtor, Dbtr, the debtor account, DbtrAcct, the debtor agent, DbtrAgt, and the charge bearer, ChrgBr, which states whether the charges are borne by the debtor, the creditor, or shared. A pain.001 with three debit accounts has three PmtInf blocks, one per account, just as an MT101 with three debit accounts has three different field 25 values in its Sequence B blocks.

The credit transfer transaction information block, CdtTrfTxInf, is the equivalent of Sequence B in an MT101. It carries the payment identification, PmtId, with the instruction identification, InstrId, and the end to end identification, EndToEndId, the amount, Amt, with the instructed amount, InstdAmt, and the currency, the creditor agent, CdtrAgt, the creditor, Cdtr, the creditor account, CdtrAcct, the purpose, Purp, the remittance information, RmtInf, which may be structured or unstructured, and the instruction for the debtor agent, InstrForDbtrAgt, and for the creditor agent, InstrForCdtrAgt. Each CdtTrfTxInf is a separate payment, with its own beneficiary, amount, and remittance information, and the bank processes each one separately.

pain.002 is the customer payment status report. It is the message the bank sends to the customer to report the status of each payment in a pain.001. It has a group header, GrpHdr, and an original group information and status block, OrgnlGrpInfAndSts, which carries the original message identification, OrgnlMsgId, the original message name identification, OrgnlMsgNmId, and the group status, GrpSts, which may be ACCP, accepted, RJCT, rejected, PART, partially accepted, or ACSP, accepted settlement in process. It has one or more transaction information and status blocks, TxInfAndSts, each carrying the original instruction identification, OrgnlInstrId, the original end to end identification, OrgnlEndToEndId, the transaction status, TxSts, and the status reason information, StsRsnInf, with the reason code, Rsn, and the additional information, AddtlInf.

The status codes in a pain.002 are the same codes used in a pacs.002, and an SME must know them. ACCP means the payment is accepted. RJCT means the payment is rejected. ACSP means the payment is accepted and settlement is in process. ACSC means the payment is accepted and settlement is complete. ACCC means the payment is accepted and the creditor account is credited. PART means the group is partially accepted, some transactions accepted and some rejected. The status reason information carries the reason code, such as AC01 for an incorrect account number, AM04 for insufficient funds, AG01 for a transaction forbidden on this account, RC01 for a bank identifier incorrect, and FF01 for an invalid file format. The bank should give a reason code for every rejected transaction, so the customer can correct and re-send it.

pain.008 is the customer direct debit initiation. It is the message a creditor sends to its bank to collect a direct debit from a debtor. It has a group header, GrpHdr, one or more payment information blocks, PmtInf, each carrying the payment method, DD, the creditor, Cdtr, the creditor account, CdtrAcct, the creditor agent, CdtrAgt, and the requested collection date, ReqdColltnDt, and one or more direct debit transaction information blocks, DrctDbtTxInf, each carrying the payment identification, the amount, the direct debit transaction, DrctDbtTx, with the mandate related information, MndtRltdInf, the mandate identification, MndtId, the date of signature, DtOfSgntr, and the debtor, Dbtr, the debtor account, DbtrAcct, and the debtor agent, DbtrAgt. The mandate is the authorisation the debtor gave the creditor to collect, and it must be identified in the message, because the debtor bank will check it.

For a business analyst, the pain messages are the customer interface. The analyst must be able to specify a pain.001 that carries a multi-transaction payment run, a pain.002 that reports the status per transaction, and a pain.008 that carries a direct debit collection with a mandate. The analyst must know the group header, the payment information block, and the transaction information block, and must know which fields are mandatory, which are optional, and which are conditional. The analyst must also know the status codes, because a pain.002 with a status of RJCT and a reason code of AC01 tells the customer exactly what to fix, and a pain.002 with a status of ACCC tells the customer that the creditor has been paid.
