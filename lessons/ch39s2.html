Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Execution date: The date on which the bank executes a payment instruction, debiting the debtor account and sending the message onward.

Charge bearer: A code that states who pays the charges for a payment. In ISO 20022 the values are DEBT for debtor, CRED for creditor, SHAR for shared and SLEV for service level.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

The pain.001 message, Customer Credit Transfer Initiation, carries a customer instruction to move money from the debtor account to one or more creditor accounts. It starts with a group header that identifies the message, the initiating party, and the number and total amount of the transactions inside it. Each transaction then carries the payment identification, the instructed amount and currency, the debtor and creditor names and accounts, the debtor and creditor agents, the charge bearer, and the remittance information.

A single pain.001 from a company in Amsterdam may contain five thousand salary payments totalling EUR 8,400,000, each with its own creditor account and end to end reference, all carried in one file. A pain.001 file must be validated before the bank accepts it, and the most common failures are easy to prevent. The group header total must equal the sum of the individual transactions, or the file is rejected as a whole. Each IBAN must pass its check digit test, each amount must have the correct number of decimal places for its currency, and each execution date must be a valid working day.

A file with a salary payment of EUR 3,250.00 to an employee with an IBAN of NL91 ABNA 0417 1643 00 will pass, but the same file will fail if one IBAN has a single digit wrong. Banks return a pain.002 to report these failures, so the customer can correct the file and resend it. The pain.002 message, Customer Payment Status Report, tells the customer what happened to each instruction in a pain.001 or pain.008 file. It uses status codes that every payment professional should know, including RJCT for rejected, ACSP for accepted for settlement in progress, ACSC for accepted for settlement completed, and ACCP for accepted for customer profile checks.

A status of RJCT is always accompanied by a reason code, such as AC01 for an incorrect account number or AM02 for insufficient funds, and the original instruction identifier so the customer can find the failed payment. A treasurer who receives a pain.002 with three rejections out of five thousand payments can fix those three and resend only those three. The pain.008 message, Customer Direct Debit Initiation, carries instructions to collect money from debtors, and it is the mirror image of pain.001.

A utility company in Madrid may send a pain.008 with ten thousand collections of EUR 85.00 each, debiting customers across Spain and France for their monthly electricity bills. Each collection carries the mandate reference, such as MAND-2026-008812, the mandate date, the creditor name and account, the debtor name and account, and the collection date. The bank checks that a signed mandate exists for each debtor before it releases the collection, because a direct debit without a mandate can be returned by the debtor bank and charged back to the creditor.

Direct debit timing and returns deserve special attention, because they differ from credit transfers in important ways. A pain.008 collection for a date of 10 November 2026 must be sent several days earlier, so the debtor bank has time to check the mandate and the balance. If the debtor has insufficient funds, the debtor bank returns the collection with a pacs.004, and the creditor sees a return reason such as MS03 for no reason specified or AM04 for insufficient funds. If the debtor disputes the collection, the return may arrive up to eight weeks later under the European direct debit rules.

A creditor that plans cash flow on direct debits must allow for these returns, and must keep every mandate on file for inspection.
