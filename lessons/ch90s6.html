What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain subledgers and the general ledger and apply it to a real banking scenario.

Why it matters. Subledger to general ledger is the accounting spine of the bank, and a break here is a major incident.

Chapter objective: Learner explains how a bank's ledgers record every payment, distinguishes customer accounts, internal accounts, nostro and vostro ledgers, suspense and transit accounts, writes the exact general ledger postings for outgoing and incoming payments, and explains intraday processing, value dating, subledger feeds, reconciliation and error correction, with correct terminology.

Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Authorisation: Approval by the issuer for a card transaction to proceed, confirming the card is valid and funds or credit are available.

Payment hub: A central system in a bank that receives payment instructions from all channels, validates them, applies screening and routing rules, and sends them to the correct clearing system or correspondent.

The general ledger is the single book of record for financial reporting, but it does not hold the detail. The detail lives in subledgers, and each business system owns one. The customer accounts subledger holds every current account, savings account and loan. The card subledger holds every card transaction, authorisation and clearing item. The trade finance subledger holds every letter of credit, guarantee and collection. The treasury subledger holds every foreign exchange trade and money market deal. The general ledger holds the totals. When the finance team produces the balance sheet, it reads the general ledger, and when it needs the detail behind a number, it goes to the subledger. Each source system feeds the general ledger with summary journals, and the journals are usually posted at the end of the day, although high-value flows can be posted intraday. The payment hub, which processes CHAPS, Faster Payments, Bacs and SEPA payments, posts a daily summary for each currency and each product: the total value of payments sent, the total value received, the fees charged, and the foreign exchange margin earned. The hub's own transaction store is the subledger, and it holds every individual payment. The general ledger receives only the totals, which keeps it small enough to close each day and to report from, while the hub retains the detail for customer queries and investigations. The card system feeds the general ledger in two stages, because authorisation and clearing are different events. An authorisation creates a hold on the customer's available balance, and it is not a ledger posting at all. When the clearing file arrives from the card scheme, usually the next day, the card system posts the ledger entries: the scheme settlement account is debited, the merchant acquiring accounts are credited, interchange is posted between the issuing and acquiring sides, and scheme fees are posted to their own accounts. If the clearing file is late or incomplete, the card subledger and the general ledger will disagree, and the difference appears on the reconciliation report the next morning. Trade finance has its own subledger because its postings are contingent as well as actual. When a bank issues a letter of credit for 2 million dollars, it does not pay 2 million dollars, but it does take on a liability, and the trade subledger records both the contingent liability and the fee earned. When the beneficiary presents documents and the bank pays, the subledger posts the payment to the general ledger and releases the contingent liability. Guarantees work the same way: the guarantee is a contingent liability until it is called. The general ledger therefore carries a summary of the contingent liabilities, and the subledger carries the detail of each one, including the expiry date and the conditions. The interface between a subledger and the general ledger is a control point, and it is governed by control totals. Each file or message the subledger sends carries a total: the number of items, the total debits, the total credits, and a hash total of the account numbers. The general ledger checks the totals before it posts anything. If the totals do not match, the whole file is rejected and nothing is posted, because a partial posting would leave the ledger out of balance. A rejected file is an incident, and the operations team must fix the cause and resend it before the end-of-day close, or the day cannot close with a complete set of postings. The chart of accounts mapping is the link between a product and the ledger, and it must be approved before the product goes live. Each product, each currency, each fee type and each charge type maps to a specific general ledger account. A new payment product that has no mapping cannot post, and a mapping that points at the wrong account will misstate the balance sheet. Finance owns the chart of accounts, and any change to it needs finance approval, a test in a non-production environment, and a record of who approved it. For a business analyst, the requirement is that every new payment flow must include its posting map and its control totals, and the acceptance criteria must include a test where a subledger file with a wrong control total is rejected in full and nothing is posted.
