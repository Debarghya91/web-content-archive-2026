Practise this lesson

Exercise

Work out what the beneficiary receives for a EUR 25,000 payment under OUR, SHA and BEN.

Use the charge arrangement rules in this lesson and the correspondent and beneficiary bank fees named in the lesson.

  1. What to do. Calculate the amount credited under each of the three arrangements and state who pays each fee.

Correct result. Under OUR the sender pays all charges and the beneficiary receives the full amount. Under SHA the sender pays the sending charges and the beneficiary pays the receiving charges. Under BEN the beneficiary pays all charges and receives the amount less all fees.

Why. The charge arrangement decides who pays and how much arrives. A customer who was promised the full amount and receives less will complain, and the complaint lands on the analyst.

Generative AI is the technology behind the chat tools you have probably already tried. A large language model is a machine learning model trained on a huge amount of written text, books, websites, documents, and code. It learns which words tend to follow which other words, and it uses that to produce new text that reads as if a person wrote it. You give it a prompt, which is simply your instruction in plain words, and it generates a reply.

It can draft a business requirements document, summarise a fifty-page scheme rulebook, or rewrite a customer letter in a friendlier tone. Here is the part that catches people out. The model sounds confident even when it is wrong, because confidence in its tone is not the same as being right. It is predicting plausible words, not checking facts. Ask it about a payment message field and it may describe a field that sounds exactly right but does not exist in that message type.

In a payments example, suppose you ask it to summarise the charges on a cross-border payment of EUR 25,000. It may produce a neat table of fees that looks professional and complete, but invent a fee of EUR 15 that your bank never charges, or miss the correspondent bank charge entirely. The output reads smoothly, so nobody questions it, and the error travels into a document, a test case, or a client communication.

That is why the rule for generative AI is simple. Use it to draft and to summarise, never to decide. Treat every output as a first draft that a person must check against a real source, a real scheme guide, or a real system screen.
