Chapter objective: Learner designs a payment data model, including parties, accounts, instructions, transactions, and statuses, and maps fields between a core banking system and ISO 20022 messages. Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Sanctions screening: Checking payment parties, countries and references against sanctions lists published by governments and international bodies.

Execution date: The date on which the bank executes a payment instruction, debiting the debtor account and sending the message onward.

Exercise

Read the pacs.008 message described in this lesson and identify its key parts.

Use the message structure, fields and example in this lesson.

  1. What to do. List the three levels of the message and name the field that carries the amount and the field that carries the end-to-end reference.

Correct result. The amount is carried in the instructed amount element and the reference in the end-to-end identification. The three levels are the group header, the payment information, and the credit transfer transaction information.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The group header identifies the message, the payment information identifies the batch, and the transaction information carries the single payment.

Emma Wilson logs in to Barclays online banking on the morning of 5 November 2026 and creates a new international payment. She enters the creditor as Schneider GmbH, Musterstrasse 12, 10115 Berlin, Germany, with IBAN DE89 3704 0044 0532 0130 00 at Deutsche Bank, BIC DEUTDEFFXXX. She enters the amount as EUR 8,750.00, the execution date as 6 November 2026, the payment purpose as supplier payment for invoice INV-2026-1147, and the end to end reference as INV-2026-1147. Her debtor account is GB29 NWBK 6016 1331 9268 19, and she confirms the payment with her mobile app. The channel assigns the instruction identifier INSTR-884120 and the time 9 42 in the morning London time.

The bank creates a canonical record from her instruction, and that record becomes the single source of truth for the payment. The payment identifier is PAY-2026-1105-004821, the type is customer credit transfer, and the status is received. The debtor is Emma Wilson, GB29 NWBK 6016 1331 9268 19, and the creditor is Schneider GmbH, DE89 3704 0044 0532 0130 00. The instructed amount is EUR 8,750.00, the debtor account currency is GBP, the exchange rate is 0.8482, and the equivalent debit is GBP 7,421.36. The execution date is 2026-11-06, the charge bearer is shared, the fee is GBP 15.00, and the end to end reference is INV-2026-1147. Every field is validated against reference data before the record moves forward.

Before release, the canonical record passes through validation, enrichment, and screening. Validation confirms that the IBAN check digits are correct, the amount has two decimal places for EUR, the execution date is a working day, and the purpose code SUPP matches the description. Enrichment confirms the BIC DEUTDEFFXXX from the IBAN, adds the country code DE, and adds the cutoff time of 2 in the afternoon London time for same day value. Sanctions screening checks Emma Wilson, Schneider GmbH, Germany, and the reference INV-2026-1147, and returns no match. The payment is then approved, the status changes to released, and the audit trail records each step with a timestamp and a user or system identifier.

The released canonical record is transformed into a pacs.008 message for the next bank in the chain. The message identifier is MSG-2026-1105-004821, the instruction identifier INSTR-884120 is carried in the payment identification, and the end to end reference INV-2026-1147 is carried unchanged so Schneider GmbH can match the payment to its invoice. The debtor is Emma Wilson with account GB29 NWBK 6016 1331 9268 19, the creditor is Schneider GmbH with account DE89 3704 0044 0532 0130 00, the instructed amount is EUR 8,750.00, and the charge bearer is shared. The debtor agent is Barclays, the creditor agent is Deutsche Bank, and the remittance information carries the invoice number. Nothing in the pacs.008 invents a value that was not in the canonical record.

Your task is to repeat this walkthrough with your own example and check your work field by field. Choose a different customer, a different creditor country, and a different currency, for example a payment of USD 12,300.00 from a customer in London to a supplier in Singapore. Write down the instruction as the customer enters it, build the canonical record with the payment identifier, debtor account, creditor account, amount and currency, execution date, payment purpose, and end to end reference, and then list each pacs.008 field that your canonical record produces. Check that every pacs.008 value can be traced back to the canonical record, that the amount precision matches the currency, and that the end to end reference is unchanged. If any value cannot be traced, your mapping has a gap.
