Key terms

Authorisation: Approval by the issuer for a card transaction to proceed, confirming the card is valid and funds or credit are available.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Scheme: A card or payment network that sets the rules, standards and fees for transactions between issuers and acquirers.

Practise this lesson

Exercise

Work out what the beneficiary receives for a EUR 15,000 payment under OUR, SHA and BEN.

Use the charge arrangement rules in this lesson and the correspondent and beneficiary bank fees named in the lesson.

  1. What to do. Calculate the amount credited under each of the three arrangements and state who pays each fee.

Correct result. Under OUR the sender pays all charges and the beneficiary receives the full amount. Under SHA the sender pays the sending charges and the beneficiary pays the receiving charges. Under BEN the beneficiary pays all charges and receives the amount less all fees.

Why. The charge arrangement decides who pays and how much arrives. A customer who was promised the full amount and receives less will complain, and the complaint lands on the analyst.

Every AI output that goes into a project needs a quality gate, and the gate is a person, not another AI tool. The gate has four steps, and you should run them in the same order every time. Step one is the human walkthrough. Read the output out loud, slowly, and ask yourself whether each sentence is true for your bank, your flow, and your systems.

Step two is the check against the real flow. Take a real payment, for example a payment of EUR 15,000 to a supplier, and walk it through the AI draft step by step. Does the draft include the validation, the sanctions check, the authorisation, the sending, the settlement, and the confirmation. If any step is missing, the draft fails the gate.

Step three is the source check. Every fact in the draft must trace to a real source, a scheme guide, a policy document, a system screen, or a named person in operations. If a fact has no source, mark it as unverified and do not let it into the BRD.

Step four is the sign-off step. A named person, usually the BA and the product owner together, signs the document and owns it. That signature means a human has checked it, not that an AI tool produced it quickly. Write the gate down in your project plan, with the names of the people who run it, and the date each draft passed it. If a stakeholder asks why the BRD took two weeks when the AI draft took three minutes, the answer is the gate. Three minutes produced a draft. Two weeks produced a document the bank can build from, test against, and defend to an auditor. That is the difference, and it is worth saying out loud.
