What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain field 57a account with institution: when and why it appears. Field 57a identifies the account with institution, the bank at which the beneficiary holds the account that must be credited.

Why it matters. A payment of 250,000 pounds to a beneficiary in London that names the wrong account with institution can sit in a repair queue for three days while operations teams exchange MT199 free format messages to correct it.

Chapter objective: Learner explains the optional MT103 fields 52a, 57a, 53B, 70 and 71G, and shows how each one changes routing, charges and remittance information. Learner uses real examples with amounts, banks and message names to show how the topic works in practice.

  • Field 57a deciding the receiver next step

  • Field 56a failure and recovery

Key terms

Suspense account: A temporary account used to hold funds or entries that cannot yet be posted to their final account. Banks use suspense accounts for unidentified receipts, pending investigations and items awaiting...

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Clearing: The process of exchanging payment instructions between banks and calculating what each bank owes the others.

Field 57a identifies the account with institution, the bank at which the beneficiary holds the account that must be credited. For example, a payment of 120,000 dollars is sent to a beneficiary whose account is held with Banco Santander in Madrid, but the MT103 is sent first to Wells Fargo in New York because the sender holds a dollar account there. Wells Fargo is the receiver, Banco Santander is the account with institution in field 57A with BIC BSCHESMM, and Wells Fargo must forward the payment to Santander rather than crediting any account of its own.

Field 57a is required whenever the receiver is not the bank that holds the beneficiary account. If the sender sends the MT103 directly to Banco Santander, the receiver and the account with institution are the same bank, so field 57a is omitted on that final leg. That omission is not an error. It is the signal that the payment has arrived at its destination bank and can be credited directly. Learners who add field 57a on the final leg create confusion, because the receiver then has to decide whether to forward the payment or credit it.

Field 57a has the same format options as field 52a. Option A carries the BIC, such as BSCHESMM for Banco Santander in Madrid, and is the normal choice when the beneficiary bank is a SWIFT member. Option D carries the name and address when the beneficiary bank has no BIC or when the sender wants to give a full postal address for a small bank. Option B can carry a party identifier, such as a local clearing code. The sender should use the most precise option available, because a vague 57D can delay the payment while the receiver searches for the right bank.

The receiver uses field 57a to decide its next step. If field 57a names a bank for which the receiver holds an account, the receiver debits its nostro account, deducts any charge it is entitled to, and sends a new MT103 to that bank. If field 57a names a bank the receiver cannot reach, the receiver must either find a route through another correspondent or return the payment. For example, if Wells Fargo receives an MT103 with field 57A naming a small bank in Peru that it cannot reach directly, it may forward the payment through Citibank in Lima, and that next hop becomes a new leg in the chain.

A missing or wrong field 57a is one of the most expensive errors in payments. If the sender omits it when the receiver is not the beneficiary bank, the receiver may credit a suspense account, hold the funds for investigation, or return the payment as unclear. If the sender enters the wrong BIC, the funds may go to the wrong bank and need to be recalled, which can take days and cost fees. A payment of 250,000 pounds to a beneficiary in London that names the wrong account with institution can sit in a repair queue for three days while operations teams exchange MT199 free format messages to correct it.

Worked example with real numbers: a bank in Paris sends USD 367,574.90 to a beneficiary in Madrid, through Wells Fargo in New York as intermediary and Bank of New York Mellon as account with institution, and the MT103 carries field 56A with Wells Fargo BIC PNBPUS3N and field 57A with Bank of New York Mellon BIC IRVTUS3N. The 56A tells the next bank to forward the payment to Wells Fargo, and the 57A tells Wells Fargo to credit the account it holds for Bank of New York Mellon, and the two fields together describe a chain with two hops, not one. Source: SWIFT MT103 format specifications; Paiementor serial analysis of field 56A.

Failure and recovery: field 56a fails when the intermediary BIC is stale, or when the intermediary is named but the account with institution is not, so the intermediary receives the payment and does not know where to send it next, and holds it in a queue. The recovery is a pacs.028 status request to the intermediary, asking what it did with the payment, followed by a corrected instruction naming the right intermediary and the right account with institution, and a resend if the payment was returned. A payment held by an intermediary for a week, because the 57A was missing, will be returned, and the sender must resend it with both fields, which takes days, so the BA must require both fields to be validated before the payment is sent, not after it is held.

For a business analyst, product owner or subject matter expert, the 56a requirement must name when an intermediary appears, how 56a differs from 57a, and the 56A plus 57A combination, with a worked serial chain showing 56a changing leg by leg. In ISO 20022 the intermediary is carried in the intermediary agent fields of the pacs.008, and the requirement must map field 56a to those fields, so that a payment migrated from MT to MX carries the same chain. A payment where the intermediary is named in MT but lost in MX is a payment that will go to the wrong bank, and the migration test must check the chain, not just the amount.
