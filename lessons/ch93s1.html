Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

The SEPA Credit Transfer scheme is the workhorse of euro payments. It was launched on 28 January 2008, it is managed by the European Payments Council, and it lets any account holder in the SEPA area send euro to any other account holder in the SEPA area using only the IBAN. The scheme covers one-off payments and bulk payments, it has no maximum amount, and it runs on the ISO 20022 pacs.008 message between banks, with the customer instruction usually arriving as a pain.001. The SCT rulebook is the single document that defines what every participating bank must do, and a bank that joins the scheme signs an adherence agreement that binds it to the rulebook.

The execution deadline is set by law, not by the scheme. The Payment Services Directive requires the payee bank to credit the payee account by the end of the next business day after the payer bank receives the instruction, a rule known as D plus 1. In practice most banks are faster, and many offer same-day execution for payments received before an internal cut-off, typically between 14:00 and 16:00. The timeline has three parts: the payer bank validates and debits, the interbank leg settles through a clearing and settlement mechanism or TARGET2, and the payee bank credits the account. A payment received after the cut-off is treated as received on the next business day, which is why the same instruction sent at 15:05 and at 14:55 can have different value dates.

The message flow is simple on the surface and exact underneath. The payer sends a pain.001 to their bank, or enters the payment in online banking, and the bank converts it into a pacs.008. The pacs.008 carries the debtor IBAN, the creditor IBAN, the amount, the end-to-end identification, the instruction identification, and the remittance information. The BIC was mandatory in the early years of SEPA and became optional in 2016, so today the IBAN alone identifies the account and the bank derives the BIC from it. Remittance information can be unstructured free text up to 140 characters, or structured, and the structured form is the one corporates use because it carries an invoice reference their reconciliation engine can match. The creditor bank answers with a pacs.002 payment status report when the payment is accepted or rejected.

When something goes wrong the scheme has a fixed vocabulary of R-transactions, and using the wrong one is itself an error. A reject happens before settlement, when the payer bank or the clearing system refuses the payment, for example because the IBAN fails the check digit test. A return happens after settlement, when the payee bank sends the money back, and it must do so within three banking business days of the settlement date for technical reasons such as a closed account. A recall is different: the payer asks for the money back after settlement, and the payee bank must respond, but the recall does not guarantee a refund because the payee may already have spent the funds. The SCT inquiry procedure, built on the pacs.028 status request, the camt.056 cancellation request and the camt.029 resolution of investigation, gives banks a standard way to chase a missing payment, correct a value date, or claim a refund of charges.

Take a worked example. A German manufacturer pays a French supplier 47,500 euro on a Tuesday. The payment is entered at 14:20, before the bank cut-off of 15:00, so the bank debits the account the same day, sends the pacs.008 through its clearing system, and the payment settles that evening. The French bank credits the supplier on Wednesday morning, which is the value date, and the supplier reconciliation matches the payment on the end-to-end identification. The charges follow the SHARE principle: each bank charges its own customer and no bank deducts from the amount in transit, so the supplier receives exactly 47,500 euro. If the same payment had been entered at 15:05, after the cut-off, the value date would move to Thursday, and the supplier would have waited an extra day.

The failure modes are where the detail matters. A wrong IBAN that passes the check digit test will reach the wrong account, and before Verification of Payee there was no systematic check that the name matched the account. A remittance reference longer than 140 characters is truncated, and the truncated reference breaks the payee reconciliation. A duplicate submission, the same file sent twice, will double-pay unless the bank checks the instruction identification and the end-to-end identification, which is why idempotency is a control, not a nice extra. A payment to a bank that is not reachable for SCT is rejected before settlement, and the payer bank must tell the customer why. A sanctions hit mid-flight stops the payment in screening, and the payment waits for a human decision while the D plus 1 clock keeps running.

For a business analyst, SCT work is a requirements discipline built on the rulebook. Every requirement should cite the rulebook clause it implements, every cut-off must be stated as a business rule with a time zone, and every exception path must name the R-transaction and the deadline that applies. The acceptance criteria must include a test where a payment entered before the cut-off is credited on the next business day, a test where a payment entered after the cut-off moves a day, a test where a return is sent within three banking business days, and a test where a duplicate file is detected and blocked.
