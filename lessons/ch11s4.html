Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Creditor: The party that is owed money and receives a payment, such as a supplier, a seller or a service provider.

Show transcript

A direct debit is a pull payment. The creditor pulls money from the debtor account, but only because the debtor signed a mandate first. It is the standard way Europe collects utility bills, insurance premiums, subscriptions, and loan instalments. There are two variants. The Core scheme is open to all customers, and the B2B scheme is built for business debtors and has different refund rules.

The mandate is the legal heart of the scheme. A mandate carries a unique reference, the creditor identifier, the debtor name and IBAN, and the signature date. The debtor can sign on paper or electronically, and the creditor must store the mandate and produce it if disputed. The debtor can cancel a mandate at any time. A mandate with no collection for 36 months lapses, and a collection presented without a valid mandate is unauthorised and must be refunded.

The creditor starts a collection with a pain.008 message. The creditor sends a pain.008, a customer direct debit initiation, to its own bank ahead of the due date. The message carries the mandate reference, the sequence type, first, recurrent, final, or one off, the due date, and the amount. The creditor bank checks the mandate reference, the creditor identifier, and the format. The creditor bank sends a pacs.003 to the debtor bank.

The interbank collection is a pacs.003, a financial institution to financial institution direct debit. It travels through the clearing system to the debtor bank, which checks the mandate data and debits the account on the due date. In the B2B scheme the debtor bank must verify the mandate before it debits, because the business debtor gives up the refund right in exchange. The creditor bank credits the creditor once the collection settles. Rejects stop a collection before the due date.

When the debtor bank cannot process a collection, it reports a pacs.002 with status RJCT and a reason code. The common codes are MD01 for no mandate, MD02 for mandate data that is missing or wrong, AC04 for a closed account, AG01 for a forbidden transaction, and MS02 or MS03 where no specific reason is given. The creditor must fix the mandate data or contact the debtor. Returns send the money back after the debit. A return happens when the debit has settled but the money cannot stay debited, and it travels back as a pacs.004.

The usual reason codes are AM04 for insufficient funds, AC04 for a closed account, AC06 for a blocked account, and MD07 for a deceased debtor. The creditor must decide whether to retry, contact the debtor, or use another method, and the reason code makes that decision possible. Refunds are a right in Core, and not in B2B. In the Core scheme the debtor can claim a refund of an authorised collection within eight weeks of the debit date, with no reason needed, under reason code MD06. For an unauthorised collection the debtor has thirteen months to claim.

In the B2B scheme there is no refund right for authorised collections, because the debtor bank verified the mandate before debiting. Reversals correct the creditor own mistakes. When a creditor collects the wrong amount or collects twice, it reverses the collection itself with a pain.007 to its bank, which sends a pacs.007, a payment reversal, to the debtor bank. The usual reason codes are AM05 for a duplicate collection and MS02 or MS03 for other creditor errors. A reversal must be sent shortly after the original collection, inside the window the rulebook sets.

R-transactions are the analyst checklist. Rejects, refusals, returns, refunds, reversals, and revocations are called R-transactions, and every one of them has a message and a reason code defined in the rulebook. The analyst turns that list into screens, files, reconciliation reports, and customer messages. A collection with no matching status message is one nobody is watching, and an unmapped reason code becomes a call to the service desk. What the analyst must check before go live.

The checklist has five items. Mandate storage and retrieval must work, including amendments and cancellations. Sequence types must be set correctly, first, recurrent, final, or one off. Due date lead times must follow the rulebook. Refund handling must differ between Core and B2B. And every reason code must map to a clear action and a clear message.

A product that gets any of these wrong fails one collection at a time.
