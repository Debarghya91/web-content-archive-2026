What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain components 7 to 12: from fraud checks to clearing. The Fraud Detection Engine scores every payment for the likelihood that it is unauthorised, manipulated or part of a scam.

Why it matters. If it fails, payments stall in unknown states, duplicate processing becomes likely, and operations teams cannot tell a customer whether a 1 million dollar payment is sent, held or lost.

Chapter objective: Learner designs enterprise payment hub architecture for a large bank, integrating channels, rails, screening, liquidity, and operations into one governed, scalable platform. Learner practises this skill on realistic banking cases, explaining the reasoning clearly and using correct terminology throughout the work.

Key learning points

  • Fraud Detection Engine scoring every payment for scam likelihood
  • Screened and fraud checked
  • Routing tables
  • Transmitted message and delivery
  • Settlement account overdraft
  • Rail disconnection consequences

Key terms

Intraday liquidity: Cash available to a bank during the business day to settle payments as they fall due. Intraday liquidity comes from account balances, incoming payments and central bank facilities.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Use the hub components and their order in this lesson.

  1. What to do. List each component the payment passes through, what it does, and what happens if it fails.

Correct result. The payment enters through a channel, passes the gateway, validation, fraud and sanctions checks, routing, and the ledger, then leaves through the right clearing system. A failure at any component stops or holds the payment, and the hub must record where it stopped.

Why. The hub is where most payment requirements live. If you cannot map a payment through it, you cannot specify, test or fix it.
