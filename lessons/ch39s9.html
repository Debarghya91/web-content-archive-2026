Show transcript

A bank that expects a large credit needs to know it is coming, so that it can plan its liquidity, match the credit when it arrives, and chase it if it does not. The notification to receive is the message that carries that warning, and in ISO 20022 it is camt.057. Its full name is Notification to Receive, and it is sent by the account owner, or by a party acting for the account owner, to the account servicing institution, to advise that a specified credit is expected on a specified account on a specified date. It does not move money. It is an advice, and its value is in the planning and the matching it enables.

The message carries the facts the receiving bank needs to prepare. It identifies the account that will be credited, the expected amount and currency, the expected value date, and the debtor who will send the payment, with enough remittance information for the bank to match the advice to the credit when it arrives. The account owner generates the advice from its own systems, usually from a treasury or enterprise system that knows a receipt is due, and sends it to the bank ahead of the value date. The bank records the expected credit, includes it in its liquidity forecast for that account, and sets up a matching rule, so that when the credit arrives, it is matched to the advice automatically, and any difference in amount or date is flagged immediately.

The notification to receive has two companions that complete the set. If the expected credit will not arrive, or the advice was sent in error, the account owner sends camt.058, the Notification to Receive Cancellation Advice, to cancel the earlier advice, so that the bank stops expecting the credit and removes it from the forecast. When the credit arrives, or fails to arrive, the bank sends camt.059, the Notification to Receive Status Report, to tell the account owner what happened: the credit was received as expected, it arrived with a different amount or on a different date, or it did not arrive. The three messages form a complete cycle: advise, cancel if needed, and report the outcome, and the analyst must design for all three, not just the advice.

In the MT world, the equivalent of camt.057 is MT210, the Notice to Receive, and the migration from MT210 to camt.057 is a straightforward mapping of the same business content into the richer ISO 20022 structure. The MT210 carries the account, the amount, the value date and the ordering party, and camt.057 carries the same facts with structured party and account identification and richer remittance information. A bank that receives MT210 advices today and camt.057 advices tomorrow must handle both during the migration, and must match both to credits in the same way, so that the liquidity forecast does not double count a credit that was advised in both formats. The analyst must include the dual format matching rule in the migration design.

The corporate use case is where the message earns its keep. A corporate treasury knows that a customer will pay a large invoice on Friday, and it sends a camt.057 to its bank on Wednesday, advising the expected credit. The bank includes the credit in the Friday liquidity forecast, and the treasury can plan its own payments for Friday knowing the credit is expected. On Friday the credit arrives, the bank matches it to the advice, and the treasury sees the matched receipt in its reporting without manual work. If the credit does not arrive, the bank sends a camt.059 reporting non receipt, and the treasury chases the customer the same day, instead of discovering the missing receipt at the month end reconciliation. The advice turns a surprise into a plan.

A worked example shows the matching. A treasury advises its bank to expect 2 million euros on 15 June from a named debtor, referencing invoice 8841. The bank records the expected credit. On 15 June a credit of 2 million euros arrives from the named debtor with the invoice reference, and the bank matches it to the advice automatically, reporting a matched receipt. In a second case, the credit arrives for 1.9 million euros, and the bank matches it with a difference flag, reporting the shortfall to the treasury, which chases the debtor for the missing 100,000 euros. In a third case, no credit arrives, and the bank reports non receipt, so the treasury knows the same day. The advice, the match and the report are three messages, and together they close the loop on every expected receipt.
