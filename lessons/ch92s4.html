Key terms

Authorisation: Approval by the issuer for a card transaction to proceed, confirming the card is valid and funds or credit are available.

Payment hub: A central system in a bank that receives payment instructions from all channels, validates them, applies screening and routing rules, and sends them to the correct clearing system or correspondent.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Same Day ACH is the faster settlement service inside the ACH network. It was introduced in 2016 and expanded in stages, and it lets an ACH entry settle on the same day it is submitted, instead of one or two days later. It did not change the ACH file structure, the SEC codes, or the return rules. It changed only the settlement timing, and that single change made ACH useful for payments that used to require a wire. The windows. There are three Same Day ACH submission deadlines each business day, at 10.30 am, 2.45 pm and 4.45 pm Eastern Time. A file submitted by 10.30 am settles by 1.00 pm. A file submitted by 2.45 pm settles by 5.00 pm. A file submitted by 4.45 pm settles by 6.00 pm. The RDFI must make the funds available to the receiver by the end of its processing day. The deadlines are absolute. A file submitted at 10.31 am does not settle at 1.00 pm, it waits for the next window, and the originator pays the same-day fee for a payment that arrives at 5.00 pm. A business analyst must write the requirement that the payment hub displays the next Same Day ACH deadline to the user, and that it warns the user when the chosen value date cannot be met. The limit. A Same Day ACH entry cannot exceed 1 million dollars. The limit was 25,000 dollars when the service launched, was raised to 100,000 dollars in 2020, and was raised to 1 million dollars in March 2022. The limit is per entry, not per file, so a file can contain many entries each up to 1 million dollars. International ACH transactions, IAT entries, are not eligible for Same Day ACH. Neither are entries that would breach the limit, and the ODFI must reject them or route them as standard ACH. The limit matters for product design, because a business that needs to pay 1.5 million dollars today cannot use Same Day ACH, and must use a wire or split the payment, and splitting a payment to get under a limit is a practice the bank should not encourage. What it changed. Same Day ACH changed the economics of urgent payments. A supplier payment that used to cost 25 dollars by wire can now cost less than a dollar by Same Day ACH. Emergency payroll, insurance claim payouts, tax payments and invoice settlements all moved to Same Day ACH, because the money arrives the same day at a fraction of the wire cost. It also changed the operational rhythm of the ODFI. The treasury team now manages three intraday deadlines instead of one end-of-day file, and the fraud team must screen same-day files faster, because there is no overnight window to catch a bad entry before it settles. What it did not change. The return rules are unchanged. A Same Day ACH debit can still be returned as unauthorised for up to 60 days. The RDFI still has the same posting obligations. The authorisation requirements are unchanged. A company that debits a consumer without authorisation faces the same return and the same NACHA scrutiny whether the entry settled the same day or two days later. The most common Same Day ACH failure is a missed deadline, and the second most common is a file that the RDFI cannot post in time because it arrived after the RDFI's own internal cutoff. Both are fixed by better cutoff management, not by faster technology. Worked example. A company owes a supplier 850,000 dollars and the invoice is due today. At 2.00 pm the accounts payable team submits a Same Day ACH file with one CCD entry for 850,000 dollars. The ODFI validates the file and submits it to FedACH by the 2.45 pm deadline. The entry settles at 5.00 pm, and the supplier's bank makes the funds available the same evening. The cost is under a dollar. Had the team submitted the file at 3.00 pm, it would have missed the second window and settled at 6.00 pm, still the same day but an hour later. Had the amount been 1.5 million dollars, the entry would have been rejected as over the limit, and the company would have needed a Fedwire payment instead, at a cost of around 25 dollars and with a manual approval step.
