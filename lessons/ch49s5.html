What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain notification, advising and confirmation. Notification starts when the issuing bank sends the standby to the advising bank.

Why it matters. This matters because cross-border payments involve more banks, more charges, more time and more ways to fail than any domestic payment. Getting the chain right is the job.

Chapter objective: Learner explains what a standby letter of credit is, distinguishes it from a commercial letter of credit, and describes issuance, notification, drawing and expiry. Learner uses real examples with amounts, banks and message names to show how the topic works in practice.

Key terms

Documentary credit: A letter of credit, a bank undertaking to pay a seller when the seller presents documents that comply with the credit terms.

Authorisation: Approval by the issuer for a card transaction to proceed, confirming the card is valid and funds or credit are available.

Reject: A payment instruction that is refused before settlement because it fails validation, screening or account checks.

Exercise

An issuing bank sends a standby to an advising bank, which must authenticate it before delivering it to the beneficiary. The beneficiary is considering asking a second bank to confirm the standby. Explain what the advising bank must do if it cannot establish authenticity, and what confirmation would add.

Use the notification, advising and confirmation rules in this lesson.

  1. What to do. State the advising bank duty on failed authentication, and state what a confirming bank undertaking means for the beneficiary.

Correct result. If the advising bank cannot establish authenticity, it must tell the issuing bank at once and must not advise the beneficiary. Confirmation adds a second bank payment undertaking to the standby, in addition to the issuing bank undertaking, so the beneficiary holds two bank promises instead of one.

Why. Advising without authentication would hand the beneficiary a document it cannot rely on. Confirmation is a credit decision by the confirming bank, and it changes who the beneficiary can call on for payment.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

Notification starts when the issuing bank sends the standby to the advising bank. In SWIFT terms, the issuing bank sends an MT760, which is the message type for the issue of a documentary credit, and the standby is a form of documentary credit. The MT760 contains the full undertaking, the amount, the expiry date, the place of presentation, and the documents required. The advising bank receives the message through the SWIFT network, where authentication is built into the system, and it must verify that the message is genuine before it passes it on to the beneficiary.

Advising means the advising bank delivers the standby to the beneficiary. The advising bank uses reasonable care to check the apparent authenticity of the message, and it must advise the standby exactly as received, without changing the text. If the advising bank cannot establish authenticity, it must tell the issuing bank at once and must not advise the beneficiary. A bank in Paris advising a standby issued by a bank in Jakarta will check the SWIFT authentication and the issuing bank details before it forwards the text to the French beneficiary by secure mail or online banking.

An advising bank may advise the credit without any undertaking to pay. That is the normal case. The beneficiary must understand that the advising bank name on the advice does not mean that bank will pay. If the advising bank is asked to confirm the standby, it makes a separate decision, based on the issuing bank credit risk, the country risk, and its own limits. Confirmation is not automatic, and the advising bank may decline to confirm even though it is willing to advise.

The beneficiary should ask for confirmation before the deal is signed, not after. When a bank agrees to confirm, it sends its confirmation to the beneficiary, often through a MT767 amendment or a separate advice, and it adds its own undertaking to honour a complying presentation. The beneficiary can then present documents to the confirming bank in its own country, and be paid in local time and local practice. A beneficiary in Mexico may prefer confirmation by JPMorgan Chase in New York on a standby issued by a bank in Argentina, for 900,000 dollars, because it trusts the confirming bank and can present documents without sending them abroad.

Amendments to a standby travel by MT767, and the same advising and confirmation rules apply. The issuing bank may amend the amount, extend the expiry date, or change the documents required, but an amendment is only effective when the beneficiary accepts it. A beneficiary who receives an MT767 reducing the amount from 500,000 dollars to 300,000 dollars can reject it, and the original terms remain in force. The beneficiary should check every amendment against the underlying agreement, because a silent acceptance can remove protection that the beneficiary still needs.
