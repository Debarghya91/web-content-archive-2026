Practise this lesson

Exercise

Work out what the beneficiary receives for a EUR 60,000 payment under OUR, SHA and BEN.

Use the charge arrangement rules in this lesson and the correspondent and beneficiary bank fees named in the lesson.

  1. What to do. Calculate the amount credited under each of the three arrangements and state who pays each fee.

Correct result. Under OUR the sender pays all charges and the beneficiary receives the full amount. Under SHA the sender pays the sending charges and the beneficiary pays the receiving charges. Under BEN the beneficiary pays all charges and receives the amount less all fees.

Why. The charge arrangement decides who pays and how much arrives. A customer who was promised the full amount and receives less will complain, and the complaint lands on the analyst.

Every new technology arrives with hype, and AI has arrived with more hype than most, so a BA needs a way to separate a real capability from a sales pitch. Hype sounds like this. The AI understands your business. The AI will replace your analysts. The AI is ninety-nine percent accurate. Reality sounds like this. The AI matches patterns in the data it was trained on. It will change what your analysts spend their time on. It is accurate on the test data the vendor chose to show you.

The difference is evidence, and asking for evidence is your job. When a vendor says a tool can read payment instructions, ask to see it read fifty of your bank's real instructions, including the messy ones, and count how many it gets right. When a vendor says it can draft requirements, ask for a draft on one of your real flows, then check it yourself for missing legs.

Here is a concrete test. A vendor claims its tool can classify payment exceptions with ninety-five percent accuracy. Ask for the test on a thousand exceptions from last month, including the EUR 60,000 payment that failed because the beneficiary bank had changed its account details. If the tool misses that case, the ninety-five percent figure means very little to your operations team.

The BA's job is not to repeat vendor claims in a business case. It is to ask for evidence, run a small test on real data, write down what the tool actually did, and record the limits alongside the benefits. A business case that says the tool handles eighty percent of cases and routes the rest to a person is honest, and it will survive contact with production. A business case that says the AI understands payments will not.

Hype Versus Reality, and What This Means for a BA

Every new technology arrives with hype, and AI has arrived with more hype than most, so a BA needs a way to separate a real capability from a sales pitch. Hype sounds like this. The AI understands your business. The AI will replace your analysts. The AI is ninety-nine percent accurate. Reality sounds like this. The AI matches patterns in the data it was trained on. It will change what your analysts spend their time on. It is accurate on the test data the vendor chose to show you.

The difference is evidence, and asking for evidence is your job. When a vendor says a tool can read payment instructions, ask to see it read fifty of your bank's real instructions, including the messy ones, and count how many it gets right. When a vendor says it can draft requirements, ask for a draft on one of your real flows, then check it yourself for missing legs.

Here is a concrete test. A vendor claims its tool can classify payment exceptions with ninety-five percent accuracy. Ask for the test on a thousand exceptions from last month, including the EUR 60,000 payment that failed because the beneficiary bank had changed its account details. If the tool misses that case, the ninety-five percent figure means very little to your operations team.

The BA's job is not to repeat vendor claims in a business case. It is to ask for evidence, run a small test on real data, write down what the tool actually did, and record the limits alongside the benefits. A business case that says the tool handles eighty percent of cases and routes the rest to a person is honest, and it will survive contact with production. A business case that says the AI understands payments will not.
