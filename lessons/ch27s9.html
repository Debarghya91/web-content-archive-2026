Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

  1. What to do. Compare the instruction, the settlement record and the statement, name the break type, and state the corrective action.

Correct result. A break is found by matching amount, currency, date and reference across the three sources. The root cause is a timing difference, an amount difference, a missing entry, or a duplicate, and the corrective action follows the cause.

Why. Reconciliation is the last line of defence. A break that is not investigated is money that cannot be proved.

Artificial intelligence is already working in payments, and it works in four places: reconciliation, fraud, customer service, and operational decisions. In reconciliation, a machine learning model reads thousands of bank statement entries and ledger items, matches them by amount, reference, value date and counterparty, and flags the breaks that need a human. It learns from past matches, so it gets better at recognising that a EUR 25,000 payment with reference INV-8841 on the statement is the same as a EUR 25,000 payment with reference INV8841 in the ledger, even though the reference is written differently. That saves hours of manual matching every day. In fraud, AI scores every payment in milliseconds. The model looks at the device, the login history, the typical amounts, the payee history and the velocity over the last 24 hours, and produces a risk score with reasons. A payment that looks normal for this customer passes straight through. A payment that is unusual, for example a first payment of 90,000 pounds to a new payee from a new device at 3 in the morning, is blocked or challenged with step up authentication. The model is not a rule that says yes or no; it is a score that says how unusual this payment is, and the bank sets the threshold for when to act. The model must be retrained regularly, because fraudsters change their patterns and a model trained on last year fraud will miss this year fraud. In customer service, AI triages the contact before a human sees it. A chatbot reads the customer message, works out whether it is a missing payment, a duplicate charge, a failed direct debit or a fraud report, and routes it to the right team with the payment reference already attached. For a missing payment, the AI can check the status itself and tell the customer where the payment is, without a human agent. For a fraud report, the AI freezes the account and starts the recovery process immediately, because minutes matter. The human agent then handles the part that needs judgement, empathy and authority, while the AI handles the part that is routine and repeatable. In operational decisions, AI forecasts what the bank will need. It predicts the liquidity a bank must hold for instant payments by learning the pattern of outbound flows through the day. It predicts which payments are likely to fail validation, so operations can fix them before the cut off. It predicts which reconciliation breaks are likely to be duplicates, so the analyst looks at those first. These are not glamorous uses, but they save real money, because holding too much liquidity costs the bank the return it could have earned, and holding too little means payments queue and customers complain. AI has limits, and a payments professional must state them. A model can be wrong, and when it is wrong in fraud it either blocks a genuine payment or lets a fraud through. A model can be biased, because it learned from historical data that reflects historical decisions. A model cannot explain itself in the way a rule can, which matters when a regulator asks why a payment was blocked. And a model cannot take responsibility. The bank remains responsible for every decision the model makes, so every AI system in payments needs human oversight, a clear audit trail, and a way to turn it off. The skill is not to trust AI blindly or to reject it, but to use it where it is strong, to check it where it is weak, and to keep a human in charge of the decisions that matter.
