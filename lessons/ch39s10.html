Key terms

Cover payment: A payment method where the customer instruction travels one route while the funds travel separately as a bank to bank cover message.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

A pacs.009 is a financial institution credit transfer, the ISO 20022 message that moves money between banks. But not every pacs.009 moves money by itself. The pacs.009 carries a code that says what kind of transfer it is, and one of those codes is ADV, for advice. A pacs.009 with the ADV code advises the receiving bank that a credit transfer has been made, or will be made, through another route, without itself instructing settlement. The advice tells the receiving bank to expect the funds, and the funds arrive through the other route, usually a clearing system or a market infrastructure. Confusing an instruction with an advice will double count a payment.

The classic use is cover settlement through a market infrastructure. A customer payment travels from the debtor agent to the creditor agent as a pacs.008, and the covering funds travel separately, through a clearing system, rather than through correspondent accounts. The bank that sends the cover through the clearing system also sends a pacs.009 ADV to the creditor agent, advising it that the cover has been sent through the infrastructure and that the funds will arrive by that route. The creditor agent receives the pacs.008, receives the ADV advice, and receives the funds through the clearing system, and it matches all three together: the instruction, the advice, and the settlement. Without the advice, the creditor agent would see funds arriving from the clearing system with no explanation of which customer payment they cover.

The ADV code sits alongside the other pacs.009 codes, and the analyst must know the set. A plain pacs.009, with no special code, instructs a transfer that settles through the accounts the message itself identifies, usually correspondent accounts. A pacs.009 with the COV code is a cover payment: it instructs the transfer of covering funds for a customer payment that travels separately, and it settles through correspondent accounts. A pacs.009 with the ADV code advises that funds are moving through another route, and it does not itself settle. The three codes describe three different settlement realities, and the receiving bank processes each differently: the plain and COV messages trigger account postings, while the ADV message triggers an expectation and a matching rule, not a posting.

The matching rule is where the design work lives. When the ADV advice arrives, the receiving bank records an expected credit, linked to the customer payment by the references the advice carries, including the UETR and the instruction references. When the funds arrive through the clearing system, the bank matches the settlement to the expected credit, and the customer payment is completed. If the funds arrive without an advice, the bank must investigate, because it has money with no explanation. If the advice arrives without funds, the bank must chase, because it has an expectation with no settlement. The analyst must design both exception paths, and must make sure the matching uses the references, not the amount alone, because two payments for the same amount to the same bank are common, and amount only matching will link the wrong advice to the wrong settlement.

The MT equivalent of the ADV advice is the MT202 with the appropriate field 72 code, or an MT202 sent as an advice rather than an instruction, and the migration to pacs.009 ADV makes the advice explicit in the message code rather than implicit in a narrative field. That explicitness is the benefit of the ISO 20022 approach: the receiving system can read the code and route the message to the advice handling path automatically, without a human reading a field to decide whether the message moves money or merely advises. The analyst migrating a cover flow must map each MT202 use case to the correct pacs.009 code, and must test the ADV path separately from the COV path, because the two paths post differently, and a test that covers only one will miss the other.

A worked example shows the three messages together. A bank sends a customer payment of 500,000 euros as a pacs.008 to the creditor agent, and sends the cover through the clearing system. At the same time, it sends a pacs.009 ADV to the creditor agent, advising that 500,000 euros is on its way through the clearing system for that customer payment, with the UETR linking the advice to the pacs.008. The creditor agent receives the pacs.008, records the expected credit from the ADV, and when the clearing system settles the 500,000 euros, matches the settlement to the advice and credits the beneficiary. The customer payment, the advice and the settlement are three separate movements, and the references tie them into one transaction. The ADV code is what tells the receiving bank that the advice is not itself the settlement.
