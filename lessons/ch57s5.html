Key terms

Direct participant: A bank or institution that holds an account in a payment system and sends and receives payments directly.

Multi-addressee: A payment system arrangement where one participant, usually a bank, acts as the addressee for payments to several other institutions, such as its branches or subsidiaries.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

Every message sent to T2 is addressed to the system itself in the Business Application Header, known as the BAH. The BAH is the head.001 message that wraps every ISO 20022 payment, and it carries a From field and a To field. The From field names the BIC of the bank or branch that sends the message, and the To field names the BIC of the T2 service itself. A pacs.008 from Deutsche Bank to BNP Paribas carries From DEUTDEFFXXX and To as the T2 RTGS service, not To BNPAFRPPXXX. The receiving bank is named inside the pacs.008, not in the header that carries it.

The rule exists because T2 is a central system, not a bilateral network. In a bilateral network, a bank sends a message directly to another bank, so the header names the other bank. In T2, a bank sends a message to the central system, the system settles the payment, and the system then tells the receiving bank what happened. The sending bank does not need to know how the receiving bank connects, which network it uses or which gateway it prefers. It only needs to reach T2 through ESMIG, and T2 takes responsibility for the rest of the delivery.

Inside the pacs.008, the agent fields name the banks that the payment is really for. The instructing agent names the bank that instructs the payment, the instructed agent names the bank that must receive the payment, the debtor agent names the bank that holds the debtor account, and the creditor agent names the bank that holds the creditor account. In a direct to direct payment of EUR 4 million from Deutsche Bank to BNP Paribas, the instructing agent is DEUTDEFFXXX, the instructed agent is BNPAFRPPXXX, and the BAH To is still the T2 service.

The header and the payment carry different addresses on purpose. The reply path follows the same rule in reverse. When T2 settles the payment, it sends a pacs.002 status report back to the sending bank, with From as the T2 service and To as the sending bank BIC. It also sends a camt.054 credit notification to the receiving bank, with From as the T2 service and To as the receiving bank BIC. Neither reply is addressed bank to bank.

Both replies come from the system, because the system is the party that settled the payment and the party that knows the final result. A bank that waits for a reply directly from the other bank will wait for ever. The technical layer also explains why a multi-addressee payment looks different in the header and in the payment. A branch that sends a payment as a multi-addressee puts its own BIC in the BAH From field, because it is the technical sender, while the pacs.008 instructing agent names the direct participant that holds the account.

The BAH To is still the T2 service, and the pacs.008 instructed agent is still the receiving bank. A developer who copies the BAH From into the instructing agent, or who copies the instructed agent into the BAH To, will build a message that T2 rejects, and the test team must check both layers separately.
