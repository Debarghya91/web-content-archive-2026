Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Liquidity: Cash or assets that a bank can use immediately to meet payment obligations. Banks manage liquidity by forecasting inflows and outflows, funding nostro and settlement accounts, and using central bank...

SCT Inst: SEPA Instant Credit Transfer, a scheme for euro payments that settle within ten seconds at any time of day.

Show transcript

TIPS stands for TARGET Instant Payment Settlement, and it is a service of the Eurosystem that settles instant payments in central bank money, around the clock, every day of the year. When a customer in France sends EUR 500 to a friend in Germany through an instant payment, the two banks involved settle that payment in TIPS, and the settlement is final within seconds. TIPS does not hold customer accounts, and it does not deal with the customer directly. It holds accounts for banks and other participants, and it moves central bank money between those accounts to settle the payments their customers have instructed.

TIPS is one of the TARGET Services, which is the family of market infrastructure services operated by the Eurosystem. The best known member is TARGET, formerly called TARGET2, which settles large value payments in real time gross settlement. Another member is TARGET2-Securities, known as T2S, which settles securities transactions. A third is the Eurosystem Collateral Management System, known as ECMS, which manages collateral. TIPS sits alongside those services, and it shares their common components for access, billing, and reference data, but it has its own settlement engine, built for speed and for very high volumes of small payments.

The key feature of TIPS is settlement in central bank money, which means the funds move between accounts held at a central bank, not between commercial bank accounts. When Bank A in Italy pays Bank B in Spain through TIPS, the TIPS account of Bank A is debited and the TIPS account of Bank B is credited, and both accounts are central bank money. That makes the settlement final and risk free in the sense that the receiving bank does not carry credit risk on the sending bank for that payment. Commercial bank money, by contrast, is a claim on a commercial bank, and it carries the risk that the bank might fail. Central bank money settlement is the safest form of settlement available.

TIPS is designed for instant payments, which are payments that must be completed within seconds, not hours or days. Under the SCT Inst scheme, the maximum execution time for an instant payment is ten seconds from the moment the payer bank receives the instruction until the payee bank must have the funds available. TIPS is built to meet that deadline, with processing times measured in milliseconds for most payments. It operates twenty four hours a day, seven days a week, including weekends and public holidays, because instant payments do not stop for the weekend, and neither can the settlement service that supports them.

The participants in TIPS are mainly banks, but the service also supports other payment service providers, ancillary systems, and central banks. A bank that wants to offer instant payments to its customers can open a TIPS Dedicated Cash Account, known as a TIPS DCA, and hold the liquidity it needs for instant payments in that account. A bank that does not want to connect directly can use an instructing party, which sends and receives messages on its behalf. That flexibility means small banks can offer instant payments without building a direct connection, while large banks can connect directly and control their own liquidity in real time.
