Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

pacs.002: An ISO 20022 message one financial institution sends to another to report the status of a payment instruction.

pacs.004: An ISO 20022 message used to return a payment that was already settled but cannot be credited to the beneficiary.

Correct result. The amount is carried in the instructed amount element and the reference in the end-to-end identification. The three levels are the group header, the payment information, and the credit transfer transaction information.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The group header identifies the message, the payment information identifies the batch, and the transaction information carries the single payment.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

A pacs.002 is a status report that an agent sends to the previous agent in the payment chain to report the outcome of a payment instruction. It carries the original instruction reference, the transaction status, and, where the payment has failed, the status reason code and additional information that explains the failure. It does not move money, and it does not create a new settlement instruction. Its job is information only, and that limitation is important, because a bank that needs to move funds back must use a pacs.004 instead. The pacs.002 is the reporting tool, not the settlement tool, and confusing the two is a common operational error.

In CBPR+, a pacs.002 becomes mandatory only in the scenario of a final negative status. That means when a payment is rejected, a pacs.002 must be sent to inform the previous agent that the payment will not proceed. The rule is deliberately narrow, because CBPR+ does not want every bank in a long chain to flood the network with status reports for every successful payment. A successful payment needs no mandatory pacs.002, and an interim status such as a payment received for processing needs no mandatory report either. The mandatory obligation is triggered by one event only, a final rejection, and that is the point to remember for examinations and for live work.

The structure of a pacs.002 follows the original instruction closely, so that the previous agent can match it without manual work. It repeats the message identification of the original pacs.008, the original end to end identification, and the original instruction identification. It then states the transaction status, which for a rejection is RJCT, and it gives the status reason information, including the reason code and a short text explanation. For example, a pacs.002 from Barclays in London to Citibank in New York might report RJCT with reason AC01, incorrect account number, for a USD 75,000 payment. The previous agent uses those references to find the original payment in its own system and to inform its customer.

A rejected pacs.002 also carries practical consequences that go beyond the message itself. When the debtor agent receives the RJCT report, it must release any funds it had reserved, reverse any nostro debit it had posted in anticipation, and inform the debtor customer that the payment failed. If the debtor agent had already debited its customer USD 100,000, it must now credit that amount back, or at least explain the delay while it checks the reason. The pacs.002 therefore starts a chain of customer communication and account corrections, and a missing or late pacs.002 leaves the debtor agent guessing, with funds held and a customer waiting for an answer that never arrives.

There are also cases where a pacs.002 is useful even though it is not mandatory, and good banks use it wisely. A bank may send a pacs.002 with status ACCP to confirm that a payment has been accepted for execution, or with status ACSP to confirm that settlement is in progress, when the customer or the previous agent has asked for tracking. These optional reports help corporate treasurers who need to know where a USD 250,000 supplier payment stands, and they help operations teams to spot a stuck payment before the customer calls. The rule to carry forward is simple. A final rejection always requires a pacs.002, while other statuses are sent when they add value, never as a substitute for moving money.
