Key terms

Credit Memorandum Balance: A balance type in TARGET2 that records a credit memorandum, used in the old TARGET2 system to manage liquidity and settlement.

Reachable party: A bank or institution that can receive payments through a payment system via a direct participant, but does not itself send payments through the system.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Show transcript

TARGET Instant Payment Settlement, TIPS, is the Eurosystem service that settles instant payments in central bank money, and it has run 24 hours a day, 365 days a year since 30 November 2018. TIPS is not a scheme and not a clearing house. It is a settlement service operated by the Eurosystem, and it settles SCT Inst payments between payment service providers, in euro and, since 2022, in Swedish krona. Settlement in central bank money means the payment is final the moment TIPS settles it, because central bank money carries no credit risk, and that finality is the reason instant payments can be offered around the clock. The settlement model is the part that separates TIPS from a deferred net settlement system. Each participating bank holds a TIPS Dedicated Cash Account, a DCA, which it funds by transferring liquidity from its RTGS account in TARGET2, now called T2. When an instant payment arrives, TIPS first reserves the amount on the originator bank DCA, then forwards the payment to the beneficiary bank, and when the beneficiary bank confirms, TIPS settles by moving the reserved amount to the beneficiary bank DCA. The reservation step is what makes the payment safe: the funds are locked before the beneficiary bank is asked to credit the payee, so the beneficiary bank never credits money that is not there. Liquidity can move back to the RTGS account at any time, and the bank must manage its DCA balance in real time because an empty DCA stops payments immediately. The payment flow through TIPS has four steps and each one is visible in the messages. The originator bank sends a pacs.008 to TIPS. TIPS validates it, reserves the funds, and forwards it to the beneficiary bank. The beneficiary bank checks the account, credits the payee, and sends a pacs.002 confirmation back through TIPS. TIPS then settles the reserved funds and sends a confirmation to the originator bank. The whole sequence must complete inside the 10-second SCT Inst window, and the typical time is two to three seconds. If the beneficiary bank does not answer in time, TIPS releases the reservation and the payment fails, and the originator bank must investigate with a pacs.028 rather than resending blindly. Connectivity is where small banks meet TIPS without building a direct connection. A bank can connect directly, with its own TIPS Distinguished Name and a connection through a network service provider, or it can be a reachable party, which means an instructing party sends and receives TIPS messages on its behalf. Most smaller banks choose the reachable party route, because a direct connection needs a 24-hour operations capability and a liquidity management desk that never sleeps. The instructing party must hold the liquidity on its own DCA or on a DCA it manages for the reachable party, and the contractual split of responsibility between the two must be written down, because when a payment fails at 03:00 on a Sunday, someone must own the investigation. Liquidity management on TIPS is a real-time discipline, and it is the most common operational failure. The DCA balance must cover the net outflow of instant payments, and on a busy day a retail bank can pay out millions of euro an hour. The bank sets a floor on the DCA and an automatic top-up from its RTGS account, and it monitors the balance continuously. A Credit Memorandum Balance, a CMB, lets a bank set a limit on how much a particular reachable party or instructing party can use, which is how a sponsor bank controls the exposure of the smaller banks it carries. When the DCA runs dry, TIPS rejects new payments, and the rejections arrive as pacs.002 messages with a liquidity reason code, which the operations team must recognise immediately. The failure modes are instructive. A beneficiary bank that does not answer in time causes a timeout, and the originator bank must not assume the payment failed, because the beneficiary bank may have credited the payee after the timeout. A TIPS outage, rare but planned for, triggers the contingency procedures, and banks fall back to regular SCT for the duration. A duplicate pacs.008, sent because the first attempt timed out at the network layer, is detected by TIPS on the instruction identification, and the duplicate is rejected. Cross-currency settlement, euro to Swedish krona, runs on the same platform but with separate DCAs per currency, and a payment cannot settle in a currency the beneficiary bank has no DCA for. The lesson for operations is that instant settlement is only as good as the liquidity behind it and the timeout handling around it. For a business analyst, TIPS work is a set of integration and liquidity requirements. Every requirement should state the connection model, direct or reachable party, the DCA funding rule, the floor and the automatic top-up, the CMB limits for sponsored parties, and the timeout investigation procedure using pacs.028. The acceptance criteria must include a test where a payment settles inside 10 seconds, a test where an empty DCA rejects a payment with the correct reason code, a test where a duplicate instruction identification is rejected, and a test where a timeout triggers an investigation instead of a blind resend. Last reviewed 8 November 2026. Further reading: the TIPS user detailed functional specifications, European Central Bank; the SCT Inst rulebook, European Payments Council; the TARGET Services documentation on dedicated cash accounts.
