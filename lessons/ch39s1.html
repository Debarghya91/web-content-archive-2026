What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain catalogue at a glance: PAIN, PACS, CAMT, admi and head. The ISO 20022 catalogue is organised into families, and each family is named by its first four letters.

Why it matters. This matters because the message is the payment instruction. A wrong field, a missing element or a misunderstood format is a failed payment, a return, or a compliance breach.

Chapter objective: Learner navigates the complete ISO 20022 catalogue, selecting the right message for each business scenario across payments, cash management, securities, and reporting. Learner practises this skill on realistic banking cases, explaining the reasoning clearly and using correct terminology throughout the work.

Key terms

Market practice: An agreed way of using a message standard in a particular market or community, covering field usage, codes and validation rules.

Charge bearer: A code that states who pays the charges for a payment. In ISO 20022 the values are DEBT for debtor, CRED for creditor, SHAR for shared and SLEV for service level.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

The ISO 20022 catalogue is organised into families, and each family is named by its first four letters. The pain family covers customer to bank messages, such as a customer instructing a credit transfer or a direct debit. The pacs family covers bank to bank messages, such as one bank sending a credit transfer to another bank. The camt family covers cash management messages, such as statements, reports, and cancellation requests. The admi family covers administration messages, such as a bank telling another bank that a system is unavailable.

The head family carries the business application header that wraps a message with routing and identification information. Every ISO 20022 message name follows the same pattern, and reading it tells you a great deal. The name pacs.008.001.08 means the pacs family, message number 008 for a customer credit transfer, variant 001, and version 08. The name pain.001.001.03 means the pain family, message number 001 for a customer credit transfer initiation, variant 001, and version 03. A higher version number usually means a newer definition with new fields or changed rules, and banks must know which version each counterparty expects.

A message sent in the wrong version can be rejected even when every business field is correct. The pain family is where most payments begin, because it carries the instruction from the customer to the bank. A company sends pain.001 to instruct credit transfers, a bank replies with pain.002 to report the status of those instructions, and a creditor sends pain.008 to instruct direct debit collections. The pacs family then carries the payment between banks, with pacs.008 for a customer credit transfer and pacs.009 for a bank to bank transfer.

The camt family closes the loop, with camt.053 giving the account holder a statement and camt.054 notifying the holder of a debit or a credit. Each family hands over to the next in a clear sequence. The camt family is often underestimated, but it is the family that lets customers and banks reconcile what actually happened. A treasurer at a company in Rotterdam starts the day with camt.052, an intraday report showing payments received so far, and ends the day with camt.053, a full statement of the account.

If a payment is missing, camt.054 provides a debit or credit notification with the details needed to match it. If a payment was sent in error, camt.056 requests its cancellation, and camt.029 reports whether the investigation succeeded. Without camt messages, a bank could send money perfectly and still leave its customers unable to prove what arrived. The admi and head families support the whole catalogue by handling the plumbing around the business messages. The admi family includes messages that report that a system is down, that a message was rejected for a technical reason, or that a participant cannot be reached.

The head family, most commonly head.001, is the business application header, a wrapper that carries the sender, the receiver, the message name, the creation time, and a unique identifier for the whole business message. When you see a message described as head.001 plus pacs.008, that means a pacs.008 business message carried inside a head.001 header, and the header is what the receiving system reads first.

ISO 20022 is the international standard for financial messaging, and it is the language that modern payments run on. It replaces the SWIFT MT messages with a richer, structured, XML-based format, and it is the standard that the major payment systems and the SWIFT community are migrating to under the CBPR+ programme. The catalogue of ISO 20022 messages is organised into business areas, each identified by a four letter code. The pain messages are customer to bank, the pacs messages are bank to bank, the camt messages are cash management, the admi messages are administration, and the head messages are the business application header. An SME must know the catalogue, because every payment, every status report, and every statement in a modern payment system is carried in one of these messages.

The pain messages are the customer to bank messages. pain.001 is the customer credit transfer initiation, the message a corporate sends to its bank to instruct a payment. pain.002 is the customer payment status report, the message the bank sends back to the customer to report the status of each payment. pain.008 is the customer direct debit initiation, the message a creditor sends to its bank to collect a direct debit from a debtor. These three messages are the customer to bank family, and they are the messages a corporate treasury system generates and receives. The pain.001 is the ISO 20022 equivalent of the MT101, and the pain.008 is the equivalent of a direct debit instruction.

The pacs messages are the bank to bank messages. pacs.002 is the FI to FI payment status report, the message a bank sends to report the status of a payment. pacs.003 is the FI to FI customer direct debit, the message a bank sends to collect a direct debit from another bank. pacs.004 is the payment return, the message a bank sends to return a payment that was previously credited. pacs.008 is the FI to FI customer credit transfer, the message a bank sends to another bank to carry a customer payment, and it is the ISO 20022 equivalent of the MT103. pacs.009 is the FI to FI payment, the message a bank sends to another bank for its own payment, and it is the equivalent of the MT202. pacs.009 also has a COV variant, the FI to FI payment with cover, which is the equivalent of the MT202 COV.

The camt messages are the cash management messages. camt.029 is the resolution of investigation, the message a bank sends to report the outcome of an investigation. camt.052 is the bank to customer account report, the message a bank sends to report the account activity intraday. camt.053 is the bank to customer statement, the message a bank sends to report the account activity for a period, normally daily, and it is the ISO 20022 equivalent of the MT940 and MT950. camt.054 is the bank to customer debit credit notification, the message a bank sends to notify a customer of a single debit or credit. camt.056 is the FI to FI payment cancellation request, the message a bank sends to ask another bank to cancel a payment. camt.060 is the account reporting request, the message a customer sends to ask its bank for a statement or a report.

The admi messages are the administration messages, used for system and administration purposes, such as the admi.002 message system event acknowledgement and the admi.004 system event notification. The head messages are the business application header, head.001, which is the envelope that carries the business message, stating the sender, the receiver, the message type, and the creation date and time. The business application header is the wrapper, and the business message is the content. An SME must understand the header, because it is where the message identification, the correlation, and the duplicate detection are carried, and it is the part of the message that the payment system reads first.

Each ISO 20022 message has a version, identified by a number such as pacs.008.001.08 or pacs.008.001.10. The version matters, because the fields and the rules change between versions, and a bank must support the version its counterparty uses. The CBPR+ usage guidelines specify which version to use for cross border payments, and they state the market practice rules that narrow the ISO 20022 standard to a specific implementation. A bank that sends a pacs.008.001.10 to a bank that only supports pacs.008.001.08 will have the message rejected, or will have the fields that the older version does not support ignored. The version is therefore a compatibility issue, and it must be agreed between the parties before the first message is sent.

The external code sets are the lists of codes that ISO 20022 uses for fields such as the charge bearer, the purpose, the service level, and the status reason. The codes are published by ISO, and they are updated regularly. For example, the charge bearer code set includes DEBT, CRED, SHAR, and SLEV, which correspond to OUR, BEN, SHA, and the service level charges in the MT world. The status reason code set includes AC01, AC04, AC06, AM04, AM05, AG01, MD07, RC01, and FF01, which are the same codes used in the MT world for rejects and returns. An SME must know the code sets, because a message with a wrong code will be rejected, and a message with a code the receiving bank does not recognise will be treated as an error.

For a business analyst, the catalogue is the starting point. The analyst must be able to name the message for each business need, state the version, and identify the fields that carry the business data. The analyst must know that a customer payment initiation is a pain.001, that a bank to bank customer payment is a pacs.008, that a payment return is a pacs.004, that a status report is a pacs.002 or a pain.002, and that a statement is a camt.053. The rest of this chapter follows each message family in depth, field by field, with the mapping from the MT world and the practical exercises that turn the catalogue into working knowledge.
