Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Recall: A request by the sending bank to the receiving bank to return funds for a payment already sent, usually because of a duplicate, a technical error or fraud.

The emerging topics in this chapter all point in the same direction for a business analyst. The job is becoming more technical, more data driven, and more connected to other disciplines. A payments BA today needs to understand APIs, data models, machine learning concepts and distributed ledger basics, in addition to the traditional skills of process mapping, requirements writing and stakeholder management. That does not mean the BA needs to become a developer or a data scientist, it means the BA needs to be able to have an intelligent conversation with developers and data scientists, to challenge their assumptions, and to translate their work into business outcomes. The BA who can read an API specification, understand a fraud model's output, and explain a CBDC design choice to a non technical stakeholder is the BA who will be most in demand. The fundamentals of the job have not changed, and they will not change. Every new payment product still needs a clear problem statement, a defined user journey, a set of rules for the edge cases, a plan for failure, and evidence that the controls work. Whether the product is a CBDC wallet, a stablecoin settlement service, or an AI powered fraud tool, the BA's questions are the same. Who is the user, what do they need, what can go wrong, how do we know it worked, and who is accountable when it does not? The tools change, the regulations change, and the technology changes, but the discipline of turning a business need into a working system is constant. That is the real skill, and it is what this course has been building throughout. The best way to stay current is to follow the work of the central banks, the standards bodies and the regulators, because they set the direction for the industry. Read the Bank for International Settlements reports on cross border payments and CBDCs, follow the ISO 20022 standard as it evolves, and watch how regulators in different countries approach stablecoins and AI. Build small experiments of your own, such as calling a sandbox API or testing a fraud model on sample data, because hands on experience teaches more than reading alone. That is the BA's value: turning a technical capability into a business requirement that can be tested. The failure mode for a BA in emerging payments is to stay in the old skills and let the new work go to someone else.
