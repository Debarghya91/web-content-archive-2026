What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain bank-to-bank: pacs.002, pacs.003, pacs.004, pacs.008, pacs.009. The pacs.008 message, FI to FI Customer Credit Transfer, is the most widely used message in cross border payments, and it carries a customer payment from the debtor bank to the creditor bank.

Why it matters. Returns must be processed promptly, because a return that sits unmatched can look like a missing payment, and a customer who is not told about a return may believe the supplier has been paid when the money is on its way back.

Chapter objective: Learner navigates the complete ISO 20022 catalogue, selecting the right message for each business scenario across payments, cash management, securities, and reporting. Learner practises this skill on realistic banking cases, explaining the reasoning clearly and using correct terminology throughout the work.

Key learning points

  • FI to FI Customer Credit Transfer
  • FI to FI Financial Institution Credit Transfer
  • FI to FI Payment Status Report
  • FI to FI Customer Direct Debit
  • Pacs.004 message

Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Charge bearer: A code that states who pays the charges for a payment. In ISO 20022 the values are DEBT for debtor, CRED for creditor, SHAR for shared and SLEV for service level.

Cover payment: A payment method where the customer instruction travels one route while the funds travel separately as a bank to bank cover message.

  1. What to do. List the three levels of the message and name the field that carries the amount and the field that carries the end-to-end reference.

Correct result. The amount is carried in the instructed amount element and the reference in the end-to-end identification. The three levels are the group header, the payment information, and the credit transfer transaction information.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The group header identifies the message, the payment information identifies the batch, and the transaction information carries the single payment.

The pacs.008 message, FI to FI Customer Credit Transfer, is the most widely used message in cross border payments, and it carries a customer payment from the debtor bank to the creditor bank. Its group header identifies the message and the settlement details, and each transaction carries the instruction identifier, the end to end reference, the instructed amount, the debtor and creditor names and accounts, the debtor and creditor agents, the charge bearer, and the remittance information. A pacs.008 for EUR 8,750.00 from Barclays in London to Deutsche Bank in Frankfurt, for Schneider GmbH against invoice INV-2026-1147, is a typical example, and the creditor bank uses the end to end reference to credit the right supplier account and to inform the supplier.

The pacs.009 message, FI to FI Financial Institution Credit Transfer, carries a payment where both the debtor and the creditor are financial institutions, with no underlying customer payment. A bank may send a pacs.009 for USD 50,000,000 to settle a foreign exchange deal, to fund a nostro account, or to repay a loan to another bank. Because there is no customer instruction behind it, pacs.009 does not carry a customer end to end reference in the same way, and it carries settlement information that pacs.008 does not need, such as the settlement method and the settlement account.

A pacs.009 is also used for cover payments, where one bank sends funds to cover a payment that another bank has already made to the final creditor. The pacs.002 message, FI to FI Payment Status Report, is how banks tell each other whether a payment was accepted, rejected, or is still being processed. It carries the original message identifier, the original instruction identifier, and a status code such as ACCP for accepted, RJCT for rejected, or ACSP for accepted and in progress. A rejection carries a reason code, such as AC01 for an incorrect account number, AG01 for a transaction forbidden on this type of account, or NARR for a free text reason.

When Barclays sends a pacs.008 to Deutsche Bank and receives a pacs.002 with status RJCT and reason AC01, Barclays knows the creditor account was wrong, and it can contact the customer to correct the IBAN and send the payment again. The pacs.003 message, FI to FI Customer Direct Debit, carries a direct debit collection between banks, and it works with the mandate that the creditor bank holds. A French utility that collects EUR 85.00 from a customer in Belgium sends a pain.008 to its own bank, and that bank sends a pacs.003 to the debtor bank in Belgium.

The pacs.003 carries the mandate reference, the creditor and debtor names and accounts, the collection amount, and the collection date. The debtor bank checks the mandate and the balance, debits the customer, and credits the creditor bank through settlement. If the debtor has no mandate, the debtor bank rejects the pacs.003, and the creditor must resolve the mandate problem before trying again. The pacs.004 message, Payment Return, sends money back when a payment cannot be applied, and it is essential for keeping ledgers correct.

A pacs.004 for EUR 8,750.00 may return the Schneider GmbH payment because the creditor account DE89 3704 0044 0532 0130 00 was closed, with return reason AC04 for a closed account number. The pacs.004 carries the original instruction identifier and end to end reference, the returned amount, and the reason code, so the debtor bank can match the return to the original payment and credit the debtor. Returns must be processed promptly, because a return that sits unmatched can look like a missing payment, and a customer who is not told about a return may believe the supplier has been paid when the money is on its way back.

pacs.008 is the FI to FI customer credit transfer. It is the message a bank sends to another bank to carry a customer payment, and it is the ISO 20022 equivalent of the MT103. It is the most important bank to bank message, because it carries the majority of cross border customer payments. The pacs.008 has a group header, GrpHdr, which carries the message identification, MsgId, the creation date and time, CreDtTm, the number of transactions, NbOfTxs, the control sum, CtrlSum, the settlement information, SttlmInf, which states the settlement method, SttlmMtd, such as INDA, INGA, COVE, or CLRG, and the clearing system, ClrSys, and the instructing agent, InstgAgt, and the instructed agent, InstdAgt. It has one or more credit transfer transaction information blocks, CdtTrfTxInf, each carrying the payment identification, PmtId, with the instruction identification, InstrId, the end to end identification, EndToEndId, and the unique end to end transaction reference, UETR, the amount, Amt, with the instructed amount, InstdAmt, and the equivalent amount, EqvtAmt, the charge bearer, ChrgBr, the debtor, Dbtr, the debtor account, DbtrAcct, the debtor agent, DbtrAgt, the creditor agent, CdtrAgt, the creditor, Cdtr, the creditor account, CdtrAcct, the purpose, Purp, the remittance information, RmtInf, and the instruction for the next agent, InstrForNxtAgt.

pacs.009 is the FI to FI payment. It is the message a bank sends to another bank for its own payment, not for a customer payment, and it is the ISO 20022 equivalent of the MT202. It has a group header, GrpHdr, and a credit transfer transaction information block, CdtTrfTxInf, which carries the payment identification, the amount, the debtor agent, the creditor agent, and the settlement information. pacs.009 has a COV variant, the FI to FI payment with cover, which is the equivalent of the MT202 COV. In the COV variant, the pacs.009 carries the underlying customer credit transfer information, UndrlygCstmrCdtTrf, which states the originator, the beneficiary, the amount, and the remittance information of the customer payment that the cover payment is funding. The COV variant is used in the cover method, where the instruction travels directly to the beneficiary bank as a pacs.008, and the funds travel through the correspondent chain as a pacs.009 COV.

pacs.002 is the FI to FI payment status report. It is the message a bank sends to another bank to report the status of a payment. It has a group header, GrpHdr, and an original group information and status block, OrgnlGrpInfAndSts, and one or more transaction information and status blocks, TxInfAndSts. The status codes are ACCP, accepted, RJCT, rejected, ACSP, accepted settlement in process, ACSC, accepted settlement complete, ACCC, accepted and credited, and PART, partially accepted. The status reason information, StsRsnInf, carries the reason code, such as AC01 for an incorrect account number, AM04 for insufficient funds, AG01 for a transaction forbidden, RC01 for a bank identifier incorrect, and FF01 for an invalid file format. A pacs.002 with a status of RJCT and a reason code of AC01 tells the sending bank exactly why the payment was rejected, and the sending bank can then advise its customer.

pacs.004 is the payment return. It is the message a bank sends to return a payment that was previously credited, because the beneficiary account is closed, the beneficiary is deceased, the payment was sent in error, or the beneficiary bank cannot apply the funds. The pacs.004 has a group header, GrpHdr, and an original group information block, OrgnlGrpInf, and a transaction information block, TxInf, which carries the original instruction identification, the original end to end identification, the returned amount, RtrdAmt, and the return reason information, RtrRsnInf, with the reason code, such as AC04 for a closed account number, AC06 for a blocked account, MD07 for a deceased beneficiary, AG01 for a transaction forbidden, and AM05 for a duplicate payment. The return is a reversal of the original payment, and it must carry the original references so the sending bank can match it to the original instruction.

pacs.003 is the FI to FI customer direct debit. It is the message a bank sends to another bank to collect a direct debit from a customer. It has a group header, GrpHdr, and a direct debit transaction information block, DrctDbtTxInf, which carries the payment identification, the amount, the mandate related information, the creditor, the debtor, and the debtor agent. The pacs.003 is used in the SEPA direct debit scheme, where the creditor bank sends the collection to the debtor bank, and the debtor bank debits the debtor account. The pacs.003 is the bank to bank leg of a direct debit, and the pain.008 is the customer to bank leg. An SME must know both, because a direct debit has two legs, and the mandate must be identified in both.

The settlement information in a pacs.008 is the field that states how the payment will be settled, and it is the field that distinguishes the serial method from the cover method. SttlmInf carries the settlement method, SttlmMtd, which may be INDA, instructed agent, meaning the instructed agent is to settle the payment, INGA, instructed agent, meaning the instructed agent is to settle the payment on behalf of the instructing agent, COVE, cover payment, meaning the payment is covered by a separate pacs.009, or CLRG, clearing system, meaning the payment will be settled through a clearing system. The settlement method is the ISO 20022 equivalent of the MT103 field 53a and field 72 codes, and it determines whether the funds travel with the instruction or separately. An SME must know the four codes, because they determine the settlement, and a wrong code will cause the payment to be settled incorrectly.

The charge bearer in a pacs.008 is carried in the ChrgBr element, and it may be DEBT, meaning the debtor bears all charges, which is the OUR instruction, CRED, meaning the creditor bears all charges, which is the BEN instruction, SHAR, meaning the charges are shared, which is the SHA instruction, or SLEV, meaning the charges follow the service level agreement. The charge bearer determines who pays the charges, and it must match the agreement between the debtor and the creditor. A pacs.008 with ChrgBr DEBT and a deducted charge is an error, because DEBT means the beneficiary must receive the full amount, and any deduction is a breach of the instruction. The charge bearer is one of the most commonly misunderstood fields, and it is a common source of disputes between banks.

For a business analyst, the pacs messages are the bank to bank interface. The analyst must be able to specify a pacs.008 that carries a customer payment with the correct settlement method and charge bearer, a pacs.009 that carries a bank payment or a cover payment, a pacs.002 that reports the status with the correct reason code, and a pacs.004 that returns a payment with the correct return reason. The analyst must know the group header, the transaction information block, and the settlement information, and must know which fields are mandatory and which are conditional. The analyst must also know the status codes and the reason codes, because they are the language the banks use to communicate about a payment.
