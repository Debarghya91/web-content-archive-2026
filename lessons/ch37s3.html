Key terms

Sanctions screening: Checking payment parties, countries and references against sanctions lists published by governments and international bodies.

Direct participant: A bank or institution that holds an account in a payment system and sends and receives payments directly.

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

Bank of America runs its payments through a central hub that connects to every US clearing system, Fedwire for high value same-day payments, the Clearing House for ACH bulk payments, and the RTP network for real-time payments. The bank is one of the largest participants in Fedwire, settling hundreds of billions of dollars a day, and it is a direct participant in CHIPS, the private US dollar clearing system used for international payments. A payment of 5 million dollars from a Bank of America corporate client in New York to a supplier in Tokyo will be routed through CHIPS if it is a cross-border dollar payment, or through Fedwire if it is a domestic payment, and the hub makes that routing decision automatically based on the destination and the currency.

Wells Fargo has a similar clearing footprint and a particular strength in corporate and commercial payments. The bank processes payments for a large share of US middle-market companies, and its CEO Portal and Commercial Electronic Office platforms let corporate treasurers send payment files, approve payments, and view balances across all their accounts. Wells Fargo is also a major provider of lockbox services, where the bank receives and processes cheques and remittance advices on behalf of a company, and of treasury management services such as account reconciliation and fraud prevention.

A company that receives 100,000 cheques a month from its customers may outsource the entire process to Wells Fargo, which opens the envelopes, scans the cheques, and posts the credits. Both banks were early adopters of real-time payments in the United States. Bank of America was one of the first banks to offer the Clearing House RTP network to its corporate clients, letting a company send money that arrives in the beneficiary account within seconds, 24 hours a day, including weekends. Wells Fargo followed with its own RTP offering and was also an early participant in FedNow, the real-time payment system launched by the Federal Reserve in July 2023.

FedNow lets banks of any size offer instant payments, and both Bank of America and Wells Fargo use it to serve smaller corporate clients who cannot afford a direct connection to the Clearing House RTP network. A payroll company can now pay 10,000 workers on a Sunday evening and the money arrives instantly. The corporate payment services at both banks go beyond simple money movement. Bank of America offers virtual accounts, where a company can create a separate account number for each of its customers or projects while keeping a single physical account, which makes reconciliation far easier.

It also offers integrated payables, where a company sends one file and the bank decides whether each payment should go by ACH, by wire, or by virtual card, based on the supplier preference. Wells Fargo offers similar services, with a focus on commercial cards and on supply chain finance, where the bank pays a supplier early at a discount and the buyer pays the bank later. These services turn the bank from a payment processor into a working capital partner for its clients. For a business analyst, the lesson from both banks is that domestic scale and global reach need different architectures.

The domestic side is about volume, automation, and cost per transaction, processing 50 million ACH payments a month at a cost of a few cents each. The international side is about corridors, correspondents, and compliance, sending 200,000 cross-border payments a day through SWIFT, with sanctions screening on every one. A bank that is excellent at one is not automatically excellent at the other, and the payment hub must serve both. Bank of America and Wells Fargo show how a bank can run a high-volume domestic engine and a complex international engine on the same platform, with shared reference data and shared screening, but with different routing and different cut-off times.
