Key terms

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

pacs.008: An ISO 20022 message for a financial institution to financial institution customer credit transfer. It replaces MT103 in cross border payments and carries structured debtor, creditor, charges and...

MT400: A SWIFT message used to advise a bank of the collection of a documentary credit or a bill, and to request payment or acceptance.

Exercise

A collecting bank has obtained payment on a EUR 250,000 documentary collection. Explain when the MT400 is sent, what its key fields carry, and how much the exporter is credited.

Use the MT400 fields and timing rules in this lesson.

  1. What to do. State when the MT400 is sent, what field 32A and field 53a carry, and how much the exporter is credited and why.

Correct result. The MT400 is sent only after the collecting bank has completed the presentation and obtained payment or its equivalent. Field 32A carries the value date, the date the collecting bank received the proceeds or will remit them. Field 53a names the sender correspondent, the bank where the proceeds are held or through which they will be remitted. BNP Paribas matches field 21 to its file, credits the exporter EUR 249,650, and closes the collection.

Why. The MT400 is the proof that the proceeds exist, and its fields are what the remitting bank reconciles against. An MT400 sent before payment would announce money that has not arrived.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

Before an MT400 is sent, the collecting bank must have completed the presentation and obtained payment or its equivalent. The importer has been contacted, the documents have been shown, and the importer has paid the full collection amount in the stated currency, or has paid the amount due under a sight draft. The collecting bank has checked that the payment is good funds, meaning the money is cleared and available, not a cheque that may bounce or a transfer that may be recalled. Only when the funds are collected and available does the collecting bank prepare the MT400.

Sending it earlier would mislead the remitting bank into crediting the exporter before the money is secure. The MT400 is sent by the collecting bank to the remitting bank, and its purpose is to advise that payment has been made under the collection and that the proceeds are being remitted or have been remitted. In a typical flow the collecting bank is Banco Santander in Madrid and the remitting bank is BNP Paribas in Paris. Santander sends the MT400 to BNP Paribas, quoting the collection reference, the amount collected, the currency, the value date and the method of remittance.

The message tells BNP Paribas that it can now credit the exporter, subject to the funds actually arriving by the stated remittance route. It is not sent by the importer, and it is not sent to the importer. The MT400 contains a small set of structured fields that identify the collection and the payment. Field 20 gives the transaction reference number, the collection reference assigned by the sending bank. Field 21 gives the related reference, usually the remitting bank collection reference, so the two banks can match the advice to the original instruction.

Field 32A gives the value date, the currency and the amount collected, for example 26 November 2026, EUR 250,000. Field 33A gives the amount remitted after charges, if different. Field 71B gives details of charges deducted, and Field 72 gives sender to receiver information, such as the remittance route or the account to be credited. The timing relationship between the MT400 and the funds is a common source of confusion, so it must be stated plainly. The MT400 is an advice of payment, not a payment instruction, and it does not itself move money.

The funds move separately, usually by an MT103 customer payment, by an MT202 bank to bank payment, or by a book transfer if the two banks hold accounts with each other. The remitting bank should check that the funds have arrived, or that the MT400 states a remittance method it can rely on, before it credits the exporter in full. In practice many remitting banks credit the exporter on receipt of the MT400 when the collecting bank is a trusted correspondent, and reverse the credit if the funds fail to arrive.

A complete example shows the fields in context for a EUR 250,000 collection. Field 20 reads COLL 2026 0847, the Santander reference. Field 21 reads BNP COLL FR 5521, the BNP Paribas reference. Field 32A reads 26 November 2026, EUR 250,000, the amount collected from the Madrid importer. Field 33A reads EUR 249,650, the amount remitted after Santander deducts EUR 350 in collection charges.

Field 71B reads OUR charges EUR 350 deducted, and Field 72 reads proceeds remitted by pacs.008 to BNP Paribas account with its Frankfurt correspondent, value 27 November 2026. BNP Paribas matches Field 21 to its file, credits the exporter EUR 249,650, and closes the collection.
