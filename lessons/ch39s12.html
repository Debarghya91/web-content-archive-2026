Use the message structure, fields and example in this lesson.

  1. What to do. List the main blocks of the message, name the field that carries the amount, and name the field that identifies the payment.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

Show transcript

Three camt messages complete two pictures: the lifecycle of a notification to receive, and the handling of investigations that do not fit a standard format. The camt.058 and camt.059 extend the camt.057 notification to receive, which tells an account holder that funds are coming. The camt.035 carries an investigation in a proprietary format, inside a standard envelope. Together they show how ISO 20022 handles the edges of the investigation world: the expected receipt that changes, and the investigation that defies standardisation.

The camt.057, covered in its own lesson, is the notification to receive: a bank tells the account holder that funds are expected, so the holder can plan. The camt.058, the NotificationToReceiveCancellationAdvice, cancels that notification. The expected funds will not arrive, or the notification was sent in error, and the account holder must not expect the credit. The cancellation carries the reference to the original notification, the reason for the cancellation, and the identification of the expected receipt that is cancelled. Without the cancellation, the holder plans around funds that never arrive, and the liquidity plan is wrong.

The camt.059, the NotificationToReceiveStatusReport, reports the status of a notification to receive. It tells the account holder whether the expected funds have arrived, are still pending, or have failed. The status report turns an expectation into a tracked item: the holder can see which expected receipts have materialised, which are outstanding, and which need chasing. The report carries the notification reference, the status, and any additional information about the receipt. The three messages form a lifecycle: notify, track the status, and cancel if the expectation changes. Expected receipts get the same structured handling as payments themselves.

The camt.035, the ProprietaryFormatInvestigation, serves a different need. Most investigations fit the standard case messages: claim non receipt, unable to apply, request for duplicate, and the rest. Some do not. A market specific investigation, a bilateral arrangement, or a new investigation type that the standard has not yet defined needs a message, and the camt.035 provides it. The envelope is standard: the case identification, the parties, the references, and the routing all follow the camt format. The content is proprietary: the investigation data follows a format agreed between the parties, carried inside the envelope.

The proprietary pattern is familiar from the MT world, where the MT198 carries proprietary content in a standard envelope. The camt.035 applies the same idea to investigations. The parties must agree the content format before exchanging messages, document it, and test it. The network validates the envelope and delivers the message; the parties validate the content. The pattern is a safety valve: it lets the standard system carry non standard investigations without breaking, while the community decides whether the investigation type deserves a standard message of its own. A proprietary investigation that becomes common should graduate to a standard message.

For a business analyst, the lesson is about lifecycle and edge cases. The notification to receive lifecycle needs all three messages in the requirements: the notification, the status report, and the cancellation, with the states and transitions between them. The proprietary investigation needs an agreement, a documented format, and a review date: proprietary formats that persist without review become permanent shadow standards. Both patterns reward the same discipline: define the lifecycle, define the messages, and define what happens when reality does not fit the standard path.
