Key terms

Direct participant: A bank or institution that holds an account in a payment system and sends and receives payments directly.

Multi-addressee: A payment system arrangement where one participant, usually a bank, acts as the addressee for payments to several other institutions, such as its branches or subsidiaries.

Authorisation: Approval by the issuer for a card transaction to proceed, confirming the card is valid and funds or credit are available.

Exercise

Trace the EUR 250,000 payment from New York to BNP Paribas in Paris through CBPR+ and T2, stating the header rule on each leg, the role of the Madrid branch, and where settlement is booked.

Use the two leg rules in this lesson: the CBPR+ header rule, the T2 header rule, the multi-addressee authorisation, and the settlement account.

  1. What to do. For each leg, state which header rule applies, where settlement is booked, and which account T2 sees.

Correct result. The payment travels first through CBPR+ to Commerzbank Frankfurt, then through T2. On the CBPR+ leg the header names the receiving bank, and on the T2 leg the header names the T2 service. The Madrid branch, COBAESMXXXX, is a multi-addressee authorised by the direct participant, recorded in CRDM and listed in the RTGS Directory as code 07, and it submits the onward pacs.008 to T2 for the EUR 250,000 payment to BNP Paribas, BIC BNPAFRPPXXX. The multi-addressee holds no RTGS DCA, so settlement is booked on the Frankfurt direct participant RTGS DCA, and Frankfurt updates the Madrid branch position in its own internal books, while T2 never sees a Madrid account.

Why. The two leg payment fails when a bank applies the CBPR+ header rule to the T2 leg, or the T2 rule to the CBPR+ leg. The header rule for each leg must be stated separately.

A direct participant can authorise its branch to submit and receive payments directly to T2 without their involvement, and that branch is the multi-addressee. The authorisation is recorded in CRDM, the branch BIC is listed in the RTGS Directory as code 07, and the branch connects through ESMIG with its own BIC. Commerzbank Frankfurt, BIC COBADEFFXXX, is the direct participant, and its Madrid branch, BIC COBAESMXXXX, is the multi-addressee. The Madrid branch can send a pacs.008 to T2 and receive a pacs.002 and a camt.054 from T2, without asking Frankfurt to send the message for it.

The multi-addressee holds no RTGS DCA, and that fact shapes every payment it sends or receives. The account debited and credited is the direct participant account, in this case the Commerzbank Frankfurt RTGS Dedicated Cash Account. A EUR 250,000 payment sent by the Madrid branch debits the Frankfurt RTGS DCA, and a EUR 180,000 payment received by the Madrid branch credits the Frankfurt RTGS DCA. Frankfurt then updates the Madrid branch position in its own internal books, but T2 never sees a Madrid account, because no such account exists in the system.

Consider a worked example with a payment that has two legs. A bank in New York sends a EUR 250,000 payment for a Spanish corporate customer to BNP Paribas in Paris, and the payment travels first through CBPR+ to Commerzbank Frankfurt, then through T2 to BNP Paribas. On the CBPR+ leg, the payment is a pacs.008 from the New York bank to Commerzbank Frankfurt. The BAH From is the New York bank BIC, the BAH To is COBADEFFXXX, the instructing agent is the New York bank, and the instructed agent is COBADEFFXXX.

The CBPR+ rule is strict, and this leg obeys it. On the T2 leg, the Madrid branch, COBAESMXXXX, submits the onward pacs.008 to T2 for the same EUR 250,000 payment to BNP Paribas, BIC BNPAFRPPXXX. The BAH From is COBAESMXXXX, because Madrid is the technical sender, and the BAH To is the T2 service itself. The instructing agent is COBADEFFXXX, the direct participant that holds the account, and the instructed agent is BNPAFRPPXXX, the receiving bank. The account debited is the COBADEFFXXX RTGS DCA, and BNP Paribas receives a camt.054 credit notification after T2 settles the payment in central bank money.

The T2 leg shows the key difference from CBPR+. In the T2 leg the instructed agent is different from BAH To, because the instructed agent is BNPAFRPPXXX while BAH To is the T2 service. That difference is NOT possible in CBPR+, where the instructing agent must equal BAH From and the instructed agent must equal BAH To. On the CBPR+ leg, the instructed agent COBADEFFXXX equals BAH To COBADEFFXXX, exactly as the rule requires. A developer who applies the CBPR+ rule to the T2 leg will address the payment wrongly, and a developer who applies the T2 rule to the CBPR+ leg will have the payment rejected by the next bank.
