Key terms

Irrevocable: A payment or instruction that cannot be cancelled or reversed once it has been made, except by agreement of all parties or by a court order.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Correct result. Real time gross settlement settles each payment individually in central bank money with immediate finality, but it needs liquidity for every payment. Why. Settlement is where money actually moves. The model decides the risk, the liquidity need, and when the payment is truly final.

Hong Kong settles interbank payments through CHATS, its real time gross settlement system. The full name is the Clearing House Automated Transfer System. Hong Kong Interbank Clearing Limited operates the system, owned jointly by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and the Hong Kong Association of Banks. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority, Hong Kong's central banking institution, oversees the system, owns and operates the Hong Kong dollar system and settles Hong Kong dollar payments. Each payment settles on its own and in full as soon as it is processed, and settlement is final and irrevocable. The CHATS systems are designated under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance, which protects the finality of settled payments.

What makes CHATS unusual is that it settles in four currencies through four parallel systems. Hong Kong dollar CHATS dates from 1996, settles across accounts at the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and therefore settles in central bank money. United States dollar CHATS followed in 2000, euro CHATS in 2003 and renminbi CHATS in 2006, each settling across accounts at an appointed settlement institution: HSBC for United States dollars, Standard Chartered for euros and Bank of China (Hong Kong) for renminbi. Foreign currency payments settle in settlement institution money rather than central bank money, which matters for risk analysis. Each system keeps separate accounts, schedules and cut off times, so a bank must fund and manage each currency on its own.

Follow a Hong Kong dollar payment through CHATS. A property buyer pays thirty million Hong Kong dollars to a seller at another Hong Kong bank, for same day settlement. The sending bank checks the instruction, runs sanctions, anti money laundering and fraud screening, and confirms its settlement account holds enough funds. It sends the payment into CHATS over the SWIFT network using ISO 20022 messages, a pacs.008 message for a customer payment or a pacs.009 message for a payment between banks. CHATS validates the message and, when funds are available, debits the sending bank and credits the receiving bank at the same moment. The receiving bank credits the seller, and a pacs.002 report tells the sending bank whether the payment settled, queued or was rejected.

Liquidity arrangements decide how smoothly CHATS absorbs a busy day. If a bank lacks the balance to settle a payment at once, the payment joins a queue, and the bank can reorder its queued payments to bring urgent items forward. Liquidity optimisers using multilateral offsetting periodically search the queues for payments that offset each other, releasing groups together against a smaller net amount of cash. For Hong Kong dollars, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority offers interest free intraday repo and interest bearing discount window facilities, both secured by Exchange Fund Bills and Notes, and an unreversed intraday repo rolls automatically into the discount window. Banks can also borrow intraday funds from other participants.

Links from CHATS to other systems remove risks that a standalone payment cannot address. In a payment versus payment link, the two legs of a foreign exchange deal settle together, so neither currency is released unless the other is released. In a delivery versus payment link, a CHATS payment settles together with a securities transfer, so cash moves only when the securities move. Renminbi CHATS connects onward into mainland China through its clearing bank, which holds a settlement account with the People's Bank of China and participates in CNAPS, the China National Advanced Payment System, so renminbi settled in Hong Kong can reach mainland accounts.

Operating discipline in CHATS rewards banks that pay steadily through the day rather than holding payments back. Banks are expected to settle a substantial share of interbank payments well before the close, keeping liquidity recycling and reducing end of day congestion. Each system publishes its own hours and cut off times, a late payment normally waits for the next operating day, and a compliance stop holds a payment for review. Wrong beneficiary details lead to a return, and in an outage banks follow published contingency procedures coordinated with the operator and the Hong Kong Monetary Authority.

For an analyst, CHATS is best understood as four settlement systems sharing one operator and one design, not as a single multicurrency account. The analyst must identify the currency system, its settlement institution, the balance in that currency's account and the cut off that applies before treating any payment as settled. Confirmation from CHATS is the only proof of settlement, and a queued payment is not a paid payment until the debit and credit have posted. CHATS serves large value business, while Hong Kong's Faster Payment System serves smaller retail payments around the clock, so the two complement rather than duplicate each other.
