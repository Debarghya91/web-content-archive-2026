Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

  1. What to do. List the three levels of the message and name the field that carries the amount and the field that carries the end-to-end reference.

Correct result. The amount is carried in the instructed amount element and the reference in the end-to-end identification. The three levels are the group header, the payment information, and the credit transfer transaction information.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The group header identifies the message, the payment information identifies the batch, and the transaction information carries the single payment.

The payment instruction in TIPS is a pacs.008.001.08, which is version eight of the FI to FI Customer Credit Transfer message. This message carries the payer and payee details, the amount, the currency, the remittance information, and the references that let every system match the payment. When a bank in Spain sends EUR 750 to a bank in Italy through TIPS, it sends a pacs.008.001.08, and TIPS uses the fields in that message to validate the payment, to check the liquidity, and to reserve the funds. The version number matters, because TIPS expects the fields and the validation rules of version eight, and a message in an older version may be rejected before it is even processed. The status report in TIPS is a pacs.002.001.10, which is version ten of the FI to FI Payment Status Report. This message carries the outcome of the payment, and it is used by TIPS to tell the payer bank what happened, and by the payee bank to tell TIPS whether it accepts or rejects the payment. The pacs.002.001.10 repeats the references from the original pacs.008, so the payer bank can match the status to the payment it sent, and it states the transaction status in a coded field. A payer bank that sends a pacs.008 for EUR 1,500 will watch for the pacs.002.001.10 that answers it, and it will not know the final outcome until that status report arrives with a final status. TIPS uses three intermediary statuses, and each one means the payment is still in progress. The first is Received, which means TIPS has received the pacs.008 and has started to process it. The second is Validated, which means TIPS has checked the message format, the references, and the participant details, and everything is correct so far. The third is Reserved, which means TIPS has reserved the funds on the payer TIPS DCA and has forwarded the payment to the payee bank for a decision. A payment that sits at Reserved is waiting for the payee bank, and the funds are set aside but not yet moved. None of these three statuses is final, and a payment can still succeed or fail from any of them. TIPS uses three final unsuccessful statuses, and each one means the payment will not settle. The first is Failed, which means the payment failed for a technical or business reason, such as a validation error or a missing account. The second is Expired, which means the payment ran out of time, because the payee bank did not answer within the ten second deadline set by the SCT Inst scheme. The third is Rejected, which means the payee bank actively refused the payment, for example because the payee account is closed or the payee name does not match. When any of these three statuses is reached, TIPS releases the reservation, the funds return to the payer bank, and the payment is closed as unsuccessful. TIPS uses one final successful status, and it is the one every participant wants to see. That status is Settled, which means the payment has been settled in central bank money, the payer TIPS DCA has been debited, the payee TIPS DCA has been credited, and the funds are available to the payee. A pacs.002.001.10 with status Settled is the confirmation that settlement is final for the instant payment, and the payer bank can then tell its customer that the EUR 2,000 it sent has arrived. The status trail for a successful payment therefore reads Received, then Validated, then Reserved, then Settled, and each step is recorded so that operations teams and auditors can reconstruct the payment in seconds if a customer asks.
