What you will learn

Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain customer accounts, internal accounts, nostro and vostro ledgers and apply it to a real banking scenario.

Why it matters. Nostro and vostro are the language of correspondent banking, and the analyst must use them correctly.

Chapter objective: Learner explains how a bank's ledgers record every payment, distinguishes customer accounts, internal accounts, nostro and vostro ledgers, suspense and transit accounts, writes the exact general ledger postings for outgoing and incoming payments, and explains intraday processing, value dating, subledger feeds, reconciliation and error correction, with correct terminology.

  • Customer deposit of 10,000 pounds: debit cash, credit account
  • They: same account seen
  • Mirror: record
  • Settlement account
  • Internal-account ownership and limit-breach escalation stated in the requirements

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

A bank keeps several different kinds of ledger, and confusing them is the most common source of errors in payment operations. Customer accounts hold the money of individuals and companies: current accounts, savings accounts, fixed deposits, loan accounts and card accounts. Internal accounts hold the bank's own money and its own obligations: fee income, interest income, staff costs, provisions, fixed assets, settlement accounts, suspense accounts and transit accounts. Nostro and vostro accounts hold the bank's positions with other banks. Knowing which ledger an entry belongs in is the difference between a payment that settles cleanly and one that lands in the wrong place. A customer deposit is a liability, and this surprises people who are new to banking. When a customer deposits 10,000 pounds, the bank debits its cash or central bank reserve account and credits the customer's current account. The customer has an asset, the bank has a liability. The bank owes the customer that money on demand. A loan is the mirror image: when the bank lends 10,000 pounds, it debits a loan asset account and credits the customer's current account. The customer has a liability, the bank has an asset. This is why a bank's balance sheet grows on both sides when it lends, and why a run on deposits is an existential event, because the liabilities can be called in faster than the assets can be turned into cash. A nostro account is our account with another bank, and a vostro account is another bank's account with us. They are the same account seen from opposite sides. When a UK bank holds a US dollar account with a US correspondent bank, the UK bank calls it a nostro account and records it as an asset in its own ledger. The US bank calls the same account a vostro account and records it as a liability. When the UK bank sends a dollar payment for a customer, it credits its nostro account, which reduces the asset, and the US bank debits the vostro account on its books. When a dollar payment arrives, the UK bank debits its nostro account and credits the customer. The bank keeps a mirror of each nostro account in its own ledger, and the mirror must be reconciled every day against the statement the correspondent sends, usually as an MT950 or MT940 message, or as a camt.053 statement in ISO 20022. The mirror is a record of what the bank thinks its balance is. The statement is a record of what the correspondent thinks the balance is. The two should agree, and when they do not, the difference is a break that must be investigated. Nostro reconciliation is one of the oldest and most important controls in banking, because a nostro account that is not reconciled can hide a payment that was sent but never arrived, or a payment that arrived but was never credited to the customer. Internal accounts are where the bank's own economics live, and they are the accounts most often misunderstood. Fee income is an internal account that is credited when a customer is charged for a payment. Interest expense is debited when the bank pays interest on a deposit. A settlement account is an internal account that holds funds in transit to a clearing system. A suspense account holds funds that cannot yet be posted to their final destination. An interbranch account records money moving between branches or legal entities of the same group, and it must net to zero across the group at the end of the day. Each of these accounts has an owner in finance, a defined purpose, and a limit on how long a balance can sit in it. For a business analyst, the practical rule is simple: every payment flow must name the ledger for each entry. A flow that says the payment is debited from the customer and credited to the beneficiary is incomplete. It must say which customer account, which internal account, which nostro account, and which subledger each entry posts to. The requirements must also state who owns each internal account and what the escalation is when a balance in it exceeds its limit. The acceptance criteria must include a test where a cross-border payment is made and the nostro mirror, the customer account and the fee income account all move by the correct amounts. Last reviewed 8 November 2026. Further reading: the SWIFT standards for MT950 and MT940 statements; the ISO 20022 camt.053 bank to customer statement; the Bank for International Settlements paper on correspondent banking.
