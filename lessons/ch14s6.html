Key terms

Serial payment: A payment method where the customer instruction and the funds travel together through each bank in the chain.

Cover payment: A payment method where the customer instruction travels one route while the funds travel separately as a bank to bank cover message.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

SWIFT messages carry codes that tell the receiving bank how the payment is being made, and four codes in particular matter for the serial versus cover distinction. INDA means instructing bank is the debtor agent, which is the code used when the sending bank is itself sending the payment and the funds are moving with the instruction, as in a serial payment. INGA means instructing bank is not the debtor agent, which is used when the bank sending the instruction is not the bank that holds the funds, for example when a bank sends an instruction on behalf of another bank.

These two codes appear in the settlement method field of the payment message, and they tell the receiving bank whether the sender is also the bank that will fund the payment. COVE means cover payment, and it is the code that identifies an MT202 COV message as a cover payment rather than a simple bank to bank transfer. When a bank receives an MT202 with the COVE code, it knows that the funds are the cover for a customer payment that has been sent separately as an MT103, and it knows that it must not treat the funds as a payment in their own right.

The bank must screen the cover payment using the originator and beneficiary information carried in the COVE message, and it must pass the funds on to the next bank in the cover chain. The COVE code is what makes the cover method work as a distinct method rather than a series of unrelated bank transfers, and the analyst must ensure that the system generates it correctly. In ISO 20022 the same cover travels as a pacs.009 COV, which carries the same cover purpose. CLRG means clearing, and it is used when the payment is being settled through a clearing system rather than through correspondent accounts.

A payment that is sent through a clearing house will carry the CLRG code to indicate that the settlement will happen through that clearing system, and the receiving bank should not expect the funds to arrive through a nostro account. The distinction between INDA, INGA, COVE and CLRG is not academic. A payment with the wrong code will be processed incorrectly, will be charged incorrectly, or will be stopped for a query. A business analyst who writes requirements for cross border payments must specify which code is used in which scenario, must test that the system generates the right code, and must be able to explain to an operations colleague why a payment that should have been COVE was sent as INDA, and what to do about it.
