Key terms

Serial payment: A payment method where the customer instruction and the funds travel together through each bank in the chain.

Cover payment: A payment method where the customer instruction travels one route while the funds travel separately as a bank to bank cover message.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Show transcript

Cancelling a payment is not one process. It is three, one for each settlement method, and the method decides what must be cancelled, who must agree, and what happens if the cancellation arrives too late. A direct payment travels between two banks with no intermediary, so the cancellation is a conversation between two parties. A serial payment travels through a chain of intermediaries, each of which must cancel its own leg. A cover payment travels as two linked messages, a customer payment and a separate cover, and both must be cancelled, in coordination.

A direct payment is the simplest case. The debtor agent sends a cancellation request to the creditor agent, in the MT world an MT192 request for cancellation, and in the ISO 20022 world a camt.056 payment cancellation request, referencing the original payment by its references and its UETR. The creditor agent checks whether the payment has settled and whether the beneficiary account has been credited. If the payment has not yet been credited, the creditor agent cancels it and confirms with a resolution message, camt.029, accepting the cancellation. If the beneficiary has already been credited, the creditor agent cannot simply take the money back: it must ask the beneficiary for consent, and if the beneficiary refuses, the cancellation is rejected, and the debtor must pursue the return through other means.

A serial payment multiplies the conversation. The payment travels through intermediaries, each of which has received the payment from the previous bank and sent it to the next. The cancellation request must travel the same chain in reverse: the debtor agent asks the first intermediary, which asks the next, and so on to the creditor agent. Each intermediary must confirm that it has cancelled its leg before the previous bank can be sure the cancellation is complete. If any bank in the chain has already passed the payment on and cannot recall it, that bank reports the failure, and the cancellation stops at that point. The debtor agent must track the cancellation leg by leg, and must not tell the customer the payment is cancelled until every leg has confirmed. A serial cancellation that is reported complete after only the first leg confirmed is a false promise.

A cover payment is the hardest case, because two messages must be cancelled together. The customer payment travels as an MT103 or pacs.008 directly to the creditor agent, and the cover travels as an MT202 or pacs.009 through the correspondent chain. The cancellation must address both: a cancellation request for the customer payment to the creditor agent, and a cancellation request for the cover to each bank in the cover chain. The two cancellations must be coordinated, because cancelling one without the other leaves the payment half done: the creditor agent may have the instruction but no funds, or the funds but no instruction. The usual practice is to cancel the cover first, so that no funds are in flight, and then cancel the customer payment, but the exact order depends on the state of each leg, and the analyst must design the coordination as a single case with two cancellation requests, not as two independent cases.

Timing decides whether cancellation is possible at all. Before settlement, a cancellation request can stop the payment, because the funds have not yet moved finally. After settlement but before beneficiary credit, the cancellation may still succeed, because the creditor agent holds the funds but has not yet credited the beneficiary. After beneficiary credit, cancellation is no longer available, and the only route is a return, which needs the beneficiary consent or a scheme return process. The cancellation request must therefore carry the urgency of the situation, and the receiving bank must prioritise it over normal processing, because every minute of delay moves the payment closer to finality. Cancellation requests must be prioritised over routine payments.

A worked example shows the cover case. A payment of 200,000 dollars travels as a pacs.008 directly to the creditor agent, with a pacs.009 cover travelling through two correspondents. The debtor discovers an error and asks for cancellation. The debtor agent sends a camt.056 for the customer payment to the creditor agent, and a camt.056 for the cover to the first correspondent, which forwards it to the second. The cover chain confirms cancellation of both legs, and the creditor agent confirms that the customer payment has not been credited and is cancelled. The debtor agent receives all confirmations and tells the customer the payment is cancelled. If the creditor agent had already credited the beneficiary, it would have rejected the cancellation, and the debtor would have needed the beneficiary consent for a return. The method decided the work, and the timing decided the outcome.
