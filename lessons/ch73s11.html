Key terms

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

The camt.037 Claim Non-Receipt is the newer ISO 20022 message a creditor bank sends when expected funds have not arrived by the value date. The claim states that a payment was due for a given amount on a given date and has not been received, and it asks the debtor bank or the intermediary to trace the funds. The message carries the original payment references, the expected amount and currency, the expected value date, and the claimant and respondent identification. The claim creates a case that must be answered, because the beneficiary is waiting for money that should already be in the account.

The difference between camt.037 and camt.027 lies in the richness of the case data and the structure of the investigation record. The camt.027 claim carries the core claim facts needed to start a trace, and it has served as the established claim message for non-receipt cases. The camt.037 claim carries richer case data, with fuller case identification and more structured detail about the investigation, so the respondent can route the case without re-keying facts. The business meaning stays the same, because both messages assert non-receipt and demand a trace. The change is in the depth and structure of the data, not in the claim itself.

The decision to use camt.037 instead of camt.027 follows the scheme rules and the capability of the counterparty. The creditor bank uses camt.037 where the scheme in force supports the newer message and the respondent bank can receive and process it. The creditor bank stays with camt.027 where the scheme or the counterparty supports only the established message, because a claim sent in a format the respondent cannot read helps nobody. A bank building a new case management capability should design for camt.037 from the start, while keeping camt.027 for counterparties that have not yet moved. A single case must use one claim message, and the bank must not send both messages for the same missing payment.

A worked example with a missing supplier payment shows how a camt.037 claim is raised and answered. A bank in Madrid expects EUR 480,000 for a manufacturing customer, with value date on the twelfth day of the month, under a pacs.008 sent by a bank in Frankfurt. The value date passes and the credit does not arrive, so the Madrid bank sends camt.037 naming the expected amount, the value date, the UETR, and the original instruction reference. The Frankfurt bank searches its records, finds the payment was sent to a correspondent bank, and forwards the trace to that correspondent. The correspondent finds the payment held in a screening queue, clears the check, and releases the funds the same day.

The investigation steps around a claim follow the same pattern for camt.037 and camt.027, and the pattern has four stages. The claim stage records the expected payment, the value date, and the evidence that the funds did not arrive. The trace stage follows the payment hop by hop through the chain, with each bank confirming whether it received the funds and whether it forwarded them. The evidence stage gathers the proof at each hop, including settlement records and status reports, so the location of the funds is established by fact rather than by assertion. The resolution stage closes the case when the funds arrive, when the payment is shown to be in transit, or when the payment is shown never to have been sent.

The discipline for claims work is the same whichever message carries the claim, and it rests on deadlines, ownership, and evidence. The claims queue needs an owner for every open claim, a deadline for the first answer, and an escalation path when the deadline passes without a factual reply. The answer must state the payment status, the evidence for that status, and the next step, rather than offering a holding reply with no facts. The analyst must specify the automatic claim trigger and the rule that prevents duplicate claims. The test for the design is a single missing payment traced from the beneficiary bank to the bank holding the funds.
