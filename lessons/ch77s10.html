Key terms

Authorisation: Approval by the issuer for a card transaction to proceed, confirming the card is valid and funds or credit are available.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Finality: The point at which a payment settlement becomes irrevocable and unconditional, so it cannot be reversed by the payer or the system.

Correct result. Real time gross settlement settles each payment individually in central bank money with immediate finality, but it needs liquidity for every payment. Deferred net settlement nets payments and settles the net position, which needs less liquidity but leaves settlement risk until the net settles.

Why. Settlement is where money actually moves. The model decides the risk, the liquidity need, and when the payment is truly final.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

Once settlement is final, it cannot be reversed by the system. Finality is a legal and operational fact: the payment has settled, the funds belong to the receiver, and no system function can unmove them. This is not a limitation to be worked around; it is the property that makes payments trustworthy. The receiver can use the funds immediately, because the settlement will not be unwound. When a payment is erroneous, a wrong amount, a wrong account, a duplicate, the fix is not to reverse the settlement. The fix is a new instruction, in the opposite direction, agreed with the parties, with its own audit trail.

Finality is protected by law as well as by system design. In the European Union, the Settlement Finality Directive protects settlement in designated systems: a settled transfer cannot be unwound by insolvency proceedings or by a system operator decision. Other jurisdictions have equivalent protections. The legal protection exists because unwinding would cascade: the receiver may have paid the funds onward, the onward recipient may have done the same, and unwinding the first payment would unravel a chain of settled transactions. Finality stops the cascade by making each settlement stand on its own.

The erroneous payment playbook is therefore a playbook of new instructions. A duplicate payment is fixed by a return: the receiving bank returns the duplicate funds with a pacs.004, carrying the reason. A wrong amount is fixed by a balancing payment: the difference is paid, or the excess is returned, by a new instruction. A wrong account is fixed by agreement: the receiving bank contacts the account holder, obtains consent to return the funds, and sends the return. In each case the original settlement stands in the record, and the correction stands alongside it, linked by reference and by case. The record shows both the error and the fix, and the audit trail is complete.

The mechanisms for new instruction corrections are the standard messages. The pacs.004 return carries funds back, with a reason code explaining why. The camt.055 and camt.056 cancellation requests work before finality: they stop a payment that has not yet settled, but they cannot unwind one that has. A goodwill or agreement based repayment is a new payment instruction, like any other, with the agreement recorded outside the message. Legal recovery, where agreement fails, follows the courts, not the payment system. Each mechanism is a new instruction with its own authorisation, its own validation, and its own settlement.

System design must enforce the principle. There is no reverse settlement function in the ledger. Corrections are new entries: in accounting, a reversing entry that offsets the original, leaving both visible; in the payment system, a new payment instruction that moves the funds back. The ledger is append only in effect: entries are added, never deleted or altered, and corrections are visible as corrections. An operator who could delete or alter a settled entry could hide errors, manipulate balances, and destroy the audit trail. The append only discipline is what makes the ledger trustworthy.

For a business analyst, error handling requirements must never specify reversing the settlement. They must specify the new instruction: what it is, who authorises it, what agreement is needed, how it is reconciled, and how the case is tracked. The requirements must cover the consent needed to debit a beneficiary account, the time limits for returns, and the escalation when agreement is refused. Finality is a constraint the design respects, not a problem to work around. A system that respects finality is a system whose settlements can be trusted, and trust is the product.
