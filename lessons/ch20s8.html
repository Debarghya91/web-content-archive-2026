Key terms

Intraday liquidity: Cash available to a bank during the business day to settle payments as they fall due. Intraday liquidity comes from account balances, incoming payments and central bank facilities.

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Correct result. Real time gross settlement settles each payment individually in central bank money with immediate finality, but it needs liquidity for every payment. Deferred net settlement nets payments and settles the net position, which needs less liquidity but leaves settlement risk until the net settles.

Why. Settlement is where money actually moves. The model decides the risk, the liquidity need, and when the payment is truly final.

CLSNow is the service that brings payment versus payment protection to foreign exchange trades that settle on the same day they are agreed. The standard CLS service, CLSSettlement, handles trades that were struck on a previous business day. CLSNow covers trades agreed and submitted on the value date itself, and settles them during that same business day. The economics of the trade are unchanged, and the payment versus payment principle is unchanged: neither currency leg settles unless the other settles too. What changes is the timeline, and compressing the timeline into a single day changes funding, operations and risk in ways that every user of the service must understand.

Only five currencies are eligible for CLSNow, and the list is worth memorising exactly. They are the Canadian dollar, CAD, the Swiss franc, CHF, the euro, EUR, the UK pound sterling, GBP, and the US dollar, USD. The other thirteen currencies settled by CLSSettlement are not covered, so a same day trade in any other CLS currency cannot use the service. Both legs of a CLSNow trade must be in eligible currencies, which means the service covers pairs formed from those five currencies only. Eligibility can change over time as the service develops, so an analyst should confirm the current list in the CLS rulebook or with the settlement member before assuming a pair is covered.

Settlement through CLSNow runs continuously during the business day rather than in a single morning window. Trades are matched trade by trade, and a trade settles upon receipt of sufficient funding for both sides, with both legs settled simultaneously in central bank money on a gross basis. Pay outs follow completion of settlement, so the bought currency reaches the member on the same day. The standard service settles once per business day. The CLSNow window is governed by the earlier closing currency in the pair: funding must arrive in time for settlement to complete before that currency's close, and a trade that remains unsettled at the currency close is rejected from the service. There is no overnight gap in which to fix a mismatch or chase a shortfall.

Funding for CLSNow is gross and immediate, which makes intraday liquidity management the central discipline for users. In the standard service, members fund net pay in amounts known a day ahead, and netting across many trades greatly reduces the funds that must move. In CLSNow, each trade settles gross, so the full amount of each leg must be funded, and the requirement is known only hours before it must be met. A member therefore needs live visibility of its nostro balances in each eligible currency and of funding it can raise at very short notice. The trades that justify that effort are those where waiting would leave a bank short of a currency it needs today, to fund a client payment, cover an unexpected position, or manage end of day liquidity, and the service also covers the out legs of in and out swaps from CLSSettlement, which spread funding across the day.

The risk CLSNow removes is principal risk, the risk of paying away one currency and receiving nothing in return. Same day trades settled bilaterally carry the full form of that risk, because the two legs move through separate payment systems at different times and a counterparty could fail in between. Payment versus payment removes it, because either both legs settle together or neither settles. What the service does not remove is liquidity risk or operational risk: a member that cannot fund its leg on time does not settle, and a member that expected to receive a currency the same day must find alternative funding at short notice. The failure is therefore a funding and operations problem on the day, not a loss of principal, but the compressed timeline leaves little room to arrange a workaround.

Using the service well demands tighter coordination than the standard service, because every step must work the first time inside a window measured in hours. Trades should be submitted immediately upon execution, matching must complete promptly, and funding teams must know a same day trade has been agreed as soon as the dealing desk agrees it. Reconciliation must also happen the same day. A trade whose two instructions fail to agree is held back and left unsettled. If a member fails to fund by the deadline, the trade does not settle and funding already provided is returned, and the counterparties must then rearrange settlement, for example on a later date or by bilateral agreement. A bank whose operations and liquidity management are ready for same day working gains same day liquidity; a bank that treats CLSNow like the standard service risks failed trades and missed payments.
