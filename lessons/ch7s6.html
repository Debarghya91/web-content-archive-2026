Example: pacs.008.001.08 (explorer scheme_version). Versions listed in the explorer: pacs.008.001.06, pacs.008.001.08, pacs.008.001.09, pacs.008.001.10. Shortened for display from the chapter walkthrough: one PmtInf and one CdtTrfTxInf, Acme Trading Ltd pays Meridian Supplies Pte Ltd 10000 USD for invoice 4567. This is the base ISO 20022 structure. Scheme rules add to it: under CBPR+ the message must carry a UETR and fully structured debtor and creditor addresses, and under SEPA SCT the charge bearer must be SLEV only. The chapter example names the agents only as the London bank and the Singapore bank, so no BIC is shown. The UETR value shown is illustrative; the chapter names no UETR value.

Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Suspense account: A temporary account used to hold funds or entries that cannot yet be posted to their final account. Banks use suspense accounts for unidentified receipts, pending investigations and items awaiting...

Execution date: The date on which the bank executes a payment instruction, debiting the debtor account and sending the message onward.

The walkthrough highlights each block of the example XML in turn, from the Group Header down to the remittance information.

  1. What to do. List the three levels of the message and name the field that carries the amount and the field that carries the end-to-end reference.

Correct result. The amount is carried in the instructed amount element and the reference in the end-to-end identification. The three levels are the group header, the payment information, and the credit transfer transaction information.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The group header identifies the message, the payment information identifies the batch, and the transaction information carries the single payment.

Imagine a pacs.008 where Acme Trading Ltd in London pays Meridian Supplies Pte Ltd in Singapore ten thousand US dollars for invoice 4567. The Document starts with the Group Header, which contains Message Identification ACME2026110501, Creation Date and Time the 5th of November 2026 at 9 in the morning, Number of Transactions 1, and Control Sum 10000. That header tells the receiving bank that this message contains one payment for ten thousand dollars, and that the message was created on the morning of 5 November 2026.

If the receiving bank counts one transaction and sums to ten thousand, the header validates. Next comes the Payment Information block, with Payment Information Identification PmtInf001, Payment Method TRF, and Requested Execution Date 2026-11-05. The Debtor is Acme Trading Ltd, the Debtor Account is the London current account, and the Debtor Agent is the London bank with its BIC. The Charge Bearer is SHAR, which means charges are shared. Inside that block is the Credit Transfer Transaction Information, with Instruction Identification Instr001, End to End Identification INV4567, and Instructed Amount 10000 US dollars.

The Creditor Agent is the Singapore bank, the Creditor is Meridian Supplies Pte Ltd, and the Creditor Account is the Singapore account. The Remittance Information is structured, with invoice number 4567, and the Purpose code is SUPP for supplier payment. Reading the message in this order, from Group Header to Payment Information to Transaction, mirrors how the payment flows in business, from the message envelope to the batch to the single payment. If the beneficiary receives less than ten thousand dollars, the analyst can point to the Charge Bearer SHAR and explain that charges were shared.

If the supplier cannot reconcile the payment, the analyst can point to the End to End Identification INV4567 and show that it matches the invoice. If the payment is delayed, the analyst can check the Requested Execution Date and the Creation Date and Time to see whether it missed a cut off. That walkthrough is the skill that turns a pacs.008 from a wall of XML into a readable business document.
