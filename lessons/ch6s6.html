Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

  1. What to do. List the main blocks of the message, name the field that carries the amount, and name the field that identifies the payment.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

ISO 20022 adoption followed a phased timeline, and the cross-border migration is now complete. The coexistence period for cross-border payments began in March 2023, when banks could send both MT and ISO 20022 messages. It ended on 22 November 2025. From that date, MT messages are no longer accepted for cross-border payments on the SWIFT network. All cross-border payment instructions now use ISO 20022. Domestic systems followed their own timelines. CHIPS, the US private-sector system for large payments, moved to ISO 20022 in April 2024. Fedwire, the Federal Reserve real-time gross settlement system, moved to ISO 20022 in July 2025. SEPA had already been using ISO 20022 for years, since the SCT and SDD schemes were built on it. The Bank of England CHAPS system also moved to ISO 20022. The result is that most major payment systems in the world now use the same standard. The CBPR Plus programme defined the usage guidelines for the cross-border migration. It specified which ISO 20022 messages to use for which purpose, which fields are mandatory, and how to populate structured data. The programme continues to publish annual guideline releases, but the migration itself is done. The current releases assume all cross-border traffic is ISO 20022. For a business analyst, the timeline matters because it explains the state of the systems you work on. If you join a bank in 2026, you will not find a live MT to MX migration project for cross-border payments, because that migration is finished. You will find ISO 20022 as the standard, and your work will be about using the rich data it carries, not about translating between formats. You will still need to understand MT, because historical records, investigations, and some domestic systems still use it, but the cross-border future is ISO 20022 only. Put the cutover date to work with a real case. A bank sends a cross border payment on Friday 21 November 2025 as an MT103, and it is accepted, because coexistence is still running. The same payment sent on Monday 24 November 2025 as an MT103 is rejected by the network, because coexistence ended on 22 November 2025 and cross border payment instructions must now travel as ISO 20022 messages. The payment has to be rebuilt as a pacs.008, with the same amount, value date, and remittance information, and sent again. The same logic applies on the domestic systems, each on its own date. A US dollar payment routed through CHIPS after April 2024 travels as a pacs.008, and a bank whose system still generates an MT103 for that corridor will see the payment rejected at the gateway. This is why the timeline is not history for its own sake. Every date in it is a day on which a working payment flow either kept working or stopped. For a business analyst, the migration dates shape three concrete pieces of work. Third, a test calendar with dress rehearsals before each cutover and an exception queue for stragglers afterwards. One detail worth knowing. The November 2025 cutover covered cross border MT103 and MT202 payment instructions only. MT101 corporate to bank instructions were not part of that cutover, and their migration was deferred, so a bank that still receives MT101 files from its corporate customers in 2026 is not behind schedule.
