Key terms

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Clearing: The process of exchanging payment instructions between banks and calculating what each bank owes the others.

SCT Inst: SEPA Instant Credit Transfer, a scheme for euro payments that settle within ten seconds at any time of day.

  1. What to do. Name the model, state when settlement becomes final, and explain what happens if the sending bank does not have enough liquidity.

Correct result. Real time gross settlement settles each payment individually in central bank money with immediate finality, but it needs liquidity for every payment. Deferred net settlement nets payments and settles the net position, which needs less liquidity but leaves settlement risk until the net settles.

Why. Settlement is where money actually moves. The model decides the risk, the liquidity need, and when the payment is truly final.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

The Single Euro Payments Area, known as SEPA, is one of the most ambitious payment projects in the world. It makes a euro payment from a bank account in Germany to a bank account in France work in exactly the same way, with the same rules, the same formats and the same timelines, as a payment between two accounts in the same country. Before SEPA, a cross border payment inside Europe was slow, expensive and unpredictable, because each country had its own clearing system, its own formats and its own cut off times.

SEPA replaced that patchwork with a single set of schemes and standards, so that a business in Spain can pay a supplier in Italy as easily as it pays a supplier in Madrid. SEPA covers more than the European Union. It includes all EU member states plus the countries of the European Economic Area and several others, including the United Kingdom for some purposes, Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Monaco, San Marino and Andorra, covering more than thirty countries and over five hundred million people.

The European Payments Council, known as the EPC, owns the SEPA rulebooks, and the European Central Bank oversees the system. Banks and payment service providers that join SEPA must follow the rulebooks, use the IBAN as the account identifier, and use the ISO 20022 message format for the interbank leg of the payment. This standardisation is what makes SEPA work. Every participant speaks the same language. For a business analyst, SEPA is a lesson in how standardisation creates scale.

A company that operates in ten European countries does not need ten different payment integrations; it needs one SEPA integration that works everywhere. A payroll file that pays employees in five countries can use the same format for all of them. A direct debit that collects a subscription from customers in eight countries can use the same mandate process. The analyst must still understand the local differences that remain, such as national cut off times, local clearing arrangements, and the way some countries handle specific use cases, but the core of the payment is the same across the whole area.

That is the achievement of SEPA, and it is why it is studied as a model for regional payment integration around the world.
