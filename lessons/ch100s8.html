Key terms

Queue: A holding area in a payment system where payments wait for sufficient funds or for their turn to settle.

Practise this lesson

Exercise

Work out what the beneficiary receives for a $10,000 payment under OUR, SHA and BEN.

Use the charge arrangement rules in this lesson and the correspondent and beneficiary bank fees named in the lesson.

  1. What to do. Calculate the amount credited under each of the three arrangements and state who pays each fee.

Correct result. Under OUR the sender pays all charges and the beneficiary receives the full amount. Under SHA the sender pays the sending charges and the beneficiary pays the receiving charges. Under BEN the beneficiary pays all charges and receives the amount less all fees.

Why. The charge arrangement decides who pays and how much arrives. A customer who was promised the full amount and receives less will complain, and the complaint lands on the analyst.

In this final lesson you turn your capstone idea into a full specification, the document a developer, a tester and an operations manager can actually work from. Your deliverable has four parts, and each must be concrete.

First, the BRD outline, one page that states the business problem, the users, the current process, the proposed AI feature, the benefits in numbers, and the risks, for example cutting the unmatched queue from forty items a week to ten, and saving six hours of analyst time a week.

Second, the user stories with acceptance criteria, written in plain words, for example as a reconciliation analyst I want to see the suggested match with the reason and the amount difference, so that I can approve it in under a minute, with criteria that say the reason must show the amount, the reference and the confidence level, and that no match above one lakh rupees is auto posted.

Third, the control framework, a table that names each control, its owner and its limit, for example the auto match tolerance of plus or minus five hundred rupees, the human approval limit of one lakh rupees, the audit log owned by the BA, and the weekly review owned by the model owner.

Fourth, the UAT scenarios, the tests a real user will run before sign off, including the happy path, a bulk payment of twelve thousand dollars matched to thirty invoices, and the AI failure cases, a payment two hundred rupees short, a missing reference, a model outage where the queue must still work manually, and a false fraud flag that must reach a human within two hours. If your specification covers all four parts, with a named owner and a number on every limit, you have shown you can design AI for payments work.
