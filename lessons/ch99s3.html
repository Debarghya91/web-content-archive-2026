Control Ownership: Naming the Owner

Every AI control needs a named human owner. Not a team, not a role on a chart, and not a system. A named person, with a name, who knows they own it, who sees the evidence, and who is expected to act when something goes wrong. That sounds basic, but it is the control that most often goes missing, because teams assume someone else is watching.

The owner has three jobs. First, they see the evidence. They get the daily total, the exceptions, the overrides, and the failures, not just a green dashboard. Second, they decide. When a payment is held, when a limit is hit, or when a model starts behaving differently, the owner decides what happens next. Third, they fix, or they make sure the fix happens.

Take a payment screening model that starts holding far more payments than usual. One morning it holds a genuine supplier payment of 15,000 pounds, then another of 8,000 pounds, then a dozen more. If no one owns that control, the operations team works around it, the holds pile up, and customers start calling. If a named owner exists, they see the spike the same day, ask what changed in the data or the model, and pause the model or adjust the threshold until it is understood.

Ownership also means cover. Name a deputy for holidays and sickness, write down what the owner must check and how often, and record when they checked it. When something goes wrong and no one owns the fix, the problem does not stay small. It grows until a customer, an auditor, or a loss finds it first. Name the owner before the product goes live, not after the first incident.
