Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

MT103 REMIT: A variant of MT103 used for remittance payments, where the payment is for a personal or family transfer, often with cash payout.

MT103 STP: A variant of MT103 for straight through processing, with restricted fields and structured data to allow fully automated handling.

The MT103, the single customer credit transfer, exists in three forms, and the form is signalled by a single code in the message header. The core MT103, the MT103+ also known as MT103 STP, and the MT103 REMIT share the same message type number and the same core fields, but they are validated differently, they carry different data, and they require different agreements between sender and receiver. The validation flag in field 119 of block 3, the user header, says which form a message claims to be, and the network validates it against the rules of that form. Confusing the forms is a reliable way to have a message rejected.

The core MT103 is a general use message. No registration in a Message User Group is necessary to send or receive it. It allows all MT103 fields except field 77T, the envelope contents field, and it allows free format options throughout: party fields can be given as names and addresses, remittance information can be free text, and the message can carry the full range of optional fields. The core MT103 is the most flexible form, and the least automatable, because free text needs human reading and manual repair when it does not match the receiver expectations.

The MT103+ is the straight through processing form, documented by SWIFT as MT103 STP. It is also a general use message, needing no MUG registration. It is a network validated subset of the core MT103: structured fields only, BIC based party identification wherever possible, and no free text options where a structured alternative exists. The validation flag must carry the code STP. The restrictions exist for one reason: a message that follows them can be processed end to end without manual intervention, which is faster, cheaper, and less error prone than a message that needs a human to read it. The MT103+ is the form banks want to receive, and the form sending systems should produce.

The MT103 REMIT is the extended remittance form, and it is different in kind. It requires registration in the Extended Remittance Information Message User Group, and both the sender and the receiver must subscribe before exchanging REMIT messages. Field 70, remittance information, is absent entirely. In its place, field 77T, envelope contents, carries up to 9,000 characters of remittance data, replacing the 140 characters of field 70. The remittance data in the envelope may be in a non SWIFT format, such as EDIFACT or ANSI X12, so that a corporate remittance advice can travel inside the payment message unchanged. The validation flag must carry the code REMIT.

Registration is what makes the REMIT form legitimate. The Message User Group is a community agreement: subscribers declare that they can send and receive the 77T envelope, and the network enforces the agreement by validating REMIT messages only between subscribers. A 77T field sent to a non subscriber fails validation. The registration model also signals capability: a bank that subscribes to the Extended Remittance Information MUG is telling its counterparties that it can handle extended remittance data, and counterparties can route rich remittance traffic to it with confidence.

The current status of the three forms reflects the ISO 20022 migration. Field 70 140 character limit, which REMIT solved with the 77T envelope, is solved better by ISO 20022 structured remittance information, which carries invoice level detail as structured data rather than as an envelope. The coexistence period for FI to FI cross border MT payments ended on 22 November 2025, and MT103 REMIT is no longer a live route for those flows. The variant survives in archives, in training, and in the lesson it teaches: when a message cannot carry the data the business needs, the answer is an envelope with an agreement behind it, and the agreement, the MUG registration, is as much a part of the standard as the field itself.
