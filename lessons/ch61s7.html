Key terms

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

SCT Inst: SEPA Instant Credit Transfer, a scheme for euro payments that settle within ten seconds at any time of day.

Exercise

Loire Components must choose between a regular SCT payment and an SCT Inst payment for EUR 48,250 to Rhine Metals. Explain which rail fits which need, the value date and settlement time for each, the price rule, and the verification of payee check.

Use the SCT Inst rules in this lesson: the mandate, the price rule, the verification of payee checks, and the SEPA area map.

  1. What to do. State which rail fits each payment, the value date and settlement time for each, the price rule, and the verification of payee check.

Correct result. The EUR 48,250 payment to Rhine Metals is a regular SCT payment with a value date of Tuesday 10 November, and it settles within one business day. An SCT Inst payment would settle within ten seconds, around the clock. The Instant Payments Regulation made SCT Inst mandatory across the European Union, and it requires that the price of an instant payment must not exceed the price of a regular credit transfer, and it requires verification of payee checks so a customer can confirm the beneficiary name matches the account before sending the money.

The bank must offer both rails, price them correctly, and make the cut-off and value date clear for each one.

Why. The product decision is about which rail the customer chooses. A bank that prices instant payments above regular transfers, or hides the cut-off and value date, breaches the regulation and confuses the customer.

The Instant Payments Regulation made SCT Inst mandatory across the European Union, and the change is already in force. Banks in the euro area had to be able to receive instant payments from January 2025, and they had to be able to send instant payments from October 2025. That means a bank can no longer offer SCT Inst as an optional premium service while refusing to receive instant payments from other banks. The regulation also requires that the price of an instant payment must not exceed the price of a regular credit transfer, and it requires verification of payee checks so a customer can confirm the beneficiary name matches the account before sending the money.

For the worked example, the mandatory SCT Inst rule changes the choice Loire Components faces, but it does not change the payment itself. The EUR 48,250 payment to Rhine Metals is a regular SCT payment with a value date of Tuesday 10 November, and it settles within one business day. If Loire Components needed the funds to arrive within seconds, it could send the same payment as an SCT Inst, and Harbour Bank Frankfurt would have to accept it, because receiving instant payments is mandatory. The product decision is about which rail the customer chooses, and the bank must offer both rails, price them correctly, and make the cut-off and value date clear for each one.

The SEPA schemes and instruments form a simple hierarchy that is worth memorising. SEPA is the area and the rulebook, the SCT scheme is the set of rules for credit transfers, and the SCT instrument is the actual payment product that a bank offers under those rules. The same structure applies to direct debits, where the SDD Core and SDD B2B schemes sit under the SEPA rulebook, and to instant payments, where the SCT Inst scheme sits alongside the SCT scheme. A bank joins the schemes, follows the rulebooks, and offers the instruments to its customers.

A business analyst who confuses a scheme with an instrument will write requirements that no scheme rule supports. Three geographies are confused constantly, and getting them right matters for scope decisions. The eurozone is the group of countries that use the euro as their currency, currently twenty countries in the European Union. The European Economic Area is the European Union plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway, and it covers the single market rules. The SEPA area is larger than both, because it includes the eurozone and the European Economic Area plus countries such as the United Kingdom, Switzerland, Monaco, San Marino, Andorra and Vatican City.

A payment in euro from a bank in London to a bank in Zurich can be a SEPA payment, even though neither country is in the eurozone.
