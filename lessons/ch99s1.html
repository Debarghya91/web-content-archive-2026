When AI touches money, it needs the same control discipline as any system that moves money, and that discipline rests on four separations that must stay apart. The first is the mechanism, the code and the model that does the work. The second is the authorisation, the permission that says this payment may go ahead. The third is the limit, the maximum amount or volume that may move without extra approval. The fourth is the owner who sees the total, the named person who watches the whole picture and can stop it. If any of these four collapses into the others, the control is gone. Take a simple example. An AI tool prepares a batch of supplier payments totalling 180,000 pounds. The mechanism builds the batch and checks the invoices. The authorisation is a separate step, done by a person or a separate control that was not involved in building the batch. The limit says no single payment above 25,000 pounds may go through without a second approver, and no batch above 100,000 pounds may go through without a senior sign-off. The owner sees the total for the day, the number of batches, and any exceptions, and can pause the flow. Each separation does a different job. The mechanism does the work, the authorisation gives permission, the limit caps the damage if something is wrong, and the owner sees what no single step can see. When you design or review an AI payment flow, check all four. Ask, who authorises, what is the limit, and who sees the total. If you cannot answer all three in one sentence each, the framework is not complete, and the product is not ready to touch live money.
