Key terms

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

pacs.009: An ISO 20022 message for a financial institution to financial institution credit transfer with no customer beneficiary.

UCP 600: Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits, revision 600, the set of rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce that governs documentary credits.

The applicant bears the risk that the beneficiary will draw even when the applicant believes it has performed. Because the bank deals in documents, a beneficiary who presents a complying demand will be paid, and the applicant must then argue its case in a separate dispute. An applicant should therefore agree the standby wording with care, and should require a document that is hard to produce without a real default, such as a copy of a court order or an independent engineer certificate. A vague demand that needs only a signed statement gives the beneficiary a very wide right to draw.

The beneficiary bears the risk that the documents will not comply, or that the standby will expire before a drawing is made. A beneficiary who presents documents one day after the expiry date loses the right to payment, even if the default is real. A beneficiary who omits a required document, or who claims an amount above the available balance, will face a refusal. The beneficiary must therefore diary the expiry date, keep a copy of the standby, and prepare the drawing documents in advance, so that a presentation can be made quickly when a default occurs.

The issuing bank bears credit risk on the applicant, because it must pay the beneficiary and then recover the money from the applicant. If the applicant is insolvent, the bank may lose the amount paid. The bank also bears operational risk, because a wrong decision to pay or to refuse can lead to a claim. A bank that pays on discrepant documents may not be able to recover from the applicant, while a bank that wrongly refuses a complying presentation may be sued by the beneficiary. Careful examination and clear refusal notices are therefore essential controls.

Most standby letters of credit are governed by ISP98, the International Standby Practices, which was written for standbys and covers demands, presentations, examination, and the honour or dishonour of a drawing. ISP98 states that a standby is independent, that the bank examines documents for apparent compliance, and that the bank has a reasonable time, not exceeding seven banking days, to examine and decide. Some standbys are governed by UCP 600, the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits, which also covers commercial credits.

The standby must state which set of rules applies, and the parties should not assume that one set applies by default. The worked example starts with a buyer in Chicago, Apex Retail Group, which asks Bank of America to issue a standby for 500,000 dollars in favour of Formosa Electronics in Taiwan, to guarantee payment for goods on 60 day terms. The standby is issued on 10 January 2026, expires on 10 July 2026, is governed by ISP98, and requires a signed demand and an unpaid invoice copy. Formosa delivers goods worth 210,000 dollars on 15 February 2026, and Apex fails to pay by 16 April 2026.

On 20 April 2026 Formosa presents a demand for 210,000 dollars and invoice FE 4421 in Taipei. The bank examines the documents on 21 and 22 April, finds them complying, and pays 210,000 dollars on 23 April 2026 by pacs.009, the ISO 20022 financial institution transfer that replaced MT202 for cross-border payments when coexistence ended on 22 November 2025. The available balance falls to 290,000 dollars, and Bank of America debits Apex for 210,000 dollars plus fees that day.
