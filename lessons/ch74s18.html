Key terms

Remittance information: Information that explains what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers, contract references or salary month.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Canada launched its Real-Time Rail, the RTR, on 9 November 2026, joining the group of countries with a national real time payment system. The RTR is owned and operated by Payments Canada, the organisation that also operates Lynx, the high value real time gross settlement system, and the Automated Clearing Settlement System, the retail batch system. The Bank of Canada oversees the RTR. The system is built on ISO 20022 messaging, operates twenty four hours a day, seven days a week, and settles payments in real time, with immediate finality and irrevocability. Eight participants joined at launch, spanning banks, trust companies, and fintech firms.

The RTR is designed as a modern instant system from the start. Payments carry rich ISO 20022 data, including structured remittance information, so that the receiver can reconcile automatically. Settlement is immediate and final: once a payment is settled, it cannot be reversed, and the beneficiary can use the funds at once. The system will include a confirmation number similar to the PCRN, giving each settled payment a unique proof of finality that the sender, the receiver, and the banks can all quote. The design reflects a decade of learning from faster payment systems elsewhere: data rich, always on, and final.

The PCRN, the Payment Confirmation Reference Number, is the confirmation pattern the RTR builds on. The PCRN is generated by Lynx for a settled wire payment: a unique alphanumeric reference that demonstrates the payment settled, finally and irrevocably. The format begins with the letters LVTS, a legacy of the Large Value Transfer System that Lynx replaced: the older form is LVTS followed by nine digits, and the newer form is sixteen alphanumeric characters beginning with LVTS. The PCRN is quoted in investigations, in customer confirmations, and in disputes, as the proof that a specific payment settled. A confirmation number that every party can verify is a small feature with large operational value.

Fraud protection is built into the RTR as mandatory centralised services, a distinctive design choice. Confirmation of payee, for consumer accounts, checks that the name on the payment matches the name on the receiving account, before the payment is sent. A fraud transaction safety score assesses each transaction for fraud risk. A shared risk indicator repository lets participants share fraud signals. And a fraud reporting and intelligence platform collects and distributes fraud information across the system. The services are centralised and mandatory: every participant uses them, so the fraud protection is a system property, not a per bank option.

The RTR sits alongside Lynx and the Automated Clearing Settlement System in the Canadian payment landscape. Lynx carries high value payments, with real time gross settlement in central bank money. The Automated Clearing Settlement System carries retail batch payments: cheques, direct deposits, and pre authorised debits, in batches. The RTR carries real time retail and commercial payments, instantly, around the clock. The three systems serve different needs, and a payment is routed to the system that fits its urgency, value, and data requirements. The landscape is the same pattern seen in other countries: a high value RTGS, a batch retail system, and a real time system, each with its role.

For a business analyst, the RTR is a case study in building a real time system with the lessons of the last decade included from day one. ISO 20022 data from the start, not migrated later. Fraud services centralised and mandatory, not added after the first scam wave. A confirmation number pattern, the PCRN, carried forward from the high value system. And a launch participant set that includes fintech firms alongside banks, reflecting who actually serves retail customers. New instant systems do not start from scratch; they start from the accumulated practice of the systems before them, and the RTR shows what that practice now includes.
