Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

  1. What to do. List the three levels of the message and name the field that carries the amount and the field that carries the end-to-end reference.

Correct result. The amount is carried in the instructed amount element and the reference in the end-to-end identification. The three levels are the group header, the payment information, and the credit transfer transaction information.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The group header identifies the message, the payment information identifies the batch, and the transaction information carries the single payment.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

Faster Payments moves sterling between UK bank accounts in seconds. It was launched in 2008 because a routine payment between two UK banks could still take three days through BACS. Today a customer can send sterling to another UK bank account at any hour, on any day, including weekends and bank holidays, and the beneficiary account is normally credited within seconds. The payment is addressed with a sort code, which identifies the receiving bank and branch, and an account number, which identifies the beneficiary account.

The happy flow starts with the customer instruction. The customer enters the sort code, account number, amount and reference, and the sending bank checks the balance, the daily limit and the payee details. The bank runs a Confirmation of Payee check, comparing the name the customer typed with the name held on the receiving account, and it runs sanctions and fraud screening before anything leaves the bank. If every check passes, the sending bank sends a pacs.008 customer credit transfer message to the Faster Payments central infrastructure.

The receiving bank validates the account, credits the beneficiary, and returns a pacs.002 payment status report confirming acceptance, and the sending bank then shows the customer a confirmation. Settlement between banks is deferred and netted. The customer sees instant credit, but the banks do not settle each payment one by one. Instead, Faster Payments calculates each bank's net position against all the other banks at defined settlement times during the day, and those net amounts settle in central bank money through the Bank of England.

Netting keeps the liquidity each bank needs far lower than gross settlement would. For reconciliation, the analyst must match the customer payments sent, the pacs.002 confirmations received, and the net settlement amounts at each settlement cycle. Limits and timing rules decide which payments can use Faster Payments. The scheme limit is currently one million pounds per payment, but most banks set lower limits for their own customers, and the limit can depend on the channel, so mobile banking may allow less than online banking, and a payment to a new payee may allow less than a payment to an existing payee.

The scheme requires a payment to complete within two hours, although in practice it normally completes in seconds. A payment that has not completed within that window must be investigated rather than assumed lost. A payment above the limit must be routed to CHAPS, and the screen should explain that it will cost more and will be processed as a high value payment. Exceptions follow the ISO 20022 message set, and each one has a distinct handling path. A reject happens before the money moves, when the sending bank or the scheme refuses the payment, for example an invalid sort code, a closed account, or a failed sanctions screen, and it is reported with a pacs.002 status report carrying a reject code.

A return happens after receipt, when the receiving bank cannot apply the payment, and the money comes back with a pacs.004 payment return message. A recall is a request to get the money back after it was credited, sent as a camt.056 cancellation request, and the receiving bank answers with a camt.029 resolution of investigation, which can accept or refuse the recall. A timeout is different again: if no pacs.002 arrives within the expected window, the payment is in an unknown state, and operations must trace it before deciding whether to resend, because resending blindly risks a duplicate payment.

Fraud is the risk the analyst must design for, because speed leaves little time to recover a payment. Authorised push payment fraud is the main threat, where a customer is tricked into sending money to a fraudster's account. Confirmation of Payee is the first defence: a full match gives confidence, a close match asks the customer to check the spelling, and no match is a clear warning to stop and verify the payee by a trusted route. The reimbursement rules require banks to refund victims in defined circumstances, so the flow must record the Confirmation of Payee result, the warning shown, and the customer's decision.

The analyst must also confirm the limits per bank and channel, handle timeouts and duplicates without resending blindly, and reconcile every payment against its pacs.002 confirmation and the net settlement.
