Key terms

Bill of exchange: A written order by one party instructing another party to pay a stated amount to a named person on a stated date.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

SWIFT Category 4 covers collections, cash letters and related advices, and it exists so that banks can exchange collection instructions and status updates in a standard structured form. The messages in this family are MT400, MT410, MT412, MT416, MT420, MT422, MT430, MT450 and MT456, among others. Each message has a defined purpose, a defined sender and receiver, and a defined set of fields. A bank that receives an MT410 knows immediately that it is an acknowledgement of a collection, while an MT400 tells it that payment has been made.

That shared vocabulary removes ambiguity between banks in different countries. The MT410 Acknowledgement is the first reply in the life of a collection. The collecting bank sends an MT410 to the remitting bank to confirm that it has received the documents and the collection instruction, and that it will present them to the importer. The message carries the collection reference, the amount and currency, and the date of receipt. If the collecting bank finds a discrepancy, such as a missing bill of lading or a wrong amount, it can note the problem in the MT410 or in a separate message.

The remitting bank uses the MT410 to confirm to the exporter that the collection is now in the hands of the importer bank. The MT412 Acceptance Advice and the MT416 Advice of Non Payment or Non Acceptance report the importer decision. The collecting bank sends an MT412 when the importer has accepted a draft under a D/A collection, giving the acceptance date, the amount and the maturity date. The exporter then knows the documents have been released and the payment date is fixed. The collecting bank sends an MT416 when the importer refuses to pay or to accept, or when the draft is dishonoured at maturity.

The MT416 states the reason, such as refusal to pay, insufficient funds, or goods not as ordered, so the exporter can decide the next step. The MT420 Tracer, the MT422 Advice of Fate, the MT430 Amendment of Instructions, the MT450 Cash Letter Credit Advice and the MT456 Advice of Non Payment cover the remaining events. The remitting bank sends an MT420 to chase a collecting bank that has not replied within the expected time, for example 15 days after presentation. The collecting bank replies with an MT422 to state the current fate of the collection.

The remitting bank sends an MT430 to change the instruction, for example to reduce the amount from EUR 250,000 to EUR 230,000 after a rebate. The MT450 advises that a cash letter has been credited, and the MT456 advises non payment of a cash letter item. A worked routing example shows how the messages chain together in practice. BNP Paribas in Paris sends the collection documents to Banco Santander in Madrid and alerts Santander by SWIFT. Santander replies with an MT410 acknowledging receipt of the EUR 250,000 collection, reference COLL 2026 0847.

The Madrid importer pays in full, so Santander sends an MT400 advising payment and remits EUR 249,650 after deducting EUR 350 in charges. BNP Paribas credits the Lyon exporter EUR 249,650 and closes the file. If the importer had refused, Santander would have sent an MT416 instead, and the documents would have stayed with Santander pending new instructions.
