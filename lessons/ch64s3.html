Your objective. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain four-corner model for a cheque payment. The four-corner model for a cheque has four parties, the drawer, who writes the cheque, the payee, who receives it, the collecting bank, which is the payee bank, and the paying bank, which is the drawer bank.

Why it matters. A business analyst must design for the delay between presentment and credit, because the payee sees the cheque as deposited on day one but cannot safely spend the money until the clearing completes, and a product owner must decide what the app shows during that window.

Chapter objective: Learner explains what a cheque is, the drawer, drawee and payee roles, bearer and order cheques, and the four-corner cheque model. Learner describes physical and image-based clearing timelines, why a cheque bounces, the Canada modernisation example, and on-us transactions and the push versus pull distinction.

Key terms

Presentment: The submission of a card transaction by the acquirer to the scheme for clearing after authorisation and capture.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Exception: A payment that cannot be processed normally and needs manual handling, investigation or repair. Exceptions arise from missing data, failed screening, account errors or system faults.

The four-corner model for a cheque has four parties, the drawer, who writes the cheque, the payee, who receives it, the collecting bank, which is the payee bank, and the paying bank, which is the drawer bank. The drawer in Leeds writes a cheque for GBP 4,750 to the supplier in Bristol and posts it, and the supplier receives it and pays it into an account at the collecting bank. The collecting bank checks the cheque, records the payee account and the amount, and sends the cheque details to the paying bank for clearing.

The paying bank checks the drawer account, the signature and the funds, and then debits the drawer and pays the collecting bank, which credits the supplier. The first step is the issue of the cheque, when the drawer writes the date, the payee name, the amount in words and figures, and the signature, and then hands the cheque to the payee. The second step is the presentment, when the payee deposits the cheque at the collecting bank, either at a branch, through an ATM or by taking a photograph in a mobile banking app.

The collecting bank records the deposit, gives the payee a receipt, and captures an image of the front and back of the cheque, including the magnetic ink characters that identify the paying bank, the account number and the cheque number. The image and the data are then sent into the clearing system. The third step is the clearing exchange, when the collecting bank sends the cheque image and data to the paying bank through the clearing system, and the paying bank decides whether to honour the cheque.

The paying bank checks the signature against the mandate, confirms the account is open, checks that the funds cover GBP 4,750, and looks for a stop instruction or a fraud flag. If the checks pass, the paying bank marks the cheque as paid and the clearing system calculates the net amount the paying bank owes the collecting bank. The fourth step is settlement, when the paying bank pays the net amount through the settlement system, and the collecting bank credits the supplier account with GBP 4,750.

The cheque and the money travel in opposite directions, and that is the heart of the pull model. The paper cheque travels from drawer to payee to collecting bank to paying bank, while the money travels from the paying bank to the collecting bank to the payee account. The ledger entries follow the money. The drawer account is debited GBP 4,750, the paying bank settlement account is debited, the collecting bank settlement account is credited, and the supplier account is credited GBP 4,750. A business analyst must design for the delay between presentment and credit, because the payee sees the cheque as deposited on day one but cannot safely spend the money until the clearing completes, and a product owner must decide what the app shows during that window.
