Key terms

Cover payment: A payment method where the customer instruction travels one route while the funds travel separately as a bank to bank cover message.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

pacs.002: An ISO 20022 message one financial institution sends to another to report the status of a payment instruction.

  1. What to do. List the three levels of the message and name the field that carries the amount and the field that carries the end-to-end reference.

Correct result. The amount is carried in the instructed amount element and the reference in the end-to-end identification. The three levels are the group header, the payment information, and the credit transfer transaction information.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The group header identifies the message, the payment information identifies the batch, and the transaction information carries the single payment.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

Out of order arrival is normal in payment networks, because messages travel different routes, systems process at different speeds, and a fast acknowledgement can overtake a slow settlement confirmation. A cover payment can arrive at the correspondent before the announcement that explains it, and a pacs.002 status report can arrive before the settlement confirmation it refers to. Each message carries a sequence or a reference that lets the receiving system place it in the right order, and the system must hold an early message until its predecessor arrives rather than acting on it alone. In the worked example, Harbour Bank receives the cover for the USD 120,000 payment before the announcement from Meridian Bank, so it holds the cover in a pending queue instead of crediting the Tokyo supplier immediately.

Resequencing is the work of putting messages back into their intended order using the sequence number, the timestamp and the correlation identifiers on each message. When a message arrives early, the system stores it in a holding area keyed by its UETR and its instruction identification, and when the missing predecessor arrives the system releases the held message for processing in the correct order. If the predecessor never arrives, the held message ages and is escalated to operations after a defined waiting period. In the worked example, Harbour Bank holds the cover under the payment UETR, receives the announcement ninety minutes later, matches the two by the end to end identification, and releases the cover for settlement. Testing resequencing means sending messages out of order, confirming the early message is held, confirming it is released when its predecessor arrives, and confirming nothing is processed twice.

Holding and matching protect the customer, because acting on an early message can credit the wrong account, release funds before the cover has arrived, or report a payment as settled when settlement has not happened. The holding area must be visible on the operations dashboard, with the age of each held message, the missing predecessor, and the time at which it will escalate. An operator can release a held message manually only with a recorded reason and a second approval for large amounts, so a manual release is a controlled act rather than a shortcut. In the worked example, the USD 120,000 cover sits in the holding area at Harbour Bank for ninety minutes, visible to operations with its missing announcement flagged, and no operator releases it early because the rule forbids crediting a supplier before the cover and the announcement are matched.

The business rules decide when to wait and when to act, balancing delay against the risk of acting on incomplete information. A bank waits when the missing piece is essential, such as the cover or the settlement confirmation that proves finality, and it acts when the missing piece is only informational, such as a remittance detail that changes neither amount nor beneficiary. Each rule carries a maximum waiting time, and on expiry the payment is escalated to operations with the full history attached. In the worked example, Harbour Bank waits for the announcement because it confirms the cover belongs to the USD 120,000 payment, but it does not wait for the remittance detail, which arrives later and is matched to the settled payment. Testing these rules means sending each message type early, confirming the wait or act decision matches the rule, and confirming escalation fires on expiry.
