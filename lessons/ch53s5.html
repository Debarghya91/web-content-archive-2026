Key terms

Serial payment: A payment method where the customer instruction and the funds travel together through each bank in the chain.

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Cover payment: A payment method where the customer instruction travels one route while the funds travel separately as a bank to bank cover message.

Field 53B identifies the sender correspondent, the bank at which the sender holds the account that will be debited to fund the payment. It is written as field 53B with a party identifier, often in the format slash D slash followed by an account number, such as /D/123456789, or with a BIC and an account number. For example, when BNP Paribas sends an MT103 to Wells Fargo, field 53B may state that BNP Paribas holds account 200019 with Wells Fargo, and Wells Fargo must debit that account for 367,574.90 dollars when it accepts the instruction. Without that instruction, the receiver would not know which account to debit.

Field 53B is used in the serial method, where the payment instruction and the funds move together through the chain. Each bank debits the nostro account of the previous bank and credits its own position, then forwards a new MT103 for the reduced amount. The funds therefore move step by step with the messages, and each bank can see the amount it must pass on. A serial payment of 500,000 dollars from London to Sydney through two intermediaries will show a falling amount at each hop, and field 53B on each leg tells the next bank which account funded that leg.

The cover method works differently. In the cover method, the sender sends the MT103 directly to the beneficiary bank, and separately sends an MT202 cover payment, or an MT202COV, to the correspondent bank to move the funds. The MT103 carries the customer details, the MT202 carries the bank to bank settlement, and the two messages are linked by field 20 and field 21. Field 53B does not appear in the same way, because the MT103 receiver is not being asked to debit a nostro account. Instead, the beneficiary bank waits for the cover funds to arrive before it credits the beneficiary. This is the pre-migration form of the cover method. Since coexistence ended on 22 November 2025, the same payment travels as a pacs.008 for the instruction and a pacs.009 COV for the cover.

The choice between serial and cover affects speed, cost and transparency. Serial is simpler to trace because each MT103 carries the full customer details and the funds move with it, but it can be slower because each bank must process the message in turn. Cover can be faster because the MT103 reaches the beneficiary bank directly, but it creates a matching problem, because the beneficiary bank must match the MT103 to the MT202 cover before it can release the funds. A cover payment of 1,000,000 dollars from Frankfurt to Hong Kong can be delayed if the MT202 arrives an hour after the MT103 and the Hong Kong bank holds the credit pending the cover.

Field 53B errors are costly because they touch real money. If the sender enters the wrong account number, the receiver may debit the wrong nostro account and create a reconciliation break that takes days to resolve. If the sender omits field 53B when the receiver expects it, the receiver may hold the payment and send an MT199 asking which account to debit. A payment of 75,000 dollars from Toronto to Zurich was held for two days because field 53B named an account that had been closed the previous month, and the receiver could not fund the onward leg until the sender confirmed a replacement account.
