Key terms

Issuer: A bank or licensed institution that issues payment cards to customers and holds their accounts. It authorises transactions, debits cardholder accounts, and manages credit limits and fraud controls.

Retry: Repeating a failed request or message delivery after a delay, usually with a limit on attempts and a back off period.

Break: A difference found during reconciliation between two records that should agree, such as a bank statement and a ledger.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

The most common cause of recurring payment failure is an expired card. Cards typically expire every three to five years, so a long running subscription will eventually hit an expiry date. The second most common cause is insufficient funds, which happens when the billing date falls just before the customer's salary arrives. The third is a generic decline, where the issuer refuses the transaction without giving a specific reason, often because of fraud rules. The fourth is a lost or stolen card, which means the stored credential is no longer valid at all. Each cause needs a different response, and a system that treats them all the same way will recover far less revenue.

Dunning is the process of communicating with a customer whose payment has failed, and it is a mix of messaging, timing, and tone. A good dunning sequence starts with a friendly message on the day of the failure, explaining what happened and asking the customer to check the card or update the details. A second message follows after two or three days, and a third after a week, each one slightly more direct. The best subscription companies test the wording, the timing, and the channel, because a message that feels like a threat will push the customer to cancel, while a helpful message will save the subscription. The BA documents the full dunning schedule, including the message templates and the rules for stopping messages when the payment succeeds.

A retry schedule defines when the system attempts the payment again after a failure, and it is one of the highest value requirements a BA can write. The simplest schedule retries after 24 hours, then after three days, then after seven days. Smarter schedules vary by failure type, so an insufficient funds decline is retried just after the typical salary date, while a generic decline is retried after a longer gap. Some merchants use machine learning to pick the best retry time for each customer based on past payment behaviour. The BA must specify the maximum number of retries, the gap between them, and the final action when all retries fail, which is usually suspension of the subscription.

Account updater services are provided by Visa and Mastercard, and they automatically update stored card details when a card is replaced or renewed. When a bank issues a new card with a new expiry date or a new card number, the account updater service tells the merchant's payment provider about the change, so the next recurring payment uses the new details. This single service can recover a large share of the revenue that would otherwise be lost to expired and replaced cards. The BA should specify that the merchant enables account updater, how often the stored credentials are refreshed, and what happens when the updater reports that the card is closed rather than replaced.

The full failure handling design brings all of these pieces together into one lifecycle. When a payment fails, the system classifies the decline code, decides whether the failure is recoverable, and picks the retry schedule. At the same time, the dunning process starts, and the account updater is checked for new card details. If the payment succeeds on a retry, the subscription continues and the customer gets a confirmation. If all retries fail, the subscription is suspended, the customer is told how to reactivate it, and finance is given a report on the lost revenue. The BA documents this as a state machine, because the subscription moves between active, past due, suspended, and cancelled, and every transition needs a clear rule.

For example, a customer pays 1,000 rupees a month for a gym membership, and the card expires in the middle of the year. The next charge fails, so the merchant sends a friendly message on the same day, asking the customer to update the card. The account updater service then supplies the new expiry date, and the retry on the third day succeeds for 1,000 rupees. The subscription continues without a break, and the customer receives a confirmation that the payment went through.
