Key terms

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

  1. What to do. Name the model, state when settlement becomes final, and explain what happens if the sending bank does not have enough liquidity.

Correct result. Real time gross settlement settles each payment individually in central bank money with immediate finality, but it needs liquidity for every payment. Deferred net settlement nets payments and settles the net position, which needs less liquidity but leaves settlement risk until the net settles.

Why. Settlement is where money actually moves. The model decides the risk, the liquidity need, and when the payment is truly final.

Show transcript

A liquidity position is not one number. The balance on an account, or the position in a settlement system, divides into states, and the states decide what the money can do. Available funds can be used for payments now. Reserved funds are set aside for a specific purpose and cannot be used for anything else. Blocked funds are frozen and cannot be used at all until released. A position that looks healthy on its total balance can be unusable if most of it is reserved or blocked. Treasury and operations must see all three states, separately, in real time, or they will fund payments from money that is not free.

Available funds are the free balance: the money that can be used for the next payment, without further action. When a payment is made, available funds fall. When a receipt arrives, available funds rise. Available is the number that matters for the immediate funding decision: can this payment be made now? Liquidity dashboards lead with available, and funding decisions, sweeping, borrowing, or delaying a payment, are taken against it. An available balance that is positive but thin means the next large payment needs funding first.

Reserved funds, also called earmarked funds, are set aside for a specific purpose. A queued payment reserves the funds it will need when it settles. A pending settlement reserves the amount due. A regulatory buffer reserves funds that must be held. Reserved funds are not gone: they are allocated. They cannot be used for other payments, because they are already promised, but they will be released if the purpose disappears: a queued payment that is cancelled releases its reservation, and the funds return to available. Reservation is how a system prevents double spending: the same funds cannot be promised to two payments.

Blocked funds are frozen. A sanctions hold blocks funds that may belong to a sanctioned party. A legal order blocks funds under a court instruction. A system hold blocks funds pending an investigation or a control action. Blocked funds cannot be used for any purpose until the block is released, and the release needs the authority that imposed the block, or a higher one. Blocked funds are reported separately from available and reserved, with an audit trail showing who blocked them, why, when, and under what authority. A block is a control action, and it leaves a record.

Funds move between states under strict rules. Available becomes reserved when funds are earmarked, and reserved becomes available when the earmark is released, or becomes settled when the payment is made. Available becomes blocked when a hold is imposed, and blocked becomes available when the hold is released. The state machine must be exact: no funds in two states at once, no funds lost between states, and every transition recorded. The total across the three states reconciles to the account balance at all times, and a break in that reconciliation is a control failure. The state machine is the ledger discipline that makes the position trustworthy.

For a business analyst, position states are a reporting and control design. Position reports must show available, reserved, and blocked separately, with the reasons for reservations and blocks. Liquidity dashboards must distinguish them, because a funding decision based on the total balance will overstate what can be spent. State transitions need authority rules: who can reserve, who can block, who can release, and with what evidence. And the reconciliation between the sum of the states and the account balance must run continuously. A position is not a number; it is three numbers and the rules that move money between them.
