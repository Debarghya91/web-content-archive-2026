Key terms

Liquidity transfer: A movement of funds between accounts held by the same participant to manage liquidity, for example from a main cash account to a dedicated cash account.

Reconciliation: The process of comparing records from two sources, such as a bank statement and an internal ledger, to confirm they agree.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

The camt.050 Liquidity Credit Transfer message is the instruction that starts a liquidity transfer in TIPS. When a bank wants to move EUR 1 million from its TARGET main cash account to its TIPS DCA, it sends a camt.050 with the source account, the destination account, the amount, and the currency. The camt.050 is the order, and it is the message that TIPS and TARGET validate before any money moves. A bank that sends a camt.050 with the wrong account number, the wrong currency, or an amount above its available balance will see the transfer rejected, and the rejection will be reported back so the bank can correct the instruction and try again. The camt.025 Receipt message is the confirmation that answers a camt.050, and it tells the instructing bank whether the liquidity transfer settled or was rejected. A camt.025 with a settled status confirms that the EUR 1 million has moved from the main cash account to the TIPS DCA, and the bank can now use that liquidity for instant payments. A camt.025 with a rejected status gives the reason code, such as insufficient funds, so the bank knows what went wrong. The camt.025 is therefore the receipt that closes the instruction, and operations teams match every camt.050 to its camt.025 before they treat a liquidity transfer as complete. The camt.054 Bank to Customer Debit Credit Notification is the notification that tells an account holder that its account has been debited or credited. After a liquidity transfer settles, TIPS sends a camt.054 to the holder of the debited account and to the holder of the credited account, so both sides can update their records. For example, after a EUR 500,000 transfer from a TARGET main cash account to a TIPS DCA, the bank receives a camt.054 for the debit to the main account and a camt.054 for the credit to the TIPS DCA. Those notifications feed the bank reconciliation, and they are the evidence that the liquidity position has changed. TIPS supports two modes of access, known as A2A and U2A, and the difference is about who sends the message. A2A means application to application, and it is the automated mode, where the bank system sends camt.050 messages directly to TIPS and receives camt.025 and camt.054 messages back without human intervention. U2A means user to application, and it is the manual mode, where a user logs into the TIPS graphical interface, types the liquidity transfer details into a screen, and submits the transfer by hand. Large banks use A2A for the thousands of transfers they send every day, while smaller banks and contingency operators use U2A for occasional transfers and for manual intervention when automation fails. The TIPS DCA and the TIPS AS Technical Account are two different account types, and confusing them causes errors. A TIPS DCA is a Dedicated Cash Account held by a TIPS participant, such as a bank, and it holds the liquidity that the participant uses to settle instant payments. A TIPS AS Technical Account is an account held by an ancillary system, such as a clearing house or a securities settlement system, and it is used for the technical settlement of that system transactions in TIPS. A bank cannot use a TIPS AS Technical Account as if it were its own TIPS DCA, and an ancillary system cannot use a TIPS DCA for its technical settlement. Each account type has its own rules, its own holder, and its own purpose, and the system enforces that separation.
