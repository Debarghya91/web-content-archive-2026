Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

Long before ISO 20022, and alongside SWIFT, another messaging standard was already moving business data between organisations: UN/EDIFACT, the United Nations rules for Electronic Data Interchange for Administration, Commerce and Transport, published as ISO 9735. EDIFACT is a cross industry standard: the same syntax carries purchase orders, shipping notices, invoices, and payment instructions. It grew up in the 1980s, when trading partners needed to exchange structured data without rekeying, and it is still in production today in trade finance, supply chain, and some corporate to bank channels. Any analyst working on legacy integrations will meet it.

An EDIFACT interchange is built from segments, not from fields or XML elements. An interchange opens with a UNB segment and closes with UNZ. Inside it, each message opens with UNH and closes with UNT. The message body is a sequence of segments, each identified by a three letter tag: BGM for the beginning of the message, DTM for dates and times, NAD for name and address, LIN for line items, and many more. Each segment holds data elements, some simple and some composite, and qualifiers that say what each element means. The format is compact and character efficient, which mattered when bandwidth was expensive, and it is unforgiving: a missing segment or a wrong qualifier breaks the parse.

The payment messages in EDIFACT form a small family. PAYORD is the payment order, instructing a bank to make a payment. PAYMUL is the multiple payment order, carrying several payment instructions in one message, typically sent by a corporate to its bank. PAYEXT is the extended payment order, for cases where PAYORD does not carry enough detail. On the reporting side, BANSTA is the banking status message, CREMUL is the multiple credit advice, and DEBMUL is the multiple debit advice: the bank telling the corporate what has been credited or debited. Together they cover the same ground as the SWIFT payment and reporting messages, in a different syntax.

EDIFACT differs from SWIFT MT and ISO 20022 in scope and in shape. SWIFT messages are financial: they assume banks, accounts, and value dates. EDIFACT is cross industry: the same standard describes a shipment and a payment, and a payment message may reference the commercial documents it settles. The syntax differs too: SWIFT MT uses tagged fields in blocks, ISO 20022 uses nested XML elements with names, and EDIFACT uses segments with qualifiers. Mapping between them is a translation task that needs a mapping sheet, a qualifier dictionary, and careful testing, because the same business fact is expressed in three different shapes.

EDIFACT survives because replacing working integrations is expensive and risky. A corporate that has exchanged PAYMUL files with its bank for twenty years, with tested mappings and reconciled reporting, has little reason to rebuild the link while it works. Trade finance keeps EDIFACT alive too: the commercial documents that surround a payment, the invoice, the bill of lading, the customs declaration, are EDIFACT messages, and the payment instruction sits naturally alongside them. The MT103 REMIT variant even allowed an EDIFACT payload inside its 77T envelope, a bridge between the two worlds that shows how long they have coexisted.

New corporate payment flows are built on ISO 20022 and APIs: pain.001 for payment initiation, pain.008 for direct debits, camt.053 for statements. EDIFACT knowledge still earns its place. Legacy integrations must be read, mapped, and eventually migrated, and the analyst who can read a PAYMUL, explain a qualifier, and trace a segment to its ISO 20022 equivalent is the analyst who can plan the migration without breaking the business. Standards rarely die outright; they fade into the corners of the estate, and the corners still need owners.
