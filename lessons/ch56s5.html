Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

pacs.002: An ISO 20022 message one financial institution sends to another to report the status of a payment instruction.

  1. What to do. Name the model, state when settlement becomes final, and explain what happens if the sending bank does not have enough liquidity.

Correct result. Real time gross settlement settles each payment individually in central bank money with immediate finality, but it needs liquidity for every payment. Deferred net settlement nets payments and settles the net position, which needs less liquidity but leaves settlement risk until the net settles.

Why. Settlement is where money actually moves. The model decides the risk, the liquidity need, and when the payment is truly final.

Transcript and full text

Show transcript

T2S settles securities transactions in central bank money, and it does so for the whole euro area on one platform. When a bank buys securities, T2S moves the securities to the buyer securities account and moves the cash to the seller Dedicated Cash Account at the same time. A purchase of EUR 5 million of French government bonds, known as OATs, settles when the bonds leave the seller account at Euroclear France and the EUR 5 million leaves the buyer T2S Dedicated Cash Account. Neither leg settles without the other, so neither party carries settlement risk after the trade.

T2S serves central securities depositories across Europe, not just one market. A German bank buying Italian bonds, a French bank buying Dutch bonds and a Spanish bank buying German bonds all settle on the same platform, in the same central bank money, under the same rules. Each bank holds a T2S Dedicated Cash Account, funded from its Main Cash Account, and each central securities depository holds the securities accounts. A EUR 20 million trade in German Bunds settles in T2S in the same way as a EUR 200,000 trade in a corporate bond, with the same delivery versus payment protection on both.

TIPS settles instant payments, and its promise is speed at any hour. A retail customer who sends EUR 250 to a friend at 20 minutes to midnight on a Saturday expects the money to arrive in seconds, not on Monday morning. TIPS debits the payer bank TIPS Dedicated Cash Account and credits the payee bank TIPS Dedicated Cash Account immediately, and it sends a pacs.002 status report to both banks confirming settlement. The whole round trip, from the customer pressing send to the payee bank receiving confirmation, must complete within ten seconds under the instant payment scheme rules.

TIPS works 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, with no cut off and no end of day pause. Banks prefund their TIPS Dedicated Cash Accounts from their Main Cash Accounts, because there is no central bank window overnight to add liquidity. A bank that expects EUR 2 million of instant payments overnight will hold EUR 2 million on its TIPS account before the evening, and it will monitor the balance through the night. If the TIPS account runs dry, new instant payments are rejected at once, so funding discipline is a daily operational duty, not a monthly task.

T2S and TIPS both depend on the Main Cash Account, and that shared funding is what makes TARGET a single liquidity system. A bank treasurer looks at one MCA balance in the morning, then decides how much to allocate to RTGS for large payments, how much to T2S for securities settlement, and how much to TIPS for instant payments. A EUR 10 million bond settlement, a EUR 4 million large value payment and EUR 1 million of instant payments can all be funded from the same EUR 15 million MCA balance, moved with camt.050 liquidity transfers.

The services are separate, but the money is one pool.
