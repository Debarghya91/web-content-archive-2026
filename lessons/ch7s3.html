Key terms

Execution date: The date on which the bank executes a payment instruction, debiting the debtor account and sending the message onward.

Charge bearer: A code that states who pays the charges for a payment. In ISO 20022 the values are DEBT for debtor, CRED for creditor, SHAR for shared and SLEV for service level.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

The Payment Information box is highlighted between the Group Header and the transaction block, with its debtor and charge fields shown in the XML panel.

Exercise

Work out what the beneficiary receives for a $10,000 payment under OUR, SHA and BEN.

Use the charge arrangement rules in this lesson and the correspondent and beneficiary bank fees named in the lesson.

  1. What to do. Calculate the amount credited under each of the three arrangements and state who pays each fee.

Correct result. Under OUR the sender pays all charges and the beneficiary receives the full amount. Under SHA the sender pays the sending charges and the beneficiary pays the receiving charges. Under BEN the beneficiary pays all charges and receives the amount less all fees.

Why. The charge arrangement decides who pays and how much arrives. A customer who was promised the full amount and receives less will complain, and the complaint lands on the analyst.

Show transcript

The Payment Information block, often shortened to PmtInf, is the second level in a pacs.008, and it contains information that applies to a group of transactions that share the same debtor, the same debtor agent, and the same payment method. It includes the Payment Information Identification, which is a unique reference for the block, the Payment Method, which is usually TRF for a credit transfer, and the Requested Execution Date, which is the date on which the debtor wants the payment to be executed. It also includes the Debtor, the Debtor Account, and the Debtor Agent, which together identify who is paying, from which account, and through which bank.

The Payment Information block also contains the Charges Account, if charges are to be debited from a different account, and the Charge Bearer, which states who bears the charges, using codes such as DEBT for the debtor, CRED for the creditor, SHAR for shared, and SLEV for service level. The Charge Bearer in a pacs.008 is the equivalent of field 71A in an MT103, and it has the same business impact. If the Charge Bearer is SHAR, the beneficiary may receive less than the instructed amount, because intermediary and creditor agent charges will be deducted.

If it is DEBT, the debtor bears all charges and the beneficiary should receive the full amount. A single pacs.008 can contain multiple Payment Information blocks, for example if it contains payments from different debtors or through different debtor agents. Each block can contain multiple transactions, all of which share the same debtor and debtor agent. An analyst must understand this hierarchy, because it affects how messages are constructed, how they are validated, and how errors are handled.

If one Payment Information block is invalid, the other blocks in the same message may still be processed, depending on the system. Your test cases should cover single block and multi block messages, and should check that the Payment Information details are correctly applied to every transaction in the block.
