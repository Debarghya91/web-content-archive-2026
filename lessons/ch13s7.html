Key terms

Correspondent banking: A service where one bank holds accounts for another bank so the second bank can make payments in currencies or countries where it has no branch.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Exception: A payment that cannot be processed normally and needs manual handling, investigation or repair. Exceptions arise from missing data, failed screening, account errors or system faults.

Correspondent banking is one of the most heavily controlled areas in finance, because a correspondent account can be used to move money for money laundering, for terrorist financing, or for evading sanctions, and the correspondent bank is responsible for checking the payments that pass through its account. Every payment that goes through a correspondent account is screened against sanctions lists, including the lists maintained by the United Nations, the United States Office of Foreign Assets Control, the European Union and the United Kingdom. If a payment mentions a sanctioned person, a sanctioned country or a sanctioned entity, it is stopped, and the correspondent bank must decide whether to reject it, to block it, or to report it.

A payment that is blocked is held and cannot be released without permission from the authorities, which is a serious outcome that the analyst must design for. The correspondent bank also has to know its respondent bank, a process known as know your customer for banks, and it must understand the respondent's customers, its business, its controls and its risk profile. The correspondent will ask for information about the respondent's ownership, its licences, its anti money laundering policies, its transaction volumes and its customer base, and it will review this information regularly.

If the correspondent is not satisfied, it can restrict the account, raise the fees, or close the relationship. This is the de-risking trend described at the start of the chapter. The cost of knowing the customer and screening the payments has made some relationships unprofitable, and the correspondent has chosen to exit rather than to take the risk. For the payment itself, compliance means that the payment message must carry enough information for the correspondent and the intermediary banks to screen it.

The message must identify the originator with a name, an account number and an address, and it must identify the beneficiary with a name and an account number. A payment with missing or vague information will be stopped for a query, and the query will delay the payment by days. The analyst must design the payment capture screen to collect the full originator and beneficiary information, must validate it before the payment is sent, and must ensure that the information is carried through every hop in the payment chain.

A payment that loses its originator information on the way is a payment that will be stopped, and the customer will ask why a payment that was sent on Monday has still not arrived on Friday.
