Key terms

Correspondent banking: A service where one bank holds accounts for another bank so the second bank can make payments in currencies or countries where it has no branch.

Indirect participant: A bank or institution that uses a payment system through a direct participant, without holding its own settlement account.

Direct participant: A bank or institution that holds an account in a payment system and sends and receives payments directly.

  1. What to do. Name the model, state when settlement becomes final, and explain what happens if the sending bank does not have enough liquidity.

Correct result. Real time gross settlement settles each payment individually in central bank money with immediate finality, but it needs liquidity for every payment. Deferred net settlement nets payments and settles the net position, which needs less liquidity but leaves settlement risk until the net settles.

Why. Settlement is where money actually moves. The model decides the risk, the liquidity need, and when the payment is truly final.

CIPS clears and settles renminbi payments moving across China's borders, giving those payments a dedicated route of their own. The full name is the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System, a wholesale payment system approved by the People's Bank of China and operated by CIPS Co., Ltd., based in Shanghai. The first phase went live in October 2015, and a second phase in May 2018 extended the service hours and added a deferred net settlement option alongside real time gross settlement. CIPS supports trade, investment and financial market transactions, and it both clears and settles the payments it carries, which sets it apart from messaging networks that carry instructions without moving money.

China built CIPS because cross border renminbi business had outgrown the correspondent banking routes available to it. A payment passing through several correspondent banks picked up time, cost and a point of failure at each step. A dedicated system under Chinese supervision gave renminbi payments a direct route independent of other countries' systems. CIPS therefore runs long hours covering Asia, Europe, Africa and the Americas, uses ISO 20022 messages in Chinese and English, and settles payments individually in real time or in netted batches for bulk business. It also supports linked settlement for financial market business, including delivery versus payment and payment versus payment arrangements.

CIPS has two tiers of participant, and the tier decides whether a bank settles in its own name. Direct participants hold a settlement account with CIPS and settle payments themselves, and include large Chinese banks and admitted overseas banks. Indirect participants hold no settlement account and reach CIPS through a direct participant acting as clearing bank. Smaller banks gain CIPS access without building their own connection, and the direct participants funding settlement accounts absorb the settlement risk. An indirect participant agrees cut off times, formats, fees and returns handling with its clearing bank, and still runs its own sanctions and anti money laundering screening.

Follow a cross border renminbi payment through CIPS to see the two tiers in action. A business in Singapore pays eight million renminbi to a supplier in Shenzhen for goods already shipped. The sending bank confirms the instruction and the beneficiary details, then completes sanctions, anti money laundering and fraud screening. A direct participant sends the payment message into CIPS itself; an indirect participant's clearing bank sends it on its behalf. Messages follow ISO 20022: pacs.008 for a customer payment, pacs.009 for a payment between banks, and pacs.002 confirming the outcome. CIPS validates the message, checks funding, and moves renminbi between the direct participants' settlement accounts. CIPS confirms settlement to both sides, the receiving bank credits the supplier, and individual instructions settle in real time while bulk payments settle on a deferred net basis.

Liquidity decides whether a CIPS payment settles at once or waits. A direct participant funds its settlement account from renminbi balances moved in through CNAPS, the China National Advanced Payment System, and incoming payments add to the balance during the day. Desk staff plan the day around expected outflows, track the settlement balance as payments arrive and leave, and replenish the account in good time. When funds are short, CIPS queues the payment and retries it as new funds arrive. If funds have still not arrived by the cut off, the payment is rejected or held for the next operating day.

The relationship between CIPS, correspondent banking and SWIFT is complementary rather than a simple replacement. SWIFT carries payment instructions between banks but does not settle payments, while CIPS both processes the instruction and settles the renminbi, so the two systems answer different parts of the same journey. In practice many CIPS payments still begin or end as SWIFT messages, with conversion between the two systems. Correspondent banking remains in use where no CIPS route exists, for other currencies, or where a bank prefers an existing correspondent. CIPS does not remove controls: flows remain subject to foreign exchange and capital account rules administered by the People's Bank of China and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange, and banks still screen for sanctions and financial crime.

For an analyst, the practical questions on a CIPS payment mirror those on any cross border payment, with the participant tier added. The analyst should establish each bank's tier, the clearing bank behind any indirect participant, and the settlement mode that applies. A format error means a return for correction, a sanctions stop means a hold for review, and wrong beneficiary details mean a return and a new payment. A payment is not final until CIPS confirms settlement, and a queued payment is not a paid payment.
