Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

ISO 20022: An international standard for financial messaging that defines XML message formats for payments, securities and trade.

Clearing: The process of exchanging payment instructions between banks and calculating what each bank owes the others.

Use the message structure, fields and example in this lesson.

  1. What to do. List the main blocks of the message, name the field that carries the amount, and name the field that identifies the payment.

Correct result. The message is made of blocks, each carrying a part of the payment instruction. The amount field and the identification field are the two a receiving bank checks first, because without them it cannot process or reconcile the payment.

Why. A message is only useful if you can read it. The blocks tell the receiving bank what to do, and the amount and identification fields are what it checks first.

The ISO 20022 message catalogue is organised by business area, and the three most important areas for payments are pacs, pain, and camt. The pacs area, which stands for payments clearing and settlement, contains messages exchanged between financial institutions, such as pacs.008 for a customer credit transfer, pacs.009 for a financial institution credit transfer, pacs.004 for a payment return, and pacs.002 for a payment status report. These are the messages that replace MT103, MT202, MT900, and related MT messages in the interbank space.

The pain area, which stands for payments initiation, contains messages exchanged between a customer and a bank, such as pain.001 for a customer credit transfer initiation, pain.002 for a payment status report to the customer, and pain.008 for a customer direct debit initiation. These messages are used when a corporate sends a bulk file to its bank, or when a payment is initiated through an API. The camt area, which stands for cash management, contains messages for statements and reporting, such as camt.052 for an intraday bank to customer account report, camt.053 for an end of day statement, and camt.054 for a debit credit notification. These replace MT940, MT942, and MT900 and MT910 in the reporting space.

The catalogue is large, and it continues to grow, but an analyst does not need to memorise every message. Focus on the messages that are relevant to your product, learn their structure, and understand how they relate to each other. A payment that starts as a pain.001 becomes a pacs.008 in the interbank space, and its status is reported back as a pacs.002 and then as a pain.002. A statement that was once an MT940 becomes a camt.053. That end to end chain, from initiation to clearing to reporting, is the real catalogue, and it is what you need to be able to trace.

Worked example with real numbers: a corporate customer initiates a EUR 20,000 supplier payment, and the message catalogue shows which message is used at each step. The customer sends a pain.001 payment initiation to Bank A. Bank A sends a pacs.008. Bank B sends a pacs.002. At the end of the day Bank B sends a camt.053 statement to the supplier, showing the EUR 20,000 credit with the end to end identifier. If the supplier account had been closed, Bank B would have sent a pacs.004 return with reason AC04, and Bank A would have sent a pain.002. Each message has a purpose, a sender, a receiver and a timing, and using the wrong message, for example a pacs.008 where a pain.001 is needed, will be rejected, because the receiver expects a different message type.

Failure and recovery: a pain.001 sent to the clearing system, instead of to the bank, is rejected, because the clearing system expects a pacs.008, not a customer initiation, and the recovery is to send the pain.001 to the bank, which creates the pacs.008. A pacs.002 sent with the wrong status, for example ACCP when the payment was actually rejected, misleads the customer, and the recovery is to correct the status and to resend the pacs.002 with RJCT and the reason code. A camt. Each failure is a catalogue failure, using the wrong message or the wrong status, and the fix is to use the correct message for the step, as defined in the catalogue on iso20022.org.

For a business analyst, product owner or subject matter expert, the test is to name, for any payment, the message used at each step, its sender, its receiver, its purpose and its timing, from the catalogue: pain for initiation, pacs for clearing and settlement, camt for cash management, admi for administration, and head for the header. The catalogue is the map, and a requirement that names a message without naming its step will be ambiguous, because the same message name can be used at different steps with different rules. The ISO 20022 catalogue on iso20022.org is the source, and the SWIFT CBPR+ usage guidelines are the source for which catalogue messages are used for cross-border payments, and both must be cited in the specification.
