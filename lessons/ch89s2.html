Key terms

Payment hub: A central system in a bank that receives payment instructions from all channels, validates them, applies screening and routing rules, and sends them to the correct clearing system or correspondent.

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

Show transcript

A clearing house does not settle continuously. It settles in cycles, and each cycle has a cut-off time, a deadline after which a payment waits for the next cycle. The cut-off is the single most important time in retail payments, because it decides the value date, which is the date the creditor is credited and the date from which interest is calculated. A payment that misses a cut-off is not lost, but it is a day late, and for a customer expecting money that day, a day late is a failure.

A typical STEP2 day has several cycles for SEPA credit transfers. A payment submitted before the morning cut-off at 09:30 is cleared in the morning cycle and settles at 11:00. A payment submitted before the midday cut-off at 12:30 is cleared in the midday cycle and settles at 14:00. A payment submitted before the afternoon cut-off at 15:30 is cleared in the afternoon cycle and settles at 17:00. A payment submitted after the last cut-off waits for the first cycle of the next business day. The value date follows the settlement: a payment settled at 17:00 on Monday has a value date of Monday, and the creditor sees it on Monday. A payment that misses the last cycle has a value date of Tuesday.

The bank's own customer cut-off is always earlier than the CSM cut-off, because the bank needs time to receive the payment, validate it, screen it for sanctions and fraud, and format it for the CSM. If the CSM cut-off is 15:30, the bank might set its customer cut-off at 14:30, giving itself one hour for processing. A customer who submits at 14:45 misses the bank's cut-off and the payment goes in the next cycle, even though the CSM would still have accepted it. That hour is the bank's safety margin, and it is where most cut-off complaints come from, because the customer sees the CSM cut-off published and does not understand why the bank's cut-off is earlier.

Direct debits have a different cycle logic because they are tied to due dates. Under the SDD rulebook, a CORE direct debit must be submitted to the CSM in time for the debtor bank to process it before the due date. The exact lead time depends on the CSM and on whether the debit is a first collection or a recurrent one, and the bank's direct debit system must calculate the submission date from the due date, not the other way round. A direct debit submitted a day late is not just late, it may be rejected by the CSM, and the creditor must present it again with a new due date.

Weekends and holidays stop the cycles. TARGET2 is closed on TARGET holidays, which include New Year's Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, 1 May, Christmas Day and 26 December. On those days there is no euro settlement, so a SEPA payment submitted on Christmas Eve settles on 27 December. National holidays stop domestic clearing in that country, so a payment to a French bank on 14 July, Bastille Day, waits even though the rest of Europe is working. The payment hub must hold payments over a holiday and release them on the next business day, and it must not release them early, because the CSM will reject a file submitted on a day it is closed.

Edge cases are common. A file submitted exactly at the cut-off may be accepted or rejected depending on whether the CSM measures the arrival time at its gateway or at its processing queue, and the difference is measured in seconds. Clock skew between the bank's system and the CSM's system can move a payment across a cut-off. Daylight saving changes in spring and autumn shift the cut-off by an hour in one direction, and a bank that does not adjust its own cut-off will either miss a cycle or submit an hour early. A cycle delayed by the CSM, because a participant failed to fund its settlement, pushes every payment in that cycle later, and the bank must tell its customers.

For a business analyst, cut-offs are requirements with customer impact. The requirements must state the customer cut-off for each instrument, the CSM cut-off for each cycle, the value date rule, and the holiday calendar. The acceptance criteria must include a test where a payment submitted before the customer cut-off settles the same day, a test where a payment submitted after the customer cut-off settles the next business day, and a test where a payment submitted on a TARGET holiday is held and released on the next business day.
