Key terms

Settlement: The final movement of funds between banks to discharge obligations created by cleared payments. Settlement is usually made in central bank money or through correspondent accounts.

Value date: The date on which funds become available to the beneficiary and on which interest starts to accrue. It is the date used for settlement and for nostro posting.

Exercise

Run the operations shift in this lesson: handle the payroll backlog, the CHAPS outage and the nostro shortfall, and write the handover note.

Use the lesson facts: 6,400 payments worth 38 million pounds, a CHAPS partial outage delaying settlement by at least two hours, a nostro balance of 4.2 million dollars against a 22 million dollar queue, and 5,900 payments released by quarter past 10, leaving 500 records for individual confirmation.

  1. What to do. State the three problems, the bulk fix, the CHAPS action, the nostro shortfall, and the handover note.

Correct result. The scenario starts at 8 in the morning on a Tuesday in London. You are the operations team leader for a bank that processes 1.8 million payments a day. The overnight batch left a backlog of 6,400 payments in the repair queue, worth 38 million pounds, because a file from a large payroll customer contained 6,400 records with an invalid sort code. The CHAPS system has reported a partial outage, with settlement delayed by at least two hours. Your US dollar nostro account holds 4.2 million dollars, but the queue of dollar payments waiting for release is worth 22 million dollars. You ask the payroll analysts to confirm whether the sort code error is the same for all 6,400 records, which would allow a bulk fix, or whether each record needs individual checking. The payroll error is the same transposed sort code in all 6,400 records, so the team applies a bulk correction, revalidates the file and releases 5,900 payments by quarter past 10 in the morning, leaving 500 records that need individual customer confirmation. You ask the CHAPS analysts to list every CHAPS payment above 500,000 pounds and its value date, so you know which customers face a real deadline. You write the handover note with the closing queue counts, the incident reference for the CHAPS outage, the names of the two customers who need a follow up call tomorrow, and the lesson learned, which is to add a sort code validation check to the payroll file intake so the same error cannot happen again.

Why. A shift is three problems at once, and the handover note is what stops the next shift repeating the same error.

The scenario starts at 8 in the morning on a Tuesday in London. You are the operations team leader for a bank that processes 1.8 million payments a day. The overnight batch left a backlog of 6,400 payments in the repair queue, worth 38 million pounds, because a file from a large payroll customer contained 6,400 records with an invalid sort code. The CHAPS system has reported a partial outage, with settlement delayed by at least two hours. Your US dollar nostro account with JPMorgan Chase holds 4.2 million dollars, but the queue of dollar payments waiting for release is worth 22 million dollars. Your first decision is what to do in the first thirty minutes, before the morning peak arrives. Your first step is to stabilise the position and get facts before you act. You call a short huddle at 5 past 8 in the morning, assign four analysts to the payroll file, two analysts to the CHAPS queue and one analyst to the dollar liquidity position. You ask the payroll analysts to confirm whether the sort code error is the same for all 6,400 records, which would allow a bulk fix, or whether each record needs individual checking. You ask the CHAPS analysts to list every CHAPS payment above 500,000 pounds and its value date, so you know which customers face a real deadline. You call treasury at quarter past 8 in the morning to ask for a dollar funding plan, and you log the incident with technology so the CHAPS outage is formally tracked. By half past 9 in the morning the facts are clearer and you can make the first hard choices. The payroll error is the same transposed sort code in all 6,400 records, so the team applies a bulk correction, revalidates the file and releases 5,900 payments by quarter past 10 in the morning, leaving 500 records that need individual customer confirmation. For CHAPS, you decide to hold all payments below 100,000 pounds in the queue, because they can settle later in the day, and to prepare the 38 payments above 500,000 pounds, worth 61 million pounds, for immediate release the moment CHAPS recovers. For dollars, treasury confirms it can sell 18 million euros for dollars by 11 in the morning, which will fund the queue, so you tell the dollar analysts to hold releases until the funds arrive rather than releasing a few and leaving the rest unfunded. At 12 noon CHAPS recovers, but the recovery creates a new risk, because the system is now facing a backlog from every bank in the country. You decide to release your 38 high value CHAPS payments in value order, starting with the largest payment of 7.5 million pounds for a house purchase completion, then the payment of 4.2 million pounds for a corporate acquisition, and then the remaining payments in descending order. You deliberately hold the low value CHAPS payments until 2 in the afternoon, because releasing 2,000 small payments at once would slow the system and risk a second outage. You update customer service with a short statement, and you call the two corporate customers with the largest payments to confirm settlement personally. By 5 in the afternoon the day is closing, and your last job is to prove that the day balanced and to hand over cleanly. The payroll file is fully cleared, with 6,400 payments settled and 500 individual confirmations obtained by phone. All 38 high value CHAPS payments have settled, and the low value CHAPS queue is below 200 items. The dollar account is funded, the queue is cleared, and the closing balance is 6.8 million dollars. You write the handover note with the closing queue counts, the incident reference for the CHAPS outage, the names of the two customers who need a follow up call tomorrow, and the lesson learned, which is to add a sort code validation check to the payroll file intake so the same error cannot happen again.
