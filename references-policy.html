References Policy

What counts as an authoritative source on Payments Training Hub, how sources are cited, and what the internal source register holds.

What counts as authoritative

An authoritative source is the documentation published by the body that owns the subject: the scheme rulebook, the message standard, or the official guidance of the scheme operator, the central bank, or the standards body. The sources used across the curriculum are:

  • SWIFT, including the CBPR+ usage guidelines for ISO 20022 migration and the MT message standards.
  • ISO 20022, the global message standard, including the pacs, pain, camt and head message definitions.
  • European Payments Council (EPC), the SEPA rulebooks for credit transfer, direct debit and instant payments.
  • Pay.UK, the operator of Faster Payments, BACS and CHAPS in the United Kingdom.
  • Federal Reserve, for Fedwire and FedNow, and Nacha for ACH in the United States.
  • NPCI, for UPI, IMPS, NEFT, RTGS and NACH in India.
  • BIS Committee on Payments and Market Infrastructures (CPMI), for payment system principles and cross-border payments guidance.

Blog posts, vendor marketing, social media posts and secondary summaries are not authoritative sources. They may point to a source, but a lesson is never written from them alone.

How sources are cited

Lessons name the scheme or standard they are describing, so the reader can check the claim against the source. The Sources page lists the authoritative sources for the whole curriculum and the review policy that keeps content current. Where a lesson relies on a specific rulebook version, the chapter review date shows when it was last checked against that version.

Naming a source is not a claim of affiliation or endorsement. Scheme operators, central banks and standards bodies are named because their documentation is the authority for the subject, not because of any relationship with this site.

The internal source register

Behind the published pages, the site keeps an internal source register that records, for each lesson, which source documents it was written from and which parts of those documents it relies on. The register is an internal working record: it is used to check corrections and to re-check lessons when a source is updated. It is not published, and it contains no personal data.

When sources conflict or change

Where two sources describe the same subject differently, the lesson says which source it follows and notes the difference where it matters. Where a scheme publishes a new rulebook version, the affected lessons are checked against the new version and updated, and the chapter review date is updated with them. A lesson reflects the rules as they stood at its last review date. Before relying on a rule for a live payment, check the current rulebook from the source listed above.
