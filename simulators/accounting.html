Payment Accounting Simulator

Every payment is a set of ledger entries, and every entry must balance: total debits equal total credits at every step. Pick a scenario, then step through the entries one at a time. Each step shows the T-account debits and credits and the running balances. "Balanced" means debits equal credits at each step; it does not prove the entries are on the right bank's books, so every scenario states whose books it shows.

Ledger map: which account is which

Ledger / accountWhat it isBalance-sheet treatment
Customer ledger (core banking)The bank's accounts for its customers: debtor and creditor accounts.Liability to the customer.
General ledger (GL)The bank's own books: double entry, trial balance proven daily, formal period close separate.Debits must equal credits.
Nostro accountThe bank's account with a correspondent bank: money the bank owns abroad.Asset ('due from banks'). A credit reduces it.
Vostro accountThe mirror image on the correspondent's books: the same balance owed to the other bank.Liability ('due to banks').
Settlement accountThe bank's account at the central bank (RTGS) or clearing house. Always state whose account at which system.Asset (central-bank money).
Clearing account (transit)An internal transit account that holds funds briefly between two postings.Must clear to zero by the end of the day.
Suspense accountHolds unexplained items pending investigation, with a named owner and daily ageing.Must be aged and cleared; never a routine transit account.

Every account in every step below is labelled with its ledger, so a customer account, a nostro account and a settlement account are never confused with each other. "Settlement account" is never used without stating whose settlement account at which system.

A customer buys goods online for EUR 120 with a card. This scenario shows the ACQUIRER bank's books only, and states that up front. Illustrative example: an EU consumer credit card, so interchange is at the 0.3% cap (EUR 0.36); for a consumer debit card the cap is 0.2% (EUR 0.24). The scheme fee (EUR 0.07) and acquirer markup (EUR 0.48) are illustrative commercial figures for this merchant's contract, not capped rates, and they vary by scheme, card and merchant contract. The merchant account is held at the acquirer. The issuer's side is shown for explanation in the final panel, not posted here.

Step 1: Authorisation hold (memo only, no ledger entry)

The customer card is authorised for EUR 120. No money moves yet. An authorisation hold is a memo / available-balance hold: the available balance falls by EUR 120, but the ledger balance is unchanged until clearing posts. There is deliberately no debit and credit in this step, because a hold is not a double-entry GL posting.

Memo only, not a GL posting. Memo only, not a GL posting. Ledger balance unchanged: EUR 0.00. Available balance: EUR 120.00 lower. If the purchase never clears, the hold simply expires and the available balance is restored, with no ledger entry at all.

A creditor collects EUR 89.99 from a debtor by SEPA direct debit, mandate reference MND-2026-00417, and the debtor then asks for a refund. This is SDD CORE, stated explicitly: for an authorised Core collection the debtor may claim a refund within eight weeks of the debit, with no reason needed, and the creditor cannot refuse it. SDD B2B has no such refund right for authorised collections. Reason code MD06 (refund requested by the debtor, Core only). An unauthorised collection can be claimed for 13 months. This scenario shows the DEBTOR bank's books only. The creditor bank's debit of the creditor's account happens on the creditor bank's own books, and is shown for explanation, not posted here.

Message sent, payment accepted, payment settled, customer account credited: five different moments

Status ladder (pacs.002, in order). ACTC: technical validation passed. ACCP: customer-profile checks passed, accepted for processing. ACSP: settlement in process. ACSC: settlement completed on the debtor account. ACCC: settlement completed on the creditor account. RJCT: rejected (before settlement). PDNG: pending. No business outcome is a status code: "rejected", "returned" and "recalled" describe what happened, the code describes the status.

Message sent. The bank has transmitted the payment message, for example a pacs.008, to the next bank. Nothing has moved yet except data. A sent message can still be rejected, returned, or lost in transit.

Payment accepted (ACCP). The receiving bank or clearing system has checked the message and accepted it for processing. Acceptance means the payment is well formed and will be processed. It does not mean the money has moved. In SEPA SCT a pacs.002 is normally sent only for rejections (no news means accepted); in SCT Inst it is a positive and negative confirmation; under CBPR+ only the negative (RJCT) notification is mandatory and positive statuses are by bilateral agreement.

Payment settling (ACSP) and settled (ACSC). ACSP means settlement is in process; ACSC means settlement is completed on the debtor account. Settlement differs by model. RTGS (CHAPS/T2/Fedwire): final and irrevocable on the central-bank posting. Nostro (correspondent banking): final on the nostro debit under the correspondent agreement, in commercial-bank money, not through a central-bank account. Deferred net settlement: the net position settles at the scheduled time. Instant payments (SCT Inst / TIPS / Faster Payments): the beneficiary is credited within the scheme maximum (10 seconds for SCT Inst), effectively simultaneous with interbank settlement; if the payee cannot be credited in time, the instant payment is rejected and the payer restored. On-us payments have no interbank settlement at all: they are an internal posting only.

Customer account credited (ACCC). The beneficiary bank has posted the funds to the beneficiary customer account, so the customer can see the money. This is the only one of the moments the beneficiary customer experiences directly. It can happen after settlement, or never: if the account is closed, the payment is returned instead, via pacs.004, which is a new payment after settlement. A rejection (pacs.002 RJCT) is different: it happens before settlement, when nothing has moved.

Learning outcome and debrief

Learning outcome. After stepping through this simulator you can post the ledger entries for a payment and show that total debits equal total credits at every step, naming whose books each entry sits on.

Debrief. A balanced set of entries proves the arithmetic, not that the entries are on the right books, so every scenario states whose books it shows. Watch the nostro, settlement and suspense accounts: a suspense item must be aged and cleared with a named owner, never left as a routine transit account.
