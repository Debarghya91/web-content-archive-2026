Payment Exception Investigation Simulator

Twenty-one exceptions, as operations sees them. Read the symptom, pick the first investigation action you would take, and learn immediately whether it is right, what evidence it produces, and what happens next. Work through all 21 and see your score for correct first try investigations. Each scenario carries a timestamped message trail (MsgId, UETR, EndToEndId, status code) where one exists, because an investigation is proved by its audit trail, not by prose alone.

1. Insufficient funds

2. Invalid account

What is your first investigation action?

3. Invalid BIC

What operations sees. A Faster Payments single immediate payment was submitted but no acknowledgement arrived within the timeout window. The clearing system may be slow, or the message may be lost. 14. Rejection

16. Reconciliation mismatch

What operations sees. A customer realises a USD 12,500 payment (INV-9100) was sent twice and asks for the duplicate back. The beneficiary account has already been credited. Trail: original pacs.008 MsgId PACS008-20261106-007, EndToEndId INV-9100, settled 2026-11-06; camt.056 not yet sent.

19. Customer cancellation before settlement

What operations sees. A customer calls an hour after instructing a USD 25,000 payment (INV-8841) and asks to cancel it. The payment has been accepted (ACCP) but has not yet settled (no ACSC). 20. Clearing rejection

What operations sees. Clearing rejects a EUR 25,000 SEPA payment with a pacs.002 RJCT, reason code AM05 (duplicate payment): the clearing house already holds a payment with the same MsgId. Trail: pacs.008 MsgId PACS008-20261106-011, EndToEndId INV-9090, sent 08:15; pacs.002 RJCT AM05 at 08:17.

21. Routing failure

What operations sees. A USD 40,000 payment to a new destination country cannot be routed: no correspondent route exists in the routing table, and the intermediary bank that would carry it has rejected the route. Trail: instruction INV-9110, value date 2026-11-07; routing engine returned 'no route' at 14:20.

What is your first investigation action?

Reject, return, recall, cancellation, reversal, repair, retry, investigation: keep them distinct

Reject (pacs.002 RJCT): before settlement. Nothing settled, the customer is re-credited in full. Return (pacs.004): after settlement, when the creditor agent cannot credit (for example AC04 closed account). Only the creditor agent sends it. Recall / cancellation request (camt.056): the debtor agent asks for funds back, before settlement or after credit; the answer is a pacs.004 with reason FOCR (positive) or a camt.029 (negative). Reversal: a distinct R-transaction, for example pacs.007 for a SEPA direct debit reversal, or an automatic scheme reversal on a Faster Payments timeout. Repair: the same payment with corrected data, re-released as a new instruction with a fresh InstrId and the same EndToEndId. Retry: resending the same message with the same MsgId and UETR, only after proving the original was not received. Investigation: a pacs.028 status request (answered by pacs.002) when a payment already sent has no confirmation; for SCT Inst it is sent if no response arrives after 25 seconds.

Learning outcome and debrief

Learning outcome. After working through this simulator you can investigate 21 payment exceptions as operations sees them and pick the correct first investigation action, with the evidence it produces and what happens next.

Debrief. A good investigation is proved by its audit trail: MsgId, UETR, EndToEndId and status code where they exist. Keep reject, return, recall, cancellation, reversal, repair, retry and investigation distinct: they are different actions with different messages and different consequences. Check your score for correct first try investigations and rework the ones you missed.
