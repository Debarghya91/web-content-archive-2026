ISO 20022 Mapping Workbench

Map fields between ISO 20022 messages. Pick a source message and a target message, connect their fields, choose a transformation, validate the result and generate the target XML. Then try the guided exercise: turn a pain.001 customer instruction into a pacs.008 interbank payment. All data is synthetic and for education only. Nothing here connects to a real banking system and nothing moves real money.

Read this first: there is no universal one-to-one transformation between these messages. Real mappings depend on the bank's canonical data model, its enrichment rules and its implementation guidelines for each message. A field that exists in one message may be split, combined, enriched or dropped in another, and the same source field can map differently at different banks. The workbench shows the standard element names and the most common mappings. The validation here is a simplified educational check of shapes and formats, not full scheme or implementation-guideline validation. Check current scheme rules and your bank's guidelines before relying on any mapping in live work.

A corporate customer sent a pain.001 with one EUR 25,000 payment. Your job is to map it into the pacs.008 that the debtor bank sends onward to the creditor bank. Map the nine fields below, check your work against the reference mapping, then generate the pacs.008 XML. Each correct mapping earns 10 points. Generated pacs.008








The generated XML contains only the fields you mapped. In a real pacs.008 the header fields (GrpHdr.MsgId, CreDtTm, NbOfTxs) would also be present; they are filled in by the sending bank's system. The UETR is also normally added by the sending bank.



Teaching note. These mappings follow the standard element names and the most common reading of the pain.001 to pacs.008 flow. Real banks differ: some enrich the pacs.008 with data the pain.001 never carried, some derive the interbank settlement amount from the instructed amount plus charges, and some apply their own implementation guidelines on top of the standard. Status codes used include ACCP (accepted) and ACSC (accepted settlement completed). Reason codes used include AC01 (incorrect account number) and AC04 (closed account). Check current scheme rules before relying on any of this in live work.
