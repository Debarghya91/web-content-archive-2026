1 What SWIFT MT is, and why it lasted so longBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain SWIFT MT is. SWIFT MT, where MT stands for Message Type, is the long established messaging standard used by banks around the world to exchange payment instructions and related information. The fixed field structure also makes MT messages relatively easy to parse, to test, and to repair, which operations teams value. 4:44Completed ›
2 MT103, the customer paymentBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain MT103, the customer payment. The MT103 is a single customer credit transfer message, and before the CBPR+ migration it was the most widely used cross border payment message in the world. A business analyst must understand the MT103 field structure, the mandatory versus optional fields, and the business meaning of each field, because those details determine whether the payment is processed automatically or falls into a repair queue. 7:13Completed ›
3 MT202, bank to bank cover paymentsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain MT202, bank to bank cover payments. The MT202 is a general financial institution transfer, and it is used for bank to bank payments where the originator and the beneficiary are both financial institutions. An analyst must be able to explain the cover method, draw the two message flows, and identify where the customer data is visible and where it is not. 6:49Completed ›
4 MT900 and MT910, confirmations of debit and creditBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain MT900 and MT910, confirmations of debit and credit. The MT900 is a confirmation of debit, and the MT910 is a confirmation of credit. Analysts should include MT900 and MT910 in their payment flow diagrams and in their reconciliation requirements, because they are the link between the payment instruction and the actual movement of funds. 4:51Completed ›
5 MT940 and MT942, account statementsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain MT940 and mt942, account statements. The MT940 is a customer statement message that provides the end of day statement for an account, usually a nostro or vostro account held with another bank. An analyst who understands MT940 and MT942 will find the camt messages familiar, because the business concepts of opening balance, transactions, and closing balance are the same. 3:55Completed ›
6 Field structure, how to read an MT messageBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain field structure, how to read an MT message. An MT message is divided into blocks, and the text block contains tagged fields. A business analyst who can read an MT103 can investigate a payment delay, can explain a deduction to a customer, and can write test cases that reflect real payment data. 7:24Completed ›
7 How to read a real MT103, worked exampleBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain to read a real MT103, worked example. This worked example is set before the CBPR+ migration, when an MT103 was still the message for a cross-border customer payment. If the beneficiary receives less than ten thousand dollars, the analyst can use the MT103 to explain why. 5:04Completed ›
8 Worked example: cover method, Paris to Madrid in USDBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain worked example: cover method, paris to madrid in USD. This worked example is set before the CBPR+ migration, so the payment travels as an MT103 announcement with an MT202 COV cover. This matters because the message is the payment instruction. A wrong field, a missing element or a misunderstood format is a failed payment, a return, or a compliance breach. 5:18Completed ›
9 Worked example: serial method, the same payment in three MT103sBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain worked example: serial method, the same payment in three mt103s. This worked example is set before the CBPR+ migration, so the payment travels as MT103s. A business analyst who can trace both methods through the same payment, field by field, understands correspondent banking at a level most practitioners never reach. 4:45Completed ›
10 MT message blocks 1 to 5: how to read a raw messageBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain MT message blocks 1 to 5: how to read a raw message. Every SWIFT MT message is built from up to five blocks, each wrapped in curly brackets and numbered 1 to 5. That skill lets an analyst read any MT message without a tool. 5:04Completed ›
11 System design deep dive: SWIFT connectivity inside a bankBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain system design deep dive: SWIFT connectivity inside a bank. A bank connects to SWIFT through Alliance Access and Alliance Gateway, which together form the bank's SWIFT interface. Operations teams use it to prove that a payment was sent, to investigate a missing payment, and to reconcile SWIFT traffic against the payment engine. 5:25Completed ›
12 MT102, many customer transfers in one messageBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain mt102, many customer transfers in one message. The MT102 is the SWIFT message that carries many customer credit transfers inside a single message. An analyst who meets one should still be able to read it: find the batch total in Sequence C, count the transactions in Sequence B, and reconcile the two before investigating any single payment inside it. 4:51Completed ›
13 MT210, notice that funds are comingBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain mt210, notice that funds are coming. The MT210 is an advance notice that funds are on their way, and it moves no money itself. For a business analyst, the MT210 is a control message rather than a payment, and the design questions are about matching rules, suspense handling, and the reconciliation of notices against statements. 5:54Completed ›
14 MT191: requesting payment of charges, interest and other expensesBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain mt191: requesting payment of charges, interest and other expenses. When a bank has incurred charges or interest on behalf of another bank and cannot deduct them from the payment itself, it needs a way to ask for the money. The analyst must check which of the two the business process needs before specifying either. 4:20Completed ›
15 MT195, MT196, MT199 and MT299: queries, answers and free format messagesBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain mt195, mt196, mt199 and mt299: queries, answers and free format messages. Not every message between banks fits a structured payment format. Sometimes one bank needs to ask another a question, answer one, or send a message that does not fit any other type. 5:12Completed ›
16 MT104: direct debits and requests for debit transferBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain mt104: direct debits and requests for debit transfer. The MT104 is the SWIFT message that pulls money instead of pushing it. A direct debit is a pull on somebody else account, so the debtor bank must be satisfied that the debtor agreed to the debit before it acts. 4:07Completed ›
17 MT200, MT201, MT203 and MT204: financial institution transfers and direct debitsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain mt200, mt201, mt203 and mt204: financial institution transfers and direct debits. Category 2 of the SWIFT MT standard holds the messages banks use to move money between themselves. Four of them form a family that every payments analyst should be able to tell apart: the MT200, the MT201, the MT203, and the MT204. 4:08Completed ›
18 MT670 and MT671: exchanging standing settlement instructions through SWIFTBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain mt670 and mt671: exchanging standing settlement instructions through SWIFT. Every payment needs to know where to settle, and that knowledge is a standing settlement instruction, usually shortened to SSI. For a business analyst, the lesson is about static data discipline. 3:58Completed ›
19 MT198: the proprietary messageBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain mt198: the proprietary message. Not every exchange between banks fits a standard message. A business analyst should treat every proprietary message as a liability to be managed: documented, versioned, and reviewed regularly against the question of whether a standard message now covers the need. 3:47Completed ›
