Clearing and settlement mechanisms

D07 Clearing, settlement and liquidity · 6 lessons

Version 1.0 | Last reviewed 8 November 2026

Learner explains what a clearing and settlement mechanism is, how STEP2, national CSMs and TIPS clear and settle payments, how a bank chooses a CSM, and how payments route when debtor and creditor banks use different CSMs, with correct terminology.

What You Can Do After This Chapter

Learning objective. Learner explains what a clearing and settlement mechanism is, how STEP2, national CSMs and TIPS clear and settle payments, how a bank chooses a CSM, and how payments route when debtor and creditor banks use different CSMs, with correct terminology.

Practical assessment. Learner receives ten short scenarios, including a 10,000 euro SCT payment through STEP2, an instant payment settling in TIPS, and a payment routed between a bank on STET and a bank on STEP2, and traces each payment from debtor bank to creditor bank. Learner then writes a one-page note on how a bank chooses a CSM. Learner submits all work with sources, calculations, and assumptions stated clearly, and reviews the result against the chapter checklist before submission.

Expected deliverables.

  • Diagram showing scheme, CSM and settlement agent layers for one SEPA payment
  • CSM selection comparison table for cost, reach, cut-offs, liquidity and resilience
  • Routing flow for a payment between banks on different CSMs

Completion criterion. Learner correctly traces at least nine of ten payments through the right CSM and settlement path and explains the choice of CSM with a concrete reason. Completion is judged on accuracy, completeness, and clear written explanation throughout.

In this chapter
  1. What a clearing and settlement mechanism is, and where it sits between banks and schemes
  2. EBA CLEARING STEP2: how it clears SEPA payments and settles in TARGET2
  3. National CSMs across Europe: how they differ and when a bank uses more than one
  4. TIPS as a CSM for instant payments: settlement in central bank money in seconds
  5. How a bank chooses a CSM: cost, reach, cut-offs, liquidity and resilience
  6. CSM interoperability and routing: when debtor and creditor banks use different CSMs
