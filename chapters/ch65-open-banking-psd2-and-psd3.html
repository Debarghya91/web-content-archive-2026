In this chapter

1 What Open Banking Is and Why Regulators Forced ItBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain open banking. Open banking is a regulated way for a customer to give a licensed third party access to a bank account, either to read information or to start a payment. For a business analyst, the point is that PSD3 is not a new idea, it is PSD2 made stronger, clearer and fairer, and the work it creates is in fraud controls, verification, consent screens and accessible authentication journeys. 5:40Completed ›
2 The Key Players: PISPs, AISPs and PIISPsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain key players: pisps, aisps and piisps. A payment initiation service provider, known as a PISP, starts a payment from a customer bank account on the customer instruction. For a business analyst, the licence type is a requirement, not a detail, because the consent screen, the API scope, and the error handling all change with it. 4:52Completed ›
3 Strong Customer Authentication and PSD3 EnhancementsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain strong customer authentication and PSD3 enhancements. Strong customer authentication requires two out of three factors, something the customer knows, such as a password or PIN, something the customer has, such as a phone or a card, and something the customer is, such as a. For a product owner, this means the authentication journey is a risk decision, not a fixed screen, and the rules for when to challenge and when to trust must be written, tested and monitored. 6:03Completed ›
4 Accessibility, Inclusive Payments and PSD3By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain accessibility, inclusive payments and PSD3. Strong customer authentication can exclude people, and that exclusion is a real risk, not an edge case. The bank must also make its screens readable, with clear language, good contrast, and compatibility with screen readers. 4:38Completed ›
5 Fraud Prevention Under PSD3 and Customer EducationBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain fraud prevention under PSD3 and customer education. Fraud in open banking takes many forms, but the most harmful is authorised push payment fraud, where a criminal tricks a customer into sending money to a criminal account. If a payment was unauthorised, meaning the customer did not approve it, the bank must refund it promptly unless it can prove the customer acted fraudulently or with gross negligence. 4:56Completed ›
6 Verification of Payee and Fraud Information ExchangeBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain verification of payee and fraud information exchange. Verification of payee is a check that runs before a payment is sent, and it compares the payee name the customer entered with the name held on the payee account. For a business analyst, the requirement is not just a check box, it is a full journey, the name entry screen, the match result screen, the warning wording, and the audit record of what the customer saw and chose. 5:31Completed ›
7 Worked Example: A Customer Pays EUR 240 Through a PISPBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain worked example: a customer pays EUR 240 through a PISP. Claire Dubois, a customer of Meridian Bank in Lyon, buys EUR 240 of kitchen goods from an online retailer called Maison Nord. A business analyst should note that the mismatch warning, the consent record, and the authentication record are all stored, because they are the evidence if Claire later disputes the payment. 2:59Completed ›
