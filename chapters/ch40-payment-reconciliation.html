Payment reconciliation: proving every payment settled where it should, and finding the ones that did not.

By the end of this chapter you will be able to

Learning objective. Learner performs payment reconciliation, matching bank statements to internal records, identifying breaks, and resolving differences with clear actions and evidence. Learner applies this skill in realistic banking scenarios, with clear examples and correct terminology throughout.

Practical assessment. Learner reconciles a week of records containing 200 payments totalling 1,250,000 dollars against a camt.053 statement, identifying five breaks, including a duplicate, a missing credit, a fee difference, a value date error, and an unmatched item. Learner then writes the resolution action and the evidence required for each break, and produces a reconciled position summary. Learner submits all work with sources, calculations, and assumptions stated clearly, and reviews the result against the chapter checklist before submission.

Expected deliverables.

  • Reconciliation worksheet for 200 payments
  • Break log with five breaks identified
  • Resolution actions with evidence per break
  • Reconciled position summary

Completion criterion. Learner finds all five breaks, states the correct resolution for each, and produces a reconciled summary that balances to the statement total. Completion is judged on accuracy, completeness, and clear written explanation throughout.

In this chapter

1 Why Reconciliation Is the Last Line of DefenceBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain reconciliation. A payment can pass every upfront check and still end up in the wrong place, which is why reconciliation exists as an independent control after processing. For a business analyst, reconciliation is also where business knowledge meets operational reality, because every break tells a story about a process gap. 3:32Completed ›
2 Nostro Reconciliation in DepthBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain nostro reconciliation. A nostro account is an account that a bank holds with another bank in a foreign currency, and its name comes from the Latin word for ours, because the money in it belongs to the holding bank. This matters because operations is where payments are reconciled, investigated and fixed. The work in this lesson is what keeps the money right. 3:36Completed ›
3 Internal Ledger, Clearing and Settlement ReconciliationBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain internal ledger, clearing and settlement reconciliation. Every payment creates at least two ledger entries inside the bank, a debit and a credit, and the discipline of double-entry bookkeeping requires those entries to balance exactly. The practical lesson for analysts is that internal reconciliation is not one check but a chain of checks, and a break in any link can hide a break further down the chain. 2:14Completed ›
4 Transaction-Level Matching: References, Amounts, Currencies and Value DatesBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain transaction-level matching: references, amounts, currencies and value dates. The payment reference is the primary key that links an internal record to an external statement line, and it must survive the whole payment journey unchanged. For example, a reference of sixteen characters sent through a system that allows only fifteen will lose its final digit, and the statement line will sit unmatched until an analyst manually links it by amount and date. 3:51Completed ›
5 Breaks: Suspense Accounts, Unmatched Transactions, Duplicates and Date MismatchesBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain breaks: suspense accounts, unmatched transactions, duplicates and date mismatches. A suspense account holds funds temporarily when the bank knows money has moved but does not yet know where it belongs, and it must never become a permanent home. The operations team posts it to a suspense account, opens an investigation, and contacts the correspondent bank to ask who sent the money and why. 3:25Completed ›
6 Automated vs Manual Reconciliation and End-of-Day ProcessingBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain automated vs manual reconciliation and end-of-day processing. Automated reconciliation uses rules to match internal records against external statements at high speed and at high volume, and it is the only practical way to reconcile a large bank. Automation therefore removes the routine work and leaves the exceptions, which is exactly where skilled analysts add the most value. 3:34Completed ›
7 Worked Example and Exercise: a bank sends 10,000 payments, 9,850 settled, 100 rejected, 30 returned, 20 have no corresponding settlement recordBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain worked example and exercise: a bank sends 10,000 payments, 9,850 settled, 100 rejected, 30 returned, 20 have no corresponding settlement record. The starting point is the control total, because the engine must first prove that it is looking at the complete population before it matches anything. A return arrives as a pacs.004 message, often with reason code AC04 for a closed account or MD07 for an end customer who is deceased, and the engine matches it to the original payment by reference. 3:52Completed ›
8 Three-way reconciliationBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain three-way reconciliation. Reconciliation proves that two records of the same activity agree. For a business analyst, reconciliation design is a requirements exercise with three inputs. 3:49Completed ›
