Controls that keep payments safe

D12 Risk, compliance and regulation · 7 lessons

Version 1.0 | Last reviewed 8 November 2026

Learner explains the control framework that keeps payments safe, distinguishes fraud typologies, transaction monitoring, SAR and STR filing, KYC and CDD, access controls, audit trails and scheme rulebooks, and applies each control to realistic payment operations with correct terminology.

What You Can Do After This Chapter

Learning objective. Learner explains the control framework that keeps payments safe, distinguishes fraud typologies, transaction monitoring, SAR and STR filing, KYC and CDD, access controls, audit trails and scheme rulebooks, and applies each control to realistic payment operations with correct terminology.

Practical assessment. Learner receives ten short scenarios, including a 25,000 pound APP scam, a suspicious transaction that needs a SAR, a new corporate customer onboarding and an audit request for a payment trail, and identifies the control that applies and what must happen. Learner then writes a one-page note on how transaction monitoring thresholds are tuned. Learner submits all work with sources, calculations, and assumptions stated clearly, and reviews the result against the chapter checklist before submission.

Expected deliverables.

  • Fraud typology map with detection signals for APP scams, mule accounts and business email compromise
  • SAR and STR filing checklist
  • Access control and segregation of duties matrix for a payments operation

Completion criterion. Learner correctly matches at least nine of ten control scenarios to the right control and explains how the control works in practice, using correct terms and a concrete example. Completion is judged on accuracy, completeness, and clear written explanation throughout.

In this chapter
  1. Fraud typologies in payments: APP scams, mule accounts and business email compromise
  2. Transaction monitoring systems: rules, thresholds, alert triage and tuning
  3. SAR and STR filing: what gets reported, when and to whom
  4. KYC and CDD: customer due diligence in payment onboarding
  5. Access controls and segregation of duties inside a payments operation
  6. Audit trails and record keeping: what must be provable after the fact
  7. Scheme rulebooks as control: how Visa, Mastercard and SEPA rules bind a bank
