Learning objective. Learner compares how ten large banks organise payments, including hub models, vendor choices, and operating models, and extracts lessons a smaller bank can apply. Learner applies this skill in realistic banking scenarios, with clear examples and correct terminology throughout.

Practical assessment. Learner researches ten large banks from public sources and completes a comparison table covering hub model, core payment systems, and operating model for each. Learner then writes a two-page lessons note, naming five practices a smaller bank can adopt, with the benefit and the implementation effort for each practice stated. Learner submits all work with sources, calculations, and assumptions stated clearly, and reviews the result against the chapter checklist before submission.

Expected deliverables.

  • Ten-bank comparison table
  • Two-page lessons note with five practices
  • Operating model summary per bank
  • Adoption roadmap for a smaller bank

Completion criterion. Learner completes the table for all ten banks accurately, names five transferable practices, and states benefit and effort for each practice. Completion is judged on accuracy, completeness, and clear written explanation throughout.

1 JPMorgan Chase and CitibankBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain jpmorgan chase and citibank. JPMorgan Chase runs one of the largest payment operations on the planet, moving more than 10 trillion dollars a day across its wholesale payment systems. A business analyst working at either bank spends much of the time mapping fields between the old and new formats and testing that no data is lost in translation. 3:42Completed ›
2 HSBC and Standard CharteredBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain hsbc and standard chartered. HSBC runs one of the largest trade and payments networks in the world, with a presence in more than 60 countries and a particular strength in Asia. A business analyst joining either bank will quickly learn that sanctions compliance is not a separate function; it is built into every step of the payment flow. 3:23Completed ›
3 Bank of America and Wells FargoBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain bank of america and wells fargo. Bank of America runs its payments through a central hub that connects to every US clearing system, Fedwire for high value same-day payments, the Clearing House for ACH bulk payments, and the RTP network for real-time. For a business analyst, the lesson from both banks is that domestic scale and global reach need different architectures. 3:46Completed ›
4 Deutsche Bank and BNP ParibasBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain deutsche bank and bnp paribas. Deutsche Bank runs its payment operations from Frankfurt, with a payment hub that connects directly to TARGET2, the real-time gross settlement system of the Eurosystem, and to STEP2, the pan-European clearing system for. The lesson for a business analyst is that consolidation is a long project, often taking five years or more, and it succeeds only when the business, operations, and technology teams agree on a single target design before the migration starts. 5:06Completed ›
5 Barclays and NatWestBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain barclays and natwest. Barclays runs one of the largest payment hubs in the United Kingdom, processing more than 1 billion payments a year for retail customers, corporate clients, and correspondent banks. For a business analyst, BACS is a good example of a system where the business rules matter more than the technology, the direct debit guarantee, the indemnity claim process, and the rules for failed collections are all defined by the scheme, not by the bank. 3:46Completed ›
6 UBS and Goldman SachsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain ubs and goldman sachs. UBS payment operations are built around its private banking and wealth management business. The lesson for a business analyst is that the right technology choice depends on the client base, a wealth manager needs security and service, an investment bank needs speed and integration, and a retail bank needs volume and cost. 3:53Completed ›
