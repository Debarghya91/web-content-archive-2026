Learning objective. Learner builds minute-by-minute timelines for an instant, a batch and a cross-border payment, and compares four rails carrying the same EUR 10,000 payment from Paris to Berlin. Learner explains the seven lifecycle stages and states the failure, the message and the recovery at each stage.

Practical assessment. Learner receives the EUR 4,500 SCT Inst payment, the EUR 1.2 million salary file of 500 payments, and the USD 85,000 cross-border payment, then draws the timeline for each, names the messages and statuses at each step, and explains what happens when each one fails at a different stage.

Expected deliverables.

  • Three timelines: instant, batch and cross-border
  • Rail comparison table for EUR 10,000
  • Failure matrix by lifecycle stage
  • Note on where the time goes in a cross-border payment

Completion criterion. Learner draws correct timelines for all three payments, names the messages and statuses at each step, and explains the failure and recovery at each lifecycle stage.

Takeaway: a batch salary file of 500 payments totalling EUR 1.2 million moves from pain.001 upload at 08:00 to beneficiary credit at 15:00, through validation, screening, debit, pacs.008 creation, STEP2 clearing and T2 settlement, with 3 IBAN rejections and 2 held payments reported separately. 9:43Completed ›

Takeaway: a USD 85,000 cross-border payment from London to Singapore moves from pain.001 at 09:00 to supplier credit at 12:00 London time, through Bank A, intermediary Bank C and Bank B, by the serial method, with pacs.008 at each step, pacs.002 with status ACCC, and charge arrangement OUR so the supplier receives the full amount. 7:45Completed ›

5 Failure at each lifecycle stageBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain failure at each lifecycle stage. Every payment passes through seven lifecycle stages, and each stage has its own failures, its own messages and its own recovery, and a business analyst must know all seven, because a failure at initiation needs a. Takeaway: initiation fails on bad data and is rejected by pain.002, validation and screening fail on data, parties or behaviour and are held or rejected by pacs.002, authorisation fails on insufficient funds with reason AM04, clearing fails on a bad message or a wrong route, settlement fails on funding or an outage under the system default procedure, and beneficiary credit fails on a closed account and is returned by pacs.004 with reason AC04. 8:12Completed ›
6 Forward-dated payment rules in depthBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain forward-dated payment rules. A forward-dated payment, also called a future-dated payment, is an instruction with a value date in the future: pay this amount, to this beneficiary, on that date. For a business analyst, forward-dating requirements cover the acceptance window, the business day calendars, the revalidation scope, the amendment and cancellation windows, and the warehousing reconciliation. 3:41Completed ›
7 Payment warehousing: holding future-dated paymentsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain payment warehousing: holding future-dated payments. Payment warehousing is the holding of future-dated payments until their release date. For a business analyst, warehouse requirements cover storage, release rules, revalidation scope, reconciliation, and operator visibility. 4:01Completed ›
