MT101 in depth: how corporations instruct banks to pay, field by field.

By the end of this chapter you will be able to

Learning objective. Learner explains MT101 use cases, multiple debit accounts, subsidiary payments, funds repatriation and the difference between Sequence A and Sequence B. Learner uses real examples with amounts, banks and message names to show how the topic works in practice.

Practical assessment. Learner receives a case where a UK parent must pay 20 suppliers from three subsidiary accounts in euros, pounds and dollars, then drafts an MT101 with Sequence A and Sequence B, maps each field to a pain.001 equivalent, and explains how the bank executes and reports the payments. Learner submits all work with sources, calculations and assumptions stated clearly, and reviews the result against the chapter checklist before submission.

Expected deliverables.

  • MT101 draft for a multi-account payment run
  • Field mapping table: MT101 to pain.001
  • Diagram of parent, subsidiaries and paying bank
  • Error handling note for rejected instructions

Completion criterion. Learner produces a correct MT101 draft with both sequences mapped to pain.001, and explains execution, reporting and error handling clearly. Completion is judged on accuracy, completeness and clear written explanation throughout.

3 Field 50a Ordering Customer in Sequence A vs Sequence BBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain field 50a ordering customer in sequence a vs sequence b. Field 50a in Sequence A names the ordering customer for the whole MT101 message. Option F carries structured party identification, with name, address, and account details in a coded format, and is used when the bank needs a precise identification for regulatory reporting. 3:19Completed ›
4 Two Domestic Transactions in One MT101By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain two domestic transactions in one mt101. A corporation may need to make two domestic payments on the same day, for example a salary payment and a supplier payment, or two supplier payments to different cities. This matters because the message is the payment instruction. A wrong field, a missing element or a misunderstood format is a failed payment, a return, or a compliance breach. 3:21Completed ›
5 Paying from Multiple Debit AccountsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain paying from multiple debit accounts. A corporation may hold an operating account, a tax account, and a payroll account at the same bank, and it may need to pay from more than one of them in a single run. This matters because the message is the payment instruction. A wrong field, a missing element or a misunderstood format is a failed payment, a return, or a compliance breach. 3:11Completed ›

Paying from a subsidiary account means the money leaves an account owned by the subsidiary, even though the parent treasury team sends the instruction. The bank must hold a mandate for either pattern before it will act. 3:13Completed ›
