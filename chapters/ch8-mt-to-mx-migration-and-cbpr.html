4 Truncation risk, the silent data lossBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain truncation risk, the silent data loss. Truncation risk is the risk that data is lost when a longer MX field is translated to a shorter MT field, and it is one of the most important risks in the MT to MX migration. An analyst who understands truncation risk can write requirements that minimise it, and can explain to stakeholders why a payment that looks correct in MX may fail after translation to MT. 4:24Completed ›
5 What banks must do, readiness and testingBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain banks must do, readiness and testing. Banks that migrate to ISO 20022 must undertake a programme of work that covers technology, operations, compliance, and customer readiness. On the compliance side, sanctions screening, anti money laundering, and fraud rules must be updated for the new data model, and the bank must be able to demonstrate to regulators that its controls work in both formats. 4:21Completed ›

The HVPS+ rulebook is the set of SWIFT ISO 20022 usage guidelines written for high value payment systems and market infrastructures. A bank must therefore validate inbound messages against CBPR+ at the border and against HVPS+ at the settlement system, and it must test the handover explicitly. 4:14Completed ›

11 Transaction Manager in depth: the central record of an ISO 20022 paymentBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain transaction manager in depth: the central record of an ISO 20022 payment. When a payment travels through several banks, each bank sees only its own leg, and no single bank holds the complete picture of the transaction from end to end. It is the data backbone of the CBPR+ migration, and the analyst who designs cross border flows must understand what it stores, who can access it, and what problems it solves. 4:48Completed ›
