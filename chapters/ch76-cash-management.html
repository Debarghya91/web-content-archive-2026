Cash management in banking: sweeping, pooling, zero balancing and cash forecasting for corporate customers.

What You Can Do After This Chapter

Learning objective. Learner explains the cash management services a bank offers a corporate treasurer, and distinguishes sweeping, pooling, zero balancing and notional pooling, including multi-currency and cross-border cases. Learner builds a cash forecast, and explains why cash management and bank liquidity management are two different jobs.

Practical assessment. Learner receives a case where a corporate group holds surplus cash in three subsidiaries in different currencies, then designs a sweeping and pooling structure, calculates the interest benefit, and builds a one-week cash forecast. Learner also explains the difference from the bank's own liquidity management.

Expected deliverables.

  • Sweeping and pooling structure diagram
  • Interest benefit calculation
  • One-week cash forecast
  • Note: cash management versus bank liquidity management

Completion criterion. Learner designs a correct sweeping and pooling structure, calculates the interest benefit, and builds a usable cash forecast.

In this chapter
  1. Cash management services: what a bank offers a corporate treasurer
  2. Sweeping: moving surplus cash to where it is needed
  3. Pooling: offsetting balances without moving money
  4. Zero balancing: sweeping every account to zero each day
  5. Notional pooling: offsetting without moving, across currencies and countries
  6. Cash forecasting: predicting the money before it moves
  7. Cash management versus bank liquidity management: two different jobs
