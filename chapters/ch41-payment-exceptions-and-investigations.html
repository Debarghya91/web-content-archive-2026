Practical assessment. Learner investigates four cases, including a 75,000 euro payment not received after three days, a 2,000 dollar duplicate, and a payment credited with the wrong amount, using a structured log. For each case Learner traces the payment, identifies the root cause, states the message sent to resolve it, and writes the customer update with a revised date. Expected deliverables.

Completion criterion. Learner finds the root cause for at least three of four cases, names the right resolution message, and writes clear customer updates with revised dates. Completion is judged on accuracy, completeness, and clear reasoning throughout the submitted work.

1 The Exception Landscape: What Can Go WrongBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain exception landscape: what can go wrong. A payment exception is any payment that does not complete its intended journey cleanly, from the customer instruction to the beneficiary credit, and the scale of the problem is larger than most people expect. The exceptions range from a simple typing error in an account number to a clearing system outage that stops thousands of payments at once, and the operations team must triage them by value, by age, and by customer impact before it starts fixing them. 3:19Completed ›
2 Technical Rejection vs Business RejectionBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain technical rejection vs business rejection. A technical rejection happens when a payment message fails a structural or format check, so the receiving system cannot even begin to process the business content. The reporting of the two types is different, and analysts must know which message to expect for each. 3:17Completed ›
3 Payment Repair, Return, Recall and CancellationBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain payment repair, return, recall and cancellation. Repair means correcting a payment that has been stopped before settlement, so that it can proceed without the customer having to start again. A payment of USD 60,000 may be held because the beneficiary address is incomplete, or because the intermediary bank details are missing, and the operations team can repair it by adding the missing information from the customer instruction or from a standing arrangement. 3:21Completed ›
4 Investigations, Duplicate Payments, Timeouts and Pending PaymentsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain investigations, duplicate payments, timeouts and pending payments. 5 Unknown Payment Status, Failed Beneficiary Credit and Clearing System OutageBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain unknown payment status, failed beneficiary credit and clearing system outage. Unknown payment status means the bank has sent a payment but has received no acknowledgement, no rejection, no return, and no settlement confirmation, so it cannot state with confidence whether the money moved. The bank must not assume the payment failed, and it must not assume it succeeded, because either assumption can cause a duplicate or a missing payment. 4:23Completed ›
6 ISO 20022 Messages and Reason Codes for Exceptions (pacs.002, pacs.004, camt.056, camt.029, pain.002 with real reason codes)By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain ISO 20022 messages and reason codes for exceptions (pacs.002, pacs.004, camt.056, camt.029, pain.002 with real reason codes). The pacs.002 message, known as the payment status report, is the standard way for a bank to tell the sender whether a payment was accepted, rejected, or is still pending. Common codes include AC01 for an incorrect account number, AC04 for a closed account, AM04 for insufficient funds, AM05 for a duplicate payment, and MD07 for an end customer who is deceased. 4:03Completed ›
