Payment exceptions and investigations: when payments go wrong, find it, fix it, prove it.

What You Can Do After This Chapter

Learning objective. Learner investigates payment exceptions, including non-receipt, wrong amount, duplicate, and delayed payments, and runs a structured investigation from first report to resolution. Learner demonstrates this skill using realistic banking examples, explaining each step clearly and using correct terminology throughout.

Practical assessment. Learner investigates four cases, including a 75,000 euro payment not received after three days, a 2,000 dollar duplicate, and a payment credited with the wrong amount, using a structured log. For each case Learner traces the payment, identifies the root cause, states the message sent to resolve it, and writes the customer update with a revised date. Learner submits the work with all calculations, sources, and assumptions stated clearly, and checks the result against the chapter checklist before final submission.

Expected deliverables.

  • Investigation log for four cases
  • Payment trace per case with root cause
  • Resolution message guide per case
  • Customer update drafts with revised dates

Completion criterion. Learner finds the root cause for at least three of four cases, names the right resolution message, and writes clear customer updates with revised dates. Completion is judged on accuracy, completeness, and clear reasoning throughout the submitted work.

In this chapter
  1. The Exception Landscape: What Can Go Wrong
  2. Technical Rejection vs Business Rejection
  3. Payment Repair, Return, Recall and Cancellation
  4. Investigations, Duplicate Payments, Timeouts and Pending Payments
  5. Unknown Payment Status, Failed Beneficiary Credit and Clearing System Outage
  6. ISO 20022 Messages and Reason Codes for Exceptions (pacs.002, pacs.004, camt.056, camt.029, pain.002 with real reason codes)
  7. Exercise: Trace One Payment Through Every Possible Failure Scenario (one USD cross-border payment traced through reject, return, recall, duplicate, timeout and outage, with what happens at each step)
