Cross-border payment exceptions: when a payment is rejected, returned or recalled, who pays for it, and how it is fixed.

By the end of this chapter you will be able to

Learning objective. Learner handles cross-border exceptions, including missing information, wrong beneficiary details, value date errors, and duplicate payments, and writes clear investigation and repair actions. Learner applies this skill in realistic banking scenarios, with clear examples and correct terminology throughout.

Practical assessment. Learner receives four exception cases, including a 30,000 euro payment returned for a wrong IBAN, a duplicate 5,000 dollar payment, and a payment held for missing purpose details, and writes the investigation steps, the message to send, and the resolution for each. Learner then drafts a customer reply for one case in plain language with a revised value date. Learner submits all work with sources, calculations, and assumptions stated clearly, and reviews the result against the chapter checklist before submission.

Expected deliverables.

  • Exception case log for four cases
  • Investigation and repair steps per case
  • Customer reply draft for one case
  • Return and recall message guide

Completion criterion. Learner resolves at least three of four cases correctly, names the right message for each repair, and writes a clear customer reply with a revised date. Completion is judged on accuracy, completeness, and clear written explanation throughout.

2 Rejects: stopped before the money movesBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain rejects: stopped before the money moves. A reject happens when a payment is stopped before the funds have moved, usually because the receiving bank or an intermediary bank cannot process the instruction as it stands. The analyst must design the reject handling to be fast and clear. 2:29Completed ›
3 Returns: the money comes backBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain returns: the money comes back. A return happens when a payment has been sent and the funds have moved, but the receiving bank cannot credit the beneficiary and sends the money back. The analyst must design the return handling to match the return to the original payment by UETR, to calculate the amount actually returned, to identify any deduction, and to notify the customer with the amount and the reason. 2:17Completed ›
4 Recalls: asking for the money backBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain recalls: asking for the money back. A recall, also called a payment recall or a request for recall, happens when the sending bank asks the receiving bank to return a payment that has already been credited to the beneficiary, usually because the payment. The recall is not guaranteed to succeed, and the analyst must make that clear to the customer. 2:12Completed ›
5 Investigations and reason codesBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain investigations and reason codes. An investigation is the process of finding out what happened to a payment that has not arrived, has arrived for the wrong amount, or has arrived with the wrong information, and it is the most common exception task in. The investigation starts with the customer reporting the problem, and the operations team then traces the payment using the UETR, the gpi tracker, the SWIFT messages, and the correspondent account statements. 2:17Completed ›
6 Timelines, costs and who bears themBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain timelines, costs and who bears them. Every exception has a timeline, and the timeline is set by the rules of the payment system, the agreements between banks, and the law of the countries involved. The analyst must include the cost allocation in the requirements, must ensure that the charges are applied correctly, and must ensure that the customer is told upfront what an exception will cost. 2:21Completed ›
7 Handling exceptions well: a practical playbookBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain handling exceptions well: a practical playbook. The best exception handling starts before the exception happens, with validation at payment capture, with clear customer instructions, and with a payment flow that prevents the most common errors. The system should detect a reject, a return, a recall or an investigation automatically, should match it to the original payment by UETR, and should alert the operations team without waiting for the customer to complain. 2:36Completed ›
