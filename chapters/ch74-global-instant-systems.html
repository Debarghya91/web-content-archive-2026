Global instant systems

Practitioner 18 lessons 1 hr 33 min in total

Why take this chapter

Instant payment systems beyond Europe and the US: NPP, Pix, PayNow, PromptPay, SPEI, Buna, RTP. This chapter has 18 lessons, from NPP: Australia instant payments and PayID to Canada Real-Time Rail and the PCRN.

Learning objective. Learner explains how NPP, Pix, PayNow and FAST, PromptPay, SPEI, Buna, RTP and SEPA Request to Pay each work, including launch dates, operators, addressing, availability and limits. Learner compares the eight systems on speed, addressing, cost and coverage, and explains when each one is the right rail for a payment.

Practical assessment. Learner receives a case where a business must pay suppliers in Australia, Brazil, Singapore, Thailand, Mexico and the United States, then recommends the instant system for each corridor, explains the addressing, and states the operating hours and the failure handling. Learner also explains SEPA Request to Pay and when it replaces a direct debit.

Expected deliverables.

  • Comparison table: eight instant systems
  • Corridor recommendation note
  • Addressing guide: PayID, Pix key, CLABE, mobile
  • SEPA Request to Pay flow diagram

Completion criterion. Learner recommends the correct system for each corridor, explains addressing and availability accurately, and compares the systems on the right dimensions.

In this chapter

1 NPP: Australia instant payments and PayIDBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain npp: australia instant payments and payid. The New Payments Platform, NPP, is Australia instant payment system, launched in February 2018 and operated by NPP Australia Limited, and it lets a payment move between accounts at different banks in seconds, 24 hours a. Takeaway: NPP settles each payment individually in seconds, and PayID plus the name check is what makes it safe to use. 6:40Completed ›
2 Pix: Brazil instant payments by keyBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain pix: brazil instant payments by key. Pix is Brazil's instant payment system, launched by the Banco Central do Brasil in November 2020, and it lets any person or business send money instantly, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, using a key instead of an account. Takeaway: Pix is instant, free for individuals and open to every licensed institution, and the name shown before confirmation is the control against mistaken payments. 6:37Completed ›
3 PayNow and FAST: Singapore instant paymentsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain paynow and fast: singapore instant payments. PayNow and FAST are Singapore instant payment systems, and they work together: FAST, Fast and Secure Transfers, launched in 2014, is the real-time interbank transfer system, and PayNow, launched in 2017, is the service. Takeaway: a PayNow payment travels on FAST, and the UEN lets a business be paid without sharing its account number. 6:05Completed ›
4 PromptPay: Thailand instant payments by ID or mobileBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain promptpay: thailand instant payments by id or mobile. PromptPay is Thailand instant payment system, launched in 2017 and overseen by the Bank of Thailand, and it lets a person or business send money instantly using a national ID number, a mobile phone number, or a bank. Takeaway: PromptPay lets anyone pay instantly by mobile number or ID, and a merchant QR code replaces cash and a terminal. 5:59Completed ›
5 SPEI: Mexico real-time payments by CLABEBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain spei: mexico real-time payments by clabe. SPEI, Sistema de Pagos Electrónicos Interbancarios, is Mexico real-time payment system, operated by the Banco de México since 2004, and it lets a payment move between accounts at different banks in seconds, 24 hours a. Takeaway: SPEI settles each payment in seconds in central bank money, and the CLABE check digit catches typing errors before the payment is sent. 6:32Completed ›
6 Buna: cross-border instant payments for the Arab regionBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain buna: cross-border instant payments for the arab region. Buna is the cross-border payment system for the Arab region, operated by the Arab Monetary Fund and launched in December 2020, and it lets a bank in one Arab country pay a bank in another Arab country in real time, in. Takeaway: Buna lets banks in the region settle directly with each other instead of through correspondents outside the region. 6:00Completed ›
7 RTP: the US real-time payment networkBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain rtp: the US real-time payment network. RTP, the Real-Time Payments network, is the United States instant payment system operated by The Clearing House, launched in November 2017, and it lets a payment move between accounts at different US banks in seconds,. Takeaway: RTP is final in seconds because it is prefunded, and a payment above USD 1 million must travel by another rail such as Fedwire. 6:21Completed ›
8 SEPA Request to Pay: asking for a payment instead of pulling itBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain SEPA request to pay: asking for a payment instead of pulling it. SEPA Request to Pay, SRTP, is the European Payments Council scheme that lets a payee ask a payer for a payment, instead of pulling the money by direct debit or waiting for the payer to remember to send it. Takeaway: SRTP gives the payee certainty and the payer control, because the payment is a credit transfer that cannot be refunded like a direct debit. 6:22Completed ›
9 PayTo: payer-controlled mandates on the NPPBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain payto: payer-controlled mandates on the npp. PayTo is the mandate service that runs on the NPP, and it lets a payee collect payments only under a mandate the payer authorised in their own bank app. This matters because it is a building block for the payments work in the rest of this course. 4:06Completed ›
10 NPP overlay services: Osko, BPAY and PayToBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain npp overlay services: osko, bpay and payto. An overlay service is a customer-facing service built on top of the NPP rails, and it uses the addressing, messaging and settlement of the platform instead of building new rails. This matters because it is a building block for the payments work in the rest of this course. 3:58Completed ›
11 NPP credit transfers and returns: inward, outward, solicited and unsolicitedBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain npp credit transfers and returns: inward, outward, solicited and unsolicited. A customer credit transfer, CCT, moves money from a payer account to a payee account, and every NPP payment a customer sends or receives is a CCT. Every flow must end in a clear status, a reconciled ledger and a customer who knows what happened. 4:27Completed ›
12 NPP rejections and recalls: clearing, settlement and camt.056By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain npp rejections and recalls: clearing, settlement and camt.056. The four rejection and cancellation flows are the paths an NPP payment takes when it does not complete normally, and each one has a different cause, timing and customer message. The bank must restore its settlement funding and decide on a retry, and the customer message must name the delay and the next step. 4:13Completed ›
13 How the NPP settles: the Fast Settlement Service and BECSBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain npp settles: the fast settlement service and becs. The Fast Settlement Service, FSS, is the settlement engine behind every NPP payment, and it is operated by the Reserve Bank of Australia. The liquidity duty is different on each system, and the bank must staff and fund for both. 4:12Completed ›
14 Proxy and alias initiation in depth: paying to a phone number, an email address or a proxyBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain proxy and alias initiation in depth: paying to a phone number, an email address or a proxy. An account number is hard to remember and easy to mistype, so instant payment systems let people pay to something easier: a phone number, an email address, a national identifier, or another alias that the system. Different systems implement proxies differently, and the analyst must know the local model. 4:29Completed ›
15 Cross-border instant linking: Project Nexus, the G20 targets and bilateral linksBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain cross-border instant linking: project nexus, the g20 targets and bilateral links. Domestic instant payment systems settle in seconds inside one country, but a payment between two countries still travels through correspondent banking, with its delays, costs and opacity. Both models are live, both are growing, and the analyst must understand both, because the corridor the business needs will use one or the other. 4:34Completed ›
16 RTP funding in depth: positions, watermarks, supplemental funding and the token serviceBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain rtp funding in depth: positions, watermarks, supplemental funding and the token service. Instant payments settle in real time, which means the funding must be in place before the payment, not after. This matters because it is a building block for the payments work in the rest of this course. 3:46Completed ›
17 IP+ and RTPG: the groups that harmonise instant paymentsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain ip+ and rtpg: the groups that harmonise instant payments. Every country that built an instant payment system built its own: its own data, its own rules, its own message usage. For a business analyst, the working rule is to start from the guidelines. 4:23Completed ›
18 Canada Real-Time Rail and the PCRNBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain canada real-time rail and the pcrn. Canada launched its Real-Time Rail, the RTR, on 9 November 2026, joining the group of countries with a national real time payment system. For a business analyst, the RTR is a case study in building a real time system with the lessons of the last decade included from day one. 4:11Completed ›
