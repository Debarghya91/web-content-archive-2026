Payments in 2025 and beyond: what comes next, and what it means for banks, BAs and architects.

By the end of this chapter you will be able to

Learning objective. Learner explains one leg out, CBPR+ after migration, open banking and cryptocurrencies, and applies them in a 2026-ready payment product design. Learner uses real examples with amounts, banks and message names to show how the topic works in practice.

Practical assessment. Learner designs a 2026-ready payment product for a business paying suppliers in three currencies, combining ISO 20022 messaging, an open banking payment initiation flow and a stablecoin settlement option, then presents the flow, the controls and the open risks in a short product pack. Learner submits all work with sources, calculations and assumptions stated clearly, and reviews the result against the chapter checklist before submission.

Expected deliverables.

  • One-page 2026-ready product summary
  • End-to-end product flow diagram
  • Control and failure-path table
  • Open risks register with owners

Completion criterion. Learner presents a coherent product pack that combines all three elements, with clear controls, failure paths and at least three named open risks. Completion is judged on accuracy, completeness and clear written explanation throughout.

In this chapter

1 ISO 20022 and One Leg Out: What Changes NextBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain ISO 20022 and one leg out: what changes next. One leg out describes a payment where one part of the journey uses ISO 20022 and the other part does not. A bank must therefore decide, field by field, what is preserved, what is mapped and what is lost, and it must record that decision in a mapping table that operations and compliance teams both understand and test. 7:20Completed ›
2 CBPR+ After Migration: What Comes Next for Cross-Border PaymentsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain CBPR+ after migration: what comes next for cross-border payments. CBPR+ moved cross border payments onto ISO 20022, but migration alone did not make payments faster or cheaper. A business analyst should ask, for every new requirement, whether it uses ISO 20022 data to remove a manual step or only adds another field to an old process. 6:43Completed ›
3 Open Banking Reshaping PaymentsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain open banking reshaping payments. Open banking lets a regulated third party start a payment directly from a customer bank account, with the customer consent, instead of using a card network. For a business analyst, this means the payment flow has extra steps that a normal transfer does not have, including consent capture, consent storage and consent expiry. 2:45Completed ›
4 Cryptocurrencies Reshaping PaymentsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain cryptocurrencies reshaping payments. A cryptocurrency is a digital asset that moves on a distributed ledger, without a central bank or a traditional clearing system in the middle. That speed is valuable, but it also means a mistaken payment cannot be recalled through a banking operations team in the same way. 6:54Completed ›
5 Open Banking Meets Crypto: Two Forces TogetherBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain open banking meets crypto: two forces together. Open banking and crypto are usually discussed separately, but together they describe a payment that starts in a regulated bank account and settles on a digital ledger. A business analyst working on this design should map every handoff, name the party responsible for each one, and test the failure cases, including a failed conversion, a lost peg and a wallet that cannot be reached. 2:46Completed ›
6 What This Means for Banks, BAs and ArchitectsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain this means for banks, bas and architects. Banks must rethink their operating model, not just their technology. The change needs product owners who understand the whole payment journey, operations teams who can work across rails, and compliance teams involved from the start. 3:40Completed ›
7 Exercise: Design a 2026-Ready Payment ProductBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain exercise: design a 2026-ready payment product. Your brief is to design a payment product for a small business that imports goods from three countries and pays suppliers in euros, dollars and pounds. This matters because the next payment product you work on may use one of these technologies. Knowing what is real and what is still a proposal is what separates a good analyst from a confused one. 2:52Completed ›
