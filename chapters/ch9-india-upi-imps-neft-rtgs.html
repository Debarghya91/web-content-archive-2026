Practical assessment. Learner receives six Indian payment needs, including a 200,000 rupee same-day supplier payment, a 500 rupee instant peer transfer, and a 5,000,000 rupee high-value settlement, and recommends UPI, IMPS, NEFT, or RTGS for each with reasons. Learner then traces a failed UPI transaction, identifying the status, the reversal rule, and the customer communication required. Learner presents the work with calculations and assumptions stated clearly, then reviews it against the chapter checklist and corrects any gaps before submission.

Expected deliverables.

  • UPI, IMPS, NEFT, RTGS comparison table
  • Rail recommendation sheet for six needs
  • Failed UPI transaction trace and reversal note
  • Cut-off and limit reference sheet

Completion criterion. Learner recommends the correct rail for at least five of six needs, states limits and timing accurately, and explains the failed UPI reversal rule correctly. Judgement covers accuracy, completeness, and clear written explanation in the submitted work.

IMPS was India's first truly instant account to account transfer system. 4 NEFT: batch settlement and why it still mattersBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain NEFT: batch settlement and why it still matters. NEFT is the Reserve Bank of India batch based system for transferring money between bank accounts across India. The analyst must never let the screen promise instant credit for an NEFT payment. 4:28Completed ›
5 RTGS: high value settlement with finalityBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain RTGS: high value settlement with finality. RTGS is the Reserve Bank of India system for high value transfers where each payment is settled individually and immediately in the books of the central bank. To settle a payment, the sending bank must have enough funds in its settlement account at the exact moment of settlement. 6:35Completed ›
6 NACH: bulk debits, credits and recurring paymentsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain NACH: bulk debits, credits and recurring payments. NACH is the NPCI system for bulk and recurring payments, and it is the quiet workhorse behind salaries, EMIs, insurance premiums, utility bills, and mutual fund SIPs. Returns arrive in files that the presenting bank must reconcile record by record. 4:43Completed ›
7 How the rails differ: limits, timing and costBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain rails differ: limits, timing and cost. The fastest way to compare the Indian rails is to put them side by side on four questions. Timing is where analysts most often make mistakes, because the words instant and same day mean different things on different rails. 6:10Completed ›
8 Choosing the right rail: a worked exampleBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain choosing the right rail: a worked example. Consider a mid sized company in Bengaluru that needs to make four payments on the same morning, ten thousand rupees to a vendor who supplied office stationery, one lakh fifty thousand rupees to a consultant, twenty five. A good analyst would not send all four through the same rail. 2:31Completed ›

Indian payments rest on three distinct parts that a beginner must keep separate, namely the regulator, the operator, and the addressing code. The analyst must state, for each requirement, whether the rule comes from the regulator or the rail is operated by the Reserve Bank, because the RBI acts as both rule setter and operator. 4:06Completed ›
