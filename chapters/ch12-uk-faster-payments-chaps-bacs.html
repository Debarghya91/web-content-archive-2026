In this chapter

1 The UK payments landscape at a glanceBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain UK payments landscape at a glance. The United Kingdom runs its domestic sterling payments on three main systems that have grown up over different decades and now sit side by side, each serving a distinct purpose. Together they move almost every pound that changes hands inside the UK, and a business analyst who works on UK payments must know all three, because a customer requirement that sounds simple, such as pay this supplier today, can mean three different things depending on the value, the urgency and the cost the customer will accept. 2:17Completed ›
2 Faster Payments: near real time for everyday paymentsBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain faster payments: near real time for everyday payments. Faster Payments moves sterling between UK bank accounts in seconds. Netting keeps the liquidity each bank needs far lower than gross settlement would. 5:05Completed ›
3 CHAPS: high value payments with same day finalityBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain CHAPS: high value payments with same day finality. CHAPS is the United Kingdom's real time gross settlement system for high value sterling payments. A payment submitted after the cutoff is processed on the next business day, so conveyancers, corporate treasurers and operations teams plan around it. 4:38Completed ›
4 BACS: the three day cycle that pays the nationBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain BACS: the three day cycle that pays the nation. BACS is the batch system that has moved most UK regular payments since the late 1960s. Bank holidays extend the timetable, and the analyst must design for the calendar, not just the cycle. 10:30Completed ›
5 How the three systems differBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain three systems differ. The fastest way to compare the UK systems is to put them side by side on four questions. Timing is where analysts most often make mistakes in the UK, because the words fast and same day mean different things on different systems. 2:08Completed ›
6 Confirmation of Payee and the fight against fraudBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain confirmation of payee and the fight against fraud. Confirmation of Payee, known as CoP, is a service that checks whether the name entered by the payer matches the name on the account that the sort code and account number identify, and it is now a standard part of the UK. The reimbursement rules for authorised push payment fraud are a second layer of protection that the analyst must understand. 2:02Completed ›
7 Choosing the right system: a worked exampleBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain choosing the right system: a worked example. Consider a small business in Manchester that needs to make four payments on the same morning, fifty pounds to a local supplier for stationery, two thousand pounds to a consultant, one hundred and fifty thousand pounds. A good analyst would not send all four through the same system. 2:20Completed ›
8 Sort codes and account numbers: how UK payments find the right accountBy the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain sort codes and account numbers: how UK payments find the right account. Every domestic payment in the United Kingdom needs an address, and that address has two parts: a six digit sort code that identifies the bank branch, and an eight digit account number that identifies the account at that. The analyst who understands this structure can read a UK IBAN at a glance, spot a missing or extra digit, and explain to a customer why an international payment needs the IBAN while a domestic payment needs only the sort code and account number. 4:34Completed ›
