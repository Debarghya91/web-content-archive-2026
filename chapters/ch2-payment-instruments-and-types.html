A practical guide to payment instruments, from cash and cheques to cards and instant transfers, and when each type of payment is the right way to move money.

What You Can Do After This Chapter

Learning objective. Learner compares cash, cheques, cards, direct debits, credit transfers, and digital wallets, and selects the right instrument for a given business need based on speed, cost, risk, and reversibility. Learner demonstrates this skill using realistic banking examples, explaining each step clearly and using correct terminology throughout.

Practical assessment. Learner receives six business needs, including a recurring 99 dollar subscription, a same-day 75,000 pound supplier payment, and a low-value retail purchase, and recommends one instrument for each with a written justification. Learner builds a comparison table covering speed, cost, reversibility, and failure modes, then defends two choices in a short written rationale. Learner submits the work with all calculations, sources, and assumptions stated clearly, and checks the result against the chapter checklist before final submission.

Expected deliverables.

  • Instrument comparison table across six instruments
  • Recommendation sheet for six business needs with justifications
  • Reversibility and risk matrix
  • Two written choice rationales

Completion criterion. Learner recommends a suitable instrument for at least five of six needs, justifies each choice with speed, cost, and risk, and states reversibility correctly. Completion is judged on accuracy, completeness, and clear reasoning throughout the submitted work.

In this chapter
  1. Credit transfers, the default business payment
  2. Direct debits and pull collections
  3. Cards, how a card payment really works
  4. Cheques, still relevant in some markets
  5. E-money, wallets and newer instruments
  6. Real time versus batch payments
  7. Domestic versus cross border, retail versus wholesale
