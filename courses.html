Guided learning tracks

This is the default route for new learners. Follow the sessions in order, from payment fundamentals to the hands-on BA workshop and the capstone. For every path with prerequisites, hours and outcomes, see the Course map.

Your 8-session guided path

Follow the sessions in order. Each session maps to the chapters below. Sessions 7 and 8 are hands-on: you write requirements, user stories, mappings, and test cases, the way a payments BA does on a real project.

  1. Session 01: Payment Fundamentals and Ecosystem · Global payments landscape, ecosystem and key stakeholders, domestic, cross-border, SEPA, instant and card payments, overview of payment systems and business scenarios.
  2. Session 02: Payment Lifecycle and Processing · End-to-end payment lifecycle, initiation, clearing and settlement, domestic and cross-border process flows, key payment events and exception scenarios.
  3. Session 03: ISO 20022 and SWIFT Messaging · Introduction to SWIFT and ISO 20022, message structure and important fields, key messages pacs.008, pacs.009, pacs.002, pacs.004, business purpose and interpretation.
  4. Session 04: CBPR+ and Payment Message Mapping · CBPR+ framework and ISO 20022 migration, SWIFT MT103 to ISO 20022 pacs.008 mapping, business events that trigger payment messages, message fields, validations and real-world scenarios.
  5. Session 05: SEPA Schemes and Instant Payments · SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Direct Debit, SEPA Instant Payments and UPI overview, scheme rules, processing timelines and cut-off times, business requirements, compliance and exception handling.
  6. Session 06: Payment Controls, Compliance and Operations · KYC, AML and transaction monitoring from a BA perspective, sanctions screening, PEPs and watchlists, fraud detection concepts and risk-based rules, payment exceptions, investigations and repair, accounting, reconciliation and operational controls.
  7. Session 07: Business Analyst Practical Workshop · Requirements gathering and business process modelling, As-Is and To-Be payment process flows, business rules and processing conditions, Jira user stories and acceptance criteria, introduction to API analysis and interface specifications.
  8. Session 08: End-to-End BA Simulation and Capstone Project · SWIFT MT103 to ISO 20022 pacs.008 message mapping, API business data, rules and validations, user stories and acceptance criteria, UAT scenarios and end-to-end test cases, hands-on BA deliverables and capstone exercises.

Fourteen learning stages

The curriculum is organised in fifteen domains and fourteen stages. A stage is a learning goal: follow the stages in order, and each one builds on the last. Stages 1 to 13 teach the subject; stage 14 is where you prove it on real projects.

  1. Stage 01: Foundations · What a payment is, the instruments, the ecosystem, and the lifecycle. Start here if payments are new to you.
  2. Stage 02: Payment Systems and Instruments · How payment systems are built, the rails in India, the US, the UK and Europe, and how cheques and paper instruments still work.
  3. Stage 03: Banking Architecture · How banks actually build payment systems: channels, hubs, gateways, platforms, data models and enterprise design.
  4. Stage 04: Messaging and ISO 20022 · SWIFT MT, ISO 20022, CBPR+, message catalogues, technical implementation, and MT to MX mapping.
  5. Stage 05: Domestic Payment Processing · UPI, IMPS, NEFT, RTGS, NACH, Fedwire, FedNow, ACH, CHIPS, SEPA, TARGET2, TIPS, Faster Payments, CHAPS and BACS.
  6. Stage 06: Cross-Border and Correspondent Banking · Correspondent banking, serial vs cover, charges, FX, gpi, exceptions, standby letters of credit, collections and trade finance.
  7. Stage 07: Clearing Settlement and Liquidity · Netting, settlement models, liquidity management and settlement risk.
  8. Stage 08: Cards and Alternative Payment Methods · Card authorisation, clearing and settlement, 3D Secure, tokenisation, chargebacks, networks, wallets, POS and recurring payments.
  9. Stage 09: Compliance and Risk · Sanctions screening, AML, KYC, fraud prevention, payment security, open banking and PSD3.
  10. Stage 10: Technology APIs and Integrations · API banking, open banking, integration patterns and banking APIs.
  11. Stage 11: Operational Processing and Reconciliation · Reconciliation, exceptions and investigations, testing, operations, reject and return in CBPR+, and the operational architecture: payment state machines, correlation, idempotency, retries, dead-letter queues, ledger posting versus settlement, and resilience.
  12. Stage 12: BA/PO Practice · The BA workshop, the capstone project, product management and how large banks do payments.
  13. Stage 13: Advanced Architecture and Emerging Payments · CBDCs, stablecoins, AI in payments, real-time architecture and what comes next.
  14. Stage 14: End-to-End Capstone Projects · Six capstone projects that pull everything together. Each one ends with the deliverables a real project produces.

Guided learning tracks

Some topics span several chapters. Follow a track in order and the pieces connect.

  • India Payments Track: One Comprehensive Track · India is covered as a single track, from everyday rails to the deep mechanics. Start with the rails, then go deep on mandates, bill payments and regulation.
  • Card Payments Track: One Complete Track · Card processing and card networks are one track: how a card payment is authorised, cleared and settled, how the networks move the messages, and how wallets and POS fit in.
  • Payment Hub vs Payment Gateway vs Payment Platform · A payment gateway sits at the front: it takes a payment from a channel, tokenises and screens it, and hands it on. A payment hub is the enterprise engine inside the bank: it validates, enriches, screens, routes, processes, clears, settles and reconciles every payment type across every rail. A payment platform is the whole enterprise design: hub plus gateways, channels, core banking, ledger, APIs and operations. Component-level design lives in the hub chapter; enterprise-level design lives in the platform chapter.
  • Liquidity Management and Nostro / Vostro · Liquidity management and nostro/vostro accounts are separate chapters that cross-reference each other: liquidity is about having the right funds at the right moment, and nostro accounts are where those funds sit for cross-border payments.
  • Cross-Border Payments and FEMA · Cross-border payment processing and FEMA compliance are linked but different: processing moves the money, FEMA decides whether the movement is permitted and what must be reported. Learn the processing first, then the regulation that governs it.
  • 4 Key Strategies to Understand Cross-Border Payments · A short guided path for anyone new to cross-border payments. Four strategies, in order.
