pacs.003: FI to FI Customer Direct Debit

pacs.003 is the ISO 20022 message a creditor agent sends to a debtor agent to collect a direct debit. It carries the mandate reference, creditor, debtor, amount and collection date for one or more debits. Banks use it for recurring collections such as utility bills, subscriptions and loan repayments. The debtor agent checks the mandate, debits the debtor account on the due date, and credits the creditor.

At a glance

  • Message: pacs.003
  • Title: FI to FI Customer Direct Debit
  • Current version: pacs.003.001.09
  • Versions: pacs.003.001.02, pacs.003.001.08, pacs.003.001.09
  • Fields: 16 (13 mandatory, 3 optional)

Participants

The sending participant is the creditor agent that instructs the collection. The receiving participant is the debtor agent that holds the debtor account, checks the mandate, and debits the account on the due date.

Structure

pacs.003 has a group header with message identification and control sum, then one or more payment information blocks. Each block holds creditor, debtor, mandate related information, instructed amount and remittance details. The document outline follows the standard ISO 20022 direct debit structure with repeating transaction blocks.

Scheme rules

pacs.003 is the base ISO 20022 direct debit message, and each scheme adds its own rules on top. SEPA Core gives the debtor an eight week refund right and SEPA B2B gives no refund right but requires a mandate check before debit. CBPR+ does not define a direct debit scheme, so pacs.003 is used mainly in domestic and SEPA collections.

Fields

FieldNameMandatoryDescription
GrpHdrGroup HeaderMThe header holding message identification, creation date and time, number of transactions and control sum. It identifies the message for tracking and duplicate detection. The message identification must be unique for the sender, and the control sum must equal the sum of the transaction amounts.
PmtInfPayment InformationMA block grouping direct debits that share the same creditor, creditor account, due date and charges bearer. One pacs.003 can hold many payment information blocks. Grouping simplifies validation and lets the debtor agent process all debits in the block under one collection instruction.
PmtInfIdPayment Information IdentificationMA unique identification for the payment information block, assigned by the creditor agent. It lets the creditor and the debtor agent refer to the whole block in status reports and queries. It must be unique so the debtor agent can detect a duplicate file or block.
PmtMtdPayment MethodMThe method used for the collection, normally DD for direct debit. It tells the debtor agent which processing channel and clearing system to use. The method must match the mandate type, since a direct debit sent with the wrong method can be rejected by the scheme.
ReqdColltnDtRequested Collection DateMThe date on which the debtor agent is asked to debit the debtor account, known as the collection date. The debtor agent checks it against cut off times and business days. It drives the debit timing, the creditor credit, and the refund window that starts on this date.
CdtrCreditorMThe creditor that will receive the collected funds, with name, postal address and identification. The debtor agent screens the creditor before debiting. Complete creditor data supports sanctions screening and lets the debtor identify the originator of the collection.
CdtrAcctCreditor AccountMThe account of the creditor to be credited with the collected funds, identified by IBAN. It tells the creditor agent exactly where to post the funds. A wrong account number causes the collection to fail reconciliation, so banks validate the IBAN check digits before processing.
CdtrAgtCreditor AgentMThe bank holding the creditor account and sending the collection instruction onward. It is identified by BIC. The creditor agent is responsible for collecting the mandate, checking the collection details, and sending the instruction to the debtor agent on the due date.
DbtrDebtorMThe debtor whose account will be debited, with name, postal address and identification. The debtor agent matches the debtor to its customer records and checks the mandate. Complete debtor data supports screening and lets the debtor recognise the collection on its statement.
DbtrAcctDebtor AccountMThe account of the debtor to be debited, identified by IBAN. The debtor agent checks that the account exists and has sufficient funds on the collection date. A wrong account number is a common cause of return, so the IBAN is validated before the debit is attempted.
DbtrAgtDebtor AgentMThe bank holding the debtor account, identified by BIC. It is the bank that debits the customer and checks the mandate before releasing the funds. The debtor agent screens the collection, applies the debit, and reports any return to the creditor agent.
MndtRltdInfMandate Related InformationMThe mandate that authorises the creditor to collect direct debits from the debtor account. It carries the mandate identification, date of signature, and the scheme reference. Without a valid mandate the debtor agent must return the collection, so the mandate data is checked first.
InstdAmtInstructed AmountMThe amount and currency of the individual direct debit, for example EUR 1,250.00. It is the amount debited from the debtor and credited to the creditor. The sum of the amounts in a payment information block must equal the control sum in the group header.
ChrgBrCharge BearerOWho pays the charges, using CRED for creditor, DEBT for debtor, SHAR for shared or SLEV for service level. The value drives fee deduction and must match the agreement between creditor and bank. It is carried into the settlement so each agent applies the agreed charge treatment.
RmtInfRemittance InformationOInformation explaining the collection, such as an invoice number, in structured or unstructured form. Structured remittance lets the debtor reconcile automatically. Customers should keep it concise and use the agreed format so the debtor system can match it without manual work.
PurpPurposeOA coded purpose for the collection, such as a utility bill or a subscription payment. Purpose codes support regulatory reporting and help the debtor agent apply the correct screening. The creditor selects the code that best describes the underlying commercial reason for the debit.

Valid example

Harbour Bank, as creditor agent, sends a pacs.003 to Meridian Bank for EUR 1,250.00 due 6 November 2026, collecting a monthly utility bill from a debtor account under mandate UTL884120. The mandate is valid, the debtor account is funded, Meridian debits the account on the due date, and Harbour credits the utility company.

Invalid example

A pacs.003 arrives with a mandate reference that the debtor bank cannot find and a collection date in the past. The debtor agent cannot confirm the creditor is authorised to collect, so it returns the debit unpaid and reports the failure to the creditor agent for correction.

Validation errors

  • Mandate reference not found causes a return because the debtor agent cannot confirm the creditor is authorised to collect from the account.
  • Collection date in the past causes rejection because the debit cannot be back valued without an explicit scheme exception.
  • Instructed amount exceeding the mandate limit causes a return because the collection breaches the maximum amount the debtor authorised.

Processing notes

The debtor agent validates the mandate, the debtor account and the collection date before debiting. Valid debits settle on the due date and the creditor is credited. Invalid or unauthorised debits are returned with a reason code, and the debtor may claim a refund within the scheme window after the debit.

Accounting notes

Each direct debit posts a debit to the debtor account on the due date and a credit to the creditor account, with the creditor agent holding the funds in a settlement account until clearing. Returned debits reverse both postings. Charges for the collection are posted separately to the creditor account under the scheme agreement.
