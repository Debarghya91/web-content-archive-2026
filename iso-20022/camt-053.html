camt.053: Bank to Customer Statement

camt.053 is the ISO 20022 message a bank sends to its customer as an account statement. It lists all entries posted to an account over a statement period, with opening and closing balances. Corporates use it for daily reconciliation, matching each bank entry to their own ledger. It carries booking date, value date, amount, currency, debtor, creditor and remittance data for every transaction.

At a glance

  • Message: camt.053
  • Title: Bank to Customer Statement
  • Current version: camt.053.001.08
  • Versions: camt.053.001.02, camt.053.001.06, camt.053.001.08
  • Fields: 12 (8 mandatory, 4 optional)

Participants

The sending participant is the bank holding the account (the account servicer). The receiving participant is the account owner, usually a corporate customer, which loads the statement into its reconciliation system.

Structure

camt.053 is an ISO 20022 XML message with a GroupHeader (GrpHdr) followed by one or more Statement blocks (Stmt). Each statement holds the account (Acct), opening and closing balances (Bal), and one Entry (Ntry) per posting, each with booking date, value date, amount, credit debit indicator, status and entry details (NtryDtls).

Scheme rules

camt.053 is a base ISO 20022 message: the structure and fields below are the base definition, and each scheme adds its own rules for its rail. Under CBPR+ the statement must carry the full structured remittance data for each entry so the customer can reconcile automatically. Banks agree the statement frequency and delivery channel bilaterally with each customer, and daily statements must balance: opening balance plus entries must equal the closing balance.

Fields

FieldNameMandatoryDescription
GrpHdrGroup HeaderMThe header with message identification, creation date and time and the account owner. It identifies the statement for tracking and duplicate detection. The customer system uses it to confirm it received the correct statement for the correct account and period before loading the entries.
StmtStatementMThe statement block holding the account, statement identification, creation date and time and the period covered. One camt.053 can hold several statements for different accounts. The statement identification must be unique so the customer can detect a missing or duplicate statement in sequence.
AcctAccountMThe account the statement covers, identified by IBAN or other account identification, with the currency and the account owner name. It tells the customer system which ledger account to reconcile. A statement must never be loaded against the wrong account, so this field is checked first.
BalBalanceMBalance information for the account, including opening balance, closing balance and sometimes interim balances. Each balance carries a type, amount, currency and date. The customer compares the closing balance with its own ledger to confirm that all entries were received and posted correctly.
NtryEntryOOne entry in the statement, representing a single debit or credit to the account. It carries booking date, value date, amount, currency, status and references. A statement can hold many entries. The customer matches each entry to an expected payment or receipt in its own system.
BookgDtBooking DateMThe date on which the entry was booked to the account in the bank system. It may differ from the value date when an entry is booked late or back valued. Reconciliation teams use the booking date to know when the entry appeared on the statement and to track late postings.
ValDtValue DateOThe date on which the funds become available for a credit or are taken for a debit. It drives interest calculation and cash flow forecasting. A value date in the future means the funds are not yet available, and the customer must not treat the entry as cleared cash.
AmtAmountMThe amount and currency of the entry, with a credit or debit indicator. For example a credit of USD 25,000 appears as a positive amount with a credit indicator. The customer sums the amounts to check the movement from opening to closing balance and to spot any missing entry.
CdtDbtIndCredit Debit IndicatorMA code showing whether the entry is a credit or a debit to the account. CRDT means money was added, DBIT means money was taken. It is essential for reconciliation, because the same amount can be a receipt or a payment depending on this indicator alone.
StsStatusMThe status of the entry, normally BOOK for a booked entry or PDNG for a pending entry. BOOK means the entry is final and included in the balance. PDNG means the entry is expected but not yet final, and the customer should not reconcile against it until it becomes booked.
NtryDtlsEntry DetailsODetailed information about the entry, including transaction details, debtor, creditor, remittance information and charges. It gives the customer the data needed to match the entry to an invoice or payment instruction. Rich entry details reduce manual reconciliation work sharply.
AddtlNtryInfAdditional Entry InformationOFree text additional information about the entry, such as the bank reference or a narrative description. It can carry the end to end identification or the remittance reference. The customer uses it as a fallback matching key when structured details do not match automatically.

Valid example

Meridian Bank London sends a camt.053.001.08 daily statement to Northgate Trading for account GB29NWBK60161331926819, period 5 November 2026: opening balance EUR 120,000, 48 entries including the EUR 25,000 supplier payment, closing balance EUR 95,000. Northgate matches all 48 entries to its ledger.

Invalid example

A camt.053 arrives with an opening balance of EUR 120,000, entries totalling EUR 25,000, and a closing balance of EUR 100,000. The statement does not balance and the customer system rejects the file for investigation (error: statement out of balance).

Validation errors

  • Statement out of balance: opening balance plus entries must equal the closing balance, or the statement is rejected.
  • Missing mandatory balance: a statement with no opening or closing balance cannot be reconciled and is rejected.
  • Duplicate statement identification: a statement whose message identification repeats an earlier statement is treated as a duplicate and ignored.

Processing notes

The customer system loads the statement, matches each entry to its ledger by amount, value date and remittance reference, and flags unmatched entries for investigation. A statement that fails to balance is held and queried with the bank rather than loaded.

Accounting notes

A camt.053 is a report, not a posting instruction: it reflects entries already posted to the account. The customer ledger is reconciled against it, and any entry on the statement that is missing from the ledger, or in the ledger but missing from the statement, is an unreconciled item to investigate.
