pain.008: Customer Direct Debit Initiation

pain.008 is the ISO 20022 message a creditor customer sends to its bank to instruct direct debit collections. It is the first message in the customer to bank direct debit chain. It carries payment information, creditor, debtor, mandate, amount and collection date for each debit. Banks receive it through host to host files or payment hubs, validate it, and then create the interbank pacs.003 to collect the funds.

At a glance

  • Message: pain.008
  • Title: Customer Direct Debit Initiation
  • Current version: pain.008.001.10
  • Versions: pain.008.001.02, pain.008.001.08, pain.008.001.09, pain.008.001.10
  • Fields: 15 (12 mandatory, 3 optional)

Participants

The sending participant is the creditor customer that instructs the collections, usually a utility or subscription business. The receiving participant is the creditor agent bank, which validates the file and creates the interbank collections.

Structure

pain.008 has a group header with message identification and control sum, then one or more payment information blocks. Each block holds creditor, debtor, mandate related information, instructed amount and remittance details. The document outline follows the standard ISO 20022 direct debit initiation structure with repeating transaction blocks.

Scheme rules

pain.008 is the base ISO 20022 customer direct debit initiation message, and each scheme adds its own rules on top. SEPA Core requires the creditor identifier and gives the debtor an eight week refund right, while SEPA B2B requires the debtor bank to check the mandate before debit. Domestic schemes may add local mandate formats and collection timing rules.

Fields

FieldNameMandatoryDescription
GrpHdrGroup HeaderMThe header with message identification, creation date and time, number of transactions, control sum and initiating party. It identifies the file for the bank and the customer. The bank uses it to acknowledge receipt, detect duplicate files and reconcile the total amount against the sum of the individual collections.
PmtInfPayment InformationMA block grouping collections that share the same creditor, creditor account, collection date and charges bearer. One pain.008 file can hold many payment information blocks. Grouping by common attributes simplifies validation and lets the bank process all collections in the block under one instruction.
PmtInfIdPayment Information IdentificationMA unique identification for the payment information block, assigned by the customer. It lets the customer and the bank refer to the whole block in status reports and queries. It must be unique for the initiating party so the bank can detect a duplicate file or block.
PmtMtdPayment MethodMThe method used for the collection, normally DD for direct debit. It tells the bank which processing channel and clearing system to use. The method must match the mandate type, since a direct debit sent with the wrong method can be rejected by the scheme.
ReqdColltnDtRequested Collection DateMThe date on which the customer asks the bank to collect the direct debit from the debtor account. The bank checks it against cut off times and business days. It drives the debit timing, the creditor credit, and the refund window that starts on this date.
CdtrCreditorMThe creditor that will receive the collected funds, with name, postal address and identification. The bank matches the creditor to its customer records and checks the mandate. Complete creditor data supports sanctions screening and lets the bank report the collection correctly.
CdtrAcctCreditor AccountMThe account of the creditor to be credited with the collected funds, identified by IBAN. The bank checks that the account exists and is authorised for direct debit collections. A wrong account number is a common cause of file rejection at validation stage.
CdtrAgtCreditor AgentMThe bank holding the creditor account, identified by BIC. It is the bank receiving the pain.008 file. The creditor agent validates the file, checks the mandates and creates the interbank pacs.003 messages needed to collect from each debtor agent.
DbtrDebtorMThe debtor whose account will be debited, with name, postal address and identification. The bank screens the debtor before collection. Complete debtor data reduces the chance of the collection being returned by the debtor agent for missing debtor information.
DbtrAcctDebtor AccountMThe account of the debtor to be debited, identified by IBAN. The bank validates the IBAN format and check digits before accepting the file. A valid IBAN does not guarantee the account exists, but it prevents many format errors that cause returns.
DbtrAgtDebtor AgentOThe bank holding the debtor account, identified by BIC when known. The customer may leave it blank and let the creditor agent derive it from the debtor IBAN. Accurate debtor agent data speeds routing and avoids the collection being sent to the wrong bank.
MndtRltdInfMandate Related InformationMThe mandate that authorises the creditor to collect direct debits from the debtor account. It carries the mandate identification, date of signature, and the scheme reference. Without a valid mandate the collection must not be sent, so the mandate data is checked before the file is accepted.
InstdAmtInstructed AmountMThe amount and currency of the individual direct debit, for example EUR 1,250.00. It is the amount debited from the debtor and credited to the creditor. The sum of the amounts in a payment information block must equal the control sum in the group header.
ChrgBrCharge BearerOWho pays the charges, using CRED for creditor, DEBT for debtor, SHAR for shared or SLEV for service level. The value drives fee deduction and must match the agreement between the customer and the bank. It is carried forward into the pacs.003 that the bank creates.
RmtInfRemittance InformationOInformation explaining the collection, such as an invoice number, in structured or unstructured form. Structured remittance lets the debtor reconcile automatically. Customers should keep it concise and use the agreed format so the debtor system can match it without manual work.

Valid example

A utility company sends a pain.008 to Harbour Bank with 400 collections for EUR 1,250.00 each, due 6 November 2026, under mandate references starting UTL884120. The control sum matches, every mandate is valid, Harbour accepts the file, and it creates pacs.003 messages to collect each debit from the debtor banks.

Invalid example

A pain.008 arrives with a control sum that does not match the sum of the instructed amounts and a mandate reference that is missing for two collections. The bank cannot accept the file as a whole, so it rejects the file and asks the creditor to correct the control sum and the mandate data.

Validation errors

  • Control sum not matching the sum of instructed amounts causes file rejection because the bank cannot confirm it received the complete file.
  • Missing mandate related information causes rejection because the bank cannot confirm the creditor is authorised to collect from the debtor.
  • Invalid debtor IBAN causes item rejection because the debtor account cannot be validated or debited by the debtor agent.

Processing notes

The bank validates the file structure, the control sum, each mandate and each debtor IBAN before accepting it. Valid collections are converted into pacs.003 messages and sent to the debtor agents. Rejected items are reported in a pain.002 status report so the creditor can correct and resend only those items.

Accounting notes

The pain.008 itself posts nothing, since it is only an instruction file. The bank posts the debit to the debtor account and the credit to the creditor account only when it creates the pacs.003 collection. Rejected instructions post nothing, so the ledger only reflects collections that passed validation and were executed.
