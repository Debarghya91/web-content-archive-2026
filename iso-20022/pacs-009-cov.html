pacs.009-cov: FI to FI Credit Transfer, Cover

pacs.009 COV is the ISO 20022 cover leg that moves settlement funds through the correspondent chain while the customer instruction travels directly to the beneficiary bank. It carries the full debtor and creditor details so every intermediary can screen the underlying payment. It links cover and customer legs by end to end identification. It is the CBPR+ replacement for the MT202 COV cover model.

At a glance

  • Message: pacs.009-cov
  • Title: FI to FI Credit Transfer, Cover
  • Current version: pacs.009.001.08
  • Versions: pacs.009.001.06, pacs.009.001.08, pacs.009.001.09, pacs.009.001.10
  • Fields: 15 (8 mandatory, 7 optional)

Participants

The sending participant is the debtor agent that moves the settlement funds. The receiving participants are the intermediary and creditor agents in the correspondent chain, which screen the full customer details and apply the funds to the customer instruction.

Structure

pacs.009 COV has a group header, payment identification, settlement amount and date, settlement information, full debtor and creditor blocks with accounts, debtor agent and creditor agent blocks, and optional intermediary and charges blocks. The document outline mirrors pacs.009 Core with the customer party details added for screening.

Scheme rules

pacs.009 COV follows the CBPR+ cover payment rules, which require full structured debtor and creditor data so intermediaries can screen the underlying customer payment. It is distinct from Core, which carries no customer beneficiary, and from ADV, which only advises. Domestic schemes do not normally use COV, since a direct pacs.008 suffices within one clearing system.

Fields

FieldNameMandatoryDescription
GrpHdrGroup HeaderMThe header holding message identification, creation date and time, number of transactions and control sum. It identifies the message for tracking and duplicate detection. The message identification must be unique for the sender, and the control sum must equal the sum of the transaction amounts.
PmtIdPayment IdentificationMThe identification block with instruction identification, end to end identification and transaction identification. End to end identification links this cover to the related customer payment sent directly to the beneficiary bank. It lets every bank match the cover and customer legs by the same reference.
IntrBkSttlmAmtInterbank Settlement AmountMThe amount and currency settled between the debtor agent and the creditor agent, for example USD 367,574.90. Settlement systems post the nostro debit and credit from this amount. It must fund the customer payment after any agreed deductions, or the cover will fail reconciliation at the beneficiary bank.
IntrBkSttlmDtInterbank Settlement DateOThe date on which interbank settlement occurs. It drives nostro funding, liquidity planning and value dating of the related customer payment. The date must be a business day for the settlement currency and must be consistent with the settlement method and the clearing system cut off times.
SttlmInfSettlement InformationMHow settlement will occur, including settlement method and settlement account. COVE means a cover payment and tells each bank that a separate customer instruction travels directly to the beneficiary bank. The method tells each bank which account to debit and credit and which channel carries the funds.
DbtrDebtorMThe customer who ordered the underlying payment, carried in the cover so every bank can screen it. It gives the account number, name and address of the originator. Intermediaries use it for sanctions screening, because a cover without ordering customer data cannot be screened or processed.
DbtrAcctDebtor AccountOThe account of the debtor from which the underlying payment is funded, identified by IBAN or other account identification. It links the cover to the correct customer account in the debtor agent system. Accurate account data is needed for debit posting and customer statement reporting.
DbtrAgtDebtor AgentMThe bank holding the debtor account and sending the cover onward. It is identified by BIC. The debtor agent is responsible for debiting the customer, applying charges, screening the payment and choosing the settlement route to the next bank in the chain.
CdtrCreditorMThe final beneficiary of the underlying customer payment, carried in the cover so every bank can screen it. It gives the account number, name and address of the recipient. Intermediaries screen it and the beneficiary bank uses it to credit the correct account when the cover is matched.
CdtrAcctCreditor AccountOThe account of the creditor to be credited, identified by IBAN or other account identification. It tells the beneficiary bank exactly where to post the funds once the cover is matched. A wrong account number is a common cause of returns, so banks validate the IBAN check digits before release.
CdtrAgtCreditor AgentMThe bank holding the creditor account and receiving the cover for onward credit. It is identified by BIC. The creditor agent screens the incoming cover, checks the creditor account and credits the beneficiary on the value date after the customer leg is matched.
IntrmyAgt1Intermediary Agent 1OAn intermediary bank in the cover chain between debtor agent and creditor agent. It forwards the funds and screens the full customer details before releasing the payment onward. Its BIC must be accurate so the chain routes correctly and screening is complete at each step.
ChrgsInfCharges InformationODetails of the charges applied to the underlying payment, including the amount, currency and bearer. The bearer can be debtor, creditor or shared. Transparent charges data lets the creditor understand any deduction and supports tracking of fees across the correspondent chain.
RmtInfRemittance InformationOStructured or unstructured information explaining what the underlying payment is for, such as invoice numbers. Structured remittance lets the creditor reconcile automatically against open items. It is carried in the cover so the beneficiary bank can pass it to the beneficiary on credit.
RltdPmtInfRelated Payment InformationOInformation about a related payment, most often the customer credit transfer that this cover settles. It can hold the related message identification and amount. The beneficiary bank uses it to match cover and customer legs and to release the customer payment only when both agree.

Valid example

BNP Paribas Paris sends a pacs.009 COV for USD 367,574.90 value 6 November 2026 to Banco Santander Madrid, covering a customer payment from a Paris corporate to a Madrid supplier. The debtor and creditor names and addresses are complete, the end to end identification matches the customer instruction, and Santander screens, matches and applies the funds.

Invalid example

A pacs.009 COV arrives without the debtor name and address, so the intermediary bank cannot screen the underlying customer payment. The funds are held in the intermediary nostro account, the cover cannot be forwarded, and the ordering bank must send a corrected message with full debtor details.

Validation errors

  • Missing debtor name and address causes a hold because intermediaries cannot screen the underlying customer payment without complete party data.
  • End to end identification not matching the customer instruction causes a reconciliation break because cover and customer legs cannot be linked.
  • Settlement amount differing from the customer payment amount causes an exception because the cover must fund the customer payment in full after agreed charges.

Processing notes

Each bank in the chain screens the debtor and creditor details against sanctions and anti money laundering lists before forwarding the cover. The beneficiary bank matches the cover to the customer instruction by end to end identification before crediting the beneficiary. Unmatched covers sit in a suspense queue until the customer leg arrives.

Accounting notes

The COV leg posts a debit to the ordering bank nostro account and a credit to the beneficiary bank account, with the funds held in a settlement account until the customer leg is matched. Only after matching does the beneficiary bank post the credit to the beneficiary customer account, so the ledger shows the two stage settlement clearly.
