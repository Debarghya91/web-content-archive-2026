pacs.009-adv: FI to FI Credit Transfer, Advice

pacs.009 ADV is the ISO 20022 advice leg that tells a bank funds are on their way, without moving the funds itself. It carries the settlement amount, settlement date, debtor agent, creditor agent and the related customer payment reference. Banks use it when the customer instruction and the funds travel by different routes, so the receiving bank can screen and prepare the credit. It is the advice variant of pacs.009.

At a glance

  • Message: pacs.009-adv
  • Title: FI to FI Credit Transfer, Advice
  • Current version: pacs.009.001.08
  • Versions: pacs.009.001.06, pacs.009.001.08, pacs.009.001.09, pacs.009.001.10
  • Fields: 13 (8 mandatory, 5 optional)

Participants

The sending participant is the debtor agent that will move the funds. The receiving participant is the creditor agent that expects the funds, screens the payment in advance, and prepares to credit the beneficiary when settlement arrives.

Structure

pacs.009 ADV has a group header, payment identification, advised settlement amount and date, settlement information, debtor agent and creditor agent blocks, and full debtor and creditor blocks. The document outline mirrors pacs.009 Core, with the settlement method marked as advice rather than a live settlement instruction.

Scheme rules

pacs.009 ADV follows the CBPR+ advice rules, which require full debtor and creditor data so the receiving bank can screen before funds arrive. It is distinct from Core, which settles funds, and from COV, which settles funds through the correspondent chain. Domestic schemes rarely use ADV, since settlement within one clearing system needs no separate advice leg.

Fields

FieldNameMandatoryDescription
GrpHdrGroup HeaderMThe header holding message identification, creation date and time, number of transactions and control sum. It identifies the message for tracking and duplicate detection. The message identification must be unique for the sender, and the control sum must equal the sum of the advised amounts.
PmtIdPayment IdentificationMThe identification block with instruction identification, end to end identification and transaction identification. End to end identification links this advice to the related customer payment. It lets the receiving bank match the advice to the expected funds before they arrive.
IntrBkSttlmAmtInterbank Settlement AmountMThe amount and currency that will be settled between the debtor agent and the creditor agent, for example USD 367,574.90. The receiving bank uses it to expect the incoming funds and to plan liquidity. It must match the related customer payment amount after any agreed charges.
IntrBkSttlmDtInterbank Settlement DateOThe date on which interbank settlement is expected to occur. It drives liquidity planning and value dating of the related customer payment. The date must be a business day for the settlement currency and must be consistent with the settlement method and the clearing system cut off times.
SttlmInfSettlement InformationMHow settlement will occur, including settlement method and settlement account. ADV means an advice of a payment and tells the receiver that funds will follow through a separate leg. The method tells the receiver which account and channel the funds will use when they arrive.
DbtrAgtDebtor AgentMThe bank sending the advice and debiting its account when the funds move. It is identified by BIC. The debtor agent is responsible for funding the payment, choosing the settlement route and sending the advice to the next agent before the funds arrive.
CdtrAgtCreditor AgentMThe bank receiving the advice and expecting the funds. It is identified by BIC. The creditor agent checks the advice against its expected receipts, screens the payment and prepares to credit the beneficiary when the settlement funds arrive on the stated date.
DbtrDebtorMThe customer who ordered the underlying payment, carried in the advice so the receiver can screen it. It gives the name and address of the originator. The receiver uses it for sanctions screening before the funds arrive, so the payment can be released without delay on settlement.
CdtrCreditorMThe final beneficiary of the underlying customer payment, carried in the advice so the receiver can screen it. It gives the name and address of the recipient. The receiver uses it to prepare the credit and to check the beneficiary account before the funds arrive.
CdtrAcctCreditor AccountOThe account of the creditor to be credited when the funds arrive, identified by IBAN or other account identification. It tells the creditor agent exactly where to post the funds. A wrong account number is a common cause of delay, so banks validate the IBAN check digits on receipt of the advice.
RltdPmtInfRelated Payment InformationOInformation about a related payment, most often the customer credit transfer that this advice refers to. It can hold the related message identification and amount. The creditor agent uses it to match the advice to the expected funds and to release the customer payment when the funds arrive.
RmtInfRemittance InformationOStructured or unstructured information explaining what the underlying payment is for, such as invoice numbers. It is carried in the advice so the beneficiary bank can pass it to the beneficiary on credit. Clear remittance information helps the beneficiary reconcile the receipt once funds arrive.
PurpPurposeOA coded purpose for the advised payment, such as a cover payment or a liquidity transfer. Purpose codes support reporting and internal accounting. Banks use them to classify expected receipts and to distinguish customer related advices from own account funding advices in their ledgers.

Valid example

BNP Paribas Paris sends a pacs.009 ADV for USD 367,574.90 value 6 November 2026 to Banco Santander Madrid, advising that settlement funds for a customer payment from a Paris corporate to a Madrid supplier will arrive that day. Santander screens the debtor and creditor, checks the beneficiary account, and prepares the credit for release on receipt of funds.

Invalid example

A pacs.009 ADV arrives advising USD 367,574.90 but the end to end identification matches no expected payment and the creditor account is closed. The receiving bank cannot prepare the credit, so it holds the advice in an exceptions queue and asks the debtor agent to confirm the reference and the beneficiary account.

Validation errors

  • End to end identification matching no expected payment causes a suspense hold because the advice cannot be linked to the payment it refers to.
  • Creditor account closed or invalid causes a repair because the receiving bank cannot prepare the credit for the beneficiary.
  • Settlement date in the past causes an exception because the advised funds are already overdue and the receiver must query the debtor agent.

Processing notes

The receiving bank screens the advice on receipt and pre checks the beneficiary account, so the credit can be released quickly when funds arrive. If the funds do not arrive on the stated date, the advice stays open and the bank queries the debtor agent. Advices never settle funds on their own.

Accounting notes

The ADV leg posts no settlement entry, since it only advises that funds will arrive. The receiving bank may record a memo entry for the expected receipt, but the ledger is not affected. The real debit and credit post only when the settlement funds arrive through the separate cover or direct leg.
