camt.054: Bank to Customer Debit Credit Notification

camt.054 is the ISO 20022 bank to customer debit credit notification that a bank sends when a single entry is posted to an account. It tells the customer that a stated amount was debited or credited on a stated value date. Corporates use it for real time reconciliation, matching each notification to an expected payment or receipt. It carries booking date, value date, amount, currency and entry details.

At a glance

  • Message: camt.054
  • Title: Bank to Customer Debit Credit Notification
  • Current version: camt.054.001.10
  • Versions: camt.054.001.02, camt.054.001.06, camt.054.001.08, camt.054.001.10
  • Fields: 12 (8 mandatory, 4 optional)

Participants

The sending participant is the account servicing bank that posted the debit or credit. The receiving participant is the account owner, usually a corporate, which needs immediate notice that a specific entry was posted to its account.

Structure

camt.054 has a group header, then one or more notification blocks. Each notification block holds the account, notification identification, and a repeating block of entries with entry details and an optional balance. The document outline follows the standard ISO 20022 notification structure with one entry per notification in most use.

Scheme rules

camt.054 is the ISO 20022 equivalent of the MT900 and MT910 confirmations, used for real time debit and credit notifications under CBPR+ and domestic reporting schemes. Each bank agrees with the customer whether to send camt.054, MT900, MT910, or both for the same entry. The message reports an account posting rather than carrying a payment instruction.

Fields

FieldNameMandatoryDescription
GrpHdrGroup HeaderMThe header with message identification, creation date and time and the account owner. It identifies the notification for tracking and duplicate detection. The customer system uses it to confirm it received the correct notification for the correct account before processing the entry.
NtfctnNotificationMThe notification block holding the account, notification identification, creation date and time. One camt.054 can hold several notifications for different accounts. The notification identification must be unique so the customer can detect a missing or duplicate notification in sequence.
AcctAccountMThe account the notification covers, identified by IBAN or other account identification, with the currency and the account owner name. It tells the customer system which ledger account the debit or credit relates to. A notification must never be loaded against the wrong account.
NtryEntryMOne entry in the notification, representing a single debit or credit to the account. It carries booking date, value date, amount, currency, status and references. A notification normally holds one entry, but it can hold several entries for the same account and period.
BookgDtBooking DateMThe date on which the entry was booked to the account in the bank system. It may differ from the value date when an entry is booked late or back valued. Reconciliation teams use the booking date to know when the entry appeared and to track late postings.
ValDtValue DateOThe date on which the funds become available for a credit or are taken for a debit. It drives interest calculation and cash flow forecasting. A value date in the future means the funds are not yet available, and the customer must not treat the entry as cleared cash.
AmtAmountMThe amount and currency of the entry, with a credit or debit indicator. For example a credit of USD 367,574.90 appears as a positive amount with a credit indicator. The customer sums the amounts to check the movement and to spot any missing entry.
CdtDbtIndCredit Debit IndicatorMA code showing whether the entry is a credit or a debit to the account. CRDT means money was added, DBIT means money was taken. It is essential for reconciliation, because the same amount can be a receipt or a payment depending on this indicator alone.
StsStatusMThe status of the entry, normally BOOK for a booked entry or PDNG for a pending entry. BOOK means the entry is final and included in the balance. PDNG means the entry is expected but not yet final, and the customer should not reconcile against it until it becomes booked.
NtryDtlsEntry DetailsODetailed information about the entry, including transaction details, debtor, creditor, remittance information and charges. It gives the customer the data needed to match the entry to an invoice or payment instruction. Rich entry details reduce manual reconciliation work sharply.
AddtlNtryInfAdditional Entry InformationOFree text additional information about the entry, such as the bank reference or a narrative description. It can carry the end to end identification or the remittance reference. The customer uses it as a fallback matching key when structured details do not match automatically.
BalBalanceOBalance information for the account after the notified entry, including the closing balance with type, amount, currency and date. The customer compares the balance with its own ledger to confirm that the entry was posted correctly and no other entry was missed.

Valid example

Banco Santander Madrid sends a camt.054 to BNP Paribas Paris notifying a credit of USD 367,574.90 on 6 November 2026 to account 44098712, reference COV77812. The value date matches the settlement date, the amount matches the expected cover receipt, and BNP Paribas reconciles the credit and releases the customer payment.

Invalid example

A camt.054 arrives notifying a credit of USD 367,574.90 but the receiving customer expected the credit on a different value date and cannot find a matching account. The notification cannot be reconciled, so it sits unmatched until Meridian Bank confirms the account number and the value date.

Validation errors

  • Unknown account number causes a reconciliation break because the customer cannot match the notification to any account it holds.
  • Credit amount differing from the expected receipt causes an exception because the customer cannot confirm full settlement of the underlying payment.
  • Notification identification duplicated causes a duplicate warning because the customer system cannot tell whether the entry was posted once or twice.

Processing notes

The customer system matches the notification against expected receipts and payments by account, amount, value date and reference. Matched notifications close the expected item automatically. Unmatched notifications go to an exceptions queue, and the customer queries the bank before treating the entry as a confirmed receipt or payment.

Accounting notes

The camt.054 itself posts nothing, since the debit or credit was already posted by the bank. The customer uses it to confirm its own ledger entry for the same movement, matching amount and value date. Any difference creates a reconciliation break that must be resolved before the books can close.
