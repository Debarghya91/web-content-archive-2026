pain.001: Customer Credit Transfer Initiation

pain.001 is the ISO 20022 message a corporate customer or payment initiator sends to its bank to instruct a credit transfer. It is the first message in the customer to bank payment chain. It carries payment information, debtor, creditor, amount, execution date and remittance details. Banks receive it through host to host files, APIs or payment hubs, validate it, and then create the interbank pacs.008 to execute the payment.

At a glance

  • Message: pain.001
  • Title: Customer Credit Transfer Initiation
  • Current version: pain.001.001.09
  • Versions: pain.001.001.03, pain.001.001.09, pain.001.001.10
  • Fields: 15 (11 mandatory, 4 optional)

Participants

The sending participant is the initiating party, usually a corporate customer. The receiving participant is the debtor agent, the bank holding the debtor account, which validates the file and executes the payments.

Structure

pain.001 is an ISO 20022 XML message with a GroupHeader (GrpHdr) followed by one or more PaymentInformation blocks (PmtInf). Each block groups payments sharing the same debtor, debtor account, execution date and charge bearer, and holds one or more CreditTransferTransactionInformation entries (CdtTrfTxInf) with creditor, creditor account, amount and remittance information.

Scheme rules

pain.001 is a base ISO 20022 message: the structure and fields below are the base definition, and each scheme adds its own rules for its rail. Under SEPA SCT the IBAN is mandatory for debtor and creditor accounts, the charge bearer is SLEV only, and the file must follow the SEPA implementation guide for the country of the debtor bank. Banks may also enforce their own file size limits, cut-off times and duplicate file checks on top of the scheme rules.

Fields

FieldNameMandatoryDescription
GrpHdrGroup HeaderMThe header with message identification, creation date and time, number of transactions, control sum and initiating party. It identifies the file for the bank and the customer. The bank uses it to acknowledge receipt, detect duplicate files and reconcile the total amount against the sum of the individual payments.
PmtInfPayment InformationMA block grouping payments that share the same debtor, debtor account, execution date and charges bearer. One pain.001 file can hold many payment information blocks. Grouping by common attributes simplifies validation and lets the bank process all payments in the block under one debit instruction.
PmtInfIdPayment Information IdentificationMA unique identification for the payment information block, assigned by the customer. It lets the customer and the bank refer to the whole block in status reports and queries. It must be unique for the initiating party so the bank can detect a duplicate file or block.
PmtMtdPayment MethodMThe method used for the payment, normally TRF for a credit transfer, TRA for a transfer advice or CHK for a cheque. TRF is the standard value for a bank credit transfer. The method tells the bank which processing channel and clearing system to use for the payments in the block.
ReqdExctnDtRequested Execution DateMThe date on which the customer asks the bank to execute the payment. The bank checks it against cut off times and business days and may move it to the next business day if it falls on a holiday. It drives value dating and the timing of the debit to the customer account.
DbtrDebtorMThe customer ordering the payment, with name, postal address and identification. The bank matches the debtor to its customer records and checks signing authority. Complete debtor data supports sanctions screening and lets the bank report the payment correctly on the customer statement.
DbtrAcctDebtor AccountMThe account from which the payment is debited, identified by IBAN or other account identification. The bank checks that the account exists, has sufficient funds and is authorised for the payment type. A wrong account number is a common cause of file rejection at validation stage.
DbtrAgtDebtor AgentMThe bank holding the debtor account, identified by BIC. It is the bank receiving the pain.001 file. The debtor agent validates the file, screens the payments and creates the interbank messages needed to move the funds to each creditor agent.
CdtrAgtCreditor AgentOThe bank holding the creditor account, identified by BIC when known. The customer may leave it blank and let the debtor agent derive it from the creditor IBAN. Accurate creditor agent data speeds routing and avoids the payment being sent to the wrong bank or held for repair.
CdtrCreditorMThe beneficiary of the payment, with name, postal address and identification. The bank screens the creditor against sanctions and fraud lists before execution. Complete creditor data reduces the chance of the payment being stopped at the creditor agent for missing beneficiary information.
CdtrAcctCreditor AccountMThe account to be credited, identified by IBAN or other account identification. The bank validates the IBAN format and check digits before accepting the file. A valid IBAN does not guarantee the account exists, but it prevents many format errors that cause returns.
AmtAmountMThe amount and currency of the individual credit transfer, for example EUR 10,000. It is the amount the creditor receives before any charges. The sum of the amounts in a payment information block must equal the control sum in the group header or the bank rejects the file.
ChrgBrCharge BearerOWho pays the charges, using DEBT for debtor, CRED for creditor, SHAR for shared or SLEV for service level. The value drives fee deduction and must match the agreement between the customer and the bank. It is carried forward into the pacs.008 that the bank creates for the interbank leg.
RmtInfRemittance InformationOInformation explaining the payment, such as invoice numbers, in structured or unstructured form. Structured remittance information lets the creditor reconcile automatically. Customers should keep it concise and use the creditor agreed format so the creditor system can match it without manual work.
PurpPurposeOA coded purpose for the payment, such as SUPP for supplier payment or SALA for salary. Purpose codes support regulatory reporting and help the bank apply the correct screening and pricing. The customer selects the code that best describes the underlying commercial reason for the payment.

Valid example

Northgate Trading sends a pain.001 file to Meridian Bank London with one payment information block for 48 supplier payments totalling EUR 120,000, requested execution date 6 November 2026, charge bearer SLEV. The bank validates the file, debits the Northgate account, and reports acceptance in a pain.002.

Invalid example

A pain.001 file arrives with a control sum of EUR 120,000 but payment amounts totalling EUR 121,500, and one creditor IBAN that fails its check digit. The bank rejects the whole file for repair (error: control sum mismatch).

Validation errors

  • Control sum mismatch: the sum of the payment amounts in a block must equal the control sum in the group header, or the bank rejects the file.
  • Invalid IBAN in a creditor account: a failed IBAN check digit means the creditor account cannot be validated and the item is rejected.
  • Requested execution date in the past: a date that has already passed cannot be executed without an explicit instruction, so the block is rejected or moved.

Processing notes

The bank validates the file structure, debtor account, balance and signing authority, then screens each payment before execution. Failed items are reported in a pain.002 so the customer can correct and resend only those items, while valid items proceed to settlement as pacs.008 messages.

Accounting notes

Each executed payment posts a debit to the debtor account on the execution date and a corresponding credit to the settlement or nostro account. Charges are posted separately to the debtor account. Rejected items post nothing, so the ledger only reflects payments that passed validation and were executed.
