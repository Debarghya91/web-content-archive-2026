pacs.008: FI to FI Customer Credit Transfer

pacs.008 is the ISO 20022 message that replaces MT103 for customer credit transfers between financial institutions. It carries the full customer payment instruction in structured XML, with rich data for debtor, creditor, charges, remittance and settlement. Banks use it under CBPR+ for cross-border payments. Its structured fields improve screening, reconciliation and straight through processing compared with the free text MT format.

At a glance

  • Message: pacs.008
  • Title: FI to FI Customer Credit Transfer
  • Current version: pacs.008.001.08
  • Versions: pacs.008.001.06, pacs.008.001.08, pacs.008.001.09, pacs.008.001.10
  • Fields: 16 (8 mandatory, 8 optional)

Participants

The sending participant is the debtor agent, the bank holding the debtor account. The receiving participant is the creditor agent, the bank holding the creditor account, with any intermediary agents in between.

Structure

pacs.008 is an ISO 20022 XML message with a GroupHeader (GrpHdr) followed by one or more CreditTransferTransactionInformation blocks (CdtTrfTxInf). Each transaction block holds payment identification (PmtId), interbank settlement amount and date (IntrBkSttlmAmt, IntrBkSttlmDt), settlement information (SttlmInf), debtor and creditor agents and accounts, charges information (ChrgsInf), purpose (Purp) and remittance information (RmtInf).

Scheme rules

pacs.008 is a base ISO 20022 message: the structure and fields below are the base definition, and each scheme adds or restricts elements for its rail. Under CBPR+ the message must carry a UETR for end to end tracking, the debtor and creditor names and addresses must be fully structured, and the charge bearer may be DEBT, CRED or SHAR. Under SEPA SCT the charge bearer is SLEV only, the IBAN is mandatory for both accounts, and the 2025 implementation guides fix version pacs.008.001.08.

Fields

FieldNameMandatoryDescription
GrpHdrGroup HeaderMThe header of the message, holding the message identification, creation date and time, number of transactions and control sum. It identifies the file or message batch for tracking and reconciliation. A unique message identification prevents duplicate processing and lets the receiver confirm receipt of the complete group.
PmtIdPayment IdentificationMThe identification block holding instruction identification, end to end identification and transaction identification. End to end identification stays unchanged through the whole payment chain and lets the debtor and creditor track the payment. Instruction identification is unique per instructing agent and transaction.
IntrBkSttlmAmtInterbank Settlement AmountMThe amount and currency settled between banks for this payment, for example USD 25,000. It is the amount the debtor agent transfers to the creditor agent. It may differ from the instructed amount when charges are deducted or currency conversion occurs, and settlement systems use it to post nostro entries.
IntrBkSttlmDtInterbank Settlement DateOThe date on which settlement between banks takes place. It is the value date for the interbank leg and drives liquidity planning and nostro funding. The date must be a valid business day for the settlement currency and must match the date agreed with the correspondent or clearing system.
SttlmInfSettlement InformationMInformation about how settlement will occur, including the settlement method, settlement account and clearing system. Values include INDA for instructed agent settlement, INGA for instructing agent settlement and CLRG for clearing system settlement. It tells each bank which account and channel to use.
DbtrDebtorMThe customer who ordered the payment, with structured name, postal address and identification. Rich debtor data supports sanctions screening and anti money laundering checks. Under CBPR+ the debtor name and address must be complete, because truncated data can cause the payment to be stopped or returned.
DbtrAcctDebtor AccountOThe account of the debtor from which the payment is debited, identified by IBAN or other account identification. It links the payment to the correct customer account in the debtor agent system. Accurate account data is needed for debit posting, balance checks and customer statement reporting.
DbtrAgtDebtor AgentMThe bank holding the debtor account and sending the payment instruction onward. It is identified by BIC and sometimes by name and address. The debtor agent is responsible for debiting the customer, applying charges, screening the payment and choosing the settlement route to the next bank.
CdtrAgtCreditor AgentMThe bank holding the creditor account and receiving the payment for onward credit. It is identified by BIC and sometimes by name and address. The creditor agent screens the incoming payment, checks the creditor account and credits the beneficiary on the value date after its own controls pass.
CdtrCreditorMThe final beneficiary of the payment, with structured name, postal address and identification. The creditor agent credits this party on receipt of funds. Complete creditor data supports screening, fraud checks and beneficiary verification, and helps the creditor reconcile the receipt against expected payments.
CdtrAcctCreditor AccountOThe account of the creditor to be credited, identified by IBAN or other account identification. It tells the creditor agent exactly where to post the funds. A wrong account number is a common cause of returns and investigations, so banks validate the IBAN check digits before releasing the payment.
ChrgsInfCharges InformationODetails of the charges applied to the payment, including the amount, currency, bearer and the agent that applied each charge. The bearer can be debtor, creditor, shared or following service level. Transparent charges data lets the creditor understand any deduction and supports gpi tracking of fees.
PurpPurposeOA coded purpose for the payment, such as SUPP for supplier payment, SALA for salary, or GDDS for goods. Purpose codes support regulatory reporting, sanctions screening and customer analytics. Banks map customer purpose descriptions to the ISO external purpose code list before sending the message.
RmtInfRemittance InformationOStructured or unstructured information explaining what the payment is for, such as invoice numbers and dates. Structured remittance information lets the creditor reconcile automatically against open items. Unstructured text is limited in length and should carry only the key references the creditor needs.
InstrForCdtrAgtInstruction for Creditor AgentOAn instruction from the debtor agent to the creditor agent, such as hold the payment for pickup or contact the creditor before crediting. It is free text with coded options. The creditor agent must read and follow it before crediting, because it can change the normal credit process.
InstrForDbtrAgtInstruction for Debtor AgentOAn instruction for the debtor agent, normally carried from the original customer instruction. It can request a specific handling such as a telephone advice to the creditor. The debtor agent applies it before sending the payment onward and records it for audit and customer service.

Valid example

Meridian Bank London sends a pacs.008.001.08 for USD 25,000 value 5 November 2026 to Harbour Bank Singapore, with a UETR, full structured debtor and creditor names and addresses, charge bearer SHAR, and remittance reference INV-1187. Harbour Bank screens the payment, credits Bright Logistics, and reports the status in a pacs.002.

Invalid example

A pacs.008 arrives under CBPR+ with no UETR and a truncated debtor address. The creditor agent cannot track the payment end to end or complete sanctions screening and rejects it (error: missing UETR).

Validation errors

  • Missing UETR under CBPR+: every pacs.008.001.08 must carry a Universally Unique Transaction Reference, and a message without one is rejected.
  • Incomplete structured debtor or creditor address: CBPR+ requires full name and address data, and truncated party data causes rejection or repair.
  • Invalid IBAN in the creditor account: a failed IBAN check digit means the creditor account cannot be validated and the payment is rejected.

Processing notes

The debtor agent debits the customer, screens the payment, and sends the pacs.008 to the next agent. Each agent validates the structure, screens the parties, and forwards or credits the payment. The creditor agent reports acceptance or rejection in a pacs.002, and a payment that cannot be credited is returned in a pacs.004.

Accounting notes

The debtor agent debits the debtor account and credits its nostro or settlement account for the interbank settlement amount. The creditor agent debits its nostro account and credits the creditor account. Charges are posted separately to the bearer named in the charges information, and the control sum in the group header must equal the sum of the transactions.
