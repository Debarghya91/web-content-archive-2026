camt.050: Liquidity Credit Transfer

camt.050 is the ISO 20022 message used to move liquidity between accounts held within the same bank or banking group. Treasury teams use it to fund a nostro account, sweep balances to a central account, or cover a shortfall before a payment run. It carries the source account, destination account, amount and execution date. It moves bank funds only and never carries a customer payment.

At a glance

  • Message: camt.050
  • Title: Liquidity Credit Transfer
  • Current version: camt.050.001.05
  • Versions: camt.050.001.02, camt.050.001.04, camt.050.001.05
  • Fields: 12 (8 mandatory, 4 optional)

Participants

The sending participant is the treasury team or system that instructs the liquidity movement. The receiving participant is the account servicing system that holds the source and destination accounts and executes the transfer between them.

Structure

camt.050 has a group header, a debtor account block, a creditor account block, a transferred amount block, an execution date, and agent blocks for both sides. The document outline follows the standard ISO 20022 liquidity transfer structure with one transfer per message and no repeating transaction blocks.

Scheme rules

camt.050 is the base ISO 20022 liquidity transfer message, used mainly for internal treasury movements with no external scheme rulebook. CBPR+ does not define a liquidity scheme, so banks apply their own internal rules for cut off times, balance checks and authorisation. Domestic clearing systems may restrict camt.050 to accounts held within the same legal entity.

Fields

FieldNameMandatoryDescription
GrpHdrGroup HeaderMThe header holding message identification, creation date and time and the instructing agent. It identifies the liquidity transfer for tracking and duplicate detection. The message identification must be unique for the sender, so a duplicate transfer can be detected and ignored.
DbtrAcctDebtor AccountMThe account from which liquidity is moved, identified by IBAN or other account identification. It tells the receiving system exactly which account to debit. A wrong account number is a common cause of failure, so the account is checked against the treasury account list before execution.
CdtrAcctCreditor AccountMThe account to which liquidity is moved, identified by IBAN or other account identification. It tells the receiving system exactly which account to credit. The account must belong to the same bank or group, since camt.050 moves liquidity internally rather than to an external customer.
TrfdAmtTransferred AmountMThe amount and currency of the liquidity transfer, for example EUR 5,000,000.00. It is the amount moved between the two accounts. The amount must not exceed the available balance in the source account, or the transfer will be rejected for insufficient funds.
ExctnDtExecution DateMThe date on which the liquidity transfer is executed. It is normally the same business day the instruction is sent. The date drives the value dating of both account postings and must be a business day for the currency involved.
DbtrAgtDebtor AgentMThe bank holding the debtor account and instructing the transfer, identified by BIC. It is responsible for checking the source balance, applying the debit and sending the instruction to the system that holds the destination account.
CdtrAgtCreditor AgentMThe bank holding the creditor account, identified by BIC. It receives the instruction, checks the destination account and applies the credit. In most camt.050 flows the debtor and creditor agents are the same bank or members of the same group.
PurpPurposeOThe purpose of the liquidity transfer, using a coded value such as a funding transfer or a sweep. Purpose codes support treasury reporting and help the bank classify the movement. The code must match the treasury instruction or the transfer may be posted to the wrong internal account.
AddtlInfAdditional InformationOAdditional information about the transfer, such as the treasury instruction reference or a narrative description. It can carry the reference the treasury team uses to reconcile the movement. The receiving system uses it as a fallback matching key when the account data alone does not match.
InstrIdInstruction IdentificationMThe identification of the liquidity transfer instruction, assigned by the instructing treasury team. It lets the treasury system track the transfer through execution and settlement. It must be unique so a duplicate instruction can be detected before the funds move twice.
EndToEndIdEnd to End IdentificationOThe end to end identification for the transfer, which stays unchanged through the whole movement. It lets the treasury team and both account systems refer to the same transfer. It is useful when a transfer is queried by its own reference rather than by account or amount.
ChrgsInfCharges InformationOCharges applied to the liquidity transfer, when the bank charges for the internal movement. It can show the amount and bearer of the fee. Treasury uses it to reconcile internal charges against its cost schedule and to query any charge it does not recognise.

Valid example

Meridian Bank treasury sends a camt.050 to move EUR 5,000,000.00 from its central euro account to its nostro account at Harbour Bank on 6 November 2026, funding the afternoon payment run. The source balance is sufficient, the destination is an internal nostro account, and both accounts update at once on execution.

Invalid example

A camt.050 arrives instructing EUR 5,000,000.00 from an account that holds only EUR 2,000,000.00, with a creditor account outside the bank group. The transfer cannot be funded and the destination is not an internal account, so the instruction is rejected for insufficient funds and invalid destination.

Validation errors

  • Insufficient funds in the source account causes rejection because the transfer cannot be funded without breaching the account balance.
  • Creditor account outside the bank group causes rejection because camt.050 moves liquidity internally and cannot pay an external account.
  • Execution date on a non business day causes a hold because the transfer cannot be value dated when the currency system is closed.

Processing notes

The receiving system checks the source balance, the destination account and the execution date before applying the transfer. Valid transfers post immediately and both accounts update at once. Failed transfers are reported to the treasury team, which corrects the amount or account and resends the instruction.

Accounting notes

The liquidity transfer posts a debit to the source account and a credit to the destination account within the same bank or group, with no customer entry involved. Treasury records it as an internal movement between its own accounts. The entries must net to zero across the group, or the liquidity position will be misstated.
