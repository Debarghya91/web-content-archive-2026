SC-01: MT103 serial payment missing field 57A

Serial customer credit transfer · United Kingdom to Singapore, SWIFT · USD 25000 · Meridian Bank London to Harbour Bank Singapore

Messages. MT103

Positive path. This scenario is set before the CBPR+ migration, so the payment travels as an MT103. The same payment today travels as a pacs.008. Northgate Trading instructs Meridian Bank London to pay USD 25,000 to Bright Logistics at Harbour Bank Singapore for invoice INV-1187. Meridian Bank validates the instruction, debits the customer account, and sends a complete MT103 with field 57A naming Harbour Bank. Harbour Bank screens the payment, credits Bright Logistics on the value date, and confirms receipt. Both banks reconcile their nostro entries the same day and the payment closes without exception.

What went wrong. Northgate Trading instructs the same USD 25,000 payment, but the MT103 omits field 57A, so the receiving bank cannot tell which bank holds the beneficiary account. The payment is held for repair while operations contact Meridian Bank to confirm the account with institution. The value date slips by one day, Bright Logistics queries the delay, and Meridian Bank must send a corrected MT103 before Harbour Bank can credit the beneficiary account.

Root cause. The payment instruction was created without the beneficiary bank, because the template did not require field 57A when the receiver was assumed to be the beneficiary bank. Recovery. Operations contact the originating bank, obtain the correct field 57A value, repair the MT103, and resend it with the original reference and UETR.

Reconciliation. The nostro debit matches the Harbour Bank statement, the beneficiary credit matches the MT103 amount, and the payment closes with a one day delay recorded.

SC-02: Choosing serial vs cover for a USD payment

Customer credit transfer, routing decision · United Kingdom to Australia, SWIFT · USD 48000 · Meridian Bank London to Pacific Rim Bank Sydney

Messages. MT103, MT202 COV

Positive path. This scenario is set before the CBPR+ migration, so the payment travels as an MT103 with an MT202 COV for the cover leg. The same payment today travels as a pacs.008 with a pacs.009 COV for the cover leg. Coral Supplies instructs Meridian Bank London to pay USD 48,000 to Pacific Rim Bank Sydney for goods received. Meridian Bank compares serial and cover routing, chooses cover because it is faster and keeps customer data on the MT103 route, and sends the MT103 and the MT202 COV with matching references. Harbour Bank settles the cover funds, Pacific Rim Bank matches both legs, credits Coral Supplies on the value date, and both banks reconcile without a break.

What went wrong. Coral Supplies instructs the same USD 48,000 payment, but Meridian Bank chooses serial routing without checking the intermediary cut off time, so the payment arrives after the Harbour Bank cut off. The funds are held overnight, Pacific Rim Bank cannot credit Coral Supplies on the value date, and the supplier queries the delay. Meridian Bank must explain the routing choice, fund the payment next day, and record the delay as a routing exception.

Root cause. The routing decision did not check the intermediary cut off time, so a serial payment missed the settlement window and was held overnight. Recovery. Operations select the cover route for the next payment, update the routing table with cut off times, and resend the payment with a revised value date.

Reconciliation. The nostro debit matches the cover settlement, the beneficiary credit matches the MT103 amount, and the payment closes with a one day delay recorded.

Alpine Bank creates a pacs.003, Meridian Bank debits the customer on the collection date, and the customer disputes the amount as wrong. Meridian Bank returns the direct debit with a pacs.004 and refunds EUR 180 to the customer, Alpine Bank debits Delta Utilities, and the creditor is notified of the return.

What went wrong. Delta Utilities sends the same EUR 180 direct debit, but the mandate reference is wrong, so Meridian Bank rejects the pacs.003 before debiting the customer and returns it as a reject. Delta Utilities is not paid, the customer is not debited, and Delta Utilities must obtain the correct mandate reference from the customer, correct the pain.008 file, and resubmit the collection with the valid mandate.

Root cause. The pacs.003 carried a wrong mandate reference, so the debtor bank rejected the collection before debiting the customer account. Recovery. Operations obtain the correct mandate reference, correct the pain.008 file, and resubmit the collection with the valid mandate.

Reconciliation. No debit and no credit were made for the rejected collection, the corrected collection settles in full, and the case closes with no break.

SC-21: Cover arriving before the customer payment message

Cover payment arriving before the customer instruction · United Kingdom to Australia, SWIFT · USD 62000 · Meridian Bank London to Pacific Rim Bank Sydney

Messages. MT202 COV, MT103

Positive path. This scenario is set before the CBPR+ migration, so the payment travels as an MT103 with an MT202 COV for the cover leg. The same payment today travels as a pacs.008 with a pacs.009 COV for the cover leg. Northgate Trading instructs Meridian Bank London to pay USD 62,000 to a supplier at Pacific Rim Bank Sydney. Meridian Bank sends the MT202 COV first, so the cover funds reach Pacific Rim Bank before the MT103 arrives. Pacific Rim Bank holds the unmatched cover in a suspense account and waits for the customer instruction. The MT103 arrives one hour later with the same reference and UETR, the bank matches them, screens the payment, and credits the supplier on the value date. Meridian Bank reconciles the nostro debit with no break.

What went wrong. Northgate Trading instructs the same USD 62,000 payment, but the MT103 is delayed by a network outage and does not arrive for two business days. Pacific Rim Bank holds the cover funds in a suspense account and cannot credit the supplier, who queries the missing payment. Operations contact Meridian Bank to chase the MT103, and Meridian Bank resends it with the original reference and UETR. The bank matches the resent MT103 to the held cover and credits the supplier two days late.

Root cause. The MT202 COV reached the beneficiary bank before the MT103, so the cover could not be matched to a customer instruction and was held in suspense. Recovery. Operations hold the cover in a suspense account, chase the MT103 from the originating bank, match the two messages on arrival, and then credit the beneficiary.

Reconciliation. The nostro debit matches the cover settlement, the suspense entry is reversed when the beneficiary is credited, and the payment closes with a two day delay recorded.

SC-22: Customer payment message arriving before the cover

Customer instruction arriving before the cover funds · United Kingdom to Australia, SWIFT · USD 54000 · Meridian Bank London to Pacific Rim Bank Sydney

Messages. MT103, MT202 COV

Positive path. This scenario is set before the CBPR+ migration, so the payment travels as an MT103 with an MT202 COV for the cover leg. The same payment today travels as a pacs.008 with a pacs.009 COV for the cover leg. Coral Supplies instructs Meridian Bank London to pay USD 54,000 to a supplier at Pacific Rim Bank Sydney. The MT103 reaches Pacific Rim Bank first, so the bank holds it as pending cover and waits for the funds. The MT202 COV arrives later the same day through Harbour Bank Singapore with the same reference and UETR. Pacific Rim Bank matches the cover to the MT103, screens the payment, and credits the supplier on the value date. Meridian Bank reconciles the nostro debit with no break.

What went wrong. Coral Supplies instructs the same USD 54,000 payment, but the MT202 COV is rejected by Harbour Bank because the settlement account is wrong, so the cover never arrives. Pacific Rim Bank holds the MT103 as pending cover until the two business day timeout expires, then returns the instruction to Meridian Bank with the reason. Meridian Bank corrects the settlement account, resends the cover and the MT103 with a revised value date, and the supplier is paid three days late.

Root cause. The MT202 COV named a settlement account that the intermediary did not recognise, so the cover was rejected and the MT103 waited past the cover timeout. Recovery. Operations correct the settlement account from the standing settlement instruction, resend the MT202 COV and the MT103 with a revised value date, and confirm settlement with the clearing system.

Reconciliation. The nostro debit matches the cover settlement, the pending cover hold is released when the cover arrives, and the payment closes with a three day delay recorded.

Source status: illustrative

SC-23: Non-connected BIC forwarding through another SWIFT participant

Payment forwarded through a connected SWIFT participant · United Kingdom to Switzerland, SWIFT · EUR 47000 · Meridian Bank London to Alpine Bank Zurich

Messages. MT103

Positive path. This scenario is set before the CBPR+ migration, so the payment travels as an MT103. The same payment today travels as a pacs.008. Helvetia Holdings instructs Meridian Bank London to pay EUR 47,000 to a supplier at Alpine Bank Zurich, which holds no RMA with Meridian Bank. Meridian Bank sends the MT103 to Harbour Bank Singapore, a SWIFT participant that holds RMA with both Meridian Bank and Alpine Bank. Harbour Bank forwards the MT103 to Alpine Bank and settles the funds from its nostro account. Alpine Bank credits the supplier on the value date, and Meridian Bank reconciles the nostro debit with no break.

What went wrong. Helvetia Holdings instructs the same EUR 47,000 payment, but Meridian Bank sends the MT103 directly to Alpine Bank Zurich without checking RMA, so the SWIFT network blocks the message and it never arrives. The supplier is not paid on the value date, and Meridian Bank must obtain the forwarding route, resend the MT103 through Harbour Bank Singapore, and explain the delay to Helvetia Holdings while the supplier waits for the funds.

Root cause. Alpine Bank Zurich held no RMA with Meridian Bank London, so a direct MT103 could not be delivered and the payment had to be forwarded through a connected participant. Recovery. Operations select Harbour Bank Singapore as the forwarding bank, confirm its RMA with both banks, and resend the MT103 through Harbour Bank.

Reconciliation. The nostro debit matches the forwarding bank statement, the beneficiary credit matches the MT103 amount, and the payment closes after the forwarded payment is credited.

Source status: illustrative
